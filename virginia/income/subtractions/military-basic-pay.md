# Military Basic Pay (Schedule ADJ, Lines 6b-6d, code 38)
## Description
Military service personnel on extended active duty for a period in excess of 90 consecutive days may subtract up to $15,000 of military basic pay received during the taxable year, using code 38. Personnel stationed inside or outside Virginia are eligible. It is one of Virginia's three military subtractions.
## Amount and phase-out
- Basic pay of $15,000 or less: the entire amount may be subtracted
- Basic pay over $15,000: the $15,000 maximum is reduced $1.00 for every $1.00 over $15,000
- Basic pay of $30,000 or more: no subtraction
- On joint returns, each spouse can qualify
## Example
Basic pay of $22,000: $15,000 - ($22,000 - $15,000) = $8,000 subtraction.
## Conditions
- Only military basic pay included in federal AGI
- Not pay included in another subtraction, such as the Virginia National Guard subtraction (code 28)
## Mutually exclusive items
If you claim this subtraction, you cannot claim the Credit for Low-Income Individuals or the Refundable Virginia Earned Income Credit.
## Questions
- Were you on active duty for more than 90 consecutive days?
- What was your basic pay for 2026 (not total pay with allowances)?
- Are you also claiming the National Guard or combat zone subtraction?
- Are you claiming the Virginia low-income credit or earned income credit?
## Common Errors
- Using total military pay instead of basic pay when applying the phase-out
- Forgetting the dollar-for-dollar reduction above $15,000
- Claiming the same pay under code 38 and code 28
- Claiming the low-income credit or refundable Virginia EIC along with this subtraction
## Prompt
- I'm active duty Army stationed out of state; my basic pay is about $25,000.
