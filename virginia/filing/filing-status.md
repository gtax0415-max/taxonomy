# Filing Status (Form 760 header)
## Description
Virginia has three filing status codes, entered in the box on page 1: 1 = Single, 2 = Joint, 3 = Married filing separately. In most cases the Virginia status matches the federal one. Virginia has no separate head of household status; a federal head of household files as Filing Status 1 and fills in the "Federal head of household? Filing Status 1 only YES" oval.
## What each status controls
| Item | FS 1 Single | FS 2 Joint | FS 3 Separate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Filing threshold (VAGI) | $11,950 | $23,900 combined | $11,950 |
| Standard deduction (Line 11) | $8,750 | $17,500 | $8,750 |
| Spouse Tax Adjustment (Line 17) | no | yes, up to $259 | no |
| Line 19b spouse withholding | no | yes | no |
## Filing Status 3 details
- Do not put the spouse's name in the spouse name field. Enter the spouse's SSN in the Spouse's Social Security Number box at the top and the spouse's name on the line beside the Filing Status code box.
- Line 1 carries only the federal adjusted gross income attributable to you.
- Each spouse determines exemptions as if separate federal returns were filed. Dependent exemptions that cannot be accounted for separately are allocated in proportion to income; as a general rule the spouse claiming a dependent must report at least half of total federal AGI. Nobody claims less than a whole exemption, and the same dependent cannot be on both returns.
- Itemized deductions that cannot be accounted for separately are split by each spouse's share of joint federal AGI. If one spouse itemizes, the other must also itemize.
## Filing Status 2 details
- Enter both names, SSNs, first four letters of each last name and both birth dates, in the same order.
- Both spouses must sign. Filing jointly makes each spouse jointly and severally liable for the tax, and any refund is paid jointly.
- One Virginia resident and one nonresident may file jointly only if both elect to compute joint Virginia taxable income as if both were residents. Otherwise the resident files Form 760 using Filing Status 3 and the nonresident files Form 763 if there is Virginia source income.
## Married taxpayers and the age deduction
For the income-based age deduction, married taxpayers always use combined income of both spouses, whether filing jointly or separately.
## Questions
- What filing status did you use on your federal return?
- Did you check head of household federally?
- Is your spouse a Virginia resident for all of 2026?
- If filing separately, which of you can document each dependent and each itemized expense?
## Common Errors
- Leaving the federal head of household oval blank for a federal head of household filer
- Entering a Filing Status 3 spouse in the spouse name field instead of beside the status box
- Filing jointly with a nonresident spouse without making the joint residency election
- One spouse itemizing while the other takes the standard deduction on separate returns
- Claiming the same dependent on both separate returns
## Prompt
- I file head of household federally; what do I pick in Virginia?
- My wife lives in another state; can we file a joint Virginia return?
- We're filing separately; how do we split our kids and deductions?
