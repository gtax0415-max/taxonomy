# Dependent on Another's Return (Form 760 header oval and Line 11)
## Description
Fill in the "Dependent on another's return" oval if you can be claimed as a dependent on someone else's return. The oval matters because a dependent's Virginia standard deduction is limited: it cannot exceed the dependent's earned income. The test is whether you can be claimed on another taxpayer's federal return, not whether they actually claim you.
## The limited standard deduction
If you can be claimed as a dependent on the federal return of another taxpayer, your standard deduction is limited to the amount of your earned income. Enter on Line 11 the smaller of:
- your earned income, or
- the standard deduction for your filing status ($8,750 for Filing Status 1 or 3, $17,500 for Filing Status 2).
A dependent whose earned income is below the standard deduction amount enters the earned income figure on Line 11.
## Filing threshold for dependents
A dependent still files only if VAGI reaches the threshold. The filing threshold for a single individual ($11,950) also applies to dependent children (2025 Form 760F instructions; confirm against the 2026 form). A dependent below the threshold files to get back Virginia withholding.
## Interaction with other lines
- The oval is informational; the dollar effect is on Line 11 only.
- If you claimed the standard deduction federally you must claim it for Virginia; if you itemized federally you must itemize for Virginia (Line 10).
- Exemptions on Line 12 are claimed in the exemption boxes; see deductions/exemptions/ for who may claim them.
## Questions
- Can your parents or anyone else claim you as a dependent on their federal return?
- How much did you earn from work (wages or self-employment) in 2026?
- Did you have unearned income such as interest or dividends?
- Did you have Virginia tax withheld?
## Common Errors
- Leaving the oval blank for a student or other dependent
- Taking the full $8,750 standard deduction when earned income is lower
- Treating interest or dividend income as earned income for the limit
- Not filing when below the threshold and losing a withholding refund
