# Active Duty Military Pay
## Description
Subtraction for pay received from the federal government for military service performed on ACTIVE DUTY. It applies to members of the armed forces, the reserves, and the national guard while in active duty status.

## Rules for 2026
- Subtract active duty pay to the extent it is in federal taxable income
- Applies whether the servicemember is an Iowa resident or not
- Combat zone pay already excluded federally is not subtracted again
- Pay for inactive-duty training (weekend drills) is not active duty pay

## Notes
- Civilian wages from a second job remain taxable
- A military spouse's own wages are not covered here; spouses may have separate residency protections under the federal Military Spouses Residency Relief Act and Veterans Benefits and Transition Act
- Iowa residents stationed elsewhere still enter their Iowa county and school district number (see return-header.md)
- Unchanged for 2026. Line number follows the 2025 Schedule 1

## Required Information
- Military W-2s
- Active duty dates for reserve and guard members

## Questions
- Were you on active duty in the armed forces, reserves, or national guard in 2026?
- For guard or reserve members, which pay was for active duty and which for drill?

## Common Errors
- Subtracting weekend drill pay
- Subtracting combat pay already excluded federally
- Subtracting a spouse's civilian wages

## Prompt
- I'm in the Army stationed in Texas. Do I owe Iowa tax on my pay?
- I was activated with the Iowa National Guard for six months.
