# Military Retirement Pay
## Description
Subtraction for military retirement benefits included in federal taxable income. It applies at ANY age, unlike the general retirement income exclusion.

## What qualifies
- Retired pay for service in the armed forces, reserves, or national guard
- Military Survivor Benefit Plan (SBP) annuities paid to a surviving spouse or other beneficiary (10 U.S.C. 1447)
- Applies to residents and nonresidents

## Amount
- The amount included in federal taxable income (Form 1099-R box 2a, as reported on Form 1040)
- No dollar cap

## Notes
- VA disability compensation is already excluded federally, so there is nothing to subtract
- Federal civil service (OPM) pensions are NOT military retirement; they go on line 7 if the recipient qualifies by age, disability, or survivor status
- Military retirement is also left out of the filing threshold and alternate tax calculations
- Unchanged for 2026. Line number follows the 2025 Schedule 1

## Required Information
- Form 1099-R from DFAS for retired pay or SBP
- Whether any portion is VA disability (excluded federally)

## Questions
- Do you receive military retired pay or a Survivor Benefit Plan annuity?
- Do you also receive a civilian federal pension?

## Common Errors
- Putting military retired pay on line 7 and applying the age-55 test
- Subtracting a civilian federal pension here
- Subtracting VA disability pay that is not in federal income

## Prompt
- I'm a 48-year-old military retiree. Is my pension taxed in Iowa?
- I receive a Survivor Benefit Plan annuity from my late husband.
