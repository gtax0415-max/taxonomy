# Interest from U.S. Government Securities
## Description
Subtraction for interest on obligations of the United States that federal law exempts from state tax. The interest is in federal taxable income, so Iowa removes it here.

## What qualifies
- U.S. Treasury bills, notes, and bonds
- U.S. savings bonds: Series E, EE, F, G, H, HH, and I (Form 1099-INT box 3)
- Bonds of Guam, Puerto Rico, the U.S. Virgin Islands, and the Northern Mariana Islands
- Listed federal agency obligations, including Farm Credit System banks, Federal Home Loan Banks, Tennessee Valley Authority, Resolution Funding Corporation, Financing Corporation, FDIC, Sallie Mae, Commodity Credit Corporation, and the U.S. Postal Service

## What does NOT qualify
- Ginnie Mae, Fannie Mae, and Freddie Mac securities, Farmer Mac, FHA, Export-Import Bank, SBA
- World Bank, Asian Development Bank, and Inter-American Development Bank bonds
- Interest on federal tax refunds
- Credit union, savings and loan, and bank deposits, even if federally insured
- Interest from repurchase agreements of U.S. securities

## Notes
- Subtract only to the extent the interest is in federal taxable income. Savings bond interest excluded federally for education (Form 8815) is not subtracted again
- Married filing separately: split by ownership; joint accounts split equally
- Unchanged for 2026. Line number follows the 2025 Schedule 1

## Required Information
- Form 1099-INT box 3
- Issuer of each security in box 1 interest that might be a qualifying agency

## Questions
- Do you own U.S. savings bonds or Treasury securities?
- Does your brokerage statement show interest from Farm Credit or Federal Home Loan Bank bonds?

## Common Errors
- Subtracting Ginnie Mae or Fannie Mae interest
- Subtracting interest on a federal tax refund
- Subtracting savings bond interest already excluded federally

## Prompt
- I cashed in Series EE savings bonds this year.
- Is Treasury bill interest taxed in Iowa?
