# Dividends Attributable to Federal Securities
## Description
Subtraction for the part of a mutual fund's taxable dividends that comes from DIRECT U.S. government obligations, such as Treasury securities held by a government bond or money market fund.

## Amount
- Taxable dividends from the fund × the fund's published percentage of income from direct federal securities
- Allowed only if the fund provides a statement giving that percentage. Attach it to a paper return

## What does NOT qualify
- Income from Ginnie Mae, Fannie Mae, or Freddie Mac securities held by the fund
- Income from repurchase agreements involving federal securities
- Capital gain distributions

## Notes
- Many fund companies publish a "U.S. government obligations" percentage table each January
- Married filing separately: divide by registered ownership; jointly held shares split equally
- Unchanged for 2026. Line number follows the 2025 Schedule 1

## Required Information
- Form 1099-DIV for each fund
- Fund's U.S. government obligation percentage for 2026

## Questions
- Do you own a Treasury, government bond, or government money market fund?
- Did the fund publish a U.S. government obligation percentage?

## Common Errors
- Applying the percentage to capital gain distributions
- Claiming it without the fund's percentage statement
- Treating mortgage-backed agency income as federal obligation income

## Prompt
- My money market fund holds Treasury bills. Can I subtract any dividends?
- How do I find the U.S. government percentage for my mutual fund?
