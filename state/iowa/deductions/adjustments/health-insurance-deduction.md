# Health Insurance Deduction (Age 65 or Older)
## Description
Subtraction for health and dental insurance premiums paid with after-tax dollars, limited to taxpayers age 65 or older with worksheet income under $100,000. Since 2023 it is no longer available to younger taxpayers.

## Amount
- 100% of post-tax premiums for health insurance, dental insurance, Medicare Part B, Medicare Part D, and long-term care coverage
- Reduce by any Health Reimbursement Arrangement (HRA) reimbursement
- If premiums were included in federal itemized medical expenses, only the portion disallowed by the 7.5%-of-AGI floor can be deducted, using the ratio of premiums to total medical expenses on federal Schedule A line 1
- 2025 excess advance premium tax credit repaid on the 2025 federal return may be included in the 2026 deduction for an eligible taxpayer

## Eligibility
- The taxpayer paying the premium is age 65 or older. A 65-year-old may deduct premiums covering a younger spouse or a dependent, but premiums paid by the under-65 spouse do not qualify
- Worksheet income must be LESS than $100,000. The $100,000 test is a cliff and is the SAME for every filing status, including married filing jointly
- Worksheet income = Iowa taxable income plus the federal standard or itemized deduction, the federal QBI deduction, the federal enhanced senior deduction (OBBBA, 2025–2028), Social Security on Form 1040 line 6b, excluded retirement income, and this deduction itself

## Notes
- Federal Schedule A line numbers cited here are from the 2025 form. The 2026 Schedule A is being redesigned (two pages, with the new 0.5% charitable floor), so confirm the line numbers on the final 2026 form
- Federal Schedule 1 line numbers cited here are from the 2025 form; the 2026 Schedule 1 was not available to verify, so confirm them when the final 2026 form is released
- Exclude premiums paid pre-tax through an employer plan or already deducted federally as self-employed health insurance (federal Schedule 1 line 17)
- Do not include Medicare tax withheld on Form W-2
- The new federal senior deduction must be added back in the income test, which pushes some filers over $100,000
- Married filing separately: allocate based on who paid; joint-account payments are split by each spouse's Iowa taxable income
- No 2026 Iowa law change. Line number follows the 2025 Schedule 1

## Required Information
- Age of each spouse on December 31, 2026
- Premiums by type and who paid them
- Whether each premium was paid pre-tax or post-tax
- Federal Schedule A medical detail and Schedule 1 line 17 amount
- HRA reimbursements

## Questions
- Are you or your spouse 65 or older?
- Do you pay Medicare Part B or D, Medigap, dental, or long-term care premiums yourself?
- Are any premiums paid through payroll before tax?
- Did you itemize medical expenses on your federal return?

## Common Errors
- Claiming it for a taxpayer under 65
- Doubling the $100,000 limit for married filing jointly
- Leaving the federal senior deduction out of the income test
- Deducting pre-tax employer premiums or self-employed health insurance already deducted federally
- Deducting the full premium when part was already used on federal Schedule A

- I'm 67 and pay Medicare Part B and a supplement plan.
