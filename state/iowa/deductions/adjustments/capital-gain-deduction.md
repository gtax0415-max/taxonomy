# Iowa Capital Gain Deduction (IA 100)
## Description
Subtraction for net capital gain from a narrow set of farm and closely held business sales. Since 2023 the deduction is mostly limited to farming; the old deductions for non-farm business real property, sale of a business, timber, and ESOP stock sales were REPEALED.

## What qualifies for 2026
- REAL PROPERTY USED IN A FARMING BUSINESS: held at least 10 years and the taxpayer materially participated in a farming business for at least 10 years (IA 100H)
- RETIRED FARMER LIFETIME ELECTION: a retired farmer (55 or older, or disabled, and no longer materially participating) who materially participated for 10 years in the aggregate may make a single, irrevocable election to deduct gain from cattle or horses, breeding livestock, and farm real property (IA 100G)
- QUALIFIED CORPORATION CAPITAL STOCK: an employee-owner's one-time election for stock of a qualified Iowa corporation; 100% of qualifying gain for tax years beginning in 2025 and later (IA 100J)

## What does NOT qualify
- Investment property, stocks, mutual funds, rental property not used in farming
- Non-farm business real property, timber, and sale of a non-farm business (repealed from 2023)
- Sales of employer securities to an Iowa ESOP (repealed from 2023)
- Assets sold by a C corporation

## Notes
- A retired farmer who makes the IA 100G election cannot later claim the farm tenancy exclusion (Schedule 1 line 18) or the beginning farmer credit, and vice versa
- Material participation follows IRC section 469(h) and the federal regulations
- Installment sales: claim the deduction each year the gain is reported, and file the IA 100 each year
- Married filing separately: divide by ownership; each spouse files a separate IA 100
- No 2026 Iowa law change. Line number follows the 2025 Schedule 1

## Required Information
- Description, acquisition date, and sale date of each asset
- Federal gain from Form 4797 / Schedule D
- Years of material participation and the farming activity
- Whether the taxpayer is a retired farmer and whether an election was already made

## Questions
- Did you sell farmland, livestock, or stock in a family-owned Iowa corporation in 2026?
- How long did you own the property, and how many years did you actively farm?
- Are you 55 or older (or disabled) and no longer farming?
- Have you ever made a retired farmer election or farm tenancy exclusion election?

## Common Errors
- Claiming the deduction for timber, non-farm business property, or investment assets under pre-2023 rules
- Counting years of ownership but not years of material participation
- Making the retired farmer election after already electing the farm tenancy exclusion
- Not filing the IA 100 for each year of an installment sale

## Prompt
- I sold farmland I have farmed for 30 years.
- I'm a retired farmer and sold my cattle herd.
