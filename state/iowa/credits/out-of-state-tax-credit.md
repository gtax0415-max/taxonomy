# Out-of-State Tax Credit (IA 130)
## Description
Nonrefundable credit for income tax paid to another state, a local jurisdiction outside Iowa, or a foreign country on income that Iowa also taxes. It prevents double taxation of residents.

## How it is computed
- Income taxed elsewhere (earned while an Iowa resident) ÷ total Iowa income, capped at 100%
- × Iowa tax after credits (IA 1040 line 14) = limit
- Credit = lesser of the limit or the tax actually paid to the other jurisdiction
- Tax paid by a pass-through entity or mutual fund on your share counts if you have the entity's schedule or a 1099-DIV statement

## Rules for 2026
- Full-year residents, and part-year residents for income earned while an Iowa resident; NOT nonresidents
- One IA 130 per jurisdiction (mutual fund foreign tax can be combined)
- Attach a copy of the other jurisdiction's return
- Withholding and estimated payments are not "tax paid"; use the final tax liability after that jurisdiction's credits
- Foreign taxes claimed for the federal foreign tax credit (Form 1116) are not used again here
- S corporation income already apportioned out through the IA 134 credit (code 11) cannot be used again
- Iowa–Illinois reciprocity: Illinois does not tax Iowa residents' Illinois wages, so no credit arises for those wages

## Source note
- The other state's return is a non-Iowa state form; it is the taxpayer's own filed return and the only proof of tax paid there

## Required Information
- Other-state returns and final tax, W-2 state boxes, entity schedules, Form 1116

## Questions
- Did you pay income tax to another state, city, or country in 2026?
- Do you own a pass-through entity that paid other states' tax for you?

## Common Errors
- Using withholding instead of the other state's final tax
- Claiming foreign tax already used for the federal foreign tax credit
- Claiming a credit for Illinois wages under reciprocity

## Prompt
- I live in Iowa but work in Nebraska and pay Nebraska tax.
