# Filing Status
## Description
Iowa filing status must MATCH the federal filing status. Iowa no longer allows married couples to choose separate filing on the combined return; that option ended with the 2023 restructuring.

## Statuses
- 1 Single — also answer whether you were claimed as a dependent on another person's Iowa return (see dependent-filer.md)
- 2 Married filing jointly — both sign; both are jointly and severally liable unless one qualifies for federal innocent spouse relief (IRC 6015)
- 3 Married filing separately — enter the spouse's name and SSN; the spouse's net income may be needed for the exemption tests (see married-filing-separately.md)
- 4 Head of household — see head-of-household.md
- 5 Qualifying surviving spouse — see qualifying-surviving-spouse.md

## How status is used for 2026
- Personal credit count: 1 for statuses 1, 3, and 5; 2 for statuses 2 and 4 ($40 each)
- Filing threshold: more than $9,000 for single and married filing separately ($24,000 if 65 or older); more than $13,500 for married filing jointly, head of household, and qualifying surviving spouse ($32,000 if either spouse is 65 or older). These amounts are set in statute and not indexed
- The tax rate is a flat 3.8% for every status, so status no longer changes the rate

## Required Information
- Federal filing status
- Spouse name and SSN

## Questions
- What filing status did you use on your 2026 federal return?
- Were you married on December 31, 2026?
- If single, did someone claim you as a dependent?

## Common Errors
- Choosing a different status for Iowa than for federal
- Leaving the dependent question blank for a single filer (the return is then treated as a dependent's)
- Married filing jointly on Iowa after filing separately federally

## Prompt
- Can we file separately on Iowa if we filed jointly federally?
- What filing status do I use on my Iowa return?
