# Fiscal or Short Tax Year
## Description
An individual whose federal tax year is not calendar year 2026 enters the beginning and ending dates of that year at the top of the IA 1040. Iowa follows the federal tax year.

## Rules
- The Iowa year must be the same as the federal year
- Fiscal-year individuals are rare; a short year usually arises from death or an IRS-approved change in accounting period
- Due date: the last day of the fourth month after the year ends (the calendar-year equivalent is April 30, 2027 for a 2026 return)
- A decedent's final return covers January 1 through the date of death, but it is filed on the regular calendar-year form and due date; enter the date of death in Step 10 rather than treating it as a fiscal year

## Notes
- Year-specific amounts (529 limit, filing thresholds, dates for the 65-or-older test) follow the form year the fiscal year begins in. Confirm with IDR before preparing a true fiscal-year return
- Unchanged for 2026. Step references follow the 2025 IA 1040

## Required Information
- Federal tax year beginning and ending dates
- Reason for the non-calendar year

## Questions
- Is your federal tax year anything other than January 1 to December 31, 2026?
- Did the IRS approve a change in your accounting period?

## Common Errors
- Treating a decedent's final return as a short fiscal year
- Using a different year for Iowa than for federal
- Missing the fiscal-year due date by assuming April 30

## Prompt
- I file on a fiscal year. How do I show that on my Iowa return?
- Is my late husband's final return a short-year return?
