# Residency: Full-year Resident
## Description
Determines whether the IA 1040 alone is filed (full-year resident) or the IA 1040 with IA 126 (nonresident or part-year resident). This taxonomy covers FULL-YEAR RESIDENTS ONLY (team decision 2026-09-30); nonresident and part-year residents are flagged for review.

## Who is a full-year resident for 2026
- Domiciled in Iowa all year (Iowa is the permanent home you intend to return to), OR
- Not domiciled in Iowa but maintained a permanent place of abode in Iowa and spent more than 183 days of the year in Iowa
- Servicemembers keep Iowa residency while stationed elsewhere if Iowa is their home of record

## Out of scope (review row)
- Part-year residents (moved in or out during 2026) and nonresidents with Iowa-source income file IA 126; the credit flows to line 13
- Nonresidents must file if Iowa-source net income is $1,000 or more, unless total income is under the general thresholds
- Iowa–Illinois reciprocity: Illinois residents working in Iowa owe Iowa no tax on wages, and vice versa

## Notes
- Income taxed by another state is handled through IA 130 (out-of-state tax credit, line 15) for residents, not through Schedule 1
- Unchanged for 2026

## Required Information
- State of domicile and any change during 2026
- Addresses of all homes and days spent in Iowa
- W-2 state boxes for any non-Iowa wages

## Questions
- Did you live in Iowa all of 2026?
- Did you move into or out of Iowa during 2026?
- Did you earn income in another state?

## Common Errors
- Treating a part-year resident as a full-year resident
- Omitting IA 126 for a nonresident
- Using Schedule 1 to remove out-of-state income instead of IA 126 or IA 130

## Prompt
- I moved to Iowa from Nebraska in August.
- Am I an Iowa resident if I live here half the year?
