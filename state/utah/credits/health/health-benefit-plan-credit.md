# Health Benefit Plan Credit
## Description
Credit for individuals who buy their own health insurance and were NOT eligible for an employer-funded plan (UCA §59-10-1023).

## Amount
1. Amount paid for a health benefit plan in 2026
2. + excess advance premium tax credit repayment from 2025 Schedule 2, line 2 (only if the credit was claimed on the 2025 Utah return)
3. − net premium tax credit from 2025 Schedule 3, line 9 (same condition)
4. − excluded amounts
5. × 4.45%
6. Maximum: $300 single with no dependents, $600 married filing jointly with no dependents, $900 any filer with dependents
- Credit = lesser of line 5 or line 6

## Eligibility
- Neither spouse could get coverage through an employer or former employer, even if declined

## Included
- Marketplace (ACA) premiums you paid
- Medicare Part A (if you must pay), Part B, Part C (Advantage) and Part D premiums
- Medicaid-connected plans (your share only), COBRA

## Excluded
- Amounts used for the federal Health Coverage Tax Credit (listed in the Utah instructions; that federal credit expired after 2021, so this rarely applies in 2026)
- HSA, Archer MSA, FSA or HRA payments
- Pre-tax cafeteria plan amounts
- Self-employed health insurance deducted federally
- Amounts deducted on Schedule A
- Amounts paid by a third party (e.g., Medicaid subsidies, premium tax credit)
- Medigap, CHIP and PCN fees
- Dental, vision, long-term care, disease-specific and fixed indemnity plans

## Notes
- No carryforward

## Required Information
- Premium records, employer-coverage eligibility, federal deductions taken

## Questions
- Did you buy your own health insurance in 2026?
- Could you or your spouse have gotten insurance through an employer?
- Did you deduct any premiums federally?

## Common Errors
- Claiming while eligible for employer coverage
- Including premiums deducted as self-employed health insurance
- Including Medigap premiums
- Including the premium tax credit portion

## Prompt
- I buy my own insurance on the Marketplace.
- Do my Medicare premiums qualify for a Utah credit?
