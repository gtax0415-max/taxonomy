# Interest from Utah Municipal Bonds and U.S. Government Obligations
## Description
States may not tax interest on U.S. government obligations, and Utah exempts its own municipal bond interest. When either is included in federal AGI, subtract it.

## Amount
- U.S. government obligation interest/dividends included in federal AGI
- Plus Utah municipal bond interest that IS in federal AGI (e.g., Build America Bonds)
- Minus related interest expense on money borrowed to buy them

## Qualifies
- Treasury bills, notes and bonds
- Series E, EE, H, HH and I savings bonds
- The part of a mutual fund dividend from direct U.S. obligations

## Does NOT qualify
- FNMA (Fannie Mae) and GNMA (Ginnie Mae) interest or dividends
- Interest on IRS or other federal refunds
- Ordinary tax-exempt Utah muni interest that is already outside federal AGI

## Notes
- The test (Smith v. Davis): a written, interest-bearing, binding U.S. promise to pay, backed by the full faith and credit of the U.S.
- See Utah Pub 33

## Required Information
- 1099-INT box 3, 1099-DIV with fund percentages
- Any related interest expense

## Questions
- Did you earn interest on Treasury bills, notes, bonds or U.S. savings bonds?
- Do you own a fund that holds Treasury securities?
- Do you hold any taxable Utah municipal bonds?

## Common Errors
- Subtracting Fannie Mae or Ginnie Mae income
- Subtracting the whole fund dividend instead of the U.S.-obligation percentage
- Subtracting ordinary tax-exempt Utah muni interest that was never in AGI
- Not netting related interest expense

## Prompt
- I cashed in I bonds this year.
- My Treasury money market fund paid dividends.
