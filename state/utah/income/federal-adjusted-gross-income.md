# Federal Adjusted Gross Income
## Description
Utah's starting point. All income — wages, interest, dividends, capital gains, business, farm, rental, retirement, Social Security, unemployment and other income — reaches Utah through federal AGI. Utah then adds (line 3), subtracts (lines 5 and 6), and taxes the result at a flat 4.45% for 2026.

## Amount
- Line 2 = federal AGI from Form 1040, 1040-SR or 1040-NR, line 11a
- NONRESIDENTS and PART-YEAR RESIDENTS: enter the FULL federal AGI, not just Utah income; the Utah share is figured on TC-40_B
- Line 4 total income = line 2 + line 3
- Line 7 Utah taxable income = line 4 − (line 5 + line 6)
- Line 8 tax = line 7 × 4.45% (2025: 4.5%, reduced by SB 60 in the 2026 session)

## Notes
- Report AGI exactly as on the federal return; do not adjust it to gain a Utah advantage (for example, choosing the federal foreign tax credit over the foreign earned income exclusion loses the Utah benefit, because federal credits do not reduce Utah tax)
- Federal deductions claimed AFTER AGI (the new 2025–2028 Schedule 1-A deductions for tips, overtime, car loan interest and seniors) do not reduce line 2. Line 10 takes only the federal standard or itemized deduction (Form 1040 line 12e); the senior deduction enters only the line 19 exempt-taxpayer test
- Federal net operating losses are treated the same on the Utah return

## Required Information
- 2026 federal return, line 11a

## Questions
- What is the adjusted gross income on line 11a of your 2026 federal return?
- Has your federal return been amended or changed by the IRS?

## Common Errors
- Using taxable income (line 15) or total income (line 9) instead of AGI
- Nonresidents entering only Utah income on line 2
- Using a 2025 rate (4.5%) on line 8
- Assuming federal tips or overtime deductions lower Utah income

## Prompt
- Where does my Utah return start?
- Does Utah tax my tips and overtime now that they're deductible federally?
