# Qualifying Surviving Spouse
## Description
Follows the federal qualifying surviving spouse status (spouse died in one of the two prior years and a dependent child lives at home). Utah applies joint-level amounts.

## 2026 amounts
- Taxpayer tax credit base phase-out (line 15): $37,392 (same as joint)
- Line 19 exempt-taxpayer worksheet standard deduction: $32,200
- TC-150 thresholds for QSS:
  - Child Tax Credit (AN002PT): $61,000
  - Retirement Credit (AN003PT): $32,000
  - Social Security Benefits Credit (AN005PT): $90,000
- Adoption credit AGI band: single band, NOT joint ($27,500–$55,000 nonrefundable)

## Notes
- For 2026, the spouse must have died in 2024 or 2025 (federal rule)
- A 1040-NR filer with federal QSS status marks QSS on the TC-40, not Single
- If the spouse died in 2026, file jointly instead; the surviving spouse signs and writes "DECEASED" in the spouse's signature block

## Required Information
- Federal QSS status
- Year of spouse's death

## Questions
- Did your spouse die in 2024 or 2025, and do you have a dependent child at home?
- Did you file as qualifying surviving spouse federally?

## Common Errors
- Using QSS when the spouse died in 2026 (should be joint)
- Using the joint adoption-credit band
- Using single-level phase-out amounts

## Prompt
- My husband died last year and I have a child at home. What status do I use in Utah?
