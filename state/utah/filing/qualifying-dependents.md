# Qualifying Dependents
## Description
Utah counts dependents for whom you could claim a federal child tax credit or credit for other dependents (IRC §24). The total on line 1 drives the Utah personal exemption on line 9, which feeds the taxpayer tax credit.

## Amount
- Line 1a: qualifying dependents age 16 or younger on December 31, 2026
- Line 1b: other persons, not in 1a, for whom you could claim a dependent tax credit (not yourself or your spouse)
- Line 1c: dependents in 1a born in 2026 (counted twice — see dependent-born-in-2026.md)
- Line 1 = 1a + 1b + 1c
- Line 9 personal exemption: line 1 × $2,167 for 2026 (2025: $2,111)
- Line 9 + federal deduction (line 10) − state income tax itemized (line 12) = line 13; × 6% = initial taxpayer tax credit (line 14)

## Eligibility
- The person must be a dependent for whom you were allowed an IRC §24 credit on your federal return
- Utah personal exemption is only for dependents — NOT for the filer or spouse

## Notes
- Line 1a ages also feed the Utah Child Tax Credit (AN002PT, TC-150 worksheet), which has its own younger age limit
- Line 1 replaces the 2025 line 2 structure; numbering changed on the 2026 form

## Required Information
- Federal Dependents section
- Each dependent's birth date

## Questions
- How many dependents did you claim on your 2026 federal return?
- How old was each on December 31, 2026?
- Were any born in 2026?

## Common Errors
- Counting yourself or your spouse on line 1b
- Counting a dependent who did not qualify for an IRC §24 credit
- Using the 2025 exemption of $2,111
- Putting a 17-year-old on line 1a

## Prompt
- I have three kids, ages 2, 10 and 18. How do I fill in line 1?
