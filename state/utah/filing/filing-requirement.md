# Utah Filing Requirement
## Description
Utah's filing requirement follows the federal one. Complete the federal return first, even if it is not filed with the IRS, because the Utah return starts from it.

## Who must file
- A Utah resident or part-year resident who must file a federal return
- A nonresident or part-year resident with Utah-source income who must file a federal return
- Anyone who wants a refund of Utah tax overpaid (e.g., withholding)

## Exceptions (no return needed)
- Nonresident whose ONLY Utah income is from a partnership, S corporation or trust that withheld Utah tax on it
- Nonresident (and spouse) who worked in Utah 20 days or less, has no other Utah income, and lives in a state with no income tax or that does not tax nonresident wages. Does NOT apply to professional athletes, entertainers, persons of prominence, real property contractors/laborers, or certain key/highly paid employees

## Exempt from tax (still may file)
- Qualified exempt taxpayer: Utah taxable income at or below the federal standard deduction (2026: $16,100 single / $24,150 HOH / $32,200 joint), plus any federal enhanced senior deduction. New basis for 2026: Utah taxable income instead of federal AGI (HB 60)

## Due dates
- April 15, 2027 for calendar 2026
- Automatic 6-month extension to file (October 15, 2027); NOT an extension to pay
- Extension prepayment safe harbors: 90% of 2026 tax, or 100% of 2025 tax liability
- Utah does not require quarterly estimated payments

## Required Information
- Federal return
- Residency status
- Utah-source income and withholding

## Questions
- Do you have to file a 2026 federal return?
- Were you a Utah resident, or did you have Utah income?
- Was Utah tax withheld that you want refunded?

## Common Errors
- Not filing a Utah return to recover withholding
- Treating the extension as an extension to pay
- Assuming the 20-day rule applies to professional athletes or contractors

## Prompt
- Do I need to file a Utah return?
- I worked in Utah for two weeks but live in Nevada.
