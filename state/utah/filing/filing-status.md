# Filing Status
## Description
Utah generally copies the federal filing status. The status drives the taxpayer tax credit phase-out (line 15), the exempt-taxpayer test (line 19) and the income thresholds for several credits.

## Rules
Mark one box:
- Single
- Married filing jointly
- Married filing separately
- Head of household
- Qualifying surviving spouse
- (Special instructions) — certain married couples where spouses' federal and Utah situations differ (see the Special Instructions for Married Couples section)

Federal Form 1040-NR filers:
- Mark "Single", regardless of marital status
- EXCEPTION: a 1040-NR filer with qualifying surviving spouse status marks "Qualifying surviving spouse"

## 2026 amounts tied to filing status
| Status | Line 15 base phase-out | Line 19 worksheet standard deduction |
|---|---|---|
| Single | $18,696 | $16,100 |
| Married filing jointly | $37,392 | $32,200 |
| Married filing separately | $18,696 | $16,100, or $0 if spouse itemized |
| Head of household | $28,045 | $24,150 |
| Qualifying surviving spouse | $37,392 | $32,200 |

The standard deduction figures are the 2026 federal amounts (2025: $15,750 / $31,500 / $23,625).

## Required Information
- Federal filing status
- Which federal form was filed (1040, 1040-SR, 1040-NR)

## Questions
- What filing status did you use on your 2026 federal return?
- Did you file Form 1040-NR?

## Common Errors
- Choosing a different status than the federal return without a special-instructions reason
- 1040-NR filers marking "Married"
- Using 2025 phase-out or standard deduction figures

## Prompt
- Do I use the same filing status in Utah as federally?
- I file 1040-NR and I'm married — what status do I mark?
