# Pass-Through Withholding and Pass-Through Entity Tax Credit — Lines 27 and 28
## Description
Two different ways an Illinois partnership, S corporation, or trust pays Illinois tax for you, both claimed as payments. Line 27 is WITHHOLDING the entity was required to make on a NONRESIDENT owner's Illinois-source share. Line 28 is the CREDIT for an entity that ELECTED to pay the 4.95% pass-through entity tax on its own income so that owners get a federal SALT-cap workaround. Both are refundable, both come only from an Illinois K-1, and both must be attached.
## Line 27 — pass-through withholding
- Illinois requires partnerships, S corporations, and trusts to withhold 4.95% on the Illinois-source business income of NONRESIDENT individual owners, unless the owner files Form IL-1000-E (a certificate of exemption) or the entity pays PTE tax
- The owner claims the withholding on Line 27 as a payment. Because it is applied after total tax, any excess is refunded
- A nonresident whose only Illinois income is from entities that withheld enough to cover the tax need not file at all — but must file to get any excess back
- Residents normally have no Line 27 amount (entities do not withhold on residents), except trusts, which may
## Line 28 — pass-through entity tax credit
- For tax years ending on or after December 31, 2021, a partnership or S corporation may ELECT to pay Illinois tax at 4.95% on its net income (IITA Section 201(p)). The entity deducts that tax federally, lowering owners' federal income, and each owner receives a credit for their share on the K-1-P
- Residents receive credit for their share of tax on the entity's ENTIRE net income; nonresidents for the Illinois-apportioned share
- The credit is refundable: an owner whose credit exceeds Illinois tax gets the difference back
- The credit is a payment for the underpayment penalty, treated like withholding
## 2026 change — PA 104-0468
For tax years ending on or after December 31, 2026, an electing partnership may choose between two PTE tax bases: (1) the Illinois-sourced portion of ALL partners' distributive shares, or (2) the full distributive shares of RESIDENT partners plus the Illinois-sourced income of nonresidents. Method 2 gives resident partners a larger federal deduction (tax on all their income, not just the Illinois-apportioned part) and a correspondingly larger Line 28 credit. Which method the partnership elected shows on the 2026 K-1-P. Bulletin FY 2027-01 provides the estimated-payment worksheet for method 2.
## Interaction with Schedule CR
If the entity paid a substantially similar PTE tax to ANOTHER state, your share may be treated as tax you paid for the Schedule CR credit (Line 15) — and the same amount is added back to income on Schedule M Line 11. Illinois PTE tax paid by the entity is a Line 28 credit here, not a Schedule CR item.
## Notes
- The entity's tax year ending must fall within your 2026 tax year; a K-1-P for a fiscal year ending in 2027 goes on next year's return
- Attach the Illinois K-1-P or K-1-T, not the federal K-1
- Pass-through income itself is in federal AGI already; Lines 27-28 are only the payments
- An owner cannot opt out of an entity's PTE election; the credit simply arrives
## Required Information
- Every Illinois K-1-P and K-1-T with the withholding and PTE credit lines
- Entity tax year ending
- For 2026 partnerships, which PTE base method was elected
## Questions
- Did you receive an Illinois K-1-P or K-1-T showing pass-through withholding or a PTE tax credit?
- Are you a resident or nonresident owner of the entity?
- Did the entity also pay PTE tax to another state?
## Common Errors
- Entering PTE credit on Line 27 or withholding on Line 28
- Attaching the federal K-1 only
- A nonresident with excess pass-through withholding not filing for the refund
- Claiming Illinois PTE tax on Schedule CR
- Using a K-1-P whose year end falls outside your tax year
