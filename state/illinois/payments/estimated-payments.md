# Estimated Income Tax Payments — Line 26
## Description
Prepayments you made directly rather than through withholding. Line 26 totals three things: quarterly IL-1040-ES installments, the IL-505-I extension payment made by April 15, and any prior-year overpayment you elected to credit forward. Illinois requires estimates from anyone expecting to owe more than $1,000 after withholding and credits, with a penalty computed on Form IL-2210 if they are short.
## Amount
Sum of all 2026 IL-1040-ES payments, the IL-505-I payment for 2026, and the 2025 overpayment credited to 2026 estimates. Confirm the total with IDOR's online estimated payment inquiry; a mismatch generates a notice.
## Who must pay estimates for 2026
You expect Illinois tax for the year to exceed $1,000 after subtracting withholding, pass-through withholding, and credits. Exempt: taxpayers 65 or older permanently living in a nursing home, and those with at least two-thirds of federal gross income from farming. Not penalized: anyone who was not required to file an Illinois return in the prior year.
## Safe harbor
No underpayment penalty if timely installments (plus withholding) equal at least 90% of the current year's tax or 100% of the prior year's tax. There is no higher-income 110% tier in Illinois.
## 2026 due dates
April 15, June 15, September 15, 2026; January 15, 2027. Each installment is one quarter of the required annual amount unless income is annualized on IL-2210 Step 6.
## The IL-505-I extension payment
The extension to file is automatic; the extension to PAY does not exist. Pay the expected balance with Form IL-505-I by April 15, 2027 to avoid late-payment penalty and interest, then claim the amount on Line 26 of the return filed by October 15.
## Credit forward from the prior year
The amount elected on last year's Line 40. It is treated as paid on the original due date of the prior return if that return was filed by its extended due date (later payments are dated when made); if the prior return was filed after its extended due date, the credit is dated the day that return was filed — which can leave the first 2026 installment late. See refund-and-amount-owed.md for the full dating rules.
## Notes
- 2026 mid-year law change: Public Act 104-0468 (QSBS add-back, PTE election) took effect July 1, 2026. Bulletin FY 2027-01 says a taxpayer whose liability rose because of it should catch up in the first installment due on or after June 16, 2026 and may use the annualized method to avoid penalty on the earlier quarters
- Payments can be made on MyTax Illinois (no IL-1040-ES voucher needed), by credit card with a fee, or by check with the voucher
- Estimated payments do not lock in an Illinois filing requirement, but recovering them requires filing
## Required Information
- Dates and amounts of each installment and the extension payment
- Prior-year Line 40 amount
## Questions
- Did you make quarterly Illinois estimated payments, and what were the dates and amounts?
- Did you pay anything with Form IL-505-I?
- Did you apply last year's refund to this year?
- Do you expect to owe more than $1,000 after withholding?
## Common Errors
- Forgetting the prior-year credit forward on Line 26
- Treating the automatic filing extension as an extension to pay
- Assuming a federal safe-harbor computation (110%) applies in Illinois
- Guessing at the total instead of checking the IDOR payment inquiry
## Prompt
- Do I need to make estimated payments to Illinois?
- I applied last year's Illinois refund to this year, where does it go?
- I filed an extension, is my Illinois tax still due April 15?
