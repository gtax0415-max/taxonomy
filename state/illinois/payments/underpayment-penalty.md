# Late-Payment Penalty for Underpayment of Estimated Tax — Line 34
## Description
The penalty for not prepaying enough Illinois tax during the year through withholding and estimates. Illinois is unusual in actively encouraging you NOT to compute it: file and pay the tax without a penalty entry, and IDOR will figure the penalty and bill you. The exception is annualized income, which must be computed on IL-2210 and attached.
## When it applies
You owe the penalty if either:
- Your tax after withholding, pass-through withholding, and credits exceeds $1,000 and you did not pay enough in timely installments, or
- You were required to make estimated payments and missed the required amounts by the due dates
The required amount is the lesser of 90% of the current year's tax or 100% of the prior year's tax, paid in four equal timely installments (April 15, June 15, September 15, 2026; January 15, 2027) unless annualized. Withholding and PTE credits count as paid evenly through the year.
## The four checkboxes — Lines 34a-34d
| Box | Who | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 34a Farmers | At least two-thirds of total federal gross income (both spouses if joint) from farming — crops, livestock for draft, breeding, or dairy, fish, orchards, nurseries, crop shares; NOT farmland or equipment sales, custom harvesting for others, farm wages, or cash rent | No estimates required, no penalty |
| 34b Nursing home residents | You or your spouse 65 or older and permanently living in a nursing home | No estimates required, no penalty |
| 34c Annualized income | Income received unevenly and you used IL-2210 Step 6 to compute installments by period | Must complete and ATTACH IL-2210 |
| 34d No prior-year return | You were not required to file an Illinois return last year | No penalty |
## Notes
- The penalty is a late-PAYMENT penalty on each installment shortfall from its due date, not a flat percentage of the balance due
- Publication 105 and the IL-1040-ES instructions cover the computation; Form IL-W-4 is the tool to raise withholding and avoid it next year
- 2026 relief: Bulletin FY 2027-01 confirms that a taxpayer whose liability increased under PA 104-0468 (effective July 1, 2026) avoids penalty by making up the difference in the first installment due on or after June 16, 2026 and using the annualized method
- A reduced credit-forward (because IDOR offset it against a debt) can create an underpayment penalty retroactively
- The penalty on Line 34 is NOT the same as the late-payment penalty and interest on a balance due after April 15, which IDOR bills separately
## Required Information
- Current-year tax after credits and prior-year tax
- Timing of withholding and each estimated payment
- Whether any exception box applies
## Questions
- Did you owe more than $1,000 after withholding and credits?
- What was last year's Illinois tax, and did you pay at least that much this year through withholding and estimates?
- Is two-thirds of your income from farming, or are you in a nursing home?
- Was your income lumpy — a bonus, a sale, a retirement distribution late in the year?
## Common Errors
- Computing and paying a penalty IDOR would have billed, often incorrectly
- Failing to attach IL-2210 when the annualized box is checked
- Checking the farmer box on the strength of a farmland sale
- Applying the federal 110% prior-year rule; Illinois is 100%
