# Illinois Gives Credit
## Description
Nonrefundable credit equal to 25% of a contribution to the PERMANENT ENDOWMENT of a Qualified Community Foundation. New for tax years ending on or after December 31, 2025, under the Illinois Gives Tax Credit Act (35 ILCS 5/243), and the first Illinois income tax incentive for general charitable giving. It runs through tax years ending before January 1, 2030.
## Amount
- 25% of the amount on each Certificate of Receipt
- Statutory limits: a per-taxpayer annual cap of $100,000 in credit, a statewide cap of $5,000,000 in credits per calendar year, with a share of the statewide cap reserved for smaller gifts — confirm the current-year figures on the IDOR Illinois Gives page before advising on a large gift
- Nonrefundable with a FIVE-YEAR carryforward
## The federal trade-off
You may NOT claim any portion of the same contribution as a federal itemized deduction. For an itemizer in the 24% federal bracket, a 25% state credit and a 24% federal deduction are roughly equal in value, and the credit is better for anyone who takes the federal standard deduction. For a high-bracket itemizer the federal deduction may be worth more. Run both.
## Eligibility
- Contribution to a Qualified Community Foundation's permanent endowment fund during the tax year. The foundation must be certified under the Act and must issue the COR
- No federal deduction claimed for any part of the gift
- Individuals, partnerships, S corporations, trusts, and corporations all qualify; pass-through credits arrive on Schedule K-1-P
## Mechanics
- One line on Schedule 1299-C per COR, with the COR certificate number in Column D (the only individual credit that uses Column D in the year earned)
- Enter "5" in Column A the first year
- Do not attach the COR; retain it. Attach any Schedule K-1-P
- Not transferable
## Notes
- The credit reduces Illinois tax; it does not reduce the federal charitable deduction you forgo. The gift must go to the ENDOWMENT, not to a donor-advised fund or a pass-through gift at the same foundation
- Because the credit is first come, first served against a statewide cap, the foundation reserves the credit at the time of the gift — ask before giving late in the year
- Easily confused with Schedule G donations, which are check-offs that cost you money rather than credits
## Required Information
- Each COR: foundation name, certificate number, contribution amount, and date
- Confirmation no federal deduction was taken
- Any Schedule K-1-P showing distributed credit
## Questions
- Did you give to a community foundation's endowment and receive a Certificate of Receipt?
- Did you deduct that gift on your federal Schedule A?
- Is the foundation on IDOR's list of Qualified Community Foundations?
- Did you exceed the per-taxpayer cap?
## Common Errors
- Deducting the gift federally AND claiming the Illinois credit
- Claiming for a gift to a donor-advised fund or a non-endowment fund at the same foundation
- Attaching the COR instead of retaining it, or omitting the certificate number from Column D
- Claiming a gift to a charity that is not a certified community foundation
- Putting several CORs on one line of Schedule 1299-C

- What is the Illinois Gives tax credit and how much is it?
