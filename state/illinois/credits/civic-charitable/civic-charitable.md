# Civic and Charitable Credits
## Description
Credits for volunteer service and for donations. Illinois has no general charitable deduction — there are no itemized deductions on Form IL-1040 — so these credits are the ONLY way charitable giving touches the Illinois return. Two of the three require a certificate from a state agency or foundation before anything can be claimed.
## Credits in this folder
- Volunteer Emergency Worker Credit (volunteer-emergency-worker-credit.md) — Schedule ICR, Section C → Form IL-1040, Line 16. Nonrefundable, no carryforward, certificate from IDOR
- Illinois Gives Credit (illinois-gives-credit.md) — Schedule 1299-C, code 5670 → Form IL-1040, Line 17. Nonrefundable, five-year carryforward, Certificate of Receipt from the community foundation
- Affordable Housing Donations Credit (affordable-housing-donations-credit.md) — Schedule 1299-C, code 5260 → Form IL-1040, Line 17. Nonrefundable, five-year carryforward, TRANSFERABLE, certificate from IHDA or the City of Chicago
## 2026 parameters
- Volunteer Emergency Worker: $500 per certificate ($1,000 if both spouses), no income limit
- Illinois Gives: 25% of endowment gifts, second year of the program
- Affordable Housing Donations: 50%, and 2026 is the LAST year to earn it — donations in tax years ending after December 31, 2026 produce no credit. Credits earned through 2026 still carry forward five years
## What changed for 2025 (prior year)
- Illinois Gives took effect for tax years ending on or after December 31, 2025 — the first Illinois credit for general charitable giving
## Also civic-adjacent, filed elsewhere
- Schedule G voluntary charitable donations — these are check-offs that ADD to your tax or reduce your refund to fund state charities. They are contributions, not credits, and are frequently confused with Illinois Gives
- The Invest in Kids scholarship credit (code 5660) EXPIRED for contributions after 2023; only carryforwards remain, through tax periods ending December 31, 2028
## Related
- Federal charitable deduction, which Illinois Gives requires you to FORGO on the same gift — see federal/deductions/
