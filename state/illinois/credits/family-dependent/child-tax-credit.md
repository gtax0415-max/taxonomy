# Illinois Child Tax Credit
## Description
Refundable credit equal to 40% of your Illinois earned income tax credit if at least one qualifying child was UNDER AGE 12 on December 31. Created for tax year 2024 at 20%, doubled to 40% for 2025, and made permanent. Illinois's first state child tax credit. It is NOT a per-child amount — one child under 12 or five children under 12 produce the same credit, because the credit is a percentage of the EITC, not a count of children.
## Amount
| Tax year | Rate |
|---|---|
| 2024 | 20% of the Illinois EITC |
| 2025 and after | 40% of the Illinois EITC |
Because the Illinois EITC is 20% of the federal EITC, the child tax credit works out to 8% of the federal credit. Approximate 2026 maximums (federal figures from Rev. Proc. 2025-32):
| Qualifying children on the federal EITC | Max federal EITC | Illinois EITC (20%) | Child Tax Credit (40% of that) |
|---|---|---|---|
| One | $4,427 | $885 | $354 |
| Two | $7,316 | $1,463 | $585 |
| Three or more | $8,231 | $1,646 | $658 |
For 2025 returns the corresponding maximums were $346, $572, and $644.
Nonresidents and part-year residents get the credit in proportion to Illinois income, because the EITC it is computed from is already prorated on Schedule IL-E/EITC line 8.
## Eligibility
Two conditions and nothing else:
1. You qualify for the Illinois EITC for the year (including under the expanded ITIN and age rules — see income-based/earned-income-credit.md)
2. At least one qualifying child was under 12 years old as of the last day of the tax year
The statute (35 ILCS 5/244) uses the IRC Section 152 "qualifying child" definition, and IDOR's credit page says a qualifying child who is not your dependent counts. The Schedule IL-E/EITC instructions and the Line 10 checkbox, however, are worded as "your DEPENDENT and under the age of 12." In practice: a Step 2 dependent under 12 is the clean case; for a Step 3 non-dependent qualifying child under 12, claim it on the strength of the statute and the IDOR web guidance and expect possible correspondence. A child with an ITIN counts either way.
## Step 5 mechanics
- Line 10: check the box confirming at least one dependent under 12 on December 31. Do not complete Step 5 at all if the box cannot be checked
- Line 11: the Illinois EITC from Step 4, Line 9 — already prorated for part-year and nonresidents, so no second proration
- Line 12: Line 11 × 40% → IL-1040 Line 30
- Attach the schedule plus federal Form 1040 pages 1-2 and Schedule 1 (the same attachment the EITC requires)
## The zero-income gap
A parent with no earned income has no federal EITC, therefore no Illinois EITC, therefore no child tax credit. Full-time caregivers, parents on disability, and families living on nontaxable benefits get nothing. This is the single most common reason an Illinois family with young children receives no child credit.
## Notes
- Fully refundable. Pays out with no tax liability
- The refund is excluded from income and resources for every means-tested benefit program administered by a governmental entity unless federal law requires otherwise
- Must attach Schedule IL-E/EITC plus pages 1 and 2 and Schedule 1 of the federal return
- The credit for a child who turns 12 during the year is lost for that year — the test is age on December 31, not age during the year
- There is no separate income limit; the EITC income limits do the work
## Required Information
- The Illinois EITC amount from Schedule IL-E/EITC, Step 4, Line 9
- Date of birth of the youngest qualifying child
- Whether that child is a dependent (Step 2) or a non-dependent qualifying child (Step 3)
## Questions
- Did you claim, or could you have claimed, the Illinois earned income credit?
- Was any qualifying child under 12 on December 31, 2026?
- Is that child listed in Step 2 or Step 3 of Schedule IL-E/EITC?
- Were you an Illinois resident all year?
## Common Errors
- Multiplying the credit by the number of children — it is a flat 40% of the EITC regardless of how many children are under 12
- Using the 2024 rate of 20% on a 2025 or 2026 return
- Skipping Step 5 because the youngest child is a non-dependent qualifying child rather than a dependent
- Assuming a family with no earned income can claim it
- Computing 40% of the FEDERAL EITC instead of 40% of the ILLINOIS EITC
