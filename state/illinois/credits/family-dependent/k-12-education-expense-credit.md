# K-12 Education Expense Credit
## Description
Nonrefundable credit for tuition, book fees, and lab fees paid to an Illinois K-12 school. Illinois is one of a handful of states that subsidizes private and parochial tuition through the income tax, and it does so with a low cap that most private-school families hit immediately.
## Amount
- 25% of qualified expenses IN EXCESS OF $250
- CAPPED at $750 per family per year
- The cap is reached at $3,250 of qualified expenses. Every dollar above that produces nothing
- Zero if federal AGI exceeds $500,000 (married filing jointly) or $250,000 (all other filers). This is a CLIFF
## Eligibility
- You were the parent or legal guardian of a full-time student who was under 21 at the close of the school year
- You and the student were Illinois residents when you paid the expenses
- The student attended kindergarten through twelfth grade at a public or nonpublic school in Illinois, or was home schooled (see Publication 119 for home school rules)
## What counts
- Tuition, including summer school classes that meet elementary or secondary graduation requirements
- Book fees for RENTAL of books required by the school's program
- Lab fees for the use of supplies, equipment, materials, or instruments required for a course that earns credit toward completion. Renting an instrument FROM THE SCHOOL for band class qualifies
## What does not count
- Daycare, preschool, college, university, tutoring, or trade school
- Purchasing books, supplies, instruments, or costumes that are not used up during the year
- Anything paid directly to a business rather than the school — renting the same instrument from a music store fails
- Enrichment classes or tutoring that do not count toward the required curriculum
- After-school care, even when the school runs it
- Expenses for yourself or your spouse
- Amounts paid with a scholarship
## Notes
- Nonrefundable with NO carryforward. The combined property tax, education expense, and volunteer emergency worker credits cannot exceed tax due
- Separated parents filing separate Illinois returns may each claim the credit, but not for the same expenses, and the combined total is still $750
- Public school registration, activity, and technology fees qualify only to the extent they meet the book-fee or lab-fee definitions — read the school's fee breakdown
- Receipts are not attached but must be kept; IDOR routinely asks for them
## Required Information
- Student name, SSN or ITIN, grade, school name, city, and school type (public, nonpublic, home)
- Total tuition, book fees, and lab fees paid per school during the regular school year
- Federal AGI
- Whether another parent or guardian is also claiming expenses for the same student
## Questions
- Which Illinois K-12 school did your child attend, and what did you pay for tuition, book rental, and lab fees?
- Was your child a full-time student under 21 at the end of the school year?
- Is your federal AGI under $500,000 joint or $250,000 otherwise?
- Did you pay for any of these expenses with a scholarship?
## Common Errors
- Claiming preschool or daycare — the credit starts at kindergarten
- Claiming purchased books or instruments rather than rented ones
- Forgetting the $250 floor and computing 25% of the full amount
- Claiming above the $500,000 / $250,000 AGI cliff
- Expecting a refund or a carryforward from a nonrefundable, no-carryforward credit
## Prompt
- I pay Catholic school tuition in Illinois.
- Can I deduct my kids' school fees in Illinois?
- Does the Illinois education credit cover preschool?
