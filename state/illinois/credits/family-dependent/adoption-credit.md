# Adoption Credit
## Description
Nonrefundable credit for qualified adoption expenses, piggybacking on the federal adoption credit. Illinois is unusual among large states in having one at all — New York, for instance, has no state adoption credit. The credit is capped low, but doubles to $5,000 for the adoption of an Illinois child who is at least a year old, which is the case the legislature wanted to encourage.
## Amount
The credit equals the FEDERAL adoption credit RECEIVED for the year (Form 8839 — do not include any federal carryover), limited to:
| Situation | Cap (joint or single) | Cap (married filing separately) |
|---|---|---|
| Any eligible child | $2,000 | $1,000 |
| Eligible child at least 1 year old AND an Illinois resident when expenses were paid or incurred | $5,000 | $2,500 |
The cap is PER CHILD, FOR THE ENTIRE ADOPTION, across all years. If the adoption takes three tax years and you claimed $1,500 in year one, only $500 (or $3,500) remains. Line b of the worksheet forces you to subtract prior-year Illinois credit for the same child.
## Timing follows the federal rule
- Expenses paid BEFORE the year the adoption becomes final → claim in the year AFTER the expense is paid
- Expenses paid DURING or AFTER the year the adoption becomes final → claim in the year paid
## Eligibility
- An "eligible child" is under 18, or physically or mentally incapable of self-care
- Available for tax years ending on or after December 31, 2018 through tax years ending on or before December 31, 2029
- Nonresidents and part-year residents get the credit in proportion to Illinois income
- Spouses filing jointly are treated as one taxpayer
## What does not count
- Expenses reimbursed by an employer program or covered by any federal, state, or local program
- Adopting your spouse's child
- Surrogacy arrangements or anything incurred in violation of state or federal law
## Notes
- Nonrefundable with a FIVE-YEAR carryforward. Enter "5" in Column A of Schedule 1299-C in the first year
- Not distributable and not transferable
- The federal credit is the input, so a family with too little federal tax to use the federal credit in full also gets a smaller Illinois credit — the Illinois credit is based on the federal amount RECEIVED, not the federal amount computed
- Special-needs adoptions receive the full federal credit without documented expenses; that flows into Illinois the same way
## Required Information
- Federal Form 8839 for the year
- Child's name, SSN or ITIN, and birth month and year
- Whether the child was an Illinois resident when expenses were paid
- Whether the adoption became final during the year
- Any Illinois adoption credit previously claimed for the same child
## Questions
- Did you receive a federal adoption credit on Form 8839 this year?
- Was the child at least one year old and living in Illinois when you paid the expenses?
- Have you claimed an Illinois adoption credit for this child in a prior year?
- Did an employer or a government program reimburse any of the expenses?
## Common Errors
- Forgetting to attach federal Form 8839, which invalidates the claim
- Including federal carryover from an earlier year in the federal credit "received"
- Applying the $5,000 cap to an out-of-state or infant adoption
- Claiming the full cap again in a second year for the same child
- Filing it on Schedule ICR instead of Schedule 1299-C
