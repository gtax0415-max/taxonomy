# Credit for Tax Paid to Other States
## Description
The double-taxation relief credit. Illinois taxes residents on ALL income wherever earned; when another state, or a city or county in another state, also taxes some of that income, Schedule CR gives a credit for that tax, limited to the Illinois tax on the same income. It is the first credit applied on the return (Line 15), before Schedule ICR and Schedule 1299-C.
## Credits in this folder
- Credit for Tax Paid to Other States (credit-for-tax-paid-to-other-states.md) — Schedule CR → Form IL-1040, Line 15. Nonrefundable, no carryforward
## What qualifies, in one line
Income taxes paid to another U.S. state, the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, a U.S. territory or possession, OR a political subdivision of any of them (county, city, local). NOT federal tax, NOT foreign countries or their subdivisions.
## The reciprocity exception
Illinois has reciprocal agreements with IOWA, KENTUCKY, MICHIGAN, and WISCONSIN. Wages an Illinois resident earns in those states are taxed ONLY by Illinois. If tax was withheld, claim a refund from that state — Schedule CR cannot be used for it. Two things the agreements do NOT cover: (1) local taxes, so a Kentucky city occupational tax on those same wages IS creditable, and (2) non-wage income, which those states can still tax and which can produce a credit.
## Related
- Federal — no analogue; the federal return deducts state taxes (SALT) rather than crediting them, and the SALT deductibility test is actually the Illinois qualifying test
- New York's IT-112-R serves the same function — see state/new-york/credits/
- Publication 111 (rules) and Publication 111-C (state-by-state formulas for the "tax paid" figure) are the authorities
## Questions
- Did you file a required return with another state on income you earned while an Illinois resident?
- Was it wages from Iowa, Kentucky, Michigan, or Wisconsin — and if so, was there also a city or county tax?
