# K-12 Instructional Materials and Supplies Credit
## Description
Nonrefundable credit for out-of-pocket classroom expenses paid by an Illinois K-12 educator. The credit was DOUBLED for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2023, from $250 to $500 per educator, and it is one of the few Schedule 1299-C credits an ordinary W-2 employee can claim.
## Amount
- 100% of qualified expenses, CAPPED at $500 per eligible educator
- $1,000 on a joint return where BOTH spouses are eligible educators, each with their own $500 cap in a separate column of the worksheet
- Nonrefundable, with a FIVE-YEAR carryforward
## Eligibility
An "eligible educator" is a kindergarten through twelfth grade TEACHER, INSTRUCTOR, COUNSELOR, PRINCIPAL, or AIDE who worked at least 900 HOURS during a school year in a qualified school — a public or nonpublic school located in Illinois.
Home schools are NOT qualified schools, and expenses for home-school instruction do not count.
## Qualified expenses
Ordinary and necessary expenses for books, supplies (including nonathletic supplies for health or physical education courses), equipment (including computer equipment, software, and services), and other materials USED IN THE CLASSROOM. "Ordinary" means common in your educational field; "necessary" means helpful and appropriate — it does not have to be required.
## Notes
- Not distributable and not transferable
- Enter "5" in Column A of Schedule 1299-C Step 3 in the first year, since the carryforward is five years
- The federal educator deduction ($350 for 2026) can be claimed on the same dollars; Illinois does not require a reduction
- The 900-hour test is per SCHOOL YEAR, not per calendar year, so a first-year teacher who started in August may not yet qualify
- An aide or counselor qualifies; a substitute who never reaches 900 hours in one school year does not
## Required Information
- Professional Educator License number, or "N/A"
- Name of the Illinois school
- Total qualified expenses paid during the taxable year, per educator
- Any unused credit carried from prior years
## Questions
- What is your role at the school, and did you work at least 900 hours there in the school year?
- Is the school a public or nonpublic school located in Illinois?
- How much did you spend on classroom materials this year, per spouse if both teach?
- Do you have unused credit carried forward?
## Common Errors
- Using the pre-2023 cap of $250
- Claiming for a home school or an out-of-state school
- Reporting the credit on Schedule ICR instead of Schedule 1299-C
- Combining both spouses' expenses in one column instead of two
- Forgetting that unused credit carries forward for five years
## Prompt
- I am a teacher in Illinois and spend my own money on supplies.
- Does Illinois give teachers a credit for classroom expenses?
- My spouse and I both teach, can we each claim the Illinois educator credit?
