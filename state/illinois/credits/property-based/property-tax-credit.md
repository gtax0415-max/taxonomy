# Illinois Property Tax Credit
## Description
Nonrefundable credit equal to 5% of the Illinois property tax you PAID during the year on your PRINCIPAL RESIDENCE. Small in percentage terms, but with Illinois property taxes among the highest in the country it is routinely worth several hundred dollars, and it is the credit most Illinois homeowners qualify for without doing anything special.
## Amount
- 5% of Illinois property tax paid during 2026 on your principal residence, including adjoining lots that are an extension of the residence
- Zero if federal AGI exceeds $500,000 (married filing jointly) or $250,000 (all other filers). A CLIFF, not a phase-out
- Nonrefundable, no carryforward, and combined with the education expense and volunteer emergency worker credits cannot exceed tax due
## The one-year lag
Illinois property taxes are assessed in one year and PAID in the next. The credit follows the PAYMENT year. On a 2026 return you claim the tax you paid in 2026, which is the 2025 tax bill, for the time you owned and lived in the home during 2025. This produces three rules that trip people up:
- Bought in 2026: NO credit on the 2026 return. The first bill you pay arrives in 2027
- Bought in 2025: credit for the portion of the 2025 tax (paid in 2026) covering the period you owned and lived there — but not for any tax the seller reimbursed you at closing
- Sold in 2026: credit for the 2025 tax paid in 2026 PLUS the portion of the 2026 tax paid at closing covering the period you owned and lived there in 2026. No credit for a home sold in 2025
## Eligibility
- Your principal residence was in Illinois during the prior year
- You OWNED it
- The tax bill was PAID during the tax year
- Nonresidents cannot claim it
## What does not count
- Vacation homes, rental property, vacant lots, farmland, or property outside Illinois
- Mobile home privilege tax
- Penalties, interest, or fees included in the bill
- The portion of the tax deductible as a business expense on the federal return — for a home office or a rented unit — EVEN IF you did not take the federal deduction
## Notes
- Married couples filing separately in Illinois may each claim, but the total between them cannot exceed 5% of the qualifying tax
- If you and your spouse each maintained a separate principal residence, both can be included on one return; list the second property on Line 4c
- The county name and property index number must be entered EXACTLY as printed on the bill or assessment notice; a mismatch triggers a denial letter. If the mortgage servicer pays the tax, get the number from the servicer or the county assessor
- Property tax exemptions (homestead, senior freeze) reduce the bill and therefore reduce the credit; they are not claimed here
## Required Information
- Total Illinois property tax paid during the year on the principal residence
- County and property index number for the residence and any adjoining lots
- Business-deductible portion of the tax, if any
- Closing statements for purchases and sales
- Federal AGI
## Questions
- What Illinois property tax did you actually pay during 2026, and on which parcel?
- Did you own and live in that home during 2024?
- Did you buy or sell the home during 2025 or 2026?
- Do you deduct any part of the home as a business expense federally?
- Is your federal AGI under $500,000 joint or $250,000 otherwise?
## Common Errors
- Claiming the credit in the year of purchase, when no tax has yet been paid
- Using the ASSESSED tax rather than the tax PAID during the year
- Including a second home, rental, or farm parcel
- Entering the property index number from memory or with a typo
- Claiming above the $500,000 / $250,000 AGI cliff
- Forgetting to back out the home-office share
