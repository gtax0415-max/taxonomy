# Illinois Individual Income Tax Credits — Index
## Description
Master index for the Illinois credit taxonomy. Illinois runs a FLAT 4.95% income tax with a small personal exemption ($2,925 for 2026, up from $2,850), so credits carry more of the progressivity than they do in New York. Every individual credit lands on one of five lines of Form IL-1040, and which line tells you almost everything about how the credit behaves.
## The five landing lines
| IL-1040 line | What lands there | Refundable | Carryforward | Income limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | Credit for tax paid to other states (Schedule CR) | No | No | None |
| 16 | Property Tax, K-12 Education Expense, Volunteer Emergency Worker (Schedule ICR) | No | No | $500,000 joint / $250,000 other (not the volunteer credit) |
| 17 | Schedule 1299-C credits — adoption, educator supplies, Illinois Gives, and the DCEO/DNR/DHS business credits | No | 0, 2, 5, or 10 years by credit code | None |
| 29 | Illinois Earned Income Tax Credit (Schedule IL-E/EITC) | YES | n/a | Federal EITC limits |
| 30 | Illinois Child Tax Credit (Schedule IL-E/EITC, Step 5) | YES | n/a | Follows the EITC |
Lines 15 through 17 are nonrefundable and are applied against tax in that order. Lines 29 and 30 are the ONLY refundable credits an individual can claim in Illinois.
One more line runs the other way: Line 13 adds RECAPTURE of previously used investment and agreement-based credits (Schedule 4255) to tax before any credit is applied — see business-investment/schedule-4255-recapture.md.
## Folders
- family-dependent/ — Child Tax Credit (line 30), K-12 Education Expense Credit (line 16), Adoption Credit (line 17)
- income-based/ — Earned Income Tax Credit (line 29)
- property-based/ — Property Tax Credit (line 16). No New York analogue at the state level
- health-care/ — EMPTY BY DESIGN. Illinois has no long-term care, nursing home, or health insurance credit. The folder exists so the taxonomy is parallel and the absence is documented
- civic-charitable/ — Volunteer Emergency Worker Credit (line 16), Illinois Gives Credit (line 17), Affordable Housing Donations Credit (line 17)
- educator/ — K-12 Instructional Materials and Supplies Credit (line 17)
- other-state-tax/ — Credit for Tax Paid to Other States (line 15)
- business-investment/ — the certificate-driven DCEO, DNR, DHS, and IDOR credits on Schedule 1299-C, plus the mechanics of the schedule itself and Schedule 4255 recapture
## The $500,000 / $250,000 AGI cliff
Illinois disallows the property tax credit, the K-12 education expense credit, AND the personal and dependent exemption allowances when federal AGI exceeds $500,000 (married filing jointly) or $250,000 (everyone else). This is a CLIFF, not a phase-out. It does not touch the volunteer emergency worker credit, the 1299-C credits, the EITC, or the child tax credit.
## Tax year 2026 parameters at a glance
- Flat rate 4.95%; exemption allowance $2,925 per person
- EITC 20% of federal; investment income cliff $12,200; federal maximums $664 / $4,427 / $7,316 / $8,231
- Child Tax Credit 40% of the Illinois EITC (max about $658)
- Property tax credit 5%; K-12 credit 25% over $250, max $750; both cut off above $500,000 joint / $250,000 other AGI
- Volunteer emergency worker $500; educator supplies $500; adoption $2,000 / $5,000; Illinois Gives 25%; Affordable Housing 50% (last year)
## What changed for 2025 (prior year, for carryforward context)
- Child Tax Credit doubled from 20% to 40% of the Illinois EITC and became permanent under 35 ILCS 5/244
- Illinois Gives Credit (code 5670) took effect — 25% of endowment gifts to qualified community foundations
- Film Production Services credit restructured (35% base rate for productions starting on or after July 1, 2025)
- REV Illinois, REV Construction Jobs, and the three MICRO credits became claimable
- Wages Paid to Ex-Felons renamed Wages Paid to Returning Citizens, now 15% of wages, $7,500 per hire cap, $1 million statewide cap
- Historic Preservation code 1000 (pre-2016 credits) carryforward ended after tax periods ending December 31, 2025
## What changed for 2026
- Exemption allowance rose to $2,925 (Informational Bulletin FY 2026-15)
- AIM for Illinois credit (code 1050, ten-year carryforward) becomes claimable for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2026
- Public Act 104-0468 (Bulletin FY 2027-01) decoupled Illinois from the federal QSBS exclusion and expanded the partnership PTE election for tax years ending on or after December 31, 2026 — neither touches credits, but the QSBS add-back raises base income and therefore the Schedule CR denominator
- Federal inflation adjustments (Rev. Proc. 2025-32) raised every EITC figure; the Illinois EITC and Child Tax Credit rise with them
- No credit rate changed. HB4680 (EITC to 30%) was introduced in the 104th General Assembly but had not been enacted as of the FY 2027-01 bulletin; the 2026 rate is 20%
## 2026 is the LAST year for
- Affordable Housing Donations (5260) — donations in tax years ending after December 31, 2026 earn nothing
- Angel Investment (5460) — tax years ending on or before December 31, 2026
- Apprenticeship Education Expense (0160) and Live Theater Production (5580) — tax years beginning before January 1, 2027
- Research and Development (5340) — the 2025 Schedule 1299-I says tax years ending before January 1, 2027; PA 103-0595 extended it to 2031, so confirm on the 2026 Schedule 1299-I
- EDGE (5300) agreements must be entered by June 30, 2027
