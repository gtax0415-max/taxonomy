# Illinois Expanded EITC Worksheet and Qualifications Questionnaire
## Description
The machinery that makes Illinois's expanded EITC work. The IRS will not compute an EITC for an ITIN filer or for a childless worker under 25 or over 64, so Illinois has the filer recompute the FEDERAL credit as if those bars did not exist, then takes 20% of the result. The Questionnaire is the federal Form 1040 Line 27 eligibility flowchart with the SSN and age tests removed; the Worksheet is federal Worksheet B rebuilt on the Illinois form. Neither is needed if you simply received the federal EITC with no additional ITIN children — those filers skip Line 5 and enter the federal credit on Line 6.
## Who must complete it
Check a box on Schedule IL-E/EITC, Step 4, Line 5 if you did not qualify for the federal EITC, or qualified for a SMALLER one, because:
- You, your spouse, or a qualifying child has an ITIN instead of a valid SSN, or
- You (and your spouse on a joint return) are aged 18 through 24, or 65 and older, with no qualifying child
Everyone else skips it. Once a box is checked, BOTH the Questionnaire and the Worksheet are mandatory.
## The Questionnaire — four steps, 2026 figures
### Step 1, all filers
1. Is federal AGI (Form 1040 Line 11) under the completed-phaseout amount? 2026: $19,540 / $51,593 / $58,629 / $62,974 for zero, one, two, three-or-more children; $26,820 / $58,863 / $65,899 / $70,244 married filing jointly. No → STOP
2. Filing federal Form 2555? Yes → STOP
3. Were you or your spouse a U.S. resident at least half the year? No → STOP
### Step 2, investment income
Add Form 1040 Lines 2a + 2b + 3b + 7 (zero if Line 7 is a loss). If you file Schedule E or Form 4797, rent out personal property outside a trade or business, file Form 8814, or have passive activity income or loss, use Worksheet 1 in federal Publication 596 instead. More than $12,200 for 2026 → STOP
### Step 3, filers with a qualifying child
- Joint return → go straight to the Worksheet
- Married filing separately or head of household while married → must have lived apart from your spouse the last six months of the year, or be legally separated under a written agreement or decree and living apart at year end; otherwise STOP
- Could you be another person's qualifying child? Yes → STOP (answer No if that person is not required to file and files only for a refund)
### Step 4, filers without a qualifying child
- Married but filing separately or head of household → STOP
- Age: you (or your spouse on a joint return) must be at least 18 at year end — for 2026, born on or before December 31, 2008. Illinois imposes NO upper age limit here; the federal 65 cap is deleted
- Main home in the U.S. more than half the year (military on extended active duty abroad count)
- Joint return → go to the Worksheet
- Could you be another person's qualifying child, or be claimed as a dependent on someone else's return? Yes to either → STOP
## The Worksheet — five parts
### Part 1, earned income from wages
Line 1: Form 1040 Line 1z. Church employees with $108.28 or more of church employee income subtract Schedule SE Part I Line 5a; clergy subtract Schedule SE Part I Line 2 (both amounts are picked up again in Part 2). Lines 2-5 follow the form.
### Part 2, self-employment income when Schedule SE is filed
Lines 6-10 mirror federal Worksheet B Part 1, Lines 1a-1e: net earnings from Schedule SE, clergy and church-employee amounts, less the deductible part of self-employment tax.
### Part 3, self-employment when Schedule SE is NOT required
Lines 11-12 for net earnings under $400: Schedule C profit, Schedule F profit, and farm partnership K-1 Box 14 Code A. Do NOT include statutory-employee income, notary-public income, amounts exempt from SE tax under an approved Form 4029 or 4361, or any other SE-exempt amount. Federal Schedule K-1 amounts must first be reduced as the federal Partner's Instructions require.
### Part 4, total earned income
Lines 14-15 add Parts 1 through 3.
### Part 5, do you qualify, and how much
Lines 16-23 apply the 2026 earned-income and AGI limits and look up the credit in the federal EIC Table for the correct filing status and number of qualifying children (counting ITIN children the IRS would have excluded). Line 23 is the recomputed FEDERAL credit → Schedule IL-E/EITC Step 4, Line 6. The 20% rate is then applied on Lines 6-7 exactly as for any other filer.

## Notes
- The pro forma Form 1040 is a real requirement for a filer with no federal filing obligation: complete the federal return in full as a reference document, do not file it, and keep it
- A single ITIN child can move a family from one federal credit tier to the next, so the worksheet credit often EXCEEDS 20% of the federal credit actually received — that is the point of Line 5
- The 2025 instructions' Questionnaire refers to ages "18 through 25" while the general information says "18-24"; the statute (35 ILCS 5/212(b-5)) and the federal rule both make 25 the first federally eligible age, so the practical rule is: under 25 or 65 and over qualify only through this worksheet, 25 through 64 qualify federally
- Married filing jointly where ONE spouse has an ITIN still qualifies; the joint return goes through the worksheet
- Worksheet results are not attached but must be kept; IDOR reconstructs them on review
## Required Information
- Which Line 5 condition applies (ITIN, age, or both)
- Form 1040 or pro forma: Line 1z, Line 11, Lines 2a/2b/3b/7, Schedule SE, Schedule C/F, farm K-1s
- Filing status and count of qualifying children INCLUDING ITIN children
- Whether Schedule E, Form 4797, Form 8814, or passive activities exist (forces the Pub. 596 investment-income worksheet)
## Questions
- Why did you not get the full federal EITC — an ITIN, your age, or both?
- Did you file a federal return, or do you need a pro forma one?
- What were your wages, self-employment earnings, and investment income?
- Are you married, and if not filing jointly, did you live apart from your spouse for the last six months?
- How many qualifying children live with you, counting those with ITINs?
## Common Errors
- Checking a Line 5 box and then entering the federal Line 27 amount on Line 6 anyway
- Applying the federal 25-64 age test in Questionnaire Step 4
- Including statutory-employee or notary income in Part 3
- Computing investment income without the Pub. 596 worksheet when Schedule E or Form 4797 is present
- Using the 2025 income limits or the $11,950 investment cap on a 2026 return
- Not preparing the pro forma Form 1040 when no federal return was required

- I am 20, single, no kids, and did not get the federal EITC — walk me through the Illinois worksheet.
