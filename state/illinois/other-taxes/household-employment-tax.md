# Household Employment Tax — Line 20
## Description
Not a tax on you. Line 20 is the Illinois income tax you WITHHELD from a household employee's wages during the year, remitted in one annual payment with your own return instead of through the employer withholding system (Form IL-941 and IL-501 deposits). Publication 121 governs. Illinois offers this so a family employing a nanny or caregiver does not have to register as a withholding agent and file quarterly.
## Who is a household employee
Someone you pay to do domestic work in or around your home — babysitter, caretaker, cleaner, driver, health aide, housekeeper, nanny, private nurse, yard worker — where YOU control what work is done and how. Same definition as IRS Publication 926. Not an employee: a worker who controls the work and serves the public (a self-employed lawn service), or a worker supplied and controlled by an agency.
## When you must withhold Illinois tax
Only if:
- You withhold FEDERAL income tax from the employee's wages (federal withholding for household employees is itself voluntary, by agreement), or
- You and the employee enter a voluntary withholding agreement
A federal voluntary withholding agreement covers Illinois too; no separate Illinois agreement is required. If you do not withhold federal tax and have no agreement, you withhold nothing and Line 20 is zero — the employee pays their own Illinois tax through estimates.
## Amount
The Illinois tax actually withheld during the calendar year, computed from Booklet IL-700-T at 4.95% of wages less the allowances on Form IL-W-4 ($2,925 per allowance for 2026). If the employee did not complete an IL-W-4, withhold as single with zero allowances.
## Mechanics
- No registration with IDOR is needed to use the IL-1040 option
- Enter the year's total on Line 20; pay it with the return by April 15
- Give the employee three copies of federal Form W-2 by January 31. Do not send the W-2 to IDOR; keep it three years
- W-2s for household employees reported on the IL-1040 are EXEMPT from Illinois's electronic W-2 filing mandate
- Never report the same withholding on Form IL-941 or pay it with IL-501. If you have been filing IL-941 solely for a household employee and want to switch to the IL-1040, notify IDOR (MyTax Illinois or 217 785-3707) or face penalties for the missing IL-941s
## Notes
- Over-withholding cannot be refunded to you; the employee claims it on their own IL-1040 as withholding. Correct errors by adjusting later pay periods in the same year
- Record-keeping: dates and amounts of all payments, names, addresses, SSNs, pay periods, W-4 and IL-W-4, W-2 copies — three years
- Unemployment insurance is separate (IDES); federal FICA and FUTA are on federal Schedule H
- A nonresident household employee working in Illinois is still subject to Illinois withholding on Illinois wages
- Because Line 20 sits after the credits, no nonrefundable credit reduces it
## Required Information
- Total Illinois tax withheld from each household employee this year
- Whether any of it was already reported on IL-941
## Questions
- Do you pay someone to work in your home, and do you control the work?
- Do you withhold federal income tax from their pay, or have a written agreement to withhold?
- How much Illinois tax did you withhold this year?
- Did you file Form IL-941 for this employee?
## Common Errors
- Reporting the withholding on both the IL-1040 and IL-941
- Entering the employee's WAGES on Line 20 instead of the tax withheld
- Assuming withholding is mandatory when there is no federal withholding or agreement
- Sending the W-2 to IDOR
- Expecting credits to offset Line 20
