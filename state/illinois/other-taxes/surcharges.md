# Medical Cannabis and Gaming Licensee Asset-Sale Surcharges — Line 22
## Description
Two surcharges that recapture, for Illinois, the FEDERAL income tax generated when a medical cannabis registrant or a gaming licensee sells its business assets. The surcharge equals the federal tax attributable to the sale — so the seller effectively pays federal tax twice, once to the IRS and once to Illinois. It applies to ANY taxpayer who bears the federal tax on those gains, including an individual partner or shareholder who never touched the license. Rare, but large when it hits.
## The two programs
| Surcharge | Who is the licensee | Tax years |
|---|---|---|
| Compassionate Use of Medical Cannabis Program Act | An "organization registrant": a corporation, partnership, trust, LLC, or other organization holding a medical cannabis cultivation center registration (Dept. of Agriculture) or dispensary registration (IDFPR) | Every tax year beginning or ending while the program is in effect — made permanent in 2019, so no sunset |
| Sale of Assets by Gaming Licensees | An organization licensee under the Illinois Horse Racing Act of 1975 or an organization gaming licensee under the Illinois Gambling Act (racetracks, racinos) | Tax years beginning 2019 through 2027 — 2026 is the second-to-last year |
## Transactions subject to the surcharge
Sales and exchanges by the registrant or licensee of:
- Capital assets
- Depreciable business property
- Real property used in the trade or business
- IRC Section 197 intangibles (licenses, goodwill, customer lists)
Ordinary operating income is NOT subject to the surcharge; only gains on these asset categories.
## Amount — the Surcharge Worksheet
1. Your federal income tax liability for the year, as filed
2. Your federal income tax liability recomputed AS IF the surcharge transactions had not occurred
3. Line 1 − Line 2 = the surcharge → IL-1040 Line 22
"Attributable" federal tax therefore includes the marginal effects — rate bracket shifts, net investment income tax interactions, AMT — of removing the gain from the federal return, not just the gain × a rate.
## Who pays it
The taxpayer who INCURS the federal tax on the income, whether or not they are the licensee. Gains passed through a partnership, S corporation, trust, or estate reach the owner on a dedicated line of Schedule K-1-P or K-1-T identifying the federal income attributable to surcharge transactions; the owner runs the worksheet on their own federal return. Adult-use (recreational) cannabis licensees under the Cannabis Regulation and Tax Act are NOT covered — only medical program registrants.
## Gaming licensee exemptions — no surcharge if
- The license or racetrack property transfers because of bankruptcy, receivership, or debt adjustment; cancellation or revocation by the Gaming Board or Racing Board; a Gaming Board finding that the transfer serves Illinois gaming; the death of an equity owner; acquisition of a controlling interest in, or substantially all assets of, a publicly traded company; a parent-to-wholly-owned-subsidiary transfer; or a transfer between persons who were both initial owners when the license issued
- The controlling interest transfers to lineal descendants, or in an IRC Section 351 exchange, with no gain or loss recognized
- Live horse racing was not conducted in 2010 at a racetrack within three miles of the Mississippi River under a Horse Racing Act license
- The transferor is someone other than the INITIAL licensee — only the first holder's sale is surcharged
## Notes
- Sits after the credits: no Schedule CR, ICR, or 1299-C credit reduces it
- The surcharge is a state tax deductible federally as SALT, subject to the cap
- Keep both federal computations; IDOR will ask for the "as if" return
- A 2026 QSBS gain add-back (income/additions) is a separate Illinois consequence and does not interact with the surcharge
## Required Information
- Federal return as filed and the pro forma federal return without the transactions
- K-1-P / K-1-T surcharge-income line
- Nature of the assets sold and, for gaming, whether an exemption applies
## Questions
- Did a medical cannabis registrant or a racetrack/casino licensee you own an interest in sell assets, a license, or real property this year?
- Does your Illinois K-1-P or K-1-T show income attributable to surcharge transactions?
- For a gaming licensee, was the transfer one of the exempt types?
## Common Errors
- Applying the surcharge to adult-use cannabis businesses
- Computing gain × rate instead of the with-and-without federal tax difference
- Missing the surcharge on a K-1 passed through from a partnership
- Claiming a gaming exemption for a transfer by a subsequent (non-initial) licensee's buyer when the seller was the initial licensee
