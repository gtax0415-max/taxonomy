# Other Additions — Lines 3, 6, 8, and 11
## Description
The remaining Schedule M additions, each narrow but each mandatory when it applies. Line 11 is the catch-all, and it is where the two items most likely to matter for 2026 sit: the pass-through entity tax add-back and the new QSBS gain add-back.
## Line 3 — Lloyd's plan of operation loss
Add back a loss from a Lloyd's plan of operation (a Lloyd's of London syndicate structure) that was included in federal AGI if the loss was reported on your behalf on Form IL-1065. The corresponding income is subtracted on Line 28. Rare outside insurance investors.
## Line 6 — Business expense recapture, NONRESIDENTS ONLY
If you treated income from an asset or activity as BUSINESS income in prior years (apportioned, so partly outside Illinois) and this year treat income from the same asset as NONBUSINESS income on Schedule M or Schedule NR (allocated, so possibly entirely outside Illinois), add back the deductions you claimed for expenses connected with that income this year and in the two most recent prior years. Anti-whipsaw rule.
## Line 8 — Student-Assistance Contribution credit
An employer who claims the Student-Assistance Contributions credit (Schedule 1299-C, code 5420 — 25% of matching contributions to Bright Start, Bright Directions, or College Illinois, up to $500 per employee) must add the credit amount from Schedule 1299-C Step 3 Column G back to income, because the contribution was also deducted as a business expense. Current-year credit only, not carryforwards.
## Line 11 — Other income, the catch-all
Add any other amount required to reach Illinois base income, with a description. Two known items:
### Pass-through entity tax add-back
For tax years ending on or after December 31, 2021, add back the amount of PTE tax you included on Schedule CR Line 51 (tax paid to another state through a pass-through entity, claimed as your credit). The 2025 instructions state this applies to tax years "beginning prior to January 1, 2026" — language tied to the original sunset of the PTE election. Public Act 104-0468 continued and expanded the PTE election for tax years ending on or after December 31, 2026 (Bulletin FY 2027-01), so the add-back is expected to continue on the 2026 Schedule M; confirm when the 2026 form is issued.
### Qualified Small Business Stock gain — NEW for 2026
For tax years ending on or after December 31, 2026, Illinois decouples from IRC Section 1202. The gain EXCLUDED federally on qualified small business stock (50%, 75%, or 100% depending on acquisition date, and up to the $10 million / $15 million per-issuer cap under the 2025 federal changes) must be ADDED BACK to Illinois base income (35 ILCS 5/203(a)(2)(D-26)). Bulletin FY 2027-01 says the 2026 Schedule M will carry a specific line; until then it is a Line 11 item. Consequences:
- A founder or early investor excluding $10 million federally owes Illinois 4.95% on it: $495,000 of Illinois tax with zero federal tax
- The gain is on an intangible, so for a resident it is Illinois-sourced and produces no Schedule CR credit even if another state also taxes it
- Estimated payments for 2026 should reflect the add-back; the bulletin's annualized-income relief is available for the first quarter after July 1, 2026
- Trusts, estates, and partnerships face the parallel add-back; a partner's share arrives on Schedule K-1-P → Line 2
## Required Information
- Line 3: Form IL-1065 reporting of the Lloyd's loss
- Line 6: prior two years' expense deductions for the reclassified activity
- Line 8: the 1299-C credit amount
- Line 11: Schedule CR Line 51 PTE tax; federal Form 8949 Section 1202 exclusion amount
## Questions
- Did you exclude gain on qualified small business stock on your 2026 federal return?
- Did you claim a Schedule CR credit for pass-through entity tax paid to another state?
- As an employer, did you claim the Student-Assistance Contributions credit this year?
- As a nonresident, did you change an asset's income from business to nonbusiness treatment?
## Common Errors
- Missing the 2026 QSBS add-back because the federal return shows no gain
- Assuming the PTE add-back expired for 2026
- Forgetting the Line 8 add-back when claiming credit 5420
- A resident using Line 6, which is nonresidents only
