# Illinois Exemption Allowance
## Description
The only thing standing between base income and the 4.95% rate. Illinois has no standard deduction; the exemption allowance is its substitute, and it is small. For 2026 it is $2,925 per person plus $1,000 each for age 65 and for blindness — and it vanishes ENTIRELY above a federal AGI cliff.
## Amounts for 2026
| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Personal exemption, you | $2,925 |
| Personal exemption, spouse on a joint return | $2,925 |
| Each dependent (Schedule IL-E/EITC Step 2) | $2,925 |
| Age 65 or older, you and/or spouse | $1,000 each |
| Legally blind, you and/or spouse | $1,000 each |
(2025: $2,850 personal and dependent; the $1,000 add-ons are not indexed.) Note the age and blindness add-ons apply to the taxpayer and spouse only, not to dependents.
## The cliff
If federal AGI exceeds $500,000 (married filing jointly) or $250,000 (single, head of household, married filing separately, widowed), Line 10 is ZERO — no personal, dependent, age, or blindness exemption. One dollar over the line costs a joint couple with two children $11,700 of exemptions, or $579 of tax. The same cliff switches off the property tax credit and the K-12 credit. It is a cliff, not a phase-out.
## If someone else can claim you (Step 1, Line C)
| Filing status | Box checked | Base income (Line 9) | Line 10a |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single* | No | any | $2,925 |
| Single* | Yes | $2,925 or below | $2,925 |
| Single* | Yes | $2,926 or more | $0 |
| Joint | No | any | $5,850 |
| Joint | one spouse | $2,925 or below | $5,850 |
| Joint | one spouse | $2,926 or more | $2,925 |
| Joint | both | $5,850 or below | $5,850 |
| Joint | both | $5,851 or more | $0 |
*Single here means single, head of household, widowed, and married filing separately. (The 2025 chart uses $2,850 / $5,700; the 2026 form will carry $2,925 / $5,850.)
A dependent whose base income stays at or below the exemption still gets it and owes nothing; a dependent with base income above it loses the exemption and pays 4.95% on everything from the first dollar. A dependent must file only if base income exceeds $2,925 or to recover withholding.
## Notes
- Widowed (federal qualifying surviving spouse) is a single-column status here, with one $2,925 exemption
- Nonresidents and part-year residents compute the allowance on Schedule NR Line 50, prorated by the Illinois-income ratio
- Injured-spouse separate returns split the dependents between the two returns; each dependent may be claimed once
- The dependent exemption requires Schedule IL-E/EITC to be attached even when no credit is claimed
- A child's Form 8814 income reported on the parent's return does not count toward the child's own filing threshold
## Required Information
- Federal AGI and filing status
- Whether you or your spouse can be claimed by someone else
- Number of dependents, each with SSN or ITIN
- Ages and blindness status for you and your spouse
## Questions
- Is your federal AGI over $500,000 joint or $250,000 otherwise?
- Can a parent or anyone else claim you as a dependent?
- Were you or your spouse 65 or older, or legally blind, at year end?
- How many dependents are you claiming?
## Common Errors
- Claiming exemptions above the AGI cliff
- Claiming the $1,000 age or blindness add-on for a dependent
- Using the prior-year $2,850 figure
- A claimed dependent taking the full exemption when base income exceeds $2,925
- Forgetting to attach Schedule IL-E/EITC for dependents
