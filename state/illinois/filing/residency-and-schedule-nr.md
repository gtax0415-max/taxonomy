# Residency and Schedule NR — Nonresidents and Part-Year Residents
## Description
Illinois taxes RESIDENTS on everything and NONRESIDENTS only on Illinois-source income. Residency turns on DOMICILE — where you live and intend to return — not on days present, so a resident on temporary assignment abroad, a student away at school, or a snowbird in Florida for the winter stays an Illinois resident. Anyone who is not a full-year resident checks a Line D box and computes net income and tax on Schedule NR instead of on IL-1040 Lines 11-12.
## Who is what
- RESIDENT: domiciled in Illinois for the entire year. Temporary absences — military duty, foreign residence, out-of-state school, winter or summer homes — do not break domicile
- PART-YEAR RESIDENT: domiciled in Illinois for part of the year (moved in or out)
- NONRESIDENT: never domiciled in Illinois during the year
Military service members keep their home-of-record domicile; spouses may elect the service member's domicile under federal law.
## What Schedule NR does
1. Column A: the full federal amounts (or, for part-year residents, resident-period amounts plus Illinois-source nonresident-period amounts)
2. Column B: the ILLINOIS portion of each income, addition, and subtraction line
3. Line 46: Illinois base income; Line 47-48: the Illinois ratio (Illinois base income ÷ total base income), the decimal that also prorates the EITC on Schedule IL-E/EITC Line 8
4. Line 50: exemption allowance × the ratio
5. Line 51: Illinois net income → IL-1040 Line 11; Line 52: tax at 4.95% → IL-1040 Line 12
The nonrefundable credits on Lines 15-17 are then applied as usual; Schedule CR is available only to residents and part-year residents (resident period).
## Illinois-source income for a nonresident
- Compensation for services performed IN Illinois. Since 2020, a nonresident employee owes Illinois tax on wages only if they work in Illinois MORE THAN 30 working days in the year; below that, none of the wages are Illinois-sourced (the employer need not withhold either)
- Business income apportioned to Illinois by the sales factor (Illinois sales ÷ total sales)
- Rents and gains from real or tangible property located in Illinois
- Illinois lottery and casino winnings, sports wagering winnings paid in Illinois
- Pass-through income from Illinois entities per the K-1-P / K-1-T apportionment
- NOT Illinois-source for a nonresident: interest, dividends, retirement income, and gains on intangibles such as stock — these follow the owner's domicile
## Reciprocal states
Residents of Iowa, Kentucky, Michigan, and Wisconsin owe no Illinois tax on Illinois wages, salaries, tips, and commissions and give the employer Form IL-W-5-NR to stop withholding. Other Illinois-source income (lottery, unemployment, business, rental) is still taxable and reported on Schedule NR.
## Part-year residents
Column A includes everything received while a resident (from any source) plus Illinois-source income received while a nonresident. The retirement subtraction, 529 subtraction, and other Schedule M items are taken only to the extent they relate to the resident period or Illinois-source income. Schedule CR is available for other-state tax on resident-period income only.
## Notes
- Schedule NR must be attached; a checked Line D box without it is an incomplete return
- Two full-year residents who file jointly federally must file jointly or separately consistently; a resident married to a nonresident should file separately — see filing-status.md
- Refund of Illinois tax withheld in error from a nonresident (e.g., a Wisconsin resident's wages) requires an employer letter on letterhead
- Publication 119 walks through Schedule NR with examples
## Required Information
- Residency dates and domicile facts
- Illinois vs. non-Illinois split of every income line
- Working days in Illinois for a nonresident employee
- K-1-P / K-1-T Illinois amounts
## Questions
- Where were you domiciled on January 1 and December 31, and did that change during the year?
- If you lived outside Illinois, did you intend to return?
- As a nonresident, how many days did you work in Illinois, and do you have Illinois rental, business, or gambling income?
- Are you a resident of Iowa, Kentucky, Michigan, or Wisconsin?
## Common Errors
- Filing as a nonresident after moving out of state "temporarily" without changing domicile
- Treating stock gains or pension income as Illinois-source for a nonresident
- A nonresident with under 30 Illinois working days paying Illinois tax on wages anyway
- Omitting Schedule NR while checking the Line D box
- A part-year resident computing the exemption in full instead of prorated
## Prompt
- I moved from Illinois to Texas in August, how does Illinois tax me?
- I live in Missouri and work in Illinois three days a week, do I owe Illinois tax?
- I am an Illinois resident working abroad for two years, am I still a resident?
- What is Schedule NR?
