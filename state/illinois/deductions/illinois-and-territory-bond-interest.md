# Exempt Illinois and Territorial Bond Interest
## Description
The narrow exception to Illinois's rule that municipal bond interest is taxable. Interest from state and local government obligations is exempt from Illinois tax ONLY where the enabling legislation for that specific issuer says so. This file is the list. Everything not on it — Illinois general obligation bonds, City of Chicago, school districts, park districts, other states' bonds — is taxable in Illinois after being added on IL-1040 Line 2.
## Line 34 — exempt Illinois issuers
- Illinois Housing Development Authority bonds and notes (except housing-related commercial facilities bonds and notes)
- Tri-County River Valley Development Authority bonds
- Illinois Development Finance Authority: venture fund and infrastructure bonds; bonds under Sections 7.80-7.87 of the IDFA Act; bonds under the Asbestos Abatement Finance Act
- Quad Cities Regional Economic Development Authority bonds and notes declared exempt by the Authority
- College savings bonds under the General Obligation Bond Act per the Baccalaureate Savings Act
- Illinois Sports Facilities Authority bonds
- Higher Education Student Assistance Act bonds
- Rural Bond Bank Act bonds and notes
- Quad Cities Interstate Metropolitan Authority bonds
- Southwestern Illinois Development Authority bonds
- Illinois Finance Authority bonds under Sections 820.60 and 825.55 of the IFA Act, or the Asbestos Abatement Finance Act
- Illinois Power Agency bonds issued by the Illinois Finance Authority
- Central, Eastern, Southeastern, Southern, and Western Illinois Economic Development Authority bonds
- Illinois Urban Development Authority bonds
- Downstate Illinois Sports Facilities Authority bonds
- Upper Illinois River Valley Development Authority Act bonds
- Will-Kankakee Regional Development Authority bonds
- Export Development Act of 1983 bonds
- New Harmony Bridge Authority and New Harmony Bridge Bi-State Commission bonds — NO LONGER exempt for tax years beginning on or after August 19, 2023
## Line 35 — non-U.S. governments
Bonds of Guam, Puerto Rico, the U.S. Virgin Islands, American Samoa, and the Northern Mariana Islands, and mutual mortgage insurance fund bonds. Federal law exempts territorial bonds from state tax; Illinois honors it here.
## Line 36 — a child's Form 8814 income
The portion of a child's income you reported on Form 8814 that would have been subtractable on Lines 22, 34, or 35 had you received it directly, provided the child's income is in your IL-1040 Line 1 or Line 3.
## The mutual fund rule
NONE of these exemptions applies to bonds held indirectly through a mutual fund. An "Illinois municipal bond fund" produces fully taxable Illinois income. Only direct holdings qualify.
## Notes
- All three lines are net of bond premium amortization
- The interest must first have been INCLUDED — on Line 1 if federally taxable, or Line 2/3 if federally exempt — before it can be subtracted; the two-step is deliberate
- Bond issuers rarely tell holders which statutory section a bond was issued under; the official statement or the trustee is the source
## Required Information
- Issuer name and, for IDFA/IFA bonds, the statutory section
- Whether held directly or through a fund
- Form 8814 amounts, if any
## Questions
- Which Illinois authority issued the bond, and do you hold it directly?
- Do you hold any Puerto Rico or other territorial bonds?
- Is the bond in a mutual fund?
## Common Errors
- Subtracting interest on ordinary Illinois or Chicago municipal bonds
- Subtracting the Illinois share of a municipal bond fund
- Continuing to subtract New Harmony Bridge bond interest
- Subtracting on Line 34 without first adding on IL-1040 Line 2
## Prompt
- Which Illinois bonds are exempt from Illinois income tax?
- I own Puerto Rico bonds, does Illinois tax the interest?
- My Illinois muni bond fund says it is state tax free, is it?
