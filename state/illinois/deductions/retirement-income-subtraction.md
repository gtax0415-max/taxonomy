# Social Security and Retirement Income Subtraction
## Description
Illinois exempts retirement income COMPLETELY. Every dollar of federally taxed Social Security, pension, annuity, 401(k), 403(b), 457, IRA, and railroad retirement income comes off on IL-1040 Line 5 — no age requirement, no income cap, no phase-out. Illinois is one of the few states with an income tax that does this, and it is the reason a retiree with $150,000 of pension and IRA income can owe no Illinois tax at all. The subtraction is limited only by what was INCLUDED in federal AGI.
## What is subtracted
Federally taxed amounts included in IL-1040 Line 1 that you received from:
- Qualified employee benefit plans — pensions, 401(k), 403(b), profit-sharing, railroad retirement Tier 1 and Tier 2 — and IRAs and self-employed (SEP, SIMPLE, Keogh) plans, reported on Form 1040 Lines 4b and 5b
- Social Security and railroad retirement benefits reported on Form 1040 Line 6b
- Government retirement and government disability plans, and group-term life insurance premiums paid by a qualified plan, reported as WAGES on Form 1040 Line 1z
- State or local government deferred compensation plans (Section 457) reported on Line 1z or 5b
- Certain capital gains on employer securities (net unrealized appreciation on a lump-sum distribution) reported on Form 1040 Line 7
- Your beneficiary share of retirement plan payments on Schedule K-1-T, and retirement payments to retired partners on Schedule K-1-P
## What is not subtracted
- Amounts not in federal AGI — the untaxed 15% of Social Security, Roth distributions, return of basis. Nothing to subtract
- Early distributions? They ARE subtractable; the federal 10% penalty is a federal matter and Illinois has no equivalent
- Non-qualified deferred compensation from a private employer, and non-qualified annuities purchased individually — taxable in Illinois. The subtraction is for QUALIFIED plans and government plans
- Disability income from a private disability policy, third-party sick pay
- Interest on a retirement account paid outside a plan
- Unemployment compensation
## Notes
- Social Security: the subtraction is the TAXABLE amount from Line 6b, not the gross benefit
- IRA-to-Roth conversions are taxed federally and subtracted here; Illinois effectively taxes neither the conversion nor the later Roth withdrawal. Converting while an Illinois resident before moving to a state that taxes retirement income is a known planning move
- Inherited IRA distributions qualify. Lump-sum distributions qualify. Required minimum distributions qualify
- Illinois Teachers' Retirement System, SURS, SERS, IMRF, and Chicago fund pensions all qualify; do NOT put them on Schedule M
- Attach Form 1040 page 1 and Schedule 1 plus every 1099-R and W-2. If the income is not on Lines 4b, 5b, or 6b, Publication 120 lists the extra attachment (e.g., a plan statement for government disability reported as wages)
- Part-year residents subtract only the retirement income received while a resident, through Schedule NR
## Required Information
- Form 1040 Lines 4b, 5b, 6b
- Any retirement amounts embedded in Line 1z (Box 11 or Box 12 codes on the W-2, or a plan letter)
- 1099-Rs with distribution codes, SSA-1099, RRB-1099s
- K-1-P or K-1-T retirement lines
## Questions
- What retirement income was taxed on your federal return — Social Security, pension, IRA, 401(k)?
- Is any of it a government plan or deferred compensation reported as wages?
- Is any of it a non-qualified private plan or a personally purchased annuity?
- Did you convert an IRA to a Roth this year?
## Common Errors
- Subtracting gross Social Security instead of the taxable Line 6b amount
- Putting pensions on Schedule M instead of Line 5
- Missing government retirement or 457 income reported on the W-2 as wages
- Subtracting non-qualified deferred compensation
- Forgetting to attach Form 1040 page 1 and Schedule 1, the most common Line 5 correspondence trigger
- Subtracting the retirement income again on Schedule M Line 14 when it came through a K-1
## Prompt
- Does Illinois tax Social Security?
- Does Illinois tax my pension or 401(k) withdrawals?
- I converted my IRA to a Roth, do I owe Illinois tax?
- I retired from the State of Illinois, is my pension taxable?
