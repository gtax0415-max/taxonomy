# Illinois Deductions — Index (IL-1040 Step 3, Lines 5-7)
## Description
Illinois calls these SUBTRACTIONS, and they are the only deductions the state has — there is no standard deduction and no itemized deduction. Amounts Illinois excludes that the federal return taxed. Two sit directly on the IL-1040 — retirement income (Line 5) and the prior-year Illinois refund (Line 6) — and everything else runs through Schedule M Step 3 to Line 7. Subtractions are entered as positive numbers, the list is CLOSED, and the retirement subtraction is by a wide margin the largest: Illinois exempts every dollar of Social Security, pension, 401(k), and IRA income, with no age test and no income limit.

| Line | Item | Attach |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Social Security benefits and certain retirement plan income included in Line 1 (generally Form 1040 Lines 4b, 5b, 6b) | Federal Form 1040 page 1 and Schedule 1, W-2s and 1099s |
| 6 | Illinois income tax overpayment included in federal Schedule 1, Line 1 | — |
| 7 | Other subtractions | Schedule M |

## 2026 parameters
- 529 and ABLE contribution caps $10,000 single / $20,000 joint, each, unchanged
- Retirement subtraction: unlimited, unchanged
- Medical debt relief subtraction continues
## Notes
- Schedule M must be attached whenever Line 7 has an amount, with the attachments each line requires (K-1-P/K-1-T, IL-4562, Schedule B, W-2s, Schedule F)
- The rule for everything on Schedule M: the amount must be INCLUDED in IL-1040 Line 1 (or Lines 2-3) to be subtracted. Income the federal return already excluded gets nothing
- Distributive-share versions of every subtraction go on Line 14, not the item's own line — except zone dividends (Line 24) and retired-partner retirement payments (IL-1040 Line 5)
