# Standard Deduction Chart
## Description
Alabama's standard deduction shrinks as Alabama AGI rises (Code of Alabama §40-18-15(b)(4)). It is read from a chart by filing status. Figure the deduction both ways and use the larger of standard or itemized.

## Amounts (unchanged for 2026)
| Status | Full amount | Reduction | Floor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Married filing jointly | $8,500 (AGI up to $25,999) | $175 per full $500 | $5,000 at $35,500+ |
| Head of family | $5,200 (AGI up to $25,999) | $135 per full $500 | $2,500 at $35,500+ |
| Single | $3,000 (AGI up to $25,999) | $25 per full $500 | $2,500 at $35,500+ |
| Married filing separately | $4,250 (AGI up to $12,999) | $88 per full $250 | $2,500 at $17,750+ |

## Notes
- The statute reduces for each FULL $500 ($250) step over $25,500 ($12,750); a partial step does not count, so the first reduced chart row starts at $26,000 ($13,000)
- Uses ALABAMA AGI (Form 40, line 10), not federal AGI
- The 2025 chart misprints the third married-filing-jointly row as "$25,500 – $26,999"; it should read $26,500 – $26,999 ($8,150)
- Married filing separately: both spouses must use the same method unless they lived apart all year
- A dependent may take the full chart amount

## Example
Married filing jointly with Alabama AGI of $27,250: three full $500 steps over $25,500, so $8,500 − $525 = $7,975 (chart row $27,000–$27,499).

## Required Information
- Alabama AGI
- Filing status
- Itemized total, to compare

## Questions
- What is your Alabama AGI?
- Is your Schedule A total larger than the standard deduction?

## Common Errors
- Using the federal standard deduction
- Using federal AGI
- Counting a partial $500 step
- Following the misprinted chart row

## Prompt
- How is the Alabama standard deduction figured?
