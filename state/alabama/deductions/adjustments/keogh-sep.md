# Keogh and SEP Deduction
## Description
Deduction for a self-employed person's contributions to their own retirement plan: SEP, SIMPLE, solo 401(k) or Keogh. Code of Alabama §40-18-15(a)(12) adopts IRC §404, so the amount matches the federal deduction.

## Amounts (2026 federal limits, which flow through)
- Total annual additions (employer + employee) cap: $72,000
- Solo 401(k) elective deferral: $24,500; catch-up $8,000 at 50+; $11,250 at ages 60–63
- SIMPLE deferral: $17,000
- Compensation counted: up to $360,000
- Employer-side contribution limited to 20% of net self-employment earnings (after half of SE tax)

## Notes
- An employee's 401(k) deferrals through a W-2 job do NOT go here; they are already excluded from W-2 wages
- Contributions for a partner are based on the partner's net SE earnings

## Example
A sole proprietor defers $24,500 to a solo 401(k) and adds a $15,000 employer contribution: line 2 is $39,500.

## Required Information
- Plan type
- Contributions made for 2026
- Net self-employment earnings

## Questions
- Are you self-employed with a SEP, SIMPLE or solo 401(k)?
- How much did you contribute for 2026, as employee and as employer?

## Common Errors
- Putting W-2 job 401(k) deferrals here
- Deducting it on Schedule A
- Exceeding the 20%-of-net-earnings limit

## Prompt
- Where do solo 401(k) contributions go?
- I contributed to a SEP IRA for my business.
