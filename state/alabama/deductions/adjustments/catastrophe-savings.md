# Catastrophe Savings Account
## Description
Alabama-only deduction for deposits to a catastrophe savings account used to pay the insurance deductible and uninsured losses on the taxpayer's legal residence. Code of Alabama §§40-18-310 to -312.

## Amounts (deposit limits for 2026)
- Insurance deductible of $1,000 or less: up to $2,000
- Deductible over $1,000: the lesser of $15,000 or twice the deductible
- Self-insured home: the lesser of $250,000 or the home's value
- Married filing jointly: one deduction, one account

## 2026 status
- Unchanged for 2026
- Act 2026-100 (HB27) applies to tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2027: adds FORTIFIED and reroofing costs as qualified expenses, raises each cap by up to $15,000, and changes "legal residence" to "residential property"

## Notes
- Interest on deposits within the cap is not taxed; interest on excess deposits is
- Non-qualified withdrawals are taxable income plus a 2.5% additional tax
- Withdrawals are not deductible

## Example
A homeowner with a $2,500 hurricane deductible deposits $6,000: deduction is $5,000 (twice the deductible).

## Required Information
- 2026 deposits and year-end balance
- Insurance deductible
- Any withdrawals and their purpose

## Questions
- Do you have an account designated as a catastrophe savings account?
- What is your homeowner's insurance deductible?
- Did you withdraw anything, and for what?

## Common Errors
- Deducting withdrawals
- Deducting more than the deductible-based limit
- Applying the 2027 Act 2026-100 limits to 2026

## Prompt
- What is a catastrophe savings account?
