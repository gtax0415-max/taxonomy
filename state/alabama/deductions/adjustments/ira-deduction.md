# IRA Deduction
## Description
Deduction for contributions to a traditional IRA. Code of Alabama §40-18-15(a)(11) adopts IRC §219, so Alabama allows the same deductible amount as the federal return. Each spouse's deduction goes on a separate line.

## Amounts (2026 federal limits, which flow through)
- Contribution limit: $7,500 per person (up from $7,000 for 2025), plus $1,100 catch-up at age 50 or older
- Deduction phase-out if covered by a workplace plan: modified AGI $81,000–$91,000 single; $129,000–$149,000 married filing jointly
- Phase-out for a spouse who is NOT covered but whose spouse is: $242,000–$252,000

## Notes
- Roth IRA contributions are never deductible
- Contributions made by April 15, 2027 can count for 2026
- Line 1a is your deduction; line 1b is your spouse's

## Required Information
- Traditional IRA contributions for each spouse
- Whether each spouse is covered by a workplace retirement plan
- Modified AGI

## Questions
- Did you or your spouse contribute to a traditional IRA for 2026?
- Is either of you covered by a retirement plan at work?
- Was any of it a Roth contribution?

## Common Errors
- Deducting a Roth contribution
- Ignoring the phase-out for workplace-plan participants
- Putting both spouses' deductions on line 1a
- Using the 2025 $7,000 limit

## Prompt
- Can I deduct my IRA contribution in Alabama?
- We each put $7,500 in a traditional IRA.
