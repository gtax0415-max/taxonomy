# Taxes: Real Estate, FICA, Self-Employment and Other
## Description
Alabama allows deductions for several taxes the federal return does not, most importantly Social Security and Medicare withheld and self-employment tax. State income tax is NOT deductible.

## Lines
- Line 5: real estate taxes on non-business property (escrowed taxes when the lender pays them)
- Line 6: FICA (Social Security and Medicare) withheld, and federal self-employment tax in full
- Line 7: Railroad Retirement Tier 1
- Line 8: other taxes — the ad valorem part of personal property tax (car tags), city, county and occupational taxes, GST tax, federal gift tax you paid
- Line 9: total

## 2026 amounts
- Social Security wage base: $184,500, so Social Security withheld counts up to $11,439 per person; excess withheld by multiple employers is NOT deductible
- Federal cap on deductible taxes (Code of Alabama §40-18-15(a)(3)f, following IRC §164(b)): $40,400 ($20,200 married filing separately), reduced by 30% of income over $505,000 ($252,500), not below $10,000 ($5,000). In Alabama it applies only to real property, personal property and GST taxes, not to FICA or SE tax. ALDOR has not published how it applies the phase-down; Alabama instructions say all limits use Alabama AGI

## What does NOT qualify
- State income tax, state and local sales tax, foreign income tax
- License fees, gasoline and excise taxes

## Example
A joint return with $136,000 of wages: FICA $10,404 on line 6 and real estate tax $6,400 on line 5.

## Required Information
- FICA withheld for each spouse
- SE tax
- Property and other taxes paid

## Questions
- How much Social Security and Medicare was withheld from each W-2?
- What property taxes did you pay?
- Did you pay self-employment tax?

## Common Errors
- Deducting state income tax
- Deducting excess Social Security withheld by multiple employers
- Deducting only half of self-employment tax
- Applying the tax cap to FICA

## Prompt
- Can I deduct FICA in Alabama?
