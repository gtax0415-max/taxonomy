# Federal Income Tax Deduction
## Description
Alabama allows a deduction for the federal income tax LIABILITY for the year, after refundable credits (Code of Alabama §40-18-15; ALDOR Rule 810-3-15-.20). It is figured on the worksheet in the Form 40 instructions.

## Worksheet (line references from the 2026 draft Form 1040, August 2026; confirm on the final 2026 Alabama worksheet)
1. Federal tax after nonrefundable credits (2026 Form 1040, line 22)
2. Net investment income tax (Form 8960, line 17)
3. Add lines 1 and 2
4. Subtract refundable credits: (a) earned income credit (line 27a), (b) additional child tax credit (line 28), (c) refundable American opportunity credit (line 29), (d) refundable adoption credit (line 30), (e) Form 2439 credit (Schedule 3, line 13a)
5. Total of line 4
6. Line 3 minus line 5, not below zero → Form 40, line 12

## Rules
- It is the LIABILITY for the year, not withholding or payments
- Self-employment tax, additional Medicare tax and other Schedule 2 taxes are not included
- Joint federal return, separate Alabama returns: each spouse deducts the liability times their share of joint federal AGI
- Part-year resident: multiply by Alabama AGI ÷ federal AGI
- Attach federal Form 1040 pages 1 and 2
- Unchanged for 2026. Note: on the 2026 Form 1040 "total tax" moves to line 24a; the Alabama deduction starts from line 22, not total tax

## Example
Federal tax of $10,200, earned income credit $0, additional child tax credit $600: line 12 is $9,600.

## Required Information
- 2026 federal Form 1040 and Form 8960
- Each refundable credit

## Questions
- What was your federal tax for 2026?
- Did you receive any refundable federal credits?
- Did you file jointly federally but separately in Alabama?

## Common Errors
- Entering federal tax withheld
- Not subtracting refundable credits, including the refundable adoption credit
- Adding self-employment tax

## Prompt
- Can I deduct federal tax on my Alabama return?
