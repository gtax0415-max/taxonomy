# Credit for Taxes Paid to Other States
## Description
Nonrefundable credit that keeps an Alabama resident from being taxed twice on income that another state also taxes. Figured on Schedule CR, one column per state, and carried to Schedule OC, Part A.

## Amount
The credit is the SMALLEST of:
1. The income tax actually paid to the other state on that income
2. The Alabama tax on that same income
3. Alabama tax before credits × (income taxed by other states ÷ total Alabama AGI)

The third limit (Code of Alabama §40-18-21(a)(3), added by Act 2018-465) applies to the total across all states.

## Eligibility
- Full-year Alabama residents filing Form 40. NOT available on Form 40A
- Part-year residents only for income earned while an Alabama resident
- The income must be taxed by both Alabama and the other state

## Notes
- Only income taxes paid to another U.S. STATE or TERRITORY qualify. States with no income tax give no credit. City and county income taxes do not appear to qualify
- Foreign-country income tax goes on Part I, not Part A
- Pass-through entity tax paid by a partnership or S corporation to another state can qualify. Any such tax deducted at the entity level must be added back to Alabama income
- Claim it in the year the income is taxable in the other state, not the year the tax is paid
- No carryforward
- No change for 2026

## Required Information
- Other state's return and the tax shown on it (after that state's credits)
- Income from that state included in Alabama AGI
- Total Alabama AGI and Alabama tax before credits

## Questions
- Did you earn income in another state that taxed it?
- Did you file a return with that state?
- Did a partnership or S corporation pay tax to another state on your share?
- Were you an Alabama resident all year?

## Common Errors
- Claiming tax withheld by the other state instead of the tax actually owed on its return
- Claiming tax paid to a state with no income tax, or to a city or county
- Skipping the ratio limit
- Filing Form 40A
- Putting foreign taxes in Part A instead of Part I

## Prompt
- I live in Alabama but work in Georgia.
- I paid tax to another state on rental income there.
- My S corporation paid tax to Tennessee on my share.
