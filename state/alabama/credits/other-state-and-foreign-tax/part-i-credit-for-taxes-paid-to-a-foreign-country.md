# Credit for Taxes Paid to a Foreign Country
## Description
Nonrefundable credit for an Alabama resident's share of foreign income tax paid by a PASS-THROUGH ENTITY or trust. Code of Alabama §40-18-21(c). Despite the name, it does NOT cover foreign tax on wages or investments the individual holds directly.

## Amount
The LESSER of:
1. 50% of the taxpayer's share of income tax paid or accrued to a foreign country (taxes creditable under IRC §901)
2. The Alabama tax on the income from that country

Unchanged for 2026.

## Eligibility
- Alabama residents filing Form 40
- Only as a resident partner or member of a partnership-taxed entity, an S corporation shareholder, or an estate or trust beneficiary

## What does NOT qualify
- Foreign tax on wages earned abroad by the individual
- Foreign tax withheld on dividends in a personal brokerage account (Form 1099-DIV)
- Taxes paid to another U.S. state (use Part A)

## Notes
- Separate from the federal foreign tax credit; claiming the federal credit does not prevent this one
- No carryforward
- Alabama deducts federal income tax, so the federal foreign tax credit also lowers that deduction

## Required Information
- Foreign tax shown on each K-1, by country
- Foreign-source income from each entity
- Alabama tax on that income

## Questions
- Did a partnership, S corporation or trust pass foreign taxes through to you?
- Which countries were the taxes paid to?
- Is any of your foreign tax from your own wages or brokerage account?

## Common Errors
- Claiming 100% of the foreign tax instead of 50%
- Claiming foreign tax on directly held investments or wages
- Putting foreign taxes in Part A

## Prompt
- My K-1 shows foreign taxes paid.
- I paid tax to another country. Does Alabama give a credit?
