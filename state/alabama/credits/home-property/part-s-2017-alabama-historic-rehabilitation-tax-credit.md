# 2017 Alabama Historic Rehabilitation Tax Credit
## Description
REFUNDABLE credit for rehabilitating historic buildings certified by the Alabama Historical Commission. Code of Alabama §§40-9F-30 to -38 (Act 2017-380, amended by Acts 2021-431 and 2023-522).

## Amounts
- 25% of qualified rehabilitation expenditures
- Cap: $5,000,000 per project
- Program cap: $20 million a year for 2018–2027, plus up to $5 million a year for 2024–2027 to clear the backlog
- 40% of the annual cap reserved for counties of 175,000 people or fewer

## 2026 status
- Unchanged for 2026. Bills to extend the program to 2032 and raise the cap (2026 HB452 and SB313) died in the Senate
- Reservations end December 31, 2027
- Since 2023, owner-occupied residences no longer qualify
- Applications after June 1, 2023 need a building at least 75 years old

## Notes
- Any amount not used against tax is refunded through Section F
- Transferable once at no less than 85% of present value; a transferee can also get a refund
- No carryforward (the excess is refunded)
- Claim no earlier than the year placed in service
- Pass-through entities claim at the entity level
- Recapture can apply; for a transferred credit it falls on the transferee

## Required Information
- Certificate amount and project number
- Placed-in-service date
- Transfer certificate, if purchased

## Questions
- Do you have a 2017-program certificate from the Alabama Historical Commission?
- Was the building placed in service this year?
- Did you buy the credit from someone else?

## Common Errors
- Confusing Part S (2017 program, refundable) with Part N (2013 program, nonrefundable)
- Claiming before the project is placed in service
- Claiming for an owner-occupied residence under post-2023 rules
- Claiming at the owner level when the entity should claim

## Prompt
- I rehabilitated a historic building in Alabama.
- I bought an Alabama historic tax credit.
