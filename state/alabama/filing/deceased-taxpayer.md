# Deceased Taxpayer or Spouse
## Description
Page 1 has a box and date of death for the primary taxpayer and for the spouse. The surviving spouse or personal representative files the final return.

## Rules
- Check the box and enter the date of death for whoever died
- A joint return may be filed for the year of death
- Attach a copy of the death certificate
- A surviving spouse writes "Filing as surviving spouse" in the signature area; a personal representative also signs
- Refund to someone other than the surviving spouse on a joint return or a court-appointed representative: attach Form 1310A
- Unchanged for 2026

## Notes
- In the two years after the year of death, a survivor with a dependent child files Single in Alabama (no qualifying surviving spouse status, and not head of family)
- The age-65 retirement exclusion: Alabama sources don't say how age is measured for a person who died before their birthday; federal practice measures age at death. Confirm before claiming

## Example
A spouse died September 14, 2026: the spouse box is checked with the date, and a joint return is filed with the death certificate.

## Required Information
- Name and date of death
- Who is filing (surviving spouse or representative)
- Whether a refund is due

## Questions
- Did the taxpayer or spouse die during 2026?
- Is a surviving spouse filing jointly, or a personal representative?
- Is a refund expected?

## Common Errors
- Missing Form 1310A when it is required
- Not attaching the death certificate
- Filing as head of family in the years after the death

## Prompt
- How do I file for a spouse who died?
