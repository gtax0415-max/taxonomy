# Filing Status and Personal Exemption
## Description
Alabama has four filing statuses, each with its own personal exemption (Code of Alabama §40-18-19). There is NO qualifying surviving spouse status.

## Amounts (unchanged for 2026)
| Line | Status | Personal exemption |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Single | $1,500 |
| 2 | Married filing jointly | $3,000 |
| 3 | Married filing separately (spouse's SSN required) | $1,500 |
| 4 | Head of family (attach Schedule HOF) | $3,000 |

## Rules
- Status is determined as of December 31
- The status also selects the standard deduction chart column and the tax table column (married filing jointly has its own column; the other three share one)
- A joint return is not allowed if one spouse is an Alabama resident and the other lives in another state
- Common-law marriages entered before January 1, 2017 are recognized
- In the year a spouse dies, the survivor may file jointly
- A "surviving spouse" under the federal definition (IRC §2(a), with a dependent child, for the two years after the death) cannot be head of family and files Single
- Part-year residents get the full exemption

## Example
A federal qualifying surviving spouse with a child at home files Single in Alabama and claims the child as a dependent.

## Required Information
- Marital status on December 31
- Spouse's SSN and residence state
- Federal filing status

## Questions
- What filing status did you use on your federal return?
- Were you married on December 31, 2026?
- Is your spouse living, and in which state does your spouse live?

## Common Errors
- Choosing head of family as a federal surviving spouse
- Filing jointly when one spouse is a nonresident
- Using the wrong tax table column

## Prompt
- Can I file as a qualifying widow in Alabama?
- What filing status should I use in Alabama?
