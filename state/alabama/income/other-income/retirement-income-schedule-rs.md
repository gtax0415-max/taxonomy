# Retirement Income (Schedule RS)
## Description
Schedule RS lists every retirement distribution by payer, separates exempt from taxable distributions, and applies the $6,000 exclusion for taxpayers 65 or older.

## How the schedule works
- Part I: EXEMPT distributions with the reason exempt (defined benefit plans, Alabama public systems, federal civil service, military, Railroad Retirement, TVA, Alabama police and firefighter retirement)
- Part II: taxpayer's taxable distributions; line 9 taxable, line 10 exclusion (if 65+), line 11 net
- Part III: the same for the spouse
- Part IV: line 3 total → Form 40, page 2, Part I, line 4
- Part V: Alabama tax withheld on 1099-Rs (exempt and taxable) → Schedule W-2, line 17. Attach all 1099-Rs

## Amounts
- First $6,000 of taxable retirement income excluded for each person 65 or older (Code of Alabama §40-18-19(a)(13), from 2023)
- Each spouse claims separately, limited to their own taxable retirement income
- Applies to taxable distributions such as IRA, 401(k) and 403(b)
- Not indexed; unchanged for 2026

## Example
A 66-year-old with a $22,000 IRA distribution: Part II, $6,000 excluded, $16,000 to Part I, line 4.

## Required Information
- Each 1099-R and payer
- Plan type (defined benefit or not)
- Ages of taxpayer and spouse

## Questions
- What kind of plan paid each distribution?
- Is the pension a defined benefit plan?
- Were you or your spouse 65 or older in 2026?
- Was any distribution rolled over?

## Common Errors
- Taxing a defined benefit or public pension
- Not claiming the spouse's own $6,000 exclusion
- Excluding more than $6,000 per person
- Reporting a rollover as taxable

## Prompt
- Is my pension taxable in Alabama?
- How does the Alabama $6,000 retirement exclusion work?
