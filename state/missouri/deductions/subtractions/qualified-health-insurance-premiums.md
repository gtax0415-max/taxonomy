# Qualified Health Insurance Premiums
## Description
Health insurance premiums for the taxpayer, spouse, and dependents paid with after-tax dollars and not deducted federally. (Missouri's Form 5695 worksheet shares its number with the unrelated federal energy credit form.)

## Amounts for 2026
- 100% of qualifying premiums, limited to federal taxable income
- Medicare premiums withheld from Social Security count only in proportion to the taxable share of benefits
- Reduced by the share deducted as medical expenses on federal Schedule A
- No change for 2026

## Eligibility
- Premiums for policies primarily providing health coverage, paid with after-tax money

## Notes
- Excluded: pre-tax payroll premiums (cafeteria plans), long-term care premiums, premiums deducted as federal self-employed health insurance
- Marketplace premiums count net of the premium tax credit (DOR does not state this explicitly)
- Example: $4,800 of Marketplace premiums paid after the credit, standard-deduction filer → subtract $4,800

## Required Information
- Premiums paid by source and by person
- Taxable Social Security percentage
- Medical expenses itemized federally

## Questions
- Did you pay health insurance or Medicare premiums yourself, not through payroll?
- Did you itemize medical expenses on your federal return?

## Common Errors
- Including pre-tax or long-term care premiums
- Not prorating Medicare premiums withheld from Social Security
- Double-counting the federal self-employed health insurance deduction
