# Property Tax Credit (Form MO-PTS)
## Description
Refundable credit for seniors and people with disabilities who paid property tax on their Missouri home or rent. 2025 HB 594 & 508 raised every limit for 2026, so 2026 is the first year of the new figures. The credit percentage comes from a chart based on household income.

## Amounts for 2026
- Maximum household income: $38,200 single renter (or part-year owner); $42,200 single who owned and lived in the home all year; $41,000 married filing combined renter; $48,000 married owner all year (2025 form limits were $27,200 / $30,000 / $29,200 / $34,000)
- Spouse exemption subtracted from household income: $2,800 married renter, $5,800 married owner all year; $0 single (was $2,000 / $4,000)
- Maximum rent that counts as property tax: $1,055 (was $750)
- Maximum property tax that counts: $1,550 (was $1,100)
- Chart: the credit percentage drops 1/16% for each $495 of income above the minimum base of $14,300 (was 1/16% per $300)
- The 2026 credit chart itself is not yet published; DOR prints it in the 2026 MO-PTS/MO-PTC instructions (expected December 2026)
- Inflation indexing of these figures starts January 1, 2027, so it does not affect 2026

## Eligibility
- Full-year Missouri resident, and one of: 65 or older; 100% disabled; 100% service-connected disabled veteran; or 60 or older receiving Social Security surviving-spouse benefits
- Household income under the limit above
- Property tax paid, or rent paid to a landlord whose property is taxed

## Notes
- Household income = Missouri AGI plus Social Security, railroad retirement, VA benefits (excluded for a 100% disabled veteran), pensions, tax-exempt interest, SSI, TANF, child support, unemployment, and non-business losses added back; minus the spouse exemption. Food stamps excluded
- Refundable: any credit above remaining tax is refunded
- Claims can be filed up to 3 years after the original due date
- Special assessments are not property tax
- Owners with more than 5 acres or a mobile home need the assessor's certification

## Required Information
- Age, disability, or surviving-spouse status
- All household income items for 2026
- Property tax paid or rent paid by location and months

## Questions
- Are you 65 or older, 100% disabled, or 60+ receiving survivor benefits?
- Did you own your home all year, or rent?
- What was your total household income, including Social Security?

## Common Errors
- Using the 2025 income limits or caps
- Leaving Social Security out of household income
- Claiming the full rent instead of 20%
- Using the $5,800 exemption for a married renter

## Prompt
- Can I get a property tax credit in Missouri?
- I'm 70 and rent an apartment in Missouri.
