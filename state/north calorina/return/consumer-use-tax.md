# Consumer Use Tax
## Description
Use tax owed on items bought for use in North Carolina when the seller did not collect North Carolina sales tax — tangible property, certain digital property, and taxable services. It is added to the tax AFTER credits (Line 19), so credits cannot reduce it. If none is due, fill in the certification circle.
## Not reported here
- Boats and aircraft → Form E-555
- Food subject to the 2% rate → Form E-554
- Motor vehicles → the 3% highway use tax is paid to NCDMV when the vehicle is titled; it is never reported on Line 18
## With complete records — worksheet
1. Out-of-state purchases for 2026, including delivery charges
2. Multiply by the combined rate for your county
3. Subtract sales tax legally paid to another state (not more than Line 2)
4. Result, rounded, goes on Line 18

## Mecklenburg 2026 split
Mecklenburg added a 1% county rate effective July 1, 2026 (combined 8.25%). Apply 7.25% to purchases through June 30 and 8.25% from July 1. The extra 1% does not apply to items taxed at their own specific rates.
## Without records — table
Taxpayers with no records may estimate from the use tax table keyed to D-400, Line 14 (the 2025 table runs from $1 up to $30 for income under $45,200, then Line 14 × 0.000675). Use the 2026 table when published.
## Questions
- Did you buy online or out of state without paying North Carolina sales tax?
- Which county did you live in, and when were the purchases made?
## Common Errors
- Leaving Line 18 blank without filling in the no-use-tax circle
- Using a single Mecklenburg rate for the whole of 2026
- Reporting boats, aircraft, or grocery food here
## Prompt
- What is North Carolina consumer use tax?
- I bought furniture online with no sales tax.
