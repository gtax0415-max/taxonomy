# Filing Requirements, Residency, and Filing Status
## Who must file for 2026
A return is required if FEDERAL GROSS INCOME exceeds:
| Filing status | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Single | $12,750 |
| Married filing jointly | $25,500 |
| Married filing separately — spouse does not itemize | $12,750 |
| Married filing separately — spouse itemizes | $0 |
| Head of household | $19,125 |
| Qualifying surviving spouse | $25,500 |
| Nonresident alien (any status) | $0 |
The thresholds equal the NC standard deduction and are unchanged for 2026. Anyone with North Carolina withholding must file to get it back, even below the threshold. If no federal return was required but North Carolina gross income exceeds the threshold, complete a federal return and attach it.
## Residency
- RESIDENT: domiciled in North Carolina at any time in the year, or living there for other than a temporary or transitory purpose. Presence of MORE THAN 183 DAYS creates a presumption of residence; absence for more than 183 days creates no presumption of nonresidence
- A resident who moves away stays a resident until a new domicile is established AND the North Carolina domicile is abandoned. Only one domicile at a time; intent alone is not enough
- PART-YEAR RESIDENT: moved in or out during the year. Must file if income while a resident, or North Carolina-source income while a nonresident, plus total gross income exceeds the threshold
- NONRESIDENT: must file if they had North Carolina-source income (real or tangible property in North Carolina, a business or occupation carried on in North Carolina, or North Carolina gambling) and total gross income from all sources exceeds the threshold
- NONRESIDENT ALIENS can be residents, part-year residents, or nonresidents for North Carolina purposes
The Residency Status questions on page 1 must be answered for you and a joint-filing spouse; answering No requires Schedule PN.
## Military
- North Carolina domiciliaries in the armed forces file and are taxed on military pay wherever stationed
- Other-state domiciliaries stationed in North Carolina on orders are not taxed on military pay, but are taxed on North Carolina property, non-military business or employment, and gambling income
- Military spouse: income for services in North Carolina is exempt if the servicemember is in North Carolina solely on orders, the spouse is there solely to be with the servicemember, and the spouse is domiciled in (or, under the Veterans Benefits and Transition Act of 2018, elects) the servicemember's state of legal residence. Otherwise use Schedule PN
## Filing status (Lines 1-5)
- Use the SAME status as the federal return, or the status you would have used if no federal return was filed
- Joint federal return and BOTH spouses residents, or both have North Carolina taxable income → joint North Carolina return required
- Joint federal return and the spouse is a nonresident with NO North Carolina taxable income → may file jointly or separately. If separately, attach a separate federal computation. After the due date, a joint election cannot be changed to separate
- Married filing separately: enter the spouse's name and SSN on Line 3. Itemized deductions go to the spouse who was obligated and actually paid
- Qualifying widow(er): enter the year the spouse died
## Which form
All individuals file Form D-400 for the same tax year used federally; a fiscal year return uses the D-400 for the year the fiscal year begins.
## Questions
- What was your federal gross income and filing status?
- Where were you domiciled, and how many days were you in North Carolina?
- Are you or your spouse in the military?
## Common Errors
- Not filing because income was under the threshold despite North Carolina withholding
- Filing separately when both spouses were residents and filed jointly federally
- Assuming a move ends residency before a new domicile is established
## Prompt
- Do I need to file a North Carolina return?
- I moved out of North Carolina this year, am I still a resident?
- My spouse is a nonresident, can I file separately?
