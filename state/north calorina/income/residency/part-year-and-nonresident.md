# Part-Year Residents and Nonresidents (Schedule PN and Schedule PN-1)
## Description
Part-year residents and nonresidents compute North Carolina tax on ALL income as if they were residents, then multiply by a TAXABLE PERCENTAGE — the share of their income that is North Carolina income. Every Schedule S addition and deduction must be split between all-source (Column A) and North Carolina (Column B).
## How the percentage works
- Column A: income from all sources (for joint filers, BOTH spouses, even if only one was a resident)
- Column B: income from North Carolina sources plus all income received WHILE a resident
- Line 24 = Line 22 (Column B total) ÷ Line 23 (Column A total), four decimal places, entered on D-400, Line 13
- The percentage may exceed 100% but not go below 0%
- D-400, Line 14 = Line 12b × Line 13; tax at 3.99%

## Column B allocation rules
- Part-year residents include ALL income received while a resident, including income from other states
- Partnership income from a multistate partnership is prorated by days of residence and nonresidence
- Example from the instructions: $1,200 of other-state bond interest received evenly, resident from July 1 → Column A $1,200, Column B $600
## Credits
Nonresidents cannot claim the credit for tax paid to another state; part-year residents claim it only for the resident period (see credits/other-state-tax/).
## Notes
- Attach Schedule PN whenever Line 13 is used; attach BOTH pages of PN-1 whenever PN Line 17e or 19h has an entry
- The NOL computation for part-year and nonresidents draws on Schedule PN amounts (see net-operating-loss/)
- Line numbers follow the 2025 schedules; NCDOR publishes 2026 forms in January 2027
## Questions
- When did your North Carolina residency begin or end?
- What income did you receive while a resident, and what came from North Carolina sources while a nonresident?
- If married filing jointly, was your spouse a resident?
## Common Errors
- Leaving the nonresident spouse's income out of Column A on a joint return
- Excluding out-of-state income received while a North Carolina resident from Column B
- Rounding the percentage to fewer than four decimals
- Attaching only one page of PN-1
