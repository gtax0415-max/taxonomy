# Interest From Obligations of States Other Than North Carolina
## Description
Interest on municipal bonds issued by OTHER states and their subdivisions is tax-exempt federally but TAXABLE in North Carolina. It must be added back. Interest on North Carolina's own obligations stays exempt.
## Amount
- Direct bond interest from non-North Carolina issuers — added in full
- Exempt-interest dividends from mutual funds — added EXCEPT the portion attributable to North Carolina obligations
## The mutual fund trap
A national municipal bond fund reports all its dividends as tax-exempt on Form 1099-DIV Box 12. Only a small percentage usually comes from North Carolina issuers. Without the fund's state-by-state supplemental statement, the whole amount must be added back.
## Notes
- Part-year residents add back only the interest received while a resident (Schedule PN, Line 17a)
- Puerto Rico, Guam, and other U.S. possession bonds are exempt under federal law and are NOT added back
## Required Information
- Tax-exempt interest by issuer state
- Fund supplemental statements showing the North Carolina percentage
## Questions
- Do you own municipal bonds or municipal bond funds?
- Were the bonds issued by North Carolina or by another state?
- Did your fund provide a state-by-state breakdown?
## Common Errors
- Leaving out out-of-state municipal interest because it was exempt federally
- Adding back the North Carolina portion of a national fund
- Adding back U.S. possession bond interest
## Prompt
- I have a municipal bond fund, is it taxable in North Carolina?
- Do I pay North Carolina tax on New York municipal bonds?
