# Debt Discharge Additions
## Description
North Carolina has not followed two federal cancellation-of-debt exclusions. Debt excluded federally under them is added back.
## Qualified principal residence indebtedness (Line 10)
- Federal IRC 108(a)(1)(E) exclusion for forgiven mortgage debt on a main home is added back in full
- The federal exclusion EXPIRED: it applies only to discharges before January 1, 2026, or discharges under a written arrangement entered into before that date (IRS Pub. 4681). A 2026 discharge without a pre-2026 written arrangement is already taxable federally, so there is nothing to add back
- Line 10 on a 2026 return is therefore limited to 2026 discharges completed under a pre-2026 written arrangement
- Insolvency is a separate matter; see NCDOR bulletins
## Student loan discharge (Line 13)
- The broad ARPA exclusion (any student loan discharge) ended with 2025 discharges. For 2026 discharges, federal law excludes only discharges on account of DEATH or TOTAL AND PERMANENT DISABILITY (made permanent by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, with a work-eligible SSN required), plus the long-standing service-based programs
- Most other 2026 discharges (for example, income-driven repayment forgiveness) are now taxable federally and therefore already in AGI — no add-back needed
- For 2026 death/TPD discharges excluded federally, whether North Carolina requires an add-back depends on the exceptions in G.S. 105-153.5(c)(22); confirm on the 2026 Schedule S instructions
## Required Information
- Form 1099-C and Form 982
- Nature of the discharge
## Questions
- Was any mortgage or student loan debt forgiven?
- Did you exclude it from federal income?
## Common Errors
- Assuming a federal exclusion carries to North Carolina
## Prompt
- My student loans were forgiven, is it taxable in North Carolina?
