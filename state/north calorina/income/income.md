# North Carolina Income
## Description
North Carolina has NO separate definition of income. The return starts with FEDERAL ADJUSTED GROSS INCOME (D-400, Line 6) and modifies it with additions (Schedule S, Part A) and subtractions (Schedule S, Part B). Everything in this folder is a modification to federal AGI.
THE HEADLINE FOR 2026: North Carolina now conforms to the Internal Revenue Code as of JULY 5, 2025 (Session Law 2026-31, signed July 2026), replacing the January 1, 2023 date printed in the 2025 instructions. Most One Big Beautiful Bill Act changes that operate ABOVE the AGI line now flow into North Carolina automatically, unless North Carolina specifically decoupled.
## 2026 parameters
| Parameter | 2026 |
|---|---|
| Tax rate | 3.99% flat (down from 4.25% in 2025) |
| IRC conformity date | July 5, 2025 |
| Local income tax | None anywhere in North Carolina |
| Rate for 2027 and later | Not a 2026 parameter. The July 2026 budget (S.L. 2026-41) set 3.49% for 2027, 3.24% for 2030, and 2.99% after 2032, with a possible 2.49% if revenue thresholds are met |
## How income flows on Form D-400
| Line | Item |
|---|---|
| 6 | Federal AGI |
| 7 | Additions — Schedule S, Line 16 |
| 8 | Line 6 + Line 7 |
| 9 | Deductions from federal AGI — Schedule S, Line 41 |
| 10b | Child deduction (see deductions/child-deduction.md) |
| 11 | NC standard or itemized deduction (see deductions/) |
| 12a-12b | Income after subtractions and deductions |
| 13 | Taxable percentage — part-year and nonresidents only (Schedule PN) |
| 14 | North Carolina taxable income |
| 15 | Tax = Line 14 × 3.99% |
Line numbers follow the 2025 D-400; NCDOR publishes the 2026 forms in January 2027.

## What conformity to July 5, 2025 means
- ABOVE-THE-LINE federal changes (exclusions, AGI adjustments) now follow federal law unless decoupled
- BELOW-THE-LINE federal deductions do NOT carry over, because North Carolina starts from AGI and uses its own deductions. The federal deductions for tips, overtime, car loan interest, and seniors are NOT available — see deductions/federal-deductions-not-allowed.md
- New decoupling: domestic research and experimental expenditures under IRC 174A (80% add-back) — see additions/domestic-research-expenditures.md
- Continuing decoupling: bonus depreciation and Section 179 — NCDOR confirmed the bonus depreciation adjustment is unchanged
## What North Carolina taxes that surprises people
- IRA, 401(k), and private pension distributions — fully taxable unless the Bailey or uniformed services rules apply
- Unemployment compensation — taxable
- Interest on OTHER states' municipal bonds — must be added back
- Gambling winnings — taxable, with losses allowed only as an itemized deduction
## What North Carolina does not tax
- Social Security and railroad retirement benefits
- Interest on U.S. obligations
- Bailey-protected government pensions and qualifying military retirement
## Questions
- What is your federal AGI?
- Did you receive Social Security, a government pension, or military retirement?
- Did you own municipal bonds from states other than North Carolina?
- Did you or a pass-through business claim bonus depreciation, Section 179, or R&E expensing?
- Were you a full-year North Carolina resident?
