# North Carolina NOL Carryover Deduction (Form NC-NOL, Parts 2A-2C)
## Description
The deduction for NOLs carried forward from earlier years. Two pools feed it: old FEDERAL NOL carryovers that were still unabsorbed at January 1, 2022 (when North Carolina switched to its own NOL), and NC NOLs incurred from 2022 on.
## Part 2A — federal NOL carryovers (loss years 2011-2021 for 2026)
A federal NOL carryover counts as an NC NOL only if ALL of these are true:
- It was not absorbed in tax years beginning before January 1, 2022
- It would have been allowed under IRC 172 as enacted on April 1, 2021
- G.S. 105-153.5(c2)(8), (9), (10), (13), and (14) do not apply to it
- It was incurred in one of the preceding 15 taxable years
| Column | Entry |
|---|---|
| A | Carryover for each loss year still available (from prior-year Part 2A, Column C) |
| B | Amount used in 2026 |
| C | A − B, available for 2027 |
Line 13 totals Column B.
## Part 2B — NC NOL carryovers (loss years 2022-2025 for 2026)
| Column | Entry |
|---|---|
| A | Unused NC NOL by year (prior-year Part 1, Line 24 and Part 2B, Column C) |
| B | Amount used in 2026 |
| C | A − B, available for 2027 |
Line 17 totals Column B.
## Part 2C — total deduction
Line 18 (Part 2A total) + Line 19 (Part 2B total) = Line 20, entered on Schedule S, Line 39.
## Ordering and limits
1. Use ALL available federal NOL carryovers (Part 2A) before any NC NOL carryover (Part 2B)
2. Within each pool, use the oldest year first
3. The total may not exceed North Carolina taxable income computed without the NC NOL deduction — stop filling Column B when that limit is reached
4. No carryback
## Part-year residents and nonresidents
- Loss year as a NONRESIDENT: only income and deductions from a business carried on in North Carolina count toward the loss or the federal carryover
- Loss year as a PART-YEAR resident: North Carolina business items while a nonresident, plus all-source business items while a resident
- 2026 limit: Column B cannot exceed Schedule PN, Line 21, Column B, computed without the NOL deduction on Schedule PN-1, Line 28, Column B
## Example
2026 NC taxable income before the NOL: $40,000. Available: $15,000 federal NOL from 2019, $50,000 NC NOL from 2023. Deduct the $15,000 federal carryover first, then $25,000 of the 2023 NC NOL. Line 20 = $40,000; $25,000 of the 2023 NC NOL carries to 2027.
## Required Information
- Last year's Form NC-NOL
- North Carolina taxable income before the NOL
## Questions
- Do you have your 2025 Form NC-NOL?
- What federal NOLs from 2011-2021 remained unused at the start of 2022?
## Common Errors
- Using NC NOL carryovers before federal carryovers
- Deducting more than North Carolina taxable income
- Applying the federal 80% limitation instead of the North Carolina 100%-of-taxable-income cap
- Losing track of Column C balances between years
## Prompt
- How much of my North Carolina NOL can I use this year?
- I have an old federal NOL from 2018, can I use it in North Carolina?
