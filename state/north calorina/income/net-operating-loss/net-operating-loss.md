# Net Operating Losses
## Description
North Carolina does NOT use the federal NOL. The federal NOL deduction is added back in full, and a separate North Carolina NOL is computed and deducted on Form NC-NOL. Form NC-NOL must be filed for any year you INCUR an NC NOL or DEDUCT one from a prior year, and only after the rest of the North Carolina return is complete.

## The core rules
| Rule | North Carolina |
|---|---|
| Carryback | NOT allowed |
| Carryforward | 15 taxable years |
| Ordering | Oldest first; federal NOL carryovers before NC NOL carryovers |
| Annual limit | Cannot exceed North Carolina taxable income computed without the NC NOL |
| Federal 80% limit | Not part of the North Carolina computation — the cap is 100% of NC taxable income |
## 2026 parameters
| Item | 2026 |
|---|---|
| Federal NOL carryover years usable (Part 2A) | 2011 through 2021 (2010 falls outside the 15-year window) |
| NC NOL carryover years usable (Part 2B) | 2022 through 2025 |
| Capital loss allowed in the Part 1 computation | $3,000 ($1,500 married filing separately) |
| Code used to measure income and deductions | IRC as of July 5, 2025, with North Carolina adjustments |
| Excess business loss | Fully allowed as an NC NOL — codified by S.L. 2026-31 for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2026 |
## Legacy 20% recoveries (not NOLs, but related)
Schedule S, Lines 34-37 (PN-1 Lines 23-26) recovered the CARES Act decoupling add-backs (NOL carrybacks added back 2013-2019; excess NOL carryforwards and business interest limitation added back 2019-2020; excess business losses added back 2018-2020) at 20% a year for the five taxable years BEGINNING WITH 2021. That window ran 2021-2025, so NO recovery remains for 2026. Expect these lines to disappear from the 2026 Schedule S.
## Questions
- Did you deduct a federal NOL in 2026? (It must be added back.)
- Do your business deductions exceed your income for 2026?
- Do you have unused NOLs on your 2025 Form NC-NOL?
- Were you a part-year resident or nonresident in the loss year or in 2026?
## Common Errors
- Leaving the federal NOL in federal AGI
- Using the federal NOL amount as the North Carolina NOL
- Trying to carry an NC NOL back
- Using an NC NOL carryover before exhausting federal NOL carryovers
- Not keeping the prior year's Form NC-NOL, which supplies the Column A carryover amounts
