# Credit for Income Tax Paid to Another State or Country
## Description
Nonrefundable credit for NORTH CAROLINA RESIDENTS whose income was taxed both by North Carolina and by another state or country because it was earned in or derived from sources there. It is the most commonly claimed North Carolina individual credit.
## Amount
The credit is the LESSER of two figures:
1. The North Carolina tax on the double-taxed income — North Carolina tax (D-400, Line 15) × (income taxed by the other state ÷ total resident-period income, modified by NC adjustments)
2. The NET TAX actually paid to the other state on that income
There is no carryforward. Any excess of the other state's tax over the North Carolina limit is simply lost.
## Line-by-line on Form D-400TC, Part 1
| Line | Entry |
|---|---|
| 1 | Total income from all sources while a NC resident, modified by NC adjustments (Schedule S, Parts A and B). May be negative |
| 2 | Portion of Line 1 taxed by the other state or country, including S corporation income taxed at either the individual or corporate level |
| 3 | Line 2 ÷ Line 1, as a decimal rounded to four places |
| 4 | North Carolina income tax from D-400, Line 15 |
| 5 | Line 4 × Line 3 |
| 6 | Net tax paid to the other state or country on the Line 2 income |
| 7a | Lesser of Line 5 or Line 6 — the credit |
| 7b | Number of states or countries for which a credit is claimed |
## Net tax paid means
Withholding, estimated payments, and extension payments, PLUS any additional tax paid, MINUS any refund received or expected. The other state's final LIABILITY is what matters, not what was withheld. A W-2 showing Virginia withholding is not proof on its own — the credit cannot be claimed on the basis of a withholding statement alone.
## More than one state
If you paid tax to two or more states or countries, DO NOT complete Lines 1 through 6. Instead complete a separate Out-of-State Tax Credit Worksheet (D-401 instructions, page 23) for each state, add the Line 7 results, and enter the total on Line 7a. Enter the count of states on Line 7b.
## Eligibility
- Resident of North Carolina for the period the income was earned
- Part-year residents: only the RESIDENT PERIOD counts; income earned while a nonresident is handled by the Schedule PN allocation, not this credit
- Nonresidents: NOT ELIGIBLE
- The income must be taxed by BOTH North Carolina and the other jurisdiction
## Notes
- Local and city income taxes do not count, even though they are real taxes on the same income
- Taxes paid to a foreign COUNTRY count; taxes paid to the federal government or to a foreign city or province-level subdivision do not
- For an S corporation taxed at the entity level by another state, your pro rata share of the corporate tax counts — attach the supporting schedule
- The credit cannot exceed the North Carolina tax on D-400, Line 15, and the cap shrinks as the North Carolina rate falls (3.99% for 2026, down from 4.25% in 2025)
## Required Information
- The other state's completed return and proof of payment
- Resident-period income from all sources, as modified by NC adjustments
- Amount of that income taxed by the other state
- Net tax paid to each other state or country
- North Carolina tax from D-400, Line 15
## Questions
- Were you a North Carolina resident when the income was earned?
- Which state or country taxed the income, and did you file a return there?
- What was your final tax liability there, after any refund?
- Did you pay tax to more than one state or country?
- Was any of the tax a local or city tax rather than a state tax?
## Common Errors
- Claiming the amount WITHHELD by the other state instead of the net tax after its refund
- Claiming as a nonresident, who is not eligible
- Including city, county, or federal taxes
- Completing Lines 1-6 for multiple states instead of using a worksheet per state
- Forgetting to attach the other state's return and proof of payment, which gets the credit disallowed
- Mixing a software-generated D-400 with a web-fill D-400TC, which the Department cannot process
## Prompt
- I live in Charlotte and work in South Carolina.
- I paid Virginia tax on my wages, do I get credit in North Carolina?
- I moved to North Carolina mid-year and paid tax to my old state.
