# Energy and Home Credits
## Description
NORTH CAROLINA HAS NO CURRENT RESIDENTIAL ENERGY CREDITS. The state's renewable energy property credit (Article 3B) sunset for property placed in service after 2015, and its five-year carryforward has long since run out for residential systems. Combined with the termination of the federal residential energy credits after December 31, 2025, a North Carolina homeowner installing solar or geothermal in 2026 GETS NO INCOME TAX CREDIT AT EITHER LEVEL.
This is the opposite of New York, where the state solar and geothermal credits survive the federal repeal.
## Credits in this folder
None current. Any remaining business energy credit carryforwards flow through Form NC-478 — see business-incentive/business-incentive-and-energy-tax-credits.md.
## Federal versus North Carolina for 2026 (no change from the July 2026 legislation)
| Improvement | Federal | North Carolina |
|---|---|---|
| Solar electric or solar thermal | Terminated after 2025 | No credit (sunset after 2015) |
| Geothermal heat pump | Terminated after 2025 | No credit |
| Insulation, windows, doors, heat pumps | Terminated after 2025 | No credit |
| Heating fuel purchases | Never had one | No credit |
## The one home-related credit that remains
A homeowner rehabilitating a HISTORIC house can still claim the Article 3L nonincome-producing historic structure credit — 15% of expenses, up to $22,500. Energy upgrades that are part of a certified rehabilitation and added to basis may count. See historic-rehabilitation/article-3l-nonincome-producing-historic-structure-credit.md.
## Land conservation
Donating land or a conservation easement can earn the NC Conservation Tax Credit (25% of fair market value) — see property-conservation/.
## Non-tax benefits to point to instead
- Utility rebate and incentive programs
- Local property tax treatment of solar equipment
These are not income tax credits and are claimed separately.
