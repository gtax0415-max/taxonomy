# NC Conservation Tax Credit
## Description
Nonrefundable credit for donating an interest in North Carolina real property, in perpetuity, for a qualifying conservation use (G.S. 105-153.11). Repealed in 2013, reenacted in 2024, and effective for donations made on or after January 1, 2025. Unlike every other North Carolina credit, it is ALLOCATED by NCDOR from a capped statewide pool — you apply, and NCDOR tells you how much you get.
## Amount
- 25% of the fair market value of the qualified donation
- Individual cap: $250,000 per taxable year for all qualified donations, whether made directly or through a pass-through entity
- Married couple: $500,000 on a joint return. If both spouses must file North Carolina returns, the credit may be claimed only on a joint return; if only one must file, that spouse may claim on a separate return
- Statewide ceiling: $5,000,000 of credits per year (individuals, pass-throughs, and corporations combined), of which $3,250,000 is PRIORITIZED for forestland or farmland preservation. If requests exceed the ceiling, NCDOR reduces allocations
- The allocated credit cannot exceed the tax against which it is claimed, reduced by all other credits (payments of tax excluded)

|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 | January 1 - April 15, 2026 (window closed) | By December 31, 2026 | 2026 return (filed 2027) |
| 2026 | January 1 - April 15, 2027 | By December 31, 2027 | 2027 return |
The credit may NOT be taken in the year of the donation.
## What qualifies
- A qualified real property interest in North Carolina (IRC 170(h)(2)): the donor's entire interest other than a qualified mineral interest, a remainder interest, or a perpetual use restriction (conservation easement)
- Donated in perpetuity to the State, a local government, or a qualified conservation body, and accepted in perpetuity for that use
- Qualifying uses: forestland or farmland preservation; fish or wildlife conservation; a buffer protecting military training or operations; floodplain protection in a county with a Type II or III gubernatorial disaster declaration in the prior five years; historic landscape conservation; public trails or access to them
- NOT eligible: land dedicated under a local regulation or ordinance, or dedications made to increase permitted building density
## Application requirements
- Form NC-74, one per qualified donation, filed online or by mail between January 1 and April 15 of the year after the donation. Late applications cannot be accepted
- NCDNCR certification of the qualifying use (for 2025 donations, the certification request was due February 15, 2026)
- A USPAP appraisal report or summary report (or, for fee simple donations, county appraised value adjusted by the sales assessment ratio)
- Missing supporting documents cause the application to be rejected
## Pass-through entities
The entity applies; the allocated credit passes to owners by distributive share, only to owners as of December 31 of the claim year. Each owner's share counts toward the owner's $250,000 / $500,000 cap.
## No double benefit
A donation that is the basis for an allocated credit cannot also be deducted as a North Carolina charitable contribution on Schedule A.
## Sunset — verify
NCDOR Directive TA-25-1 (revised February 2026) states the credit expires for donations made on or after January 1, 2027. The General Assembly's statute index now shows G.S. 105-153.11 effective until January 1, 2031, suggesting a later extension. Confirm the current sunset before advising on donations made in 2027 or later.
## Notes
- The 2025 D-400TC had no line for this credit; NCDOR says it will explain how to claim on the applicable form. Check the 2026 D-400TC when published in January 2027
- Nonrefundable at the 3.99% rate: a $250,000 credit needs about $6.27 million of North Carolina taxable income to use in one year; check the carryforward rules in G.S. 105-153.11 before assuming full use
## Required Information
- Donation date, property location, qualifying use, appraised value
- NC-74 filing date and NCDOR allocation notice
- Filing status and North Carolina tax liability
- For pass-throughs, the owner's distributive share
## Questions
- When was the donation made and to whom?
- Did NCDNCR certify the qualifying use?
- Was Form NC-74 filed by April 15 of the following year, with the appraisal and certification?
- How much did NCDOR allocate?
- Are you also deducting the donation as a charitable contribution for North Carolina?
## Common Errors
- Claiming the credit in the donation year
- Missing the April 15 application deadline, which cannot be cured
- Claiming more than the NCDOR allocation
- Also deducting the donation on North Carolina Schedule A
- Filing separately when both spouses must file North Carolina returns
