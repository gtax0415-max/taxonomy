# North Carolina Individual Income Tax Credits
## Description
Index of credits available on the North Carolina individual return. THE HEADLINE: NORTH CAROLINA HAS NO REFUNDABLE CREDITS ON THE D-400. Every credit on Form D-400TC can only reduce tax to zero, and the total on Form D-400TC, Line 20 cannot exceed the tax on Form D-400, Line 15.
This is the single biggest difference from New York and from the federal return. There is no North Carolina child tax credit, no earned income credit, no dependent care credit, and no residential energy credit for current-year activity. Family relief is delivered through the CHILD DEDUCTION on Form D-400, Line 10b, which is a deduction, not a credit.
## How credits reach the return
Every credit flows through Form D-400TC to a single line:
- Form D-400TC, Part 1 (out-of-state tax credit) → Line 7a
- Form D-400TC, Part 2 (historic rehabilitation) → Lines 8 through 13
- Form D-400TC, Part 3 (carryovers and business credits) → Lines 14 and 19
- Form D-400TC, Line 20 → Form D-400, Line 16
Form D-400TC must be attached whenever a credit is claimed on Line 16, and it must be filed for any year in which you are eligible for a credit or an installment of a credit, EVEN IF YOUR TAX IS TOO SMALL TO USE IT.

## The ordering inside Form D-400TC
1. Lines 7a through 15 are added on Line 16
2. Line 18 limits that sum to the North Carolina tax on Line 17 (from D-400, Line 15)
3. Line 19 adds business incentive and energy credits from Form NC-478, which are separately limited to 50% of tax
4. Line 20 is the total and cannot exceed D-400, Line 15
## Tax rate that the credits offset
- 2026: 3.99% flat rate (down from 4.25% in 2025)
- 2027 and later: 3.49% for 2027, 3.24% for 2030, 2.99% after 2032 under the July 2026 budget (S.L. 2026-41). Credits carried forward into those years will be absorbed by less tax
A lower rate shrinks the tax available to absorb nonrefundable credits and shrinks the out-of-state credit cap computed on D-400TC Line 5.
## Line numbers
Line references follow the 2025 Form D-400TC. NCDOR publishes the 2026 forms in January 2027; the credit structure is not expected to change, but confirm the line numbers then.
## Federal decoupling to remember
Session Law 2026-31 (July 2026) moved North Carolina's Internal Revenue Code conformity date from January 1, 2023 to July 5, 2025, so most One Big Beautiful Bill Act changes to AGI now apply unless North Carolina decoupled. No new individual credits were created, and none of the credits here became refundable. For credits this matters mainly where a North Carolina credit piggybacks on a federal one — the Article 3L income-producing historic credit requires that the federal Section 47 credit be allowed.
