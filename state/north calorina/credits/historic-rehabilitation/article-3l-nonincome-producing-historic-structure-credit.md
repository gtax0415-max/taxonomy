# Article 3L Nonincome-Producing Historic Structure Credit
## Description
Nonrefundable credit for HOMEOWNERS who rehabilitate a historic house they live in. Unlike the income-producing credit, it requires that you NOT be allowed the federal Section 47 credit — which is automatic for a personal residence. This is the only North Carolina credit a typical homeowner can generate from spending on their own home.
## Amount
- 15% of rehabilitation expenses
- Expenses must be AT LEAST $10,000
- MAXIMUM CREDIT $22,500 PER DISCRETE PROPERTY PARCEL, reached at $150,000 of expenses
So $60,000 of certified work earns $9,000; $150,000 or more earns the full $22,500; $9,500 earns nothing.
## The 24-month window
Qualifying expenses must be incurred within ANY 24-MONTH PERIOD per parcel. Spreading work over three years can leave some of it outside the window. Plan the project schedule around the window, not the calendar year.
## Timing and carryforward
- Claimed in the year the structure is placed in service
- Unused credit carries forward for up to NINE succeeding years
- The carryforward stays on Form NC-Rehab, not D-400TC, Line 14
## Eligibility
- The building must be a STATE-CERTIFIED HISTORIC STRUCTURE — listed on the National Register individually, contributing to a National Register historic district, or contributing to a certified local historic district (Raleigh, Goldsboro, or Madison)
- The rehabilitation must be certified by the State Historic Preservation Office, generally before work begins
- The taxpayer must NOT be allowed the federal Section 47 credit
- Expenses must be added to the property's basis — routine maintenance does not count
## The nonrefundability problem
$22,500 is a large credit against a 3.99% tax. A household with $80,000 of North Carolina taxable income owes about $3,192 for 2026 — roughly seven years to absorb the full credit, inside the nine-year carryforward but only just, and longer if the rate falls further after 2026. Retirees with little taxable income can lose part of it. Estimate absorption before counting the full amount as real money.
## Notes
- The credit may transfer with the property if the property changes hands before it is placed in service
- Get SHPO approval before starting work
- Attach Form NC-Rehab to the FRONT of Form D-400
## Required Information
- Rehabilitation expenses per parcel and the dates incurred
- SHPO certification of the structure and the work
- Placed-in-service date
- Any prior credit claimed on the same parcel
- North Carolina tax liability, to project absorption
## Questions
- Is the house on the National Register or in a historic district?
- Did the State Historic Preservation Office approve the work before it started?
- Did the work cost at least $10,000, within a 24-month period?
- Is this your own home rather than rental property?
- How much North Carolina tax do you owe each year?
## Common Errors
- Starting work without SHPO certification
- Counting expenses outside the 24-month window
- Including maintenance, furnishings, or landscaping that are not added to basis
- Claiming for a rental property, which belongs on the income-producing line if it qualifies federally
- Expecting a refund from a nonrefundable credit
- Putting the carryforward on D-400TC, Line 14 instead of Form NC-Rehab
## Prompt
- I'm restoring an old house in a historic district in Raleigh.
- Is there a North Carolina tax credit for fixing up my historic home?
