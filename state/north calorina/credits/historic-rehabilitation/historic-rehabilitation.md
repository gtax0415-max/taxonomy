# Historic Rehabilitation Credits
## Description
Credits for rehabilitating certified historic structures and historic mills. Three generations of law coexist on Form D-400TC, Part 2. Article 3L is the general credit for new projects; Article 3D survives only for installments and carryovers. Article 3H is legacy for ordinary mills BUT gained a NEW 40% corporate campus credit for expenditures incurred on or after January 1, 2026.

## Which article applies
| Situation | Article | D-400TC line |
|---|---|---|
| New project, commercial or rental, qualifies for federal Section 47 | 3L | 12 |
| New project, owner-occupied home, no federal credit | 3L | 13 |
| Old 3D project still receiving installments | 3D | 8 or 9 |
| Certified historic mill project under the old mill program | 3H | 10 or 11 |
| Former corporate headquarters campus, 20+ acres, $10M+ QREs incurred from 2026 | 3H corporate campus (40%) | 10 |
## Income-producing versus nonincome-producing
The split follows the federal credit. A building that qualifies for the federal Section 47 rehabilitation credit is INCOME-PRODUCING. A personal residence cannot get the federal credit and is NONINCOME-PRODUCING. Getting this wrong routes the credit to the wrong line.
## Sunset for 2026 planning
NCDOR's economic incentives reporting lists a January 1, 2030 sunset for Article 3L. A project started now still qualifies, but the window is closing — check G.S. 105-129.110 for the current date before relying on it.

## Questions
- Is the building listed on the National Register or a contributing building in a historic district?
- Is it income-producing or your own residence?
- When were the expenses incurred, and when was the building placed in service?
- Did the State Historic Preservation Office certify the work?
- Are you still receiving installments from an older 3D or 3H project?
