# North Carolina Standard Deduction
## Description
A flat amount by filing status. North Carolina sets its own figure; it does not follow the federal standard deduction and is NOT indexed for inflation. The 2026 amounts are unchanged from 2025.
## Amounts for 2026
| Filing status | Standard deduction |
|---|---|
| Single | $12,750 |
| Head of household | $19,125 |
| Married filing jointly | $25,500 |
| Qualifying surviving spouse | $25,500 |
| Married filing separately — spouse does NOT itemize | $12,750 |
| Married filing separately — spouse itemizes | $0 |
| Not eligible for the federal standard deduction | $0 |
No additional amount for age 65 or older or for blindness.
## Filing threshold
The North Carolina filing requirement uses the same figures: a return is required if federal gross income exceeds the amount for your filing status (nonresident aliens: $0). A return is also needed to recover any North Carolina withholding.
## Notes
- The federal standard deduction increase under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act does NOT carry over — NCDOR confirmed this after the 2026 conformity update
- Choose standard OR itemized and fill in exactly one circle on Line 11
- Dependents: if you are not eligible for a federal standard deduction, the NC standard deduction is zero
## Questions
- What is your filing status?
- If married filing separately, is your spouse itemizing?
## Common Errors
- Entering the federal standard deduction
- Adding an extra amount for age 65 or blindness
- Taking $12,750 on a separate return when the spouse itemizes

- What is the North Carolina standard deduction for 2026?
