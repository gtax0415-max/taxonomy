# Gambling Losses (NEW)
## Description
NEW itemized deduction created by Session Law 2026-41 (G.S. 105-153.5(a)(2)), effective for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2025. Before this, North Carolina taxed all gambling winnings with NO offset for losses. Especially relevant since North Carolina legalized online sports betting in 2024.
## Amount
- The amount allowed as a deduction for wagering losses under IRC 165(d)
- Only to the extent NOT deducted in arriving at AGI
## 2026 note
Because conformity is now July 5, 2025, the post-OBBBA IRC 165(d) applies: beginning in 2026, only 90% of wagering losses are deductible, still limited to winnings. The North Carolina deduction follows that federal figure.
## Requirements
- Must ITEMIZE North Carolina deductions — the loss deduction is lost on the standard deduction
- Report ALL winnings in income; do not net winnings against losses
- Keep records of winnings and losses
## Example
$10,000 of 2026 winnings and $12,000 of losses: federal allowable loss = lesser of 90% × $12,000 ($10,800) or winnings ($10,000) = $10,000. The North Carolina itemized deduction is $10,000 — worth it only if total itemized deductions exceed the standard deduction.
## 2025 returns
2025 returns filed before this law may be amended to claim it; write "Gambling Loss" on Schedule A, Line 9.
## Questions
- Did you have gambling winnings, including sports betting?
- Do you have a record of losses?
- Will you itemize for North Carolina?
## Common Errors
- Netting losses against winnings in income
- Claiming losses while taking the standard deduction
- Ignoring the 90% federal limit starting in 2026
## Prompt
- I lost money sports betting, can I deduct it in North Carolina?
- Does North Carolina tax gambling winnings?
