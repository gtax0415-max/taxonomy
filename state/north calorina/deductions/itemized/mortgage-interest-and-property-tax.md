# Home Mortgage Interest and Real Estate Property Taxes
## Description
Qualified home mortgage interest (IRC 163(h)) and real estate property taxes (IRC 164), COMBINED and capped at $20,000.
## Amount for 2026
- Line 1: mortgage interest allowable under IRC 163(h) on a qualified residence
- Line 2: real estate property taxes allowable under IRC 164
- Line 4: $20,000 combined limit — the same for single, joint, and head of household
- Line 5: the smaller of Lines 3 and 4
## Change after the 2026 conformity update
The 2025 instructions capped real estate taxes at $10,000 ($5,000 MFS), reflecting the pre-OBBBA federal SALT cap. With conformity moved to July 5, 2025, NCDOR says the real estate tax deduction is the amount allowable under post-OBBBA IRC 164 — the federal SALT cap is $40,400 for 2026 ($20,200 MFS), reduced for high incomes. The $20,000 combined cap still applies and usually controls. Anyone who filed 2025 with the $10,000 property tax limit should review whether to amend.
## Married filing separately
The $20,000 cap applies to both spouses combined. A joint obligation is deductible by the spouse who actually paid; if both paid and the total exceeds $20,000, prorate by the share each paid (or, from a joint account, by each spouse's income).
## Required Information
- Form 1098, property tax statements, payer records for MFS
## Questions
- How much mortgage interest and property tax did you pay?
- Are you married filing separately, and who paid?
## Common Errors
- Claiming the full combined amount above $20,000
- Still limiting property tax to $10,000 after the conformity update
- Including state income tax

- How much mortgage interest can I deduct in North Carolina?
