# Federal Deductions North Carolina Does Not Allow
## Description
The One Big Beautiful Bill Act created several federal deductions taken AFTER AGI (on federal Schedule 1-A or as below-the-line deductions). Because North Carolina starts from federal AGI and uses its own deductions, NONE of them reduce North Carolina taxable income. NCDOR confirmed this after the July 2026 conformity update, and the General Assembly did not enact a state version.
## Not allowed in North Carolina
| Federal deduction | Federal rule | North Carolina |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified tips (IRC 224) | Up to $25,000, 2025-2028 | Not allowed |
| Qualified overtime premium (IRC 225) | Up to $12,500 ($25,000 MFJ), 2025-2028 | Not allowed |
| Qualified passenger vehicle loan interest | Up to $10,000, 2025-2028 | Not allowed |
| Senior deduction, age 65+ | $6,000 per person, 2025-2028 | Not allowed |
| Qualified business income deduction (IRC 199A), including Section 199A REIT dividends | 20% of QBI, permanent | Not allowed — taken after AGI |
| Non-itemizer charitable deduction | $1,000 ($2,000 MFJ), permanent from 2026 | Not allowed |
| Federal standard deduction | $16,100 single / $24,150 HOH / $32,200 MFJ for 2026 | NC amounts unchanged ($12,750 / $19,125 / $25,500) |
## Why
These deductions are below the AGI line. The IRC conformity update changes how AGI is computed, not North Carolina's deductions. A state "no tax on tips, overtime" bill (House Bill 11) was introduced in 2025 but not enacted; neither the July 2026 conformity law (S.L. 2026-31) nor the budget (S.L. 2026-41) added a state version.
## Notes
- No Schedule S entry is needed — these never entered AGI, so there is nothing to add back
- Recheck for 2027 in case the General Assembly acts
## Questions
- Did you claim the federal tips, overtime, car loan, or senior deduction?
## Common Errors
- Deducting tips or overtime on the North Carolina return
- Expecting the federal senior deduction to reduce North Carolina tax on retirement income
