# Catastrophe Savings Tax
## Description
Additional 2.5% tax on money withdrawn from a Mississippi Catastrophe Savings Account for anything other than qualified catastrophe expenses. The withdrawal is ALSO added back to income on Schedule N.
## The account
- A savings or money market account labeled "Catastrophe Savings Account" and set up to pay hurricane, flood, windstorm, or other catastrophe deductibles and uninsured losses on the taxpayer's Mississippi legal residence, or to self-insure it
- Contributions and interest are deducted on Form 80-105, line 60, within limits:
  - Insurance deductible $1,000 or less: up to $2,000
  - Deductible over $1,000: lesser of $15,000 or twice the deductible
  - Self-insured: up to $350,000, not more than the home's value
## When the 2.5% does NOT apply
- The taxpayer no longer owns a Mississippi legal residence that qualifies for homestead exemption
- The distribution is made on or after the taxpayer turns age 70 (any account, not only self-insured)
## Example (2026)
- $4,000 withdrawn for a vacation; taxpayer age 52 with homestead → $4,000 added on Schedule N, plus $100 catastrophe savings tax on line 22
## Required Information
- Account statements showing withdrawals
- Receipts for qualified catastrophe expenses
- Homestead status and date of birth
## Questions
- Did you withdraw money from a Catastrophe Savings Account in 2026?
- What was the money used for?
- Do you still own a homestead-eligible home in Mississippi?
- Were you 70 or older at the time of the withdrawal?
## Common Errors
- Reporting the 2.5% tax but not adding the withdrawal to income on Schedule N (or the reverse)
- Applying the tax after age 70
- Limiting the age-70 exception to self-insured accounts
## Prompt
- I took money out of my catastrophe savings account for something else.
- Is there a penalty for using my Mississippi catastrophe savings account early?
