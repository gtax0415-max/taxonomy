# Filing Status
## Description
Mark one box based on status on the LAST DAY of the year. Mississippi's statuses do not match the federal ones one-for-one.
## Statuses, exemption, and standard deduction for 2026 (same as 2025)
| Line | Status | Exemption | Standard deduction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Married – joint or combined return | $12,000 | $4,600 |
| 2 | Married – spouse died in 2026 | $12,000 | $4,600 |
| 3 | Married – filing separate returns | $6,000 each | $2,300 each |
| 4 | Head of family | $8,000 (+$1,500 dependent) | $3,400 |
| 5 | Single | $6,000 | $2,300 |
## Federal → Mississippi mapping
- Married filing jointly → line 1 (joint if one earner, combined if both earn; see married-joint-return.md and married-combined-return.md)
- Married filing separately → line 3, or line 1 combined (spouses may choose either)
- Head of household → line 4 ONLY if a dependent lived in the home all year; otherwise line 5
- Qualifying surviving spouse → line 4 or 5 (see qualifying-surviving-spouse.md)
- Single → line 5
## Notes
- Married spouses can pick the method with the lowest tax; the federal choice does not bind Mississippi
- Mississippi does not recognize common-law marriage
## Required Information
- Marital status on December 31, 2026
- Whether a dependent lived in the home all year
## Questions
- Were you married on December 31, 2026?
- Do both spouses have income?
- Did a dependent live with you the whole year?
## Common Errors
- Copying the federal status without applying Mississippi rules
- Using head of family when the dependent lived elsewhere
- Treating a common-law spouse as married
## Prompt
- Which filing status should I use in Mississippi?
