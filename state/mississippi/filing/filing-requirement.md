# Mississippi Filing Requirement
## Description
Who must file a Mississippi return. The thresholds equal the exemption plus the standard deduction, so they are statutory and do not change for 2026.
## Thresholds for 2026 (same as 2025)
- Single resident: gross income over $8,300 ($6,000 exemption + $2,300 standard deduction), plus $1,500 for each dependent
- Married resident: combined gross income over $16,600 ($12,000 + $4,600), plus $1,500 for each dependent
- Minor resident: gross income over the exemption plus standard deduction for the filing status
- Head of family is not listed separately in the instructions; by the same formula it is $12,900 ($9,500 + $3,400) plus $1,500 per additional dependent
## Must also file
- Any Mississippi tax was withheld from wages (other than gambling) — needed to get a refund
- A resident working outside Mississippi, reporting total income from all sources
- A resident working abroad temporarily (see foreign-earned-income.md)
- A nonresident or part-year resident with Mississippi-taxed income other than gambling (Form 80-205)
## Does NOT need to file
- A nonresident whose only Mississippi income is Mississippi casino winnings; the casino's 3% withholding is the final tax
## Notes
- "Gross income" here means income taxable in Mississippi; Social Security, retirement income, and Mississippi casino winnings are excluded
- Due date April 15, 2027 for calendar-year 2026 returns
## Required Information
- Gross income by type
- Mississippi withholding
- Number of dependents
## Questions
- What was your total income in 2026, excluding Social Security and retirement income?
- Was any Mississippi tax withheld?
- Did you work outside Mississippi?
## Common Errors
- Counting exempt retirement income or Social Security toward the threshold
- Not filing when tax was withheld, and so losing the refund
- A resident skipping the return because the income was earned in another state
## Prompt
- Do I need to file a Mississippi return?
- I'm retired and only have Social Security and a pension.
