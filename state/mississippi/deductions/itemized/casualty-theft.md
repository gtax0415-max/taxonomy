# Casualty and Theft Losses
## Description
Personal casualty and theft losses, computed exactly as under federal law. Deductible only if attributable to a declared disaster. Federal Form 4684 must be attached to the Mississippi return.
## 2026 change
- The One Big Beautiful Bill Act made the declared-disaster requirement permanent and, starting in 2026, added STATE-declared disasters alongside federally declared disasters
- Mississippi's 2025 instructions still say "federally declared disaster areas only". Because the amount comes from federal Schedule A and Form 4684, state-declared disaster losses should flow through for 2026. Confirm against the 2026 instructions
## Amounts
- Two reductions per federal rules:
  1. $100 per casualty event ($500 for a qualified disaster loss)
  2. 10% of AGI on the total (waived for a qualified disaster loss)
- Reduce by insurance received or expected
## Notes
- Ordinary theft, fire, or accident losses with no disaster declaration are not deductible
- A disaster loss may be claimed in the disaster year or on an amended return for the prior year
- Losses paid from a Mississippi Catastrophe Savings Account are not also deductible here
## Required Information
- Disaster declaration number (FEMA or state)
- Date and description of the event
- Adjusted basis and decline in value
- Insurance reimbursement received or expected
- Federal Form 4684
## Questions
- Was your property damaged in a federally or state-declared disaster?
- Did you file an insurance claim, and what was paid?
- Did you pay any repairs from a Catastrophe Savings Account?
- Do you want to claim the loss this year or the prior year?
## Common Errors
- Claiming a loss with no disaster declaration
- Missing a 2026 state-declared disaster loss
- Forgetting the $100 and 10% of AGI reductions
- Not attaching Form 4684
## Prompt
- My house was damaged by a tornado in Mississippi.
- Can I deduct losses from a state-declared disaster in 2026?
