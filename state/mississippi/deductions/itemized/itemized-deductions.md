# Mississippi Itemized Deductions
## Description
Mississippi starts from the federal Schedule A line items, then removes two things federal law allows: state income taxes (or sales taxes in lieu) and Mississippi gaming losses. Itemizing for Mississippi is allowed even when the federal return used the standard deduction.
## How the total is built (Form 80-108, Part I)
- Line 1: Federal AGI (Form 1040, line 11a; unchanged on the 2026 draft form)
- Line 2c: Medical and dental over 7.5% of federal AGI (see medical-expenses.md)
- Line 3c: Taxes paid less state income taxes (see taxes-paid.md)
- Line 4: Interest paid (see interest-paid.md)
- Line 5: Charitable contributions (see charitable-contributions.md)
- Line 6: Casualty and theft losses (see casualty-theft.md)
- Line 7c: Other miscellaneous deductions less Mississippi gambling losses (see other-miscellaneous.md)
- Line 8: Total, carried to Form 80-105, line 14
## 2026 federal changes that flow in through Schedule A (One Big Beautiful Bill Act)
- SALT cap $40,400 ($20,200 MFS) for 2026, phased down above $505,000 MAGI
- Charitable deduction floor: only gifts above 0.5% of AGI count for itemizers
- Mortgage insurance premiums again treated as deductible mortgage interest
- Casualty losses from STATE-declared disasters newly deductible (in addition to federal)
- Gambling losses limited to 90% of losses (still capped at winnings)
- Educator expenses: qualifying educator expenses not deducted on Schedule 1 can be itemized on federal Schedule A, line 17k (not subject to the 2% floor; also covers coaches and athletic administrators)
- 2% miscellaneous deductions permanently gone; Pease limit permanently repealed
## Open item for 2026
- The new federal overall limit for 37%-bracket taxpayers (2/37 reduction) is applied to the federal TOTAL on the 2026 draft Schedule A, line 18, not to the line items. Because Mississippi rebuilds its total from the line items, the reduction likely does not carry over. Confirm against the 2026 Form 80-108 and instructions when released (expected December 2026)
## Notes
- Married filing separately: if one spouse itemizes, both must itemize
- Combined return: itemized deductions may be divided between Columns A and B in any manner
- Part-year residents may claim only expenses incurred while a Mississippi resident, then prorate
- Form 80-108 must be attached when itemizing
## Required Information
- Federal Schedule A (or a Schedule A worksheet)
- Federal AGI
- State income tax withheld or paid, and any sales tax claimed in lieu
- Mississippi gaming losses included in federal Schedule A
## Questions
- Did you itemize on your federal return? If not, are your expenses still large enough to itemize for Mississippi?
- How much state income tax is included in your federal taxes-paid amount?
- Did you have gambling losses at Mississippi casinos?
- Are you a part-year resident?
## Common Errors
- Copying the federal Schedule A total without removing state income taxes
- Leaving Mississippi gaming losses in the total
- Assuming Mississippi itemizing requires federal itemizing
- One spouse itemizing while the other takes the standard deduction on separate returns
- Carrying the federal 37%-bracket reduction into the Mississippi total without confirming the 2026 rule
## Prompt
- Should I itemize on my Mississippi return?
- I took the federal standard deduction but have high mortgage interest.
- What itemized deductions does Mississippi allow?
