# Catastrophe Savings Account
## Description
Deduction for deposits (plus interest earned) into a labeled "Catastrophe Savings Account" set up to pay the insurance deductible and uninsured catastrophe losses on the taxpayer's Mississippi legal residence, or to self-insure it.
## Limits for 2026 (statutory, unchanged)
- Insurance deductible of $1,000 or less: up to $2,000
- Deductible over $1,000: the lesser of $15,000 or twice the deductible
- Self-insured (no insurance): up to $350,000, not more than the value of the legal residence
## Notes
- Only one account per taxpayer, kept separate and labeled "Catastrophe Savings Account"
- Covers the legal residence (homestead) only
- Non-qualified withdrawals are added back to income (see catastrophe-savings-distribution.md) and taxed an extra 2.5% (see catastrophe-savings-tax.md)
## Example (2026)
- Hurricane deductible $5,000 → limit is the lesser of $15,000 or $10,000 → up to $10,000 deductible
## Required Information
- Deposits and interest for 2026
- The policy's hurricane, wind, or flood deductible
## Questions
- Do you have an account labeled "Catastrophe Savings Account"?
- What is your homeowner's insurance deductible for storm damage?
## Common Errors
- Deducting deposits into an ordinary savings account
- Exceeding the limit tied to the deductible
- Using it for a second home or rental
## Prompt
- I set aside money for hurricane deductibles. Is it deductible in Mississippi?
