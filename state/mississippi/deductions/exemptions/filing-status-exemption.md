# Filing Status Exemption
## Description
Personal exemption set by filing status on the last day of the year. Amounts are fixed by Mississippi statute (Miss. Code Ann. §27-7-21) and are NOT indexed for inflation. Mississippi kept its personal exemption even though the federal exemption is $0.
## Amounts for 2026 (same as 2025)
- Married filing joint or combined return (line 1): $12,000
- Married, spouse died in 2026 (line 2): $12,000
- Married filing separate returns (line 3): $12,000 entered on line 11, but each spouse claims only HALF ($6,000) on line 15
- Head of family (line 4): $8,000, plus $1,500 for the required dependent through line 10 (effectively $9,500)
- Single (line 5): $6,000
## Mississippi status rules that differ from federal
- HEAD OF FAMILY requires a dependent who LIVES WITH the taxpayer for the entire year. Supporting a dependent in a separate household does not qualify, even if federal head of household would
- A married person must live apart from the spouse for the ENTIRE year to use head of family
- There is no qualifying surviving spouse status. "Spouse died" applies only in the year of death
- Mississippi does not recognize common-law marriage
- Legally separated on the last day of the year = single
## Required Information
- Marital status on December 31, 2026
- For head of family, each dependent living in the home all year
- Spouse's date of death, if applicable
## Questions
- Were you married on December 31, 2026?
- Did a dependent live with you all year?
- If married, did you live apart from your spouse the entire year?
- Did your spouse die during 2026?
## Common Errors
- Using federal head of household status when the dependent did not live in the home all year
- Claiming the full $12,000 on each separate return
- Using "spouse died" in the year after the death
- Forgetting the $1,500 dependent amount that goes with head of family status
