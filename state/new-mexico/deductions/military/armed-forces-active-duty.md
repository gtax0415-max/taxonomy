# Armed Forces Active Duty Pay
## Description
Salary paid by the United States for active duty in the armed forces is fully exempt in New Mexico, for residents and nonresidents alike.

## What counts as active duty
- Full-time duty in active service in the Army, Navy, Air Force, Marine Corps, Space Force, or Coast Guard
- Full-time training duty and annual training duty
- Full-time National Guard duty
- Attendance at a designated service school while in active service

## Amount
- All active duty pay included in federal AGI; no cap
- Combat pay already excluded federally is not entered again

## Notes
- U.S. Public Health Service active duty pay does NOT go here; nonresident USPHS members use PIT-ADJ line 22
- Drill pay for part-time Guard or Reserve weekends is not "full-time" active duty; check orders before including it
- Military retirement pay is a different exemption (PIT-ADJ line 24)
- Residents and nonresidents with income both inside and outside New Mexico also follow the PIT-B allocation rules

## Status for 2026
No change from the 2026 legislative session. Confirm line numbers on the 2026 PIT-ADJ.

## Required Information
- W-2 wages from military pay
- Periods and type of duty
- Residency status

## Questions
- Were you on active duty in the U.S. armed forces this year?
- Was any of your military pay for weekend drills only?
- Are you in the U.S. Public Health Service instead?

## Common Errors
- Including USPHS pay here
- Including retirement pay here instead of on line 24
- Exempting combat pay that was never in federal income

## Prompt
- I am active duty Air Force stationed at Kirtland.
- I did two weeks of annual training with the National Guard.
