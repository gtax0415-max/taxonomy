# Interest on U.S. Government Obligations
## Description
Federal law bars states from taxing interest on U.S. government obligations. The interest is in federal AGI, so New Mexico subtracts it.

## What qualifies
- U.S. savings bonds (Series EE, I)
- Treasury bills, notes, and bonds
- Notes issued by the Federal Home Loan Banks (interest only, not dividends)
- Your share of U.S. obligation interest passed through by a partnership, S corporation, LLC, unit investment trust, or simple trust
- Mutual fund dividends attributable to U.S. government obligations, by the percentage the fund reports, if the fund gives investors that statement

## What does NOT qualify
- Ginnie Mae (GNMA), Fannie Mae (FNMA), and Freddie Mac (FHLMC) securities
- Interest on federal tax refunds
- Interest earned inside an IRA or 401(k)

## Amount
- No cap; all qualifying interest included in federal AGI

## Notes
- Savings bond interest excluded federally for education (Form 8815) is not in AGI and is not entered again
- The same U.S. obligation interest is added to a New Mexico net operating loss in a loss year (see deductions/business-expenses/nm-net-operating-loss.md)

## Status for 2026
No change from the 2026 legislative session. Confirm line numbers on the 2026 PIT-ADJ.

## Required Information
- 1099-INT box 3 amounts
- Fund statements with the U.S. government percentage
- K-1 or trust statements with U.S. obligation interest

## Questions
- Did you cash savings bonds or hold Treasury bills, notes, or bonds?
- Do you own a Treasury or government bond fund?
- Do you hold Ginnie Mae, Fannie Mae, or Freddie Mac securities?

## Common Errors
- Deducting Ginnie Mae, Fannie Mae, or Freddie Mac interest
- Deducting a fund's whole dividend instead of the U.S. government share
- Deducting interest earned inside a retirement account
- Deducting education-excluded savings bond interest that never reached AGI

## Prompt
- I cashed in Series I savings bonds this year.
- I have Treasury bill interest; is it taxed in New Mexico?
