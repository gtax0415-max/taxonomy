# Net Capital Gains Deduction
## Description
New Mexico subtracts part of the net capital gain in federal AGI. Since the 2024 overhaul (HB 252, from tax year 2025) the deduction is small for most people, but generous for the sale of a New Mexico business.

## Amount (the GREATER of)
- 100% of net capital gain, up to $2,500, OR
- 40% of up to $1,000,000 of net capital gain from the sale of a BUSINESS, to the extent allocated or apportioned to New Mexico (maximum $400,000)
- Married filing separately: each spouse gets half of the joint amount

## Definitions
- "Net capital gain" is the federal Section 1222(11) figure: net long-term capital gain minus net short-term capital loss
- Short-term gains never count

## Notes
- Before 2025 the deduction was a percentage of ALL net capital gain. Older guides showing that rule are out of date
- The business-sale prong needs gain from selling a business, with New Mexico allocation; an ordinary stock sale uses the $2,500 prong
- Part-year residents and nonresidents count only gain allocated or apportioned to New Mexico

## Status for 2026
Same rules as 2025 (NMSA 7-2-34 as amended by 2024 HB 252). No change from the 2026 legislative session. Confirm line numbers on the 2026 PIT-ADJ.

## Required Information
- Net long-term gain and net short-term loss from Schedule D
- Whether any gain came from selling a business, and the New Mexico portion
- Filing status

## Questions
- Did you have long-term capital gains this year?
- Did you sell a business or your interest in one?
- How much of the business-sale gain is allocated to New Mexico?

## Common Errors
- Using the pre-2025 percentage-of-all-gains rule
- Including short-term gains
- Applying the business prong to ordinary stock or fund sales
- Taking the full amount on each of two married-separate returns

## Prompt
- I sold stock at a long-term gain; what does New Mexico let me deduct?
- I sold my New Mexico business for a $900,000 gain.
