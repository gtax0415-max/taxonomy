# New Mexico Tax-Exempt Interest and Dividends
## Description
The partner to the tax-exempt interest addition. New Mexico first adds back ALL federally tax-exempt bond interest (PIT-ADJ line 1), then lets you subtract the part that New Mexico also exempts.

## What qualifies
- Bonds of the State of New Mexico or its agencies, institutions, instrumentalities, or political subdivisions
- Obligations of Puerto Rico, Guam, the U.S. Virgin Islands, American Samoa, and the Northern Mariana Islands
- Federally TAXABLE bonds issued by the State of New Mexico
- Mutual funds, unit investment trusts, or simple trusts holding those obligations, limited to the amount the fund's annual statement shows flowing through

## Amount
- No dollar limit
- Usually equals or is smaller than the PIT-ADJ line 1 addition. If it is larger (possible with federally taxable New Mexico bonds), attach an explanation

## Notes
- IRAs are not simple trusts; interest earned inside an IRA cannot be deducted here
- Bonds of other states are taxable in New Mexico and stay in income
- U.S. Treasury interest is a different deduction (PIT-ADJ line 9)

## Status for 2026
No change from the 2026 legislative session. Confirm line numbers on the 2026 PIT-ADJ.

## Required Information
- Each payer's tax-exempt interest and the New Mexico/territory portion
- Fund statements with state-by-state percentages
- Any federally taxable New Mexico bond interest

## Questions
- Do you own New Mexico municipal bonds or a fund that holds them?
- What percentage of your muni fund's income came from New Mexico?
- Do you own any federally taxable New Mexico bonds?

## Common Errors
- Deducting all muni interest instead of only the New Mexico/territory share
- Claiming the deduction without the matching line 1 addition
- Including IRA interest
- Applying a fund's percentage to the wrong income figure

## Prompt
- I own New Mexico municipal bonds.
- How much of my muni fund interest is exempt in New Mexico?
