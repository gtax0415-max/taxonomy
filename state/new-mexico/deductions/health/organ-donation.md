# Organ Donation-Related Expenses
## Description
Deduction for costs a living donor (the taxpayer or a dependent) incurred to donate a human organ to another person.

## Amount
- Up to $10,000 of expenses incurred during the tax year
- Married filing separately: each spouse gets half of the joint amount

## What counts
- Lost wages
- Lodging
- Travel
- For a donation by the taxpayer or the taxpayer's dependent

## Qualifying organs
- All or part of a heart, liver, pancreas, kidney, intestine, lung, or bone marrow

## Notes
- Blood, plasma, and other tissue donations do not qualify
- Expenses reimbursed by the recipient, an insurer, or a program should be left out
- Only expenses incurred in the tax year count

## Status for 2026
No change from the 2026 legislative session. Confirm line numbers on the 2026 PIT-ADJ.

## Required Information
- Date of donation and organ
- Receipts for travel and lodging
- Documentation of lost wages
- Any reimbursements received

## Questions
- Did you or a dependent donate an organ or bone marrow this year?
- What did you spend on travel and lodging, and how much pay did you lose?
- Were any costs reimbursed?

## Common Errors
- Claiming over $10,000
- Including blood or plasma donations
- Including reimbursed costs
- Claiming the full amount on each of two married-separate returns

## Prompt
- I donated a kidney to my brother and missed three weeks of work.
- My son donated bone marrow; can we deduct the travel?
