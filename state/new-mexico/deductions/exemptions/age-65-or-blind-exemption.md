# Exemption for Persons 65 or Older or Blind
## Description
A New Mexico exemption of up to $8,000 per qualifying person who is 65 or older, or blind for federal tax purposes. It phases out by federal AGI in $1,000 steps and is gone above the top of the table.

## Amounts (Table 1; same for 2026)
- $8,000 per person: joint, head of household or surviving spouse AGI $0–30,000; single AGI $0–18,000; married filing separately AGI $0–15,000
- $7,000 per person: joint, head of household or surviving spouse AGI $30,001–33,000; single AGI $18,001–19,500; married filing separately AGI $15,001–16,500
- $6,000 per person: joint, head of household or surviving spouse AGI $33,001–36,000; single AGI $19,501–21,000; married filing separately AGI $16,501–18,000
- $5,000 per person: joint, head of household or surviving spouse AGI $36,001–39,000; single AGI $21,001–22,500; married filing separately AGI $18,001–19,500
- $4,000 per person: joint, head of household or surviving spouse AGI $39,001–42,000; single AGI $22,501–24,000; married filing separately AGI $19,501–21,000
- $3,000 per person: joint, head of household or surviving spouse AGI $42,001–45,000; single AGI $24,001–25,500; married filing separately AGI $21,001–22,500
- $2,000 per person: joint, head of household or surviving spouse AGI $45,001–48,000; single AGI $25,501–27,000; married filing separately AGI $22,501–24,000
- $1,000 per person: joint, head of household or surviving spouse AGI $48,001–51,000; single AGI $27,001–28,500; married filing separately AGI $24,001–25,500
- $0 per person: joint, head of household or surviving spouse AGI over $51,000; single AGI over $28,500; married filing separately AGI over $25,500

- On a joint return, the table amount applies to EACH qualifying spouse
- One amount per person: being both 65+ and blind does not double it
- The bands are fixed in statute and NOT indexed for inflation

## Eligibility
- 65 or older on the last day of the tax year (for 2026: born before January 2, 1962, using the federal convention that you turn 65 the day before your birthday), or blind for federal purposes
- Age/blind boxes on PIT-1 page 1 must be marked or the exemption is denied
- Not combinable with the age-100 exemption for the same person

## Notes
- The H.R.1 federal senior deduction ($6,000 per person, 2025–2028) is a separate federal item; it does not change federal AGI, so it does not affect this table

## Status for 2026
No change from the 2026 legislative session. Confirm line numbers on the 2026 PIT-ADJ.

## Required Information
- Federal AGI (Form 1040 line 11)
- Filing status
- Dates of birth, and blindness status, for taxpayer and spouse

## Questions
- Are you or your spouse 65 or older, or blind?
- What is your federal AGI and filing status?

## Common Errors
- Not marking the age/blind boxes on PIT-1
- Taking the per-person amount only once on a joint return where both qualify
- Doubling the amount for someone who is both 65 and blind
- Using New Mexico income instead of federal AGI to look up the table

## Prompt
- I am 70 with income of $25,000; what New Mexico exemption do I get?
- My wife and I are both over $65 and earn $40,000.
