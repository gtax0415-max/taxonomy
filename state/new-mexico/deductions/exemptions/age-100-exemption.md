# Income of Persons Age 100 or Older
## Description
A New Mexico resident who is 100 or older at the end of the tax year, and who is not someone else's dependent, owes no New Mexico income tax on their income.

## Amount
- Unmarried centenarian: all income
- Married centenarian (joint or separate): ALL of the centenarian's separate income plus HALF of community income (New Mexico is a community property state)
- Combined exemptions on PIT-ADJ lines 11 and 12 cannot exceed 100% of income

## Eligibility
- Age 100 or older on the last day of the tax year (for 2026: born on or before December 31, 1926)
- Not claimed as a dependent by another taxpayer
- Box 12a (taxpayer) and/or 12b (spouse) must be marked or the exemption is denied

## Notes
- An unmarried centenarian need not file at all unless claiming low-income rebates and credits
- Cannot be combined with the 65-or-older/blind exemption (line 13) for the same person
- Exempt income still counts as income when figuring PIT-RC rebates and credits
- If the split is other than 50% of joint income, attach a statement showing the community/separate division

## Status for 2026
No change from the 2026 legislative session. Confirm line numbers on the 2026 PIT-ADJ.

## Required Information
- Date of birth of the taxpayer and spouse
- Dependency status
- For a married couple, separate versus community income

## Questions
- Will you or your spouse be 100 or older by December 31, 2026?
- Is anyone else claiming you as a dependent?
- Is any income the separate property of one spouse?

## Common Errors
- Leaving boxes 12a and 12b unmarked
- Exempting 100% of joint community income for a married centenarian
- Also claiming the line 13 exemption for the same person
- Leaving exempt income out of PIT-RC modified gross income

## Prompt
- My mother turned 100 this year; does she owe New Mexico tax?
- My husband is 101 and I am 85; how much of our income is exempt?
