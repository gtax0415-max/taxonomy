# Armed Forces Retirement Pay Exemption
## Description
Exempts up to $30,000 a year of U.S. armed forces retirement pay for each retiree, or for the surviving spouse of a retiree.

## Amount
- Up to $30,000 of armed forces retirement pay included in federal AGI, per qualifying person
- On a joint return where both spouses are military retirees, EACH can exempt up to $30,000
- Disability retirement qualifies; VA disability compensation is already excluded federally and is not entered

## Eligibility
- Armed forces retiree: a former member who qualified by years of service or disability to separate with lifetime benefits
- Or the surviving spouse of such a retiree receiving the retirement pay or survivor annuity

## Notes
- Amounts above $30,000 per person remain taxable unless another exemption applies
- Active duty pay is a separate exemption (PIT-ADJ line 17)
- Civil service pensions for civilian work do not qualify, even if paid by the Department of Defense

## Status for 2026
$30,000 per person for 2026 (NMSA 7-2-5.13); the amount is fixed, not indexed. SB 116 (2026) proposed removing the cap for tax years beginning in 2027, but it is not among the enacted tax changes in the LFC post-session review, and it would not affect 2026 in any case. Confirm line numbers on the 2026 PIT-ADJ.

## Required Information
- Each 1099-R for military retirement or survivor annuity
- Which spouse is the retiree or surviving spouse

## Questions
- Do you receive U.S. military retirement pay?
- Are you the surviving spouse of a military retiree receiving a survivor annuity?
- Are both spouses military retirees?

## Common Errors
- Exempting more than $30,000 for one person
- Limiting a couple of two retirees to one $30,000
- Including civilian federal pensions
- Entering VA disability compensation that was never in federal AGI

## Prompt
- I retired from the Army after 22 years and get $38,000 a year.
- I receive my late husband's military Survivor Benefit Plan annuity.
