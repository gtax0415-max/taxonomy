# New Mexico Net Operating Loss Carryforward
## Description
New Mexico disallows the federal NOL deduction (added back on PIT-ADJ line 2) and replaces it with its own carryforward, computed under New Mexico rules.

## Amount
- Starting loss = the federal Section 172(c) NOL for the loss year PLUS interest on U.S. government obligations (net of related expenses) reported on the New Mexico return for that year
- Minus New Mexico loss already used in earlier years
- No percentage-of-income cap like the federal 80% rule

## Carryforward rules
- NO carryback
- Losses from tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2013: carry forward 19 years or until used
- Losses from earlier years: 5 years only
- First usable year: the year after the loss if the return was timely, or the first year starting after the return establishing the loss was filed
- There is no New Mexico NOL without a federal NOL

## Notes
- The carryforward schedule (RPD-41369) must be attached every year or the deduction is disallowed
- Taken together with the PIT-ADJ line 2 addback, this replaces the federal number with the New Mexico one

## Status for 2026
No change from the 2026 legislative session. SB 151's decoupling from federal depreciation and interest rules applies only to corporations from 2027 and does not change individual NOLs. Confirm line numbers on the 2026 PIT-ADJ.

## Required Information
- Year and amount of each federal NOL
- U.S. obligation interest in each loss year
- New Mexico loss used each year since

## Questions
- Did you have a net operating loss on a federal return since 2013?
- Was that loss reported on a timely New Mexico return?
- How much of the New Mexico loss have you used?

## Common Errors
- Carrying the loss back
- Applying the federal 80% limit or the federal remaining balance instead of the New Mexico balance
- Forgetting to add the U.S. obligation interest to the loss
- Not attaching RPD-41369

## Prompt
- I have a New Mexico NOL carryforward from my business loss.
- How long can I carry a New Mexico net operating loss?
