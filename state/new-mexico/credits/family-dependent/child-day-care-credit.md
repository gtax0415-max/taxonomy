# New Mexico Child Day Care Credit
## Description
A refundable credit for low-income working families who pay for child care in New Mexico, reduced by any federal child and dependent care credit.

## Amount
- 40% of child care costs, counting no more than $8 per day per child
- Maximum $480 per child and $1,200 per return
- Minus the federal child and dependent care credit (Schedule 3, line 2)
- Married filing separately: half, so $600 maximum

## Eligibility
- Modified gross income $30,160 or less for 2026 (the statute sets it at annual earnings at double the federal minimum wage, $7.25 an hour, so it changes only if the federal minimum wage changes)
- Child under 15 at year-end
- Care in New Mexico, less than 24 hours a day, by a caregiver 18 or older whom you cannot claim as a dependent
- Gainfully employed during the care period, and provided over half the household's support
- Did not receive TANF or similar assistance; expenses not reimbursed
- Not a dependent of another taxpayer
- Attach each caregiver's statement and the worksheet

## 2026 interaction
H.R.1 raised the federal child and dependent care credit rate from 2026. A larger federal credit is subtracted here, so the New Mexico credit can shrink or disappear for some families. Use the 2026 federal credit actually allowed.

## Status for 2026
No New Mexico change from the 2026 legislative session.

## Required Information
- Each child's age and days of care
- Caregiver statements and amounts paid
- Federal credit on Schedule 3, line 2
- MGI

## Questions
- Did you pay for child care so you could work?
- How old is each child, and how many days of care?
- Did you receive TANF?

## Common Errors
- Counting more than $8 a day
- Forgetting to subtract the federal credit
- Missing caregiver statements
- Claiming with MGI over $30,160

## Prompt
- I am a single mom earning $25,000 and pay a sitter for my 3-year-old.
