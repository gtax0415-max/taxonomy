# Exemptions (Line 5)
## Description
A head count of the household, not a dollar amount. New Mexico uses it for the low- and middle-income exemption (PIT-1 line 14, up to $2,500 per counted person) and for rebates and credits on PIT-RC.

## How to count (line 5 worksheet)
- 1 for yourself
- 1 for your spouse on a joint return
- Plus the number of dependents and other dependents on your federal return
- Do NOT count yourself (or your spouse on a joint return) if you can be claimed as a dependent by someone else, even if they did not actually claim you
- A filer who is a dependent of another taxpayer enters 00 (see filing/dependent-filer.md)

## Notes
- The federal personal exemption amount (IRC 151) is zero, now permanently under H.R.1. New Mexico still uses this count for its own purposes
- The deduction for certain dependents (line 13) depends on the 151 exemption amount staying zero, so it continues for 2026

## Status for 2026
No change from the 2026 legislative session.

## Required Information
- Filing status
- Number of dependents and other dependents on the federal return
- Whether the taxpayer or spouse can be claimed by someone else

## Questions
- Can anyone else claim you or your spouse as a dependent?
- How many dependents are on your federal return?

## Common Errors
- Counting yourself when you can be claimed by another taxpayer
- Counting a spouse on a separate return
- Counting people who are not on the federal return

## Prompt
- How many exemptions do I enter on line 5?
- My parents can claim me; what goes on line 5?
