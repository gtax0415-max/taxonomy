# Community Property Statement
## Description
New Mexico is a community property state. Income earned by either spouse during the marriage, and income from community assets, belongs half to each. When spouses file separately, each reports half of community income and payments plus all of their own separate income.

## When a statement is required
- Married filing separately, if community income or payments are NOT divided evenly between the two returns
- Married filing jointly, if the instructions call for a division (for example, a centenarian exemption based on a split other than 50%)

## What the statement shows
- Total community income and how it is divided
- Each spouse's separate income
- How withholding and estimated payments are divided

## Notes
- Separate property generally includes assets owned before marriage and gifts or inheritances to one spouse, plus income they earn unless an agreement says otherwise
- The federal Form 8958 used for community property states can supply the same breakdown
- The split also matters for the age-100 exemption, where only half of community income belongs to the centenarian

## Status for 2026
No change from the 2026 legislative session.

## Required Information
- All income by spouse and by community/separate character
- Withholding and payments by spouse

## Questions
- Are you married and filing separately?
- Does either of you have separate property or separate income?
- Is your income and withholding split exactly in half?

## Common Errors
- Each spouse reporting only their own paycheck
- Splitting separate-property income as if it were community
- Omitting the statement when the split is uneven

## Prompt
- How does community property work on a New Mexico separate return?
- My husband has rental income from a house he owned before we married.
