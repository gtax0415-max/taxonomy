# New Mexico Filing Requirement
## Description
A full-year New Mexico resident files a PIT-1 when ANY of these is true:
- The resident is required to file a federal income tax return for 2026
- New Mexico income tax was withheld and the resident wants it refunded
- The resident wants to claim New Mexico rebates or credits (for example, those on PIT-RC), even with no tax due

## Threshold
- New Mexico has no separate income threshold; it follows the federal filing requirement
- For 2026, the federal threshold for most filers equals the federal standard deduction: $16,100 single, $24,150 head of household, $32,200 married filing jointly (higher for those 65 or older or blind; $5 for married filing separately)

## Due date
- Tax year 2026 returns are due April 15, 2027, paper or electronic
- There is no longer a later e-file due date. HB 218 (2025) removed the old April 30 electronic date starting with 2025 returns
- See payments/other-payments.md for extensions

## Notes
- A low-income resident with no federal requirement often still should file, to claim refundable New Mexico rebates and credits
- Nonresidents and part-year residents have their own rules; they are out of scope (see filing/first-year-part-year-nonresident.md)

## Status for 2026
No change to who must file from the 2026 legislative session.

## Required Information
- Gross income and filing status (federal requirement)
- New Mexico withholding
- Eligibility for New Mexico rebates and credits

## Questions
- Are you required to file a federal return for 2026?
- Was New Mexico tax withheld from your pay or pension?
- Might you qualify for New Mexico rebates or refundable credits?

## Common Errors
- Not filing when income is below the federal threshold but New Mexico tax was withheld
- Skipping the return and missing refundable rebates
- Using the old April 30 e-file date

## Prompt
- Do I need to file a New Mexico return?
- My income is low; should I still file in New Mexico?
