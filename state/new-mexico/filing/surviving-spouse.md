# Surviving Spouse with Dependent Child
## Description
Available on the New Mexico return only when it is the filing status on the federal return. For 2026, that generally means a spouse who died in 2024 or 2025, with a dependent child living in the home.

## Benefits in New Mexico
- Uses the JOINT rate schedule (1.5% up to $8,000 of taxable income; top 5.9% rate above $315,000)
- Joint-level thresholds: Social Security exemption up to $150,000 AGI, low- and middle-income exemption up to $55,000 AGI, 65-or-blind table to $51,000

## Not available
- The deduction for certain dependents (PIT-1 line 13), which is limited to married filing jointly and head of household

## Notes
- In the year of death itself, the survivor usually files jointly instead, with the spouse's date of death entered on the return
- Federal rules decide eligibility

## Status for 2026
No change from the 2026 legislative session.

## Required Information
- Year of the spouse's death
- Dependent child's details
- Federal filing status

## Questions
- When did your spouse die?
- Do you have a dependent child living with you?
- Are you using surviving spouse status on your federal return?

## Common Errors
- Using surviving spouse status in the year of death instead of joint
- Claiming the line 13 dependent deduction
- Using single thresholds

## Prompt
- My husband died last year and I have two kids at home.
