# Married Filing Separately
## Description
Allowed only when the federal status is married filing separately. New Mexico is a COMMUNITY PROPERTY state, so each spouse generally reports half of community income and half of community withholding and payments.

## What changes
- Separate rate schedule: 1.5% up to $4,000; top 5.9% rate above $157,500 of taxable income
- Lower thresholds: Social Security exemption $75,000 AGI; low- and middle-income exemption $27,500 AGI; 65-or-blind table $25,500
- HALF amounts, each spouse: net capital gains deduction, 529 contribution deduction, organ donation deduction, and PIT-CR business-related credits (each spouse may claim half of the joint amount)
- PIT-RC rebates and credits follow that schedule's married-separate rules

## How to report
- Mark box (3) on line 7; enter the spouse's name and SSN on 2a and 2b
- If community income or payments are not split evenly, attach a statement showing the division (see filing/community-property.md)

## Status for 2026
No change from the 2026 legislative session.

## Required Information
- Spouse's name and SSN
- Each income item and payment, classified community or separate

## Questions
- Are you filing separately from your spouse on the federal return?
- Which income is community income and which is separate?
- Did you and your spouse split withholding evenly?

## Common Errors
- Reporting only your own wages instead of half of community income
- Each spouse claiming the full capital gains, 529, or organ donation amount
- Leaving out the spouse's SSN
- Not attaching a community property statement when the split is not 50/50

## Prompt
- My wife and I file separately; how do we split income in New Mexico?
- Do I get the full capital gains deduction filing separately?
