# Federal Adjusted Gross Income
## Description
New Mexico starts from federal AGI. Wages, interest, dividends, capital gains, business, farm, rental, retirement income, and taxable Social Security all reach New Mexico through this one number. New Mexico then adds and subtracts its own items.

## How New Mexico taxable income is built (PIT-1)
- Line 9: federal AGI
- Plus line 10: state and local income or sales tax deducted on Schedule A
- Plus line 11: additions (PIT-ADJ)
- Minus line 12: federal standard or itemized deduction (from Form 1040, line 12)
- Minus line 13: deduction for certain dependents
- Minus line 14: low- and middle-income exemption
- Minus line 15: deductions and exemptions (PIT-ADJ)
- Equals line 17: New Mexico taxable income (not below zero)

## 2026: federal deductions that do NOT carry to New Mexico
- H.R.1's new federal deductions for tips, overtime, car loan interest, and the $6,000 senior deduction are taken AFTER AGI and are not part of the standard or itemized deduction on line 12. They do not reduce New Mexico income
- The federal qualified business income deduction is also after AGI and does not carry over
- HB 264 (2026) would have created New Mexico tip and overtime deductions, but it was not enacted

## Notes
- A negative AGI is entered with a minus sign directly before the number (no parentheses)
- If the federal return changes, the New Mexico return must be amended (out of scope here)

## Status for 2026
No New Mexico change. Confirm the Form 1040 line references on the 2026 federal form.

## Required Information
- Federal AGI
- Federal standard or itemized deduction amount

## Questions
- What is your federal AGI for 2026?
- Did you claim the federal tips, overtime, senior, or car loan interest deductions?

## Common Errors
- Starting from federal taxable income instead of AGI
- Subtracting the federal tips, overtime, senior, or car loan interest deductions on the New Mexico return
- Writing a loss in parentheses

## Prompt
- Where does New Mexico start when figuring my tax?
- Does New Mexico give me the no-tax-on-tips deduction?
