# Surplus Tax Refund (Georgia)
## Description
When Georgia has a budget surplus, the legislature periodically authorizes a one-time surplus refund on top of any regular refund. It is not a permanent feature — it depends on the legislature authorizing it each year.
## Amount (2026 surplus refund)
By filing status, capped at the prior-year (2024) Georgia tax liability:
- Single or married filing separately: up to $250
- Head of household: up to $375
- Married filing jointly: up to $500
## Eligibility (2026)
- Generally must have filed BOTH the 2024 and 2025 Georgia returns by the applicable deadline (April 15, 2025, or October 15, 2025 with extension)
- Must have had a 2024 Georgia tax liability
- Full-year, part-year, and certain nonresident filers can qualify; part-year and nonresident amounts are typically prorated
- Capped at the 2024 Georgia tax liability
## Tax treatment
Surplus refunds are NOT taxable for Georgia individual income tax purposes, but may be federally taxable in some circumstances.
## Required Information
- 2024 Georgia return (establishes the liability cap)
- Confirmation both 2024 and 2025 returns were timely filed
- Filing status
## Questions
- Did you file both your 2024 and 2025 Georgia returns on time?
- Did you have a 2024 Georgia tax liability?
- What is your filing status? (sets the maximum)
## Common Errors
- Assuming the surplus refund is automatic every year (it requires legislative authorization)
- Expecting more than the 2024 liability cap
- Overlooking proration for part-year/nonresident filers
## Prompt
- Is Georgia issuing a surplus refund this year?
- How much is the Georgia surplus refund?
- Do I qualify for the Georgia surplus tax refund?
## Notes
- This is separate from the regular overpayment refund — see items/refund-and-direct-deposit.md. Confirm the current year's authorization and amounts, as they change each time the legislature acts
