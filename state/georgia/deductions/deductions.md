# Deductions and Exemptions (Georgia)
## Description
After Georgia adjusted gross income is set on Form 500, Line 10, the return reduces it by either the standard deduction (Line 11) OR itemized deductions (Line 12c) — never both — then by the dependent exemption (Line 14) to reach Georgia taxable income (Line 15a). Unlike New York, Georgia has no separate exemption for the taxpayer and spouse; that value is folded into the (much larger, post-HB 463) standard deduction.
## How the flow works
- Form 500, Line 10 = Georgia adjusted gross income
- Line 11 = Standard deduction (leave blank if itemizing) — OR —
- Lines 12a–12c = Itemized deductions from federal Schedule A, adjusted (leave blank if using the standard deduction)
- Line 13 = Line 10 minus Line 11 or Line 12c
- Line 14 = Dependent exemption (number of dependents × per-dependent amount)
- Line 15a = Line 13 minus Line 14 = Georgia taxable income before NOLs
- Line 15b = Georgia NOL utilized (80%-of-income limit for post-2017 NOLs); Line 15c = Line 15a − Line 15b
- Line 16 = Line 15c × the flat rate (4.99% for 2026)
## The standard-vs-itemized lock
If you itemize on your FEDERAL return, you MUST itemize on Georgia; if you take the federal standard deduction, you must take the Georgia standard deduction. You cannot mix. If married filing separately and your spouse itemizes, you must itemize too. Itemizers include a copy of federal Schedule A.

## 2026 amounts (HB 463)
- Standard deduction: $15,000 single / HOH / MFS / qualifying surviving spouse; $30,000 married filing jointly (raised from $12,000 / $24,000 for 2025)
- Dependent exemption: $5,000 per dependent (raised from $4,000 for 2025)
- Both step up annually from 2027 (standard deduction toward $18,000 single / $36,000 MFJ; dependent exemption toward $6,000)
## Related
- These reduce Georgia AGI (income/ branch) to reach taxable income; the flat rate is then applied — see the tax-computation branch (Line 16)
- Certain income items (HDHP premiums, organ donation, first-responder benefits) can appear as Schedule 1 SUBTRACTIONS instead of, or interacting with, itemized deductions — see income/subtractions/
- SALT cap stays at $10,000 for Georgia ($5,000 MFS); Georgia did not adopt the federal increase
## Questions
- Did you take the standard deduction or itemize on your federal return? (Georgia must match)
- What is your filing status? (sets the standard deduction amount)
- How many dependents do you claim?
- Do you have a Georgia net operating loss carryforward?
