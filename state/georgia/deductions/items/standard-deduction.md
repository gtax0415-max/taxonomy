# Standard Deduction (Georgia)
## Description
A flat reduction of Georgia adjusted gross income by filing status, claimed on Form 500, Line 11. HB 463 (2026) raised the amounts substantially and set them to step up annually thereafter. Georgia has no separate personal/spouse exemption; that value is built into the standard deduction.
## Amount (2026, HB 463)
- Married filing jointly: $30,000
- Single: $15,000
- Married filing separately: $15,000
- Head of household: $15,000
- Qualifying surviving spouse: $15,000
## Prior year (2025) for comparison
- Married filing jointly: $24,000
- Single / MFS / HOH / qualifying surviving spouse: $12,000
## Future years
Under HB 463, from 2027 the single/MFS/HOH amount rises $375/year toward $18,000, and the MFJ amount rises $750/year toward $36,000, subject to the Act's schedule.
## The federal-conformity lock
If you use the standard deduction on your FEDERAL return, you MUST use the Georgia standard deduction. If you itemize federally (or are MFS and your spouse itemizes), you cannot take the Georgia standard deduction — leave Line 11 blank and complete Lines 12a–12c instead.
## No extra age/blindness add-on
Georgia's standard deduction does not add a separate age-65 or blindness amount to Line 11 (unlike the federal standard deduction). Age-based relief comes through the Retirement Income Exclusion instead — see income/subtractions/retirement-income-exclusion.md.
## Required Information
- Filing status
- Confirmation you took the standard deduction federally
## Questions
- What is your filing status?
- Did you take the standard deduction on your federal return?
## Common Errors
- Using 2025 amounts ($12,000/$24,000) on a 2026 return
- Taking the Georgia standard deduction after itemizing federally
- Expecting a federal-style age-65/blindness add-on
## Prompt
- What is Georgia's standard deduction?
- I took the standard deduction federally — what do I use for Georgia?
- Did Georgia's standard deduction go up for 2026?
