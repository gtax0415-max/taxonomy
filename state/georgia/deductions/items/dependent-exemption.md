# Dependent Exemption (Georgia)
## Description
A per-dependent reduction of income, claimed on Form 500, Line 14, computed as the number of dependents (Line 7c) times a fixed per-dependent amount. HB 463 (2026) raised the amount to $5,000. Georgia provides no separate exemption for the taxpayer or spouse — only for dependents.
## Amount (2026, HB 463)
- $5,000 per dependent (raised from $4,000 for 2025)
- Under HB 463, rises $125/year from 2027 toward $6,000
## What counts as a dependent
- Line 7a: qualified dependents (Georgia follows the federal rules for qualified dependents; do not include yourself, your spouse, or unborn dependents here)
- Line 7b: unborn dependents (the Unborn Dependent Exemption; a child born during the tax year is claimed on 7a, NOT 7b)
- Line 7c: total dependents = Line 7a + Line 7b (this is the number multiplied on Line 14)
## The unborn dependent rule
Georgia allows an exemption for an unborn child with a detectable human heartbeat (LIFE Act guidance). If the child is born during the same tax year, include them on Line 7a instead of 7b — not both. See dor.georgia.gov/life-act-guidance.
## MFS rule
If married filing separately, only one spouse may claim a given dependent; the prior federal rules (those applicable when federal dependent exemptions existed) determine who is entitled to claim the dependent.
## Required Information
- Number of qualified dependents (Line 7a)
- Number of unborn dependents (Line 7b)
- Total on Line 7c
- Dependent details for Line 7d
## Questions
- How many dependents do you claim?
- Did you have an unborn dependent with a detectable heartbeat during the year?
- Was any dependent born during the tax year? (goes on 7a, not 7b)
- If MFS, which spouse claims each dependent?
## Common Errors
- Using the 2025 $4,000 amount on a 2026 return
- Counting a child both as unborn (7b) and born (7a) in the year of birth
- Including the taxpayer or spouse in the dependent count
- Both MFS spouses claiming the same dependent
