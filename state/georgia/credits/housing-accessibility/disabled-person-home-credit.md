# Disabled Person Home Purchase or Retrofit Credit (Georgia)
## Description
Nonrefundable credit for a permanently disabled taxpayer (or spouse) who buys a new accessible single-family home or retrofits an existing one with accessibility features.
## Amount
- New single-family home containing ALL four accessibility features: the lesser of $500 per residence or the tax liability
- Retrofit of an existing home: the lesser of the cost or $125 to add one or more features, but total credit cannot exceed $500 per residence
- Statutory basis: O.C.G.A. Section 48-7-29.1; Regulation 560-7-8-.44
## Qualifying features
1. One no-step entrance allowing access into the residence
2. Interior passage doors providing at least a 32-inch-wide opening
3. Reinforcements in bathroom walls allowing installation of grab bars around the toilet, tub, and shower where provided
4. Light switches and outlets placed in accessible locations
## Cap and carryover
Nonrefundable, cannot exceed the tax liability. Carryforward of 3 years.
## Eligibility
- The disabled person must be the taxpayer or the taxpayer's spouse (if filing jointly)
- Must be permanently disabled and have been issued a permanent parking permit
## Required Information
- The permanent parking permit
- Documentation of the home purchase or retrofit features
- Receipts for each retrofit feature (for the $125-per-feature limit)
- Any unused carryover from a prior year
## Questions
- Are you or your spouse permanently disabled with a permanent parking permit?
- Did you buy a new home with all four accessibility features, or retrofit an existing home?
- Which features were added, and what did each cost?
- Do you have unused credit carried forward?
## Common Errors
- Claiming the full $500 for a new home missing one of the four features
- Exceeding the $500-per-residence total on a retrofit
- Claiming without the permanent parking permit
- Overlooking the 3-year carryforward
## Prompt
- I'm disabled and modified my home for accessibility.
- Is there a Georgia credit for making a home wheelchair-accessible?
- I bought a new accessible house.
