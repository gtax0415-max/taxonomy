# Other State(s) Tax Credit (Georgia)
## Description
Nonrefundable credit that prevents double taxation when the same income is taxed by both Georgia and another U.S. state. A Georgia resident who earns income taxed by another state claims a credit for the tax paid there, limited so it never exceeds what Georgia would charge on that income.
## Amount
The credit is the LESSER of:
1. The Georgia tax on the doubly-taxed income (income taxed by the other state, at the applicable Georgia rate), or
2. The tax actually shown on the other state's return for that income, reduced by any credits the other state allowed
Separate full-year and part-year worksheets appear on IT-511 page 34. Part-year residents prorate using the ratio of other-state income to Georgia AGI.
## What qualifies
- State income tax, including Washington, D.C., and U.S. local income tax, imposed on NET income
- NOT foreign taxes, foreign city/province/country tax, or U.S. possession tax
## Eligibility
- Georgia resident (full-year or part-year) with income taxed by both Georgia and another U.S. state
- The other state's tax must be an income tax on net income
## Cap and carryover
Nonrefundable, cannot exceed the Georgia tax liability. No carryforward.
## Required documentation
A copy of the return filed with the other state(s) MUST be included with the Georgia return, or the credit will be disallowed. This is the single most common reason the credit is denied.
## Required Information
- Income taxed by both states
- Tax shown on the other state's return, net of that state's credits
- The applicable Georgia tax rate
- For part-year filers, Georgia AGI from Schedule 3, Line 8, Column C
## Questions
- Did you pay income tax to another U.S. state on income Georgia also taxes?
- Do you have a copy of that state's filed return to attach?
- Was the other tax a state or local income tax, not a foreign or possession tax?
- Are you a full-year or part-year Georgia resident?
## Common Errors
- Failing to attach the other state's return, which disallows the credit
- Claiming credit for foreign or U.S.-possession taxes, which do not qualify
- Using tax withheld rather than the actual tax liability shown on the other state's return
- Not reducing the other state's tax by credits that state allowed
- Expecting a refund from a nonrefundable credit
## Prompt
- I live in Georgia but paid income tax to another state.
- Does Georgia give credit for taxes I paid to South Carolina?
- I moved during the year and had income in two states.
