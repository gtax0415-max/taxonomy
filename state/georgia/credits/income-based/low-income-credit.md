# Low Income Credit (Georgia)
## Description
Nonrefundable credit for low-income Georgia filers, computed from a small fixed table by federal AGI and number of exemptions. It is Georgia's closest analogue to an earned income credit, but it is NOT a percentage of the federal EIC and it is NOT refundable — Georgia has no state earned income credit.
## Amount
Credit per exemption is read from the worksheet table by federal AGI:
- Under $6,000: $26 per exemption
- $6,000 but not more than $7,999: $20
- $8,000 but not more than $9,999: $14
- $10,000 but not more than $14,999: $8
- $15,000 but not more than $19,999: $5
Multiply the per-exemption figure (Worksheet Line 5) by the number of exemptions (Worksheet Line 4). Exemptions are self, spouse, and natural or legally adopted children, plus one additional exemption each for taxpayer and/or spouse age 65 or older.
## The income ceiling
You may claim the credit only if federal AGI is LESS THAN $20,000. At $20,000 or above the credit is zero — the table simply stops.
## Eligibility
- Federal adjusted gross income less than $20,000
- NOT claimed, or eligible to be claimed, as a dependent on another person's federal or Georgia return
- Part-year residents may claim it only if they were Georgia residents at the END of the tax year
- Married filing separately for a year in which a joint return could have been filed may claim only the credit a joint return would have allowed
- NOT available to an inmate in a correctional facility
## Cap and carryover
Nonrefundable. The credit cannot exceed the taxpayer's income tax liability, and there is no carryforward.
## Filing deadline
Must be claimed on or before the end of the 12th month following the close of the tax year, including on an amended return.
## Required Information
- Federal AGI (Form 500, Line 8)
- Number of exemptions
- Taxpayer and spouse dates of birth (Form 500, Line 6) for any age-65 exemptions
## Questions
- Is your federal adjusted gross income under $20,000?
- Can anyone else claim you as a dependent?
- If part-year, were you a Georgia resident on the last day of the year?
- Are you 65 or older, or is your spouse?
## Common Errors
- Expecting a refund from a nonrefundable credit
- Claiming it when someone else can claim you as a dependent
- Part-year residents claiming it without being resident at year-end
- Missing the extra age-65 exemptions because dates of birth were left off Line 6
- Confusing it with an earned income credit — Georgia has none
## Prompt
- I made under $20,000 last year in Georgia.
- Does Georgia have a credit for low-income filers?
- Is there a Georgia version of the earned income credit?
