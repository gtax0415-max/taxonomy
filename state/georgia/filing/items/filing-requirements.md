# Filing Requirements (Georgia)
## Description
Whether you must file a Georgia return depends on residency, whether a federal return is required, and whether income exceeds the Georgia standard deduction.
## Full-year residents — must file if
- You are required to file a Federal income tax return, OR
- You have income subject to Georgia income tax that is not subject to Federal income tax, OR
- Your income exceeds the standard deduction for your filing status
Full-year residents are taxed on ALL income (except tax-exempt income) regardless of source, even while temporarily absent from Georgia. A credit for taxes paid to another state is allowed.
## Standard-deduction filing thresholds (2026, HB 463)
- Married filing jointly: $30,000
- Single / MFS / head of household / qualifying surviving spouse: $15,000
(2025 thresholds were $24,000 / $12,000.)
## Nonresidents — must file if
- You work in Georgia or receive income from Georgia sources AND are required to file a Federal return
- EXCEPTION: legal residents of other states need not file if their only Georgia activity is employee services whose wages do not exceed the LESSER of 5% of total-everywhere income or $5,000
- Deferred compensation / stock-option income earned in Georgia in a prior year is taxable under the same 5%/$5,000 threshold, unless federal law bars state taxation (e.g., certain retirement income paid over life expectancy or 10+ years)
## Part-year residents and nonresidents
Must file Form 500 and complete Schedule 3 to calculate Georgia taxable income. Married part-year residents with a nonresident spouse may file separately (claiming their own exemptions/deductions) or jointly.
## Form 1040NR filers
Individuals required to file Federal Form 1040NR must file Georgia Form 500, generally may not take the standard deduction, and are allowed only the limited itemized deductions shown on Form 1040NR Schedule A.
## Military residents
Georgia legal residents in the military are taxed on all income regardless of source unless specifically exempt; those serving outside the continental U.S. may file within six months of returning, with no penalties or interest during that period.
## Required Information
- Federal filing requirement and status
- Residency status
- Georgia-source income (for nonresidents)
- Income vs the standard-deduction threshold
## Questions
- Are you required to file a federal return?
- Were you a Georgia resident, part-year resident, or nonresident?
- Did your income exceed the standard deduction for your status?
- If a nonresident, is your Georgia income above the 5%/$5,000 threshold?
## Common Errors
- Assuming no Georgia filing is needed when a federal return is required
- Nonresidents missing the 5%/$5,000 wage threshold analysis
- Using 2025 thresholds ($12,000/$24,000) for a 2026 return
## Prompt
- Do I have to file a Georgia tax return?
- I'm a nonresident with a little Georgia income — do I file?
- I'm in the military stationed overseas — when is my Georgia return due?
