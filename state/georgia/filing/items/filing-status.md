# Filing Status (Georgia)
## Description
Georgia recognizes five filing statuses, which set the standard deduction amount and certain rules. Georgia generally follows federal filing status.
## The five statuses and 2026 standard deduction
- Married filing jointly: $30,000
- Single: $15,000
- Married filing separately: $15,000
- Head of household: $15,000
- Qualifying surviving spouse: $15,000
## Key status rules
- Married filing separately: if one spouse itemizes, the other must itemize too; only one spouse may claim a given dependent (prior federal dependent-exemption rules determine who)
- Married part-year residents with a nonresident spouse may file separately or jointly
- The flat 4.99% rate (2026) applies regardless of status — status affects the deduction, not the rate
## Required Information
- Marital status at year-end
- Federal filing status
- For MFS, allocation of dependents between spouses
## Questions
- What is your filing status?
- Are you married filing jointly or separately?
- If MFS, does your spouse itemize? (then you must too)
## Common Errors
- Mixing standard and itemized between MFS spouses
- Both MFS spouses claiming the same dependent
- Assuming filing status changes the flat rate (it does not)
