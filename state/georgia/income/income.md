# Income and Adjustments (Georgia)
## Description
Georgia is a Federal-AGI-conformity state: the Georgia return STARTS with federal adjusted gross income (Form 500, Line 8), then adjusts it up or down on Schedule 1 for items Georgia and the federal government treat differently. The net of those adjustments lands on Form 500, Line 9, and Line 8 plus Line 9 equals Georgia adjusted gross income on Line 10. Because Georgia starts from federal AGI, anything already in federal AGI needs no restatement — only the differences are captured here.
## How the flow works
- Form 500, Line 8 = Federal adjusted gross income
- Schedule 1, Lines 1–6 = ADDITIONS to income (things federal AGI leaves out that Georgia taxes)
- Schedule 1, Lines 7–13 = SUBTRACTIONS from income (things federal AGI includes that Georgia does not tax)
- Schedule 1, Line 14 = net additions minus subtractions → carried to Form 500, Line 9
- Form 500, Line 10 = Line 8 + Line 9 = Georgia adjusted gross income
## Two folders
- additions/ — Schedule 1, Lines 1–6. Items ADDED back to federal AGI. See additions.md
- subtractions/ — Schedule 1, Lines 7–13. Items SUBTRACTED from federal AGI. See subtractions.md
## Filing mechanics
- Include the federal return (Form 1040 primary pages and federal Schedule 1) with the Georgia return; the federal Schedule 1 must be included if one was submitted federally
- A taxpayer with multiple additions (Schedule 1, Line 5) or multiple subtractions (Schedule 1, Line 12) must attach a separate worksheet listing every adjustment and enter only the total on those lines
- Part-year residents and nonresidents SKIP Form 500, Lines 9–14 and instead follow the Schedule 3 instructions — their adjustments run through Schedule 3 columns, not Line 9
## The flagship subtraction
The Retirement Income Exclusion is by far the largest and most-claimed Georgia subtraction (up to $65,000 per person at 65+, up to $35,000 at 62–64). It has its own multi-part calculation on Schedule 1, Page 2, and a separate Military Retirement Income Exclusion on Page 3. See subtractions/retirement-income-exclusion.md and subtractions/military-retirement-income-exclusion.md.
## Related
- Credits reduce tax after Georgia taxable income is computed — see the credits/ taxonomy
- Georgia does NOT allow the federal 20% qualified business income deduction (IRC 199A), but because Georgia starts from federal AGI (which is before QBI), no Schedule 1 adjustment is needed
- ABLE program contributions are NOT deductible as a Georgia subtraction (Georgia ABLE or any other state's)
## 2026 conformity and new items (HB 463)
- IRC conformity date updated to January 1, 2026
- Georgia DECOUPLED from the federal OBBBA "no tax on tips" and "no tax on overtime" deductions (IRC 224/225) and from federal car-loan-interest deductibility (IRC 163(h)(4)); instead Georgia enacted its own smaller, temporary $1,750 exclusions for overtime and cash tips (TY2026–2028) — see subtractions/overtime-compensation-exclusion.md and subtractions/cash-tips-exclusion.md
- Georgia retains the $10,000 SALT deduction cap (did not adopt the federal increase)
- Parameters OUTSIDE this Schedule 1 branch that changed for 2026: flat rate 4.99% (was 5.19%), standard deduction $15,000 single/$30,000 MFJ (was $12,000/$24,000), dependent exemption $5,000 (was $4,000). These belong to the tax-computation and deductions branches, not Schedule 1 additions/subtractions
