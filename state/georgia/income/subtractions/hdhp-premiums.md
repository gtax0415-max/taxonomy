# High Deductible Health Plan Premiums (Subtraction)
## Description
100% of premiums paid during the year for a high deductible health plan (as defined by IRC Section 223) may be subtracted, subject to anti-double-dipping rules. Georgia offers this to encourage HDHP/HSA-eligible coverage.
## Amount
- 100% of the premium paid during the taxable year for high deductible health plans defined by IRC Section 223
## Anti-double-dipping conditions
The subtraction is allowed only to the extent the amount:
- Has NOT already been excluded from federal AGI (e.g., paid pre-tax), AND
- Was NOT provided from a health reimbursement arrangement (HRA), AND
- Was NOT included in itemized deductions
If the premiums ARE claimed as itemized deductions, multiply the premium by the ratio of total allowed itemized deductions AFTER the federal limitation to total allowed itemized deductions BEFORE the federal limitation, to find the portion not allowed as this subtraction. (Booklet example: $1,000 HDHP premium within $8,000 total medical expenses → the $1,000 is reduced by $250, leaving $750 deductible here.)
## Eligibility / applicability
- You paid HDHP premiums with after-tax dollars during the year
## Required Information
- HDHP premium statements (IRC Section 223 plan)
- Confirmation the premiums were after-tax and not from an HRA
- Federal Schedule A, if the premiums were also itemized
## Questions
- Did you pay premiums for a high deductible health plan?
- Were they paid with after-tax dollars (not pre-tax or from an HRA)?
- Did you also itemize those premiums federally?
## Common Errors
- Subtracting pre-tax or employer-paid premiums
- Subtracting HRA-funded premiums
- Failing to apply the itemized-deduction ratio limitation when premiums were itemized
## Prompt
- I pay premiums for a high deductible health plan.
- Does Georgia give a deduction for HDHP premiums?
- I have an HSA-eligible plan.
