# Retirement Income Exclusion (Georgia)
## Description
The largest and most widely claimed Georgia income subtraction. Qualifying retirement income is excluded up to an age-based maximum, per taxpayer. This is what makes Georgia a favorable state for retirees — most pension, interest, dividend, and capital gain income of an older filer can fall inside the exclusion.
## Amount
- $35,000 maximum for taxpayers who are 62–64 years of age, OR under 62 and permanently disabled to an extent that they cannot perform any type of gainful employment
- $65,000 maximum for taxpayers 65 years of age or older
- Up to $5,000 of the maximum may be EARNED income; the remainder applies to unearned retirement income
## Year note (2026 → 2027)
For tax year 2026 the amounts are UNCHANGED from 2025: $35,000 (62–64) and $65,000 (65+). Under HB 463, the 65-and-older exclusion RISES to $70,000 beginning in tax year 2027. Confirm the applicable year's figure before filing.
## Per-spouse rule
The exclusion is available to the taxpayer AND spouse, but each must qualify on a separate basis. If both qualify, each may claim the full amount for their age. Income from jointly owned property is allocated 50% to each taxpayer; income or loss on individually owned items is allocated to the owner.
## What counts as retirement income
Unearned retirement income includes interest, dividends, capital gains, taxable IRA and pension distributions, rents and royalties, and similar. Up to $5,000 of earned income (wages, other earned) may also be excluded.
## What is NOT in the exclusion base
- Social Security and Railroad Retirement paid by the RRB (these are separately subtracted; do not include in the exclusion calculation)
- Exempt interest or other income not taxable to Georgia
- Income from lotteries, gambling, illegal sources, or similar
- Rental, royalty, or partnership income subject to FICA/self-employment tax goes on Line 2, not Line 13
- Trade or business income from an S corporation in which the taxpayer or spouse materially participated goes on Line 2, not Line 13
## Part-year and nonresident proration
Part-year residents and nonresidents MUST prorate. The earned and unearned portions are prorated separately: earned by the ratio of Georgia-source earned income to total earned income (computed as if a full-year resident); unearned by the ratio of Georgia-source unearned retirement income to total unearned retirement income (same basis).
## Required Information
- Date of birth for each taxpayer claiming (REQUIRED)
- Date of disability, if claiming on the disability basis
- Retirement income by type and by owner
- Schedule 3 figures, for part-year/nonresident proration
## Questions
- Are you (or your spouse) 62 or older, or under 62 and permanently disabled?
- What types of retirement income do you receive, and who owns each?
- Is any of it Social Security or Railroad Retirement (excluded from this calculation)?
- Are you a full-year, part-year, or nonresident filer?
## Common Errors
- Including Social Security or Railroad Retirement in the exclusion base
- Omitting the required date of birth, which invalidates the exclusion
- Claiming one spouse's unused exclusion against the other spouse's income
- Putting materially-participated S-corp or FICA-taxed rental income on Line 13 instead of Line 2
- Failing to prorate as a part-year or nonresident filer

- I'm retired in Georgia — does the state tax my pension?
- My spouse and I are both over 65.
