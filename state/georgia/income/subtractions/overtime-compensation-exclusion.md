# Overtime Compensation Exclusion (Georgia — 2026, NEW)
## Description
NEW for tax year 2026 under HB 463 (Georgia Economic Growth and Tax Relief Act of 2026). Georgia allows a temporary exclusion of a portion of qualified overtime compensation from Georgia taxable income. Georgia decoupled from the federal OBBBA "no tax on overtime" provision and instead enacted its own capped exclusion, so the Georgia amount is smaller and separately computed.
## Amount
- Up to $1,750 of qualified overtime compensation may be excluded
- Temporary: available for tax years 2026, 2027, and 2028 only (automatic repeal thereafter)
- The amount may be excluded only to the extent it is included in federal AGI
## Statutory basis and status
- HB 463 (2026), signed May 11, 2026, effective for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2026
- Georgia does NOT follow the federal IRC Section 225 no-tax-on-overtime deduction; this Georgia exclusion is a separate, reduced amount
- Employer reporting: HB 463 provides for employer reporting of qualified overtime; DOR rules and the 2026 IT-511 booklet will specify the Schedule 1 line and documentation
## Eligibility / applicability
- You received qualified overtime compensation that was included in federal AGI, in tax year 2026, 2027, or 2028
## Required Information
- Employer-reported qualified overtime compensation amount
- Pay records identifying the overtime portion
- The amount included in federal AGI
## Questions
- Did you earn overtime pay in 2026?
- Did your employer report the qualified overtime amount?
- Was the overtime included in your federal AGI?
## Common Errors
- Applying the larger federal OBBBA overtime figure instead of Georgia's $1,750 cap
- Claiming it for a year outside the 2026–2028 window
- Excluding overtime that was never in federal AGI
## Prompt

- I heard Georgia has a new overtime exclusion.
- Can I exclude overtime like the federal no-tax-on-overtime rule?
