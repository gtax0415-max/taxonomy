# Military Retirement Income Exclusion (Georgia)
## Description
A separate exclusion specifically for military retirement income of taxpayers UNDER age 62, computed on Schedule 1, Page 3. It stacks a base exclusion with an additional amount unlocked by earned income, and is distinct from the general Retirement Income Exclusion (which covers taxpayers 62+).
## Amount
- Up to $17,500 of military retirement income may be excluded for taxpayers under 62
- An ADDITIONAL $17,500 may be excluded for such taxpayers with more than $17,500 of earned income in Georgia (up to $35,000 total)
## Per-spouse rule
Available to the taxpayer and spouse; each must qualify on a separate basis. If both qualify, each may claim the amounts.
## Part-year and nonresident rule
Part-year residents and nonresidents may claim the $17,500 base exclusion against total military retirement income received, but may only claim the ADDITIONAL $17,500 after meeting the earned-income threshold with GEORGIA-SOURCE earned income.
## Relationship to the general exclusion
- Under 62: use this military exclusion (Schedule 1, Page 3)
- 62 or older: military retirement income is generally handled under the general Retirement Income Exclusion instead — confirm the current-year Schedule 1 routing
## Required Information
- 1099-R showing military retirement income
- Date of birth (to confirm the under-62 basis)
- Georgia-source earned income (to unlock the additional $17,500)
## Questions
- Do you receive military retirement pay?
- Are you under 62?
- Do you have more than $17,500 of earned income (Georgia-source if part-year/nonresident)?
## Common Errors
- Claiming the additional $17,500 without meeting the earned-income threshold
- Part-year/nonresident filers using total (not Georgia-source) earned income for the additional amount
- Double-counting the same military pension under both this and the general exclusion
## Prompt
- I'm a military retiree under 62 in Georgia.
- Does Georgia tax military retirement pay?
- I get a military pension and also work.
