# U.S. Government Interest and Dividends (Subtraction)
## Description
Interest and dividends on U.S. Government bonds and other U.S. obligations are taxable federally but exempt from Georgia tax, so they are subtracted. This must be reduced by attributable interest expenses.
## What is subtracted
- Interest and dividends on U.S. Government bonds and other U.S. obligations, reduced by direct and indirect interest expenses attributable to the income
## What does NOT qualify
Interest from the Federal National Mortgage Association (Fannie Mae), Government National Mortgage Association (Ginnie Mae), and Federal Home Loan Mortgage Association (Freddie Mac), and/or from a repurchase agreement, is TAXABLE and must NOT be subtracted.
## Eligibility / applicability
- You received interest on direct U.S. obligations (e.g., Treasury bills, notes, bonds, savings bonds)
## Required Information
- 1099-INT/brokerage statements identifying U.S. obligation interest
- Mutual fund breakdown of the U.S. government portion
- Attributable interest expenses
## Questions
- Do you hold U.S. Treasury securities or savings bonds?
- Does a bond fund of yours report a U.S. government interest percentage?
- Any of it from Fannie/Ginnie/Freddie or repos (which do NOT qualify)?
## Common Errors
- Subtracting Fannie Mae/Ginnie Mae/Freddie Mac or repo interest, which is taxable
- Failing to reduce by attributable interest expense
- Missing the U.S. government portion inside a mutual fund
## Prompt
- I have Treasury bond interest.
- Does Georgia tax interest on U.S. savings bonds?
- My bond fund holds government securities.
