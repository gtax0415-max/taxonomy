# Pass-Through Entity Tax Paid to Another State (Subtraction)
## Description
A Georgia resident may subtract the portion of pass-through entity income that was taxed at the ENTITY level by ANOTHER state — where that state does not recognize the Subchapter S election, or taxes partnerships/LLCs at the entity level. This prevents Georgia from taxing income another state already taxed at the entity level. Income subject to the Texas Margin Tax qualifies.
## Who it applies to
- Shareholders of an S corporation whose S election is not recognized by Georgia or another state
- Partners in a partnership or members in an LLC whose income was taxed at the entity level by another state
## Amount — how it is calculated
1. Determine the Georgia taxable net income received from the entity (income, gains, losses, deductions separately reported and included on your return, including guaranteed payments; NOT wages paid to you)
2. Multiply that income by the entity's apportionment ratio in the other state
The subtraction is allowed only for the portion of income on which tax was actually paid by the entity to the other state.
## When it does NOT apply
- If the other state recognizes the Subchapter S election, do not subtract; the other state(s) tax credit may apply instead
- Wages paid to the owner are excluded; if wages are taxed by another state, the other state(s) tax credit may apply
## Statutory basis
Code Section 48-7-27(d); Regulation 560-7-4-.01. Texas Margin Tax income is eligible.
## Required Information
- The entity K-1
- The other state's entity-level return and apportionment ratio
- Documentation of non-recognition of the S election or entity-level taxation
## Questions
- Do you own an S corporation, partnership, or LLC that paid tax at the entity level in another state?
- Does that state recognize the S election?
- Is any of it Texas Margin Tax income?
## Common Errors
- Subtracting when the other state recognizes the S election (use the credit instead)
- Including owner wages in the subtraction base
- Subtracting more than the income actually taxed at the entity level
## Prompt
- My S corp paid tax at the entity level in another state.
- I have Texas Margin Tax income from my LLC.
- Does Georgia tax income another state already taxed at the entity level?
