# First Responder Benefit Exclusions (Subtraction)
## Description
Three related subtractions shield benefits paid to first responders (and certain survivors) from Georgia tax, each to the extent the amount was included in federal AGI and is not otherwise exempt.
## What is subtracted
1. Disabled first responder indemnification — 100% of payments made to and received by a disabled first responder under O.C.G.A. Section 45-9-85 (indemnification for death or disability). "Disabled first responders" means a law enforcement officer, fireman, publicly employed emergency medical technician, or a surviving spouse of such an individual, receiving payments due to total permanent disability, organic brain damage, or death occurring in the line of duty.
2. First responder PTSD benefits — insurance benefit payments for occupational PTSD received by a first responder, to the extent included in federal AGI.
3. Firefighter cancer benefits — for a firefighter, payments received under O.C.G.A. Section 25-3-23(b)(2) (certain cancer-related insurance benefits), to the extent included in federal AGI and not otherwise exempt.
## Eligibility / applicability
- You are a first responder (or qualifying surviving spouse) receiving one of the three benefit types, and the amount reached federal AGI
## Required Information
- Documentation of the specific benefit (indemnification, PTSD insurance, or firefighter cancer benefit)
- Confirmation the amount was included in federal AGI and is not otherwise exempt
## Questions
- Are you a first responder or surviving spouse receiving a disability or indemnification payment?
- Did you receive insurance benefits for occupational PTSD?
- Are you a firefighter who received cancer-related insurance benefits?
## Common Errors
- Subtracting amounts that were never in federal AGI or are already otherwise exempt
- Applying the disabled-first-responder subtraction to payments outside O.C.G.A. Section 45-9-85
## Prompt
- I'm a first responder receiving disability indemnification.
- I got insurance benefits for job-related PTSD as a first responder.
- I'm a firefighter who received cancer benefits.
