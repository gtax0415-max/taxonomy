# Student Loan Deductions (Schedule Y, Lines 10, 12 and 18b)
## Line 10 — Student Loan Interest Deduction
Same as the federal deduction (U.S. Schedule 1, Line 21), not to exceed $2,500, including the federal income phase-out.
## Line 12 — Undergraduate Student Loan Interest Deduction
Massachusetts-only. ALL interest paid on a qualified UNDERGRADUATE loan, with no dollar cap and no income limit, if:
- The loan was administered by the financial aid office of a two- or four-year college where the taxpayer or a dependent was an undergraduate
- It came through a state or federal student loan program or a commercial lender
- Proceeds were spent solely on tuition and directly related enrollment expenses
Cannot be claimed for the same interest deducted on Line 10.
## Line 18b — Student Loan Repayment Assistance Deduction
Deduction for employer payments of an employee's qualified education loan principal or interest (IRC 221 loans), to the extent not already excluded from federal gross income. For 2025 the instructions direct employees to enter the amount above $5,250.
2026 caution: P.L. 119-21 made the federal $5,250 exclusion (IRC 127) permanent, but Massachusetts does NOT adopt that change (TIR 26-4) and follows the Code as of January 1, 2024, under which the exclusion for loan payments ended after 2025. How 2026 payments are split between Massachusetts wages and the Line 18b deduction is pending DOR guidance and the 2026 Schedule Y instructions.
## Questions
- Do you pay interest on student loans? Were they undergraduate loans through the college's financial aid office?
- Did your employer pay any of your student loans?
## Common Errors
- Claiming the same interest on both Line 10 and Line 12
- Missing Line 12 when the federal deduction phases out
## Prompt
- I pay student loan interest but make too much for the federal deduction.
- My employer pays part of my student loans.
