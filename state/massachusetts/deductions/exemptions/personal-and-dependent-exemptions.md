# Personal, Dependent, Age 65 and Blindness Exemptions
## Description
Fixed-dollar exemptions that reduce 5.0% Part B income. They play the role of the federal standard deduction and are NOT indexed for inflation.
## Amounts
- Personal (Line 2a): $4,400 single or married filing separately; $6,800 head of household; $8,800 married filing jointly
- Dependents (Line 2b): $1,000 each, for dependents claimed on the U.S. return (not yourself or your spouse)
- Age 65 or over (Line 2c): $700 each for taxpayer and spouse, if 65 BEFORE January 1, 2027 (for tax year 2026)
- Blindness (Line 2d): $2,200 each; visual acuity 20/200 or less in the better eye with correction, or field of vision 10 degrees or less
## Excess exemptions
If total exemptions (Line 18) exceed 5.0% income after deductions (Line 17), the excess can reduce, in order: interest and dividends and 8.5%/12% income (Schedule B, Line 36), then long-term capital gains (Schedule D, Line 20). Only single, head of household and joint filers. Fill in the excess-exemption oval on Line 24.
## Notes
- The dependent exemption ($1,000) is separate from the $440 Child and Family Tax Credit
- Exemption figures also drive No Tax Status and the Limited Income Credit thresholds — credits/income-based/limited-income-credit.md
- Schedule DI is required for every dependent
## Questions
- What is your filing status?
- How many dependents did you claim federally?
- Were you or your spouse 65 or older by December 31, 2026? Legally blind?
## Common Errors
- Claiming a dependent not claimed on the federal return
- Using the age-65 amount for someone who turned 65 on or after January 1, 2027
- Married filing separately using excess exemptions
