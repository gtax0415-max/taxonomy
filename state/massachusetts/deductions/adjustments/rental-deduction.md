# Rental Deduction
## Description
Massachusetts deduction for 50% of rent paid for the taxpayer's PRINCIPAL RESIDENCE in Massachusetts, up to $4,000 per return (raised from $3,000 beginning 2023; not indexed).
## Calculation
Line 14a: total qualified rent paid in 2026. Line 14 = lesser of 14a ÷ 2 or $4,000 ($2,000 married filing separately).
## Qualifying rent
- Paid to a landlord for the rental or lease of the principal residence in Massachusetts
- Co-tenants each deduct based on the rent each actually paid
## NOT qualifying
- Vacation residences; housing for temporary assignment; students or faculty with a principal residence elsewhere
- A nonresident's Massachusetts apartment
- Hotels, motels, rooming houses unless under a rental agreement
- Cooperative housing payments by tenant-stockholders; condominium association fees
- Separately stated utilities, furnishings or parking
- Security deposits and last month's rent until actually applied as rent
- Rent paid by a third party who maintains a principal residence elsewhere
## Notes
- Renters 65+ may also qualify for the Senior Circuit Breaker based on 25% of rent — see credits/senior/senior-circuit-breaker-credit.md
## Required Information
- Total rent paid in 2026 for the Massachusetts principal residence, excluding utilities and parking
- Landlord name and address
## Questions
- Do you rent your Massachusetts home? How much rent did you pay?
- Does the rent include utilities or parking that are billed separately?
- Did anyone else pay your rent?
## Common Errors
- Deducting the full rent instead of 50%
- Including separately stated parking or utilities
- A student deducting rent while maintaining a principal residence elsewhere
## Prompt
- I rent an apartment in Massachusetts. Is there a deduction?
