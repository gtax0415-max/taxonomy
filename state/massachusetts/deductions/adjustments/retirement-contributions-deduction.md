# Deduction for Social Security, Medicare, Railroad, U.S. or Massachusetts Retirement Contributions
## Description
Massachusetts lets each taxpayer deduct up to $2,000 of amounts paid into Social Security (FICA), Medicare, Railroad Retirement, or the U.S. or Massachusetts retirement systems. Nearly every W-2 employee hits the $2,000 cap.
## What counts
- Social Security tax withheld (W-2 Box 4)
- Medicare tax withheld (W-2 Box 6)
- Self-employment tax (U.S. Schedule SE)
- Railroad retirement tax
- Contributions to U.S. or Massachusetts public retirement systems (Massachusetts state and municipal employees' contributions are taxed in Box 16 wages and deducted here)
## What does NOT count
- IRA, Keogh, SEP, SIMPLE contributions
- 401(k), 403(b), 457 deferrals (already excluded from wages)
- Medicare premiums withheld from benefits
## Limit
$2,000 per person, not indexed. A joint return can deduct up to $4,000 only if EACH spouse paid at least $2,000.
## Questions
- Did you (and your spouse) each have W-2 or self-employment income?
- Are you a Massachusetts public employee contributing to a pension?
## Common Errors
- Combining spouses' amounts to reach $4,000 when one spouse paid less than $2,000
- Including IRA contributions
## Prompt
- Can I deduct my Social Security tax in Massachusetts?
