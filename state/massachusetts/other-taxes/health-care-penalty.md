# Massachusetts Health Care Penalty (Schedule HC)
## Description
Massachusetts requires most residents 18 and older who can AFFORD health insurance to maintain coverage meeting MINIMUM CREDITABLE COVERAGE (MCC). Those who do not may owe a monthly penalty through the income tax return. Every full-year resident (and part-year residents in Massachusetts three or more full months) must file Schedule HC, even with full coverage. This is the Massachusetts counterpart to the federal health-insurance category — but it is a PENALTY, never a credit.
## Minimum Creditable Coverage
Automatically met by: Medicare Part A or B; any Qualified Health Plan from the Health Connector or a carrier (including ConnectorCare and catastrophic plans); MassHealth (not MassHealth Limited); qualified student health plans; Indian Health Service or tribal coverage; TRICARE; VA health care; federal employee plans; Peace Corps, VISTA, AmeriCorps, NCCC coverage.
Other plans must meet MCC standards, including an annual deductible cap:
| MCC dollar limit | 2026 (individual / family) | 2025, for reference |
|---|---|---|
| Annual deductible cap (in-network) | $3,200 / $6,400 | $2,950 / $5,900 |
| Separate prescription drug deductible cap | $400 / $800 | $360 / $720 |
| Out-of-pocket maximum | $10,150 / $20,300 | $9,200 / $18,400 |
Source: Health Connector Administrative Bulletin 01-25 (2026 MCC limits). The Health Connector board kept the deductible limits unchanged for 2027 (Administrative Bulletin 01-26). The Health Safety Net is NOT insurance and does NOT meet MCC. Short-term plans, health-sharing ministries and fixed-indemnity plans generally do not meet MCC.
## Who the mandate covers, and when
- Turning 18: starts the first day of the third month after the birthday
- Moving in: starts the first day of the third month after becoming a resident
- Moving out: ends the last day of the last full month as a resident
- Death: ends the last day of the last full month alive
- For 2026, taxpayers born on or after October 1, 2008 complete only Schedule HC Line 1 (the mandate does not start for them until 2027)
- No penalty for a lapse of 63 days or less (the Health Connector treats this as three calendar months)
## 2026 penalty schedule (TIR 26-1)
Per adult, per month without MCC. Married couples pay the sum of each spouse's penalty.
| Income (% of FPL) | Monthly | Full year |
|---|---|---|
| 150% or less | $0 | $0 |
| 150.1-200% | $26 | $312 |
| 200.1-250% | $51 | $612 |
| 250.1-300% | $76 | $912 |
| 300.1-400% | $117 | $1,404 |
| Above 400% | $211 | $2,532 |
For 2026, ConnectorCare eligibility ends at 400% FPL (in 2025 a pilot extended it to 500%), so the penalty tiers changed: 2025 had six columns, the top at $187/month above 500% FPL.

## Schedule HC flow
1. Line 1: dates of birth; family size
2. Line 2: federal AGI (married filing separately and living together: combine both spouses)
3. Line 3: Full-year MCC / Part-year MCC / No MCC
4. Line 4: type of coverage (private incl. ConnectorCare, MassHealth, Medicare, military, other government); attach Forms MA 1099-HC
5. Line 5: full-year MCC, or Medicare/military/other government at any point → STOP, no penalty
6. Line 6: at or below 150% FPL → STOP, no penalty
7. Line 7: months covered; only a gap of FOUR OR MORE consecutive months continues
8. Line 8: religious exemption (no medical care received → no penalty)
9. Line 9: Health Connector Certificate of Exemption → no penalty
10. Lines 10-12: affordability — employer coverage (Line 10), government-subsidized eligibility (Line 11), private coverage (Line 12) using the affordability and regional premium tables
11. Penalty Worksheet: monthly amount × (uninsured months − 3 per gap) → Form 1, Line 35a/35b

## Regional premium table (Schedule HC Table 4)
The lowest-cost premiums by region, age and filing status are set yearly and will appear in the 2026 Schedule HC instructions. Do not reuse the 2025 premium table.
## Government-subsidized coverage test (Line 11) for 2026
ConnectorCare eligibility ends at 400% FPL in 2026 (it was 500% in 2025), so the Line 11 income ceiling uses the 400% column above. A taxpayer under the ceiling is still treated as ineligible if an employer offered individual coverage meeting minimum value that cost less than the federal affordability percentage of household income (9.02% in 2025; 9.96% for 2026 under IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-25), if the taxpayer was not lawfully present, or if MassHealth/ConnectorCare denied the application.
## Appeals
Taxpayers may appeal to the Health Connector on hardship grounds (homelessness or arrears, utility shutoff, unexpected essential expenses from domestic violence, death, caregiving or disaster, serious deprivation, inequitable family size, coverage close to MCC, employer offered only non-MCC coverage) or other circumstances. Fill in the appeal oval; do NOT enter the penalty; do NOT enclose documentation with the return. Only one appeal opportunity.

## Federal link
There is no federal penalty since 2019. The federal premium tax credit (federal/credits/health-insurance/premium-tax-credit.md) is unrelated to Schedule HC, but the 2026 end of enhanced federal subsidies may push more Massachusetts residents above affordability limits.
## Required Information
- Forms MA 1099-HC for each insurer
- Federal AGI and family size
- Months of coverage and type of plan
- Employer's lowest-cost premium offer during uninsured months
- County of residence and age (for the premium table)
- Certificate of Exemption number, if any
## Questions
- Were you insured all of 2026? Did the plan meet Massachusetts MCC?
- If not, how many months in a row were you uninsured?
- What was your federal AGI and household size?
- Did your employer offer coverage, and at what monthly cost?
- Do you have a Health Connector Certificate of Exemption?
## Common Errors
- Not filing Schedule HC because you were fully insured
- Treating the Health Safety Net or a health-sharing ministry as insurance
- Using Massachusetts income instead of federal AGI
- Counting months when the mandate did not apply
- Entering the penalty while also appealing
## Prompt
- Do I have to file Schedule HC?
- I was uninsured for five months. Will Massachusetts penalize me?
- My insurance is through a health-sharing ministry.
