# Nonresident and Part-Year Resident Returns (Form 1-NR/PY)
## Description
Form 1-NR/PY is the Massachusetts return for nonresidents with Massachusetts-source income and for anyone who moved into or out of Massachusetts during the year. It follows the same structure as Form 1, with income sourcing and proration added.
## What is taxed
- PART-YEAR RESIDENT: all income received while a resident, plus Massachusetts-source income received while a nonresident
- NONRESIDENT: Massachusetts-source income only — wages for work performed in Massachusetts, Massachusetts business and rental income, gains on Massachusetts real estate, Massachusetts lottery and gaming winnings. Generally not interest, dividends or gains on intangibles
## Proration
- Personal and dependent exemptions: part-year by days of residency ÷ 365; nonresident by Massachusetts income ÷ total income
- Rental deduction: only for rent while a resident (nonresidents get none)
- Alimony paid: nonresidents multiply by the Line 14g ratio; part-year residents use amounts paid while a resident
- Schedule Y items must relate to income on Form 1-NR/PY
## Schedules
- Schedule R/NR — Resident/Nonresident Worksheet for someone who was both a part-year resident and a nonresident in the same year
- Schedule NTS-L-NR/PY — No Tax Status and Limited Income Credit for 1-NR/PY filers (thresholds measured on income while a resident plus Massachusetts-source income)
- Schedule HC — required for part-year residents who were residents three or more full months; mandate months begin the third month after moving in and end the last full month before moving out
## Credits
- Earned Income Credit: prorated; nonresidents not eligible
- Senior Circuit Breaker: Form 1-NR/PY, Line 48; residents and part-year residents only
- Other-jurisdiction credit: generally for resident periods
- Septic credit: a seller who moves out can still claim it on the 1-NR/PY for the year of sale
## Key line references on Form 1-NR/PY (2025)
Line 12 total 5.0% income; Line 14g income ratio; Line 19 Schedule Y; Line 24 interest and dividends; Line 27a 8.5% income; Line 28 long-term gains; Line 32 total tax (32a/32b); Lines 39a/39b health care penalty; Line 42c other withholding; Line 48 Circuit Breaker; Line 54 real estate (NRW) withholding; Line 56 overpayment applied; M-2210 amount under Line 59.
## 2026 notes
- Nonresident sellers of Massachusetts real estate at $1,000,000 or more may have withholding on Form NRW (closings on or after November 1, 2025) — see payments/withholding.md
- The 4% surtax applies to nonresidents' Massachusetts-source taxable income over $1,107,750
## Questions
- On what date did you move into or out of Massachusetts?
- How many days did you work in Massachusetts vs. elsewhere?
- Did you sell Massachusetts property as a nonresident?
## Common Errors
- Taxing all income of a nonresident
- Using full exemptions without proration
- A part-year resident filing Form 1
- Claiming the rental deduction for rent paid while a nonresident
## Prompt
- I moved from Massachusetts to Florida in July.
- I live in New Hampshire and work in Boston.
