# Filing Status (Form 1, Line 1)
## Description
Massachusetts has four filing statuses: single, married filing jointly, married filing separately, and head of household. There is no separate qualifying surviving spouse status.
## Statuses
- SINGLE: unmarried on December 31, widow(er) whose spouse died before the year, legally separated under a final probate judgment, or divorced by a judgment not yet final. Not single merely because of a temporary support order or living apart
- MARRIED FILING JOINTLY: legally married on December 31; allowed if only one spouse had income or one spouse died during the year; both sign and both are liable
- MARRIED FILING SEPARATELY: married and not filing jointly; enter spouse's name and SSN
- HEAD OF HOUSEHOLD: same qualification as federal; list the qualifying person on Schedule DI; fill in the custodial-parent oval if you released a dependency exemption (Form 8332)
## Consistent joint filing requirement (since 2024; TIR 24-4)
Couples filing a joint federal return must file a joint Massachusetts return — except where one spouse's Massachusetts gross income does not exceed $8,000 (fill in the joint filing exemption oval).
## What married filing separately costs in Massachusetts
- No Earned Income Credit (narrow exceptions), no Child and Family Tax Credit, no Senior Circuit Breaker
- No No Tax Status or Limited Income Credit
- No use of excess exemptions against interest, dividends and capital gains
- Rental deduction capped at $2,000
- 4% surtax threshold applies per return (one threshold each) — but the consistent filing rule now prevents splitting when filing jointly federally
## Questions
- Were you married on December 31, 2026?
- Did you file jointly on your federal return?
- Does either spouse have Massachusetts gross income of $8,000 or less?
- Is your spouse a nonresident alien?
## Common Errors
- Filing separately in Massachusetts after filing jointly federally without an exception
- Using qualifying surviving spouse
## Prompt
- Can we file separately in Massachusetts if we filed jointly federally?
