# Credit for Income Tax Due to Other Jurisdictions (Schedule OJC)
## Description
Nonrefundable credit for income tax due to another state, a U.S. territory or dependency, or Canada and its provinces, on income that Massachusetts also taxes. Generally a RESIDENT credit — the resident state gives the credit, the source state taxes first.
## The limitation
The credit is the SMALLER of:
1. The tax due to the other jurisdiction on income also reported on the Massachusetts return (before credits and payments), or
2. The Massachusetts tax attributable to that income
## Line 30 Worksheet (5.0% income)
1. 5.0% income included in Form 1, Line 10 that was taxed elsewhere
2. Total Form 1, Line 10
3. Line 1 ÷ Line 2 (not more than 1)
4. Form 1, Line 19 × 0.05 (use 0.0585 if electing the optional 5.85% rate)
5. Limited Income Credit (Form 1, Line 29)
6. Line 4 minus Line 5
7. Line 6 × Line 3
8. Tax due to the other jurisdiction on the same income (Canada: net of the federal foreign tax credit)
9. Smaller of Line 7 or Line 8 → Form 1, Line 30
## Separate worksheets
- Interest (other than Massachusetts bank interest), dividends, and capital gains taxed elsewhere need their OWN worksheet, substituting Schedule B Line 7, Schedule B Line 13c, or Schedule D Line 13 for Form 1 Line 10, and the corresponding tax lines for Line 19 × 0.05
- Combine the results of all worksheets for the total Line 30 credit
## 2026: 4% surtax
Residents subject to the 4% surtax (taxable income over $1,107,750 for 2026; 2025: $1,083,150) must use DOR's online Schedule OJC worksheet(s) so the credit accounts for the surtax.
## Eligible jurisdictions
- Other U.S. states, including payments under the Rhode Island Temporary Disability Insurance Act (DOR Directive 12-1)
- U.S. territories and dependencies, including Puerto Rico, the Virgin Islands, Guam and the District of Columbia
- Canada and its provinces (postal code "FC" on Schedule OJC), reduced by any U.S. foreign tax credit
## Schedule OJC itself
One row per jurisdiction: two-letter postal code ("FC" for Canada, a province, or a U.S. territory or dependency), amount of income on which tax was paid, and total tax due BEFORE credits, W-2 withholding and payments. Prerequisite: you must have FILED a return and PAID tax in the other jurisdiction.
## Not eligible
- Tax paid to the U.S. government
- City or local income taxes
- Foreign countries other than Canada
- Property tax on capital stock or property (but tax on GAIN from selling stock or property is eligible)
- Interest and penalties paid to the other jurisdiction
## Pass-through entity tax interaction
If an entity paid an elective PTE tax to another state on your behalf, see DOR Directive 19-1 for how that affects the Massachusetts credit. Massachusetts's own PTE excise credit is separate — see business/pass-through-entity-excise-credit.md.
## Notes
- The other state's tax is the tax DUE after its own computation, not the amount withheld
- New Hampshire has no wage tax, so commuters to New Hampshire generally have nothing to credit
- Nonresidents and part-year residents file Form 1-NR/PY; the credit rules differ by period of residency
## Required Information
- Copy of each other-state return and the tax due
- Income taxed by each jurisdiction, separated into 5.0% income vs. interest/dividends/capital gains
- Federal foreign tax credit, if Canadian tax is involved
- Whether the 4% surtax applies
## Questions
- Which states (or Canada) taxed your income, and how much tax was due there?
- What kind of income was it — wages, business income, or investment income?
- Are you subject to the Massachusetts 4% surtax?
- Did you take a federal foreign tax credit for Canadian tax?
## Common Errors
- Using the other state's WITHHOLDING instead of the tax due on its return
- Combining wage income and investment income on one worksheet
- Claiming city or local taxes
- Claiming taxes paid to a foreign country other than Canada
- Not reducing Canadian tax by the federal foreign tax credit (double benefit)
- Surtax filers using the paper worksheet instead of the online Schedule OJC worksheet
- Forgetting to enter the two-letter postal code on Schedule OJC
## Prompt
- I live in Massachusetts and work in Rhode Island.
- I paid Connecticut tax on my rental property there.
- I paid tax in Canada.
