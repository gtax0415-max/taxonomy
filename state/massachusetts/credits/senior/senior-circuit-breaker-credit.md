# Senior Circuit Breaker Credit (Schedule CB)
## Description
Fully refundable Massachusetts credit for taxpayers 65 or older whose property tax (or 25% of rent) exceeds 10% of their total income. It "trips the circuit" when housing costs are high relative to income. Purely a state credit — no federal counterpart.
## How it works
CREDIT = housing cost − 10% of qualifying income, capped at the annual maximum.
- HOMEOWNER housing cost = real estate tax paid − adjustments (abatements, exemptions, late interest, betterments) + 50% of water and sewer use charges
- RENTER housing cost = 25% of rent paid (treated as the real estate tax portion of rent)

## Key eligibility rules
- AGE: you, or your spouse if filing jointly, must be at least 65 BEFORE January 1, 2027 (for tax year 2026)
- FILING STATUS: single, head of household, or married filing jointly. Married filing separately is NOT eligible
- RESIDENCY: Massachusetts resident or part-year resident; nonresidents are not eligible
- Must own or rent and OCCUPY the Massachusetts property as principal residence (no PO box)
- INCOME LIMIT is a CLIFF: one dollar over and the credit is lost entirely
- ASSESSED VALUE CEILING (homeowners) is also a cliff
- RENTERS: not eligible if receiving any federal or state rent subsidy, or renting from a tax-exempt entity
## Qualifying income is broader than taxable income
Schedule CB Line 9 = Massachusetts AGI (Schedule CB Line 3 worksheet) + FULL Social Security benefits before Medicare premiums + pensions, annuities, IRA and Keogh distributions not taxed in Massachusetts (e.g., public pensions) + miscellaneous income including cash public assistance − Form 1 Lines 2b-2d exemptions (dependents, age 65, blindness).
The Line 3 worksheet adds back items Massachusetts excludes, including tax-exempt interest, U.S. Treasury interest, and long-term capital gains before losses.
## Notes
- Line 17 / Line 21: credit is the LESSER of the computed amount or the maximum
- Over-one-acre property: only the residence plus up to one acre of surrounding land counts; use the Line 10 worksheet to prorate the tax
- Joint owners: only your own proportional share of the tax paid counts
- Abatements and exemptions REDUCE the tax counted, even if already reflected on the bill (Line 11 worksheet)
- Late-payment interest and betterments/special assessments do NOT count as real estate tax
- Full-year residents who filed before may file Form 1 and claim the credit free on MassTaxConnect
- A senior with NO filing requirement must still file Form 1 to receive the refund
## Required Information
- Date of birth (taxpayer and spouse)
- Homeowner or renter; principal residence address
- Homeowners: January 1 assessed value, real estate tax paid in 2026, water/sewer charges, abatements, exemptions, betterments, acreage and land value if over one acre
- Renters: total rent paid in 2026, landlord name and address, any subsidy
- Full Social Security benefits, nontaxed pensions, IRA distributions, public assistance
- Massachusetts AGI components
## Questions
- Were you or your spouse 65 or older by December 31, 2026?
- Do you own or rent your Massachusetts principal residence?
- How much real estate tax (or rent) did you pay in 2026?
- Did you receive any rent subsidy, or is your landlord tax-exempt?
- What was your total income, including all Social Security and nontaxed pensions?
- What is the assessed value of your home as of January 1, 2026?
## Common Errors
- Using taxable Social Security instead of the FULL benefit before Medicare premiums
- Omitting nontaxed public pensions from qualifying income
- Counting the full property tax on land over one acre
- Not subtracting abatements and exemptions
- Claiming it while married filing separately
- Renters in subsidized housing claiming it
- Seniors with no filing requirement never filing and leaving the refund unclaimed
