# Lead Paint Credit (Schedule LP)
## Description
Nonrefundable Massachusetts credit for an OWNER of residential premises who removes or covers lead paint, plaster or other materials containing dangerous levels of lead (Massachusetts Lead Law, G.L. c. 111, § 197). Purely a state credit — no federal counterpart.
## Amounts (since tax year 2023; unchanged for 2026)
| Type | Calculation | Maximum per dwelling unit |
|---|---|---|
| Interim control (emergency lead management plan) | 50% of qualified cost | $1,000 |
| Full compliance deleading | Qualified cost, minus prior interim control credit on the unit | $3,000 total, including any interim credit |
## Schedule LP flow
- Part 1, per unit: address, risk assessor license, compliance/payment date, cost (col. d), 50% (col. e), lesser of col. e or $1,000 (col. f) → Line 4
- Part 2, per unit: address, inspector license, date, cost (col. d), lesser of cost or $3,000 (col. e), minus any interim control credit from Part 1 or 2018-2025 Schedules LP (col. f) → Line 4
- Line 5: total current credit; Line 6: prior-year unused (2025 Schedule LP, Line 11, col. c); Line 7: available
- Line 8: Form 1, Line 28 tax, less Limited Income Credit, other-jurisdiction credit, and other credits claimed
- Line 9: smaller of Line 7 or Line 8 → Schedule CMS
- Part 4: carryover ledger by year of origin
## Timing
The credit is claimed in the taxable year compliance is certified OR the year payment is made, WHICHEVER IS LATER.
## Carryforward
7 years. For 2026: unused 2019 credit expires after 2026; 2026 credit usable through 2033.
## Qualifying premises
Single-family homes, individual apartment units, condominium units, and units in multi-family homes. Only "residential premises."
## Key eligibility rules
- Only the OWNER of the residential premises may claim it
- Only legally required deleading qualifies: removal or covering of materials readily accessible to children under six
- Inspection by a Department of Public Health-licensed inspector or risk assessor establishing dangerous lead levels
- Work by a licensed deleader or authorized person
- Reinspection and a Letter of Compliance or Letter of Interim Control
- Keep the letters; enclose Schedule LP or the credit is disallowed
## Stacking with other credits
Line 8 is reduced by the Limited Income Credit, other-jurisdiction credit, and other credits (Solar/Wind Energy, EOAC, Septic, Brownfields, Low-Income Housing, Historic Rehabilitation, Film, Medical Device, Farming and Fisheries, Low-Income Housing Donation, Veterans Hire, etc.). Use the credit expiring first.
## Required Information
- Address and unit number of each deleaded unit
- Letters of Compliance / Interim Control with dates
- Inspector or risk assessor license numbers
- Paid invoices and payment dates
- Prior interim credits claimed on each unit (2018 onward)
- Prior-year Schedule LP carryover ledger
## Questions
- Do you own the property? Is it residential?
- Did you get a Letter of Compliance or a Letter of Interim Control, and when? When did you pay?
- What did the deleading cost for each unit?
- Did you claim an interim control credit on the same unit in an earlier year?
- Is the property a rental?
## Common Errors
- Tenants claiming the credit (owners only)
- Claiming in the letter year when payment came later (use the LATER year)
- Taking 100% of interim control cost instead of 50%
- Exceeding $3,000 per unit by not netting earlier interim credits
- Deducting the same costs on Schedule E
- Not enclosing Schedule LP
## Prompt
- I de-leaded my rental unit.
- I got a letter of interim control for lead paint.
