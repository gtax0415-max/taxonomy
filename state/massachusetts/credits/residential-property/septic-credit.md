# Septic (Title 5) Credit (Schedule SC)
## Description
Nonrefundable Massachusetts credit for the actual costs of repairing or replacing a FAILED cesspool or septic system — and, since 2024, upgrades or sewer connections required by Title 5 (310 CMR 15.000) or a DEP watershed permit — at the owner's Massachusetts principal residence. Purely a state credit.
## Amounts (since tax year 2023; unchanged for 2026)
| Item | Limit |
|---|---|
| Qualifying costs | Lesser of actual costs (net of grants/reimbursements) or $30,000 |
| Credit rate | 60% |
| Maximum per year | $4,000 (certificates issued 2023 and later); $1,500 for carryforwards from certificates issued 2022 or earlier |
| Lifetime maximum per residential property per owner | $18,000 |
| Carryforward | 5 years after the year first claimed |
## Schedule SC flow
- Part 1: date Certificate of Compliance or verification letter issued; approving authority; ownership percentage (Line 2); co-owners; pre-2023 subsidized loan or betterment (Line 3)
- Part 2 (only if the certificate was issued in the current year): describe expenditures, including actual costs incurred 1995 onward (Line 4); total (5a); less grants/reimbursements (5b); net (5c); lesser of 5c or $30,000 (6); × 60% (7)
- Part 3: Line 8 = smaller of Line 7 or $4,000 (or carryforward amount, capped per issuance year); × ownership % (9); less interest subsidy (10); available (11); tax after other credits (12); allowable = smaller of 11 or 12 (13) → Schedule CMS
- Part 4: carryover ledger by year of certificate
- Part 5: interest subsidy computation (pre-2023 certificates with subsidized loans or betterments only)
## Who qualifies
- Owner with legal title to Massachusetts residential property
- Occupies it as PRINCIPAL residence
- Not a dependent of another taxpayer
- Co-owners each claim in proportion to the costs each actually paid
## Actual costs
Reasonable and necessary costs paid by the owner: materials, equipment, demolition, relocation, design, engineering, testing and inspection, reduced by grants and reimbursements. NOT: work on a system that has not failed, work not done under DEP regulations, or landscaping replacement.
## Stacking with other credits
Line 12 is reduced by the Limited Income Credit, other-jurisdiction credit, and other credits (Solar/Wind, Lead Paint, EOAC, EDIP, Brownfields, Low-Income Housing, Historic Rehabilitation, Film, Medical Device, Farming and Fisheries, etc.). Use the credit expiring first.
## Notes
- Keep the Certificate of Compliance or verification letter; enclose Schedule SC or the credit is disallowed
- Because only $4,000 per year can be used and carryforward is 5 years, a low-liability taxpayer may never use the full credit. A bill to make it refundable (H.4082) was filed but is NOT law for 2026
- See TIRs 97-12, 98-8, 99-5, 99-20, 24-14 and DOR Directive 01-6
## Required Information
- Certificate of Compliance or verification letter and issue date
- Itemized costs and payment dates, grants and reimbursements
- Ownership percentage and co-owner details
- Prior Schedule SC carryover by certificate year
- Loan or betterment terms (pre-2023 certificates only)
## Questions
- Was the system officially failed, or was the work required by Title 5 or a watershed permit?
- When was the Certificate of Compliance or verification letter issued?
- Is the property your principal residence, and do you own it alone?
- What did the work cost, and did you receive any grant?
- Do you have unused septic credit from earlier years?
## Common Errors
- Claiming more than $4,000 in a year ($1,500 for pre-2023 certificates)
- Not prorating for co-ownership
- Including landscaping or work on a non-failed system
- Claiming for a vacation home or rental
- Letting carryforward expire
## Prompt
- My septic failed Title 5 and I replaced it.
- We had to connect to the town sewer.
