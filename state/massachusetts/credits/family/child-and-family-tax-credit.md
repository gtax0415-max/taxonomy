# Child and Family Tax Credit (CFTC)
## Description
Fully refundable Massachusetts credit of a flat $440 for each qualifying individual in the taxpayer's household. No income limit, no phase-out, no cap on the number of qualifying individuals. Massachusetts calls it the most generous universal child and dependent credit in the country; the Legislature estimated it benefits more than 565,000 families.
## 2026 amount
$440 per qualifying individual. The amount is set in statute "for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2024" and is NOT inflation-indexed.
| Year | Per individual | Cap on number |
|---|---|---|
| 2022 and earlier | $180 (household dependent / dependent care credits) | 2 |
| 2023 | $310 | None |
| 2024 onward (including 2026) | $440 | None |
## Who is a qualifying individual
1. A child UNDER AGE 13 who qualifies as a dependent for federal purposes
2. A dependent, OR the taxpayer's SPOUSE, who is physically or mentally incapable of self-care and principally lives with the taxpayer
3. A dependent who is AGE 65 OR OVER, or disabled
## Filing status
- Allowed: single, head of household, married filing jointly
- NOT allowed: married filing separately (no exception)
## Noncustodial parents (since 2024)
A noncustodial parent may claim the credit for a child dependent AGE 13 OR OLDER who is physically or mentally incapable of self-care. Fill in the noncustodial parent oval on Form 1, page 1. A custodial parent who released the exemption fills in the separate release oval.
## Calculation
Number of qualifying individuals (Line 46a) × $440 = Line 46. That is the entire calculation — no worksheet, no income test.
## Notes
- Because it is refundable and has no earned-income requirement, a family with ZERO income can receive it — but only by filing a return
- A child under 13 who is also a qualifying child for the EIC appears on Schedule DI once, with both ovals filled in as applicable
- Unlike the old dependent care credit, no child care expenses are required
## Required Information
- Each qualifying individual's name, SSN or ITIN, date of birth, relationship (Schedule DI)
- For disabled individuals or spouse: nature of incapacity and that the person principally lives with the taxpayer
- Filing status
- Custodial or noncustodial status for divorced or separated parents
## Questions
- How many children under 13 do you claim as dependents?
- Do you claim any dependents who are 65 or older, or disabled?
- Is your spouse unable to care for himself or herself?
- Are you filing married filing separately (which bars the credit)?
- Are you a noncustodial parent of a disabled child age 13 or older?
## Common Errors
- Claiming a child age 13 or older who is not disabled
- Claiming it on a married-filing-separately return
- Missing that elderly or disabled adult dependents also qualify
- Low- or no-income families not filing at all and leaving the refund unclaimed
- Omitting Schedule DI
