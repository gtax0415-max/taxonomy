# Massachusetts Earned Income Credit (EIC)
## Description
Fully refundable state credit equal to 40% of the federal Earned Income Credit. Massachusetts does not recompute eligibility from scratch — if you qualify for and claim the federal EIC, you take 40% of that federal amount. Raised from 30% to 40% beginning tax year 2023.
## How it works
1. Compute the federal EIC (see federal/credits/income-based/earned-income-credit.md)
2. Enter the number of qualifying children on Line 43a and the federal EIC on Line 43b
3. Multiply by 0.40 and enter on Line 43
Because it is a straight percentage, every federal rule — phase-in, plateau, phase-out, the investment income cliff, SSN rules — passes through automatically.
## 2026 — derived from federal Rev. Proc. 2025-32
MAXIMUM MASSACHUSETTS CREDIT (40% of federal maximum):
| Qualifying children | Federal maximum | Massachusetts maximum |
|---|---|---|
| None | $664 | $265.60 |
| One | $4,427 | $1,770.80 |
| Two | $7,316 | $2,926.40 |
| Three or more | $8,231 | $3,292.40 |
INVESTMENT INCOME LIMIT: $12,200 (federal limit; one dollar over and both the federal AND the Massachusetts credit are lost)
Income limits by filing status: same as federal — see federal/credits/income-based/earned-income-credit.md and Rev. Proc. 2025-32.
## 2025 reference (for comparison)
Massachusetts maximums: $259.60 / $1,731.20 / $2,860.80 / $3,218.40. The 2025 Form 1 instructions describe the credit as available to earned income under $68,675 (the federal joint, three-or-more-children limit).
## Which federal number to use
- Federal EIC Worksheet A filers → Worksheet A, Line 6
- Federal EIC Worksheet B filers → Worksheet B, Line 11
- If the IRS is computing your federal EIC, wait for the IRS figure before entering Line 43
## Massachusetts-specific rules (where it differs from federal)
- DOMESTIC ABUSE EXCEPTION for married filing separately: allowed if you live apart from your spouse when you file and cannot file jointly because you are a victim of domestic abuse. Fill in the oval on Line 43. Compute using the federal worksheet answering "No" to question 4 and use the SINGLE column of the EIC table. Keep records (restraining order, police report, doctor's letter, notarized witness statement, or sworn statement) but do NOT enclose them
- Massachusetts also adopts the federal ARPA separated-spouse expansion (effective 2022)
- Schedule DI must be completed when claiming qualifying children; omitting it delays processing
- Part-year residents claim on Form 1-NR/PY with the credit prorated; nonresidents cannot claim it
## Joint filing requirement (since 2024)
Married couples must file a joint Massachusetts return in any year they file a joint federal return, unless an exception applies. This closes the gap where a couple filed jointly federally but separately in Massachusetts.
## Notes
- The federal hidden marginal rate in the phase-out range rises further: each dollar of federal EIC lost also costs 40 cents of Massachusetts EIC
- Federal disallowance or a federal EIC ban (Form 8862) flows through — no federal EIC means no Massachusetts EIC
- A federal amended return that changes the EIC requires a Massachusetts amended return (fill in "Federal amendment" on page 1)
## Required Information
- Federal EIC amount (Form 1040 Line 27a and the worksheet line used)
- Number of qualifying children
- Schedule DI: each qualifying child's name, SSN, date of birth, relationship
- Filing status and, if married filing separately, which exception applies
- Residency period, if part-year
## Questions
- Did you claim the federal Earned Income Credit? How much?
- How many qualifying children did you claim federally?
- Were you a Massachusetts resident all year?
- Are you filing married filing separately? If so, are you living apart and a victim of domestic abuse, or do you meet the federal separated-spouse rule?
## Common Errors
- Entering the federal EIC on Line 43 instead of 40% of it
- Omitting Schedule DI for qualifying children
- Claiming the credit as a nonresident
- Claiming it while married filing separately without filling in the exception oval
- Not amending the Massachusetts return after an IRS EIC adjustment
## Prompt
- I got the earned income credit on my federal return. Do I get it in Massachusetts too?
- I work part-time in Massachusetts and have two kids.
- I'm separated and filing separately because of an abusive spouse.
