# Schedule D-IS — QSB Stock, Massachusetts Start-Up Stock, and 1996-2002 Installment Sales
## Description
Replaces Schedule D for taxpayers with gains on qualified small business stock, stock in certain Massachusetts-based start-ups, or installment sales made between 1996 and 2002.
## Qualified small business (QSB) stock exclusion
Massachusetts excludes 100% of gain on QSB stock held more than five years, to the same extent as IRC 1202 as in effect on January 1, 2024, for stock acquired on or after September 27, 2010 (TIR 23-5). Massachusetts does NOT adopt the P.L. 119-21 expansion of Section 1202 (shorter holding periods, higher caps).
## 3% rate for Massachusetts start-up stock
Gains not qualifying for the exclusion may be taxed at 3% if:
- The investment was made within five years of the corporation's incorporation
- The stock generally meets IRC 1202(c) (C-corporation status not required)
- Held three years or more
- The corporation is domiciled in Massachusetts, incorporated on or after January 1, 2011, had under $50 million in assets at investment, and meets IRC 1202(e)(1), (e)(2), (e)(5) and (e)(6) active-business tests
## 1996-2002 installment sales
Long-term gains received on installment sales that occurred January 1, 1996 through December 31, 2002 are reported on Schedule D-IS under the holding-period rate rules of that era. Pre-1996 installment sales go to Schedule B (12%) or Schedule X (ordinary).

- Did you sell stock in a startup or small company? When was it acquired and incorporated, and where is it domiciled?
- Do you still receive payments on an installment sale from 1996-2002?
## Common Errors
- Applying the federal P.L. 119-21 QSBS expansion in Massachusetts
- Filing Schedule D instead of D-IS
