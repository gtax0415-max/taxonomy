# IRA, Keogh, Qualified Charitable and Roth Conversion Distributions
## Description
Massachusetts NEVER allowed a deduction for IRA, Keogh, SEP or SIMPLE contributions. As a result, contributions made while a Massachusetts resident were already taxed, and distributions are tax-free until those contributions are recovered. After recovery, distributions are 5.0% income.
## Schedule X, Line 2 Worksheet
1. Total IRA/Keogh, qualified charitable IRA, and Roth conversion distributions received in 2026
2. Total contributions previously taxed by Massachusetts
3. Total distributions received in prior years
4. Line 2 minus Line 3 (not less than 0) — remaining Massachusetts basis
5. Line 1 minus Line 4 (not less than 0)
6. Qualified charitable distributions included in Line 1
7. Line 5 minus Line 6 → Schedule X, Line 2
## Key rules
- Keogh contributions made BEFORE 1975 were deductible — no basis for those
- 401(k) or other deferred-plan money rolled to an IRA was never taxed by Massachusetts → no basis; fully taxable when distributed
- 401(k) money converted to a Roth and taxed at conversion → basis when later distributed
- Roth conversions: taxable portion included federally is included in Massachusetts, EXCEPT amounts previously taxed by Massachusetts
- Qualified Roth distributions not taxable federally are not taxable in Massachusetts
## Notes
- Contributions made while a resident of ANOTHER state that did not tax them are not Massachusetts basis
- Keep a running basis ledger; DOR does not track it for you
- The federal Form 8606 basis is a different number from Massachusetts basis
## Required Information
- All IRA-type Forms 1099-R
- Lifetime record of IRA/Keogh contributions made while a Massachusetts resident
- Total prior-year distributions
- Qualified charitable distribution amounts
## Questions
- Did you contribute to an IRA while living in Massachusetts? How much in total?
- How much have you withdrawn in prior years?
- Was any distribution a qualified charitable distribution?
- Did the IRA receive a rollover from a 401(k)?
## Common Errors
- Using the federal taxable amount (Form 1040 Line 4b) without recovering Massachusetts basis
- Treating 401(k) rollover money as Massachusetts basis
- Reporting IRA distributions on Form 1, Line 4
- Including qualified charitable distributions
## Prompt
- I took money out of my IRA. How much is taxable in Massachusetts?
- I converted my IRA to a Roth this year.
