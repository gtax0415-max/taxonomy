# Underpayment of Massachusetts Estimated Tax (Form M-2210)
## Description
Interest-based penalty for not paying enough Massachusetts tax through withholding and estimated payments during the year. The Massachusetts equivalent of federal Form 2210, but with an 80% current-year safe harbor instead of 90%, and no higher prior-year percentage for high-income taxpayers.
## Who must pay estimated tax
Anyone who reasonably expects to owe more than $400 of Massachusetts income tax not covered by withholding. 4% surtax filers must include the surtax in estimates and must file and pay electronically.
## 2026 installment due dates (calendar year)
| Installment | Due |
|---|---|
| 1 | April 15, 2026 |
| 2 | June 15, 2026 |
| 3 | September 15, 2026 |
| 4 | January 15, 2027 |
A due date falling on a weekend or legal holiday moves to the next business day. Fiscal-year filers: 15th day of months 4, 6 and 9, and the 15th day of the next fiscal year.
## Required annual payment (Part 1)
Smaller of:
- 80% of 2026 tax after credits (66.67% for qualified farmers and fishermen), or
- 100% of 2025 tax after credits (only if the 2025 return covered 12 months)
Divided into four equal installments unless using the annualized method.
## Penalty rate (Part 2)
Federal short-term rate plus 4 percentage points, applied per day on each underpaid installment.
| Period | Rate |
|---|---|
| 2025, entire year | 8% |
| 2026 Q1 (Jan-Mar) | 8% |
| 2026 Q2 (Apr-Jun) | 7% |
| 2026 Q3 (Jul-Sep) | 8% |
| 2026 Q4 (Oct-Dec) | 8% |
| 2027 Q1 (to April 15, 2027) | Pending DOR TIR |
Source: TIRs 26-2, 26-3 and 26-5. The 2026 M-2210 will split the penalty days by these quarters.
## Annualized income installment method (Part 3)
For uneven income. Periods: Jan 1-Mar 31, Jan 1-May 31, Jan 1-Aug 31, Jan 1-Dec 31. Annualization factors 4, 2.4, 1.5, 1; applicable percentages 20%, 40%, 60%, 80%. Computes tax separately on 5.0%, 8.5% and 12% income, plus the 4% surtax on annualized income over the threshold — $1,107,750 for 2026 (the 2025 form used $1,083,150).
## Withholding
Withholding is treated as paid in equal parts on each due date, unless the taxpayer shows the actual dates withheld.
## Exceptions vs. waivers
- EXCEPTION: complete through Line 13, fill in the "Exception. Enclose Form M-2210" oval on Form 1, skip Lines 14-24
- WAIVER: complete Lines 7-13 for the affected installment, write WAIVER on Line 14, fill in the Exception oval, attach explanation
## Notes
- Form M-2210 also applies to Form 3M, Form NRCR and Form 63D-ELT (PTE excise) filers; 63D-ELT filers have only the $400 and prior-year exceptions
- DOR can abate penalties for reasonable cause but generally cannot abate interest — see filing/procedures/abatement-form-abt.md
- Electronic filing requirements may apply (TIRs 16-9, 21-9)
## Required Information
- 2026 tax and credits (Form 1, Lines 28-31, 48)
- 2025 tax after credits and whether that return covered 12 months
- Date and amount of every estimated payment and withholding
- Quarterly income breakdown if annualizing
## Questions
- Did you make estimated payments in 2026? When and how much?
- What was your 2025 Massachusetts tax after credits?
- Was your income uneven during the year (e.g., a large gain late in the year)?
- Do you qualify as a farmer or fisherman?
- Did you retire after 62, become disabled, or suffer a casualty or disaster?
## Common Errors
- Using the federal 90% / 110% safe harbors instead of Massachusetts 80% / 100%
- Forgetting the 4% surtax in estimates
- Not annualizing when a large gain came late in the year
- Claiming an exception without enclosing Form M-2210 and filling in the oval
## Prompt
- Do I owe a penalty for not paying Massachusetts estimated tax?
- I sold stock in December. Will Massachusetts charge me an underpayment penalty?
