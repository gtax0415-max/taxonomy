# Noncustodial Parent Earned Income Credit
## Description
Refundable credit for noncustodial parents who pay child support in full through a New York State Support Collection Unit. New York was the FIRST STATE IN THE COUNTRY to offer this and it remains rare — there is no federal equivalent. A noncustodial parent gets little or nothing from the federal EIC because the child lives elsewhere; this credit fills that gap.
## Amount
The credit is the GREATER of two calculations:
1. 20% of the federal EIC that would have been allowed if the noncustodial child WERE a qualifying child, or
2. 2.5 times the federal EIC allowed for a taxpayer with NO qualifying children
Form IT-209 computes both and takes the larger. Calculation 2 usually wins at very low income; calculation 1 usually wins in the middle of the range.
## Eligibility
- Full-year New York State resident
- Age 18 or older
- A parent of a MINOR CHILD who does not reside with you
- A child support order payable THROUGH A NEW YORK STATE SUPPORT COLLECTION UNIT, in effect for at least half the tax year
- You paid the FULL AMOUNT DUE for the year under that order
The order-through-an-SCU requirement is strict. Informal arrangements, direct payments to the other parent, and out-of-state collection units do not qualify, no matter how faithfully the support was paid.
## Paid in full means paid in full
Partial payment disqualifies you entirely. There is no proration. Someone who paid 90% of the year's obligation gets nothing. Check the SCU payment record against the order amount before claiming.
## The mutual exclusion
You cannot claim this AND the New York State EIC. Complete Form IT-209 — it computes both credits and identifies the better one.
- Claiming the NONCUSTODIAL credit: enter it on Form IT-201 line 66 and submit IT-209
- Claiming the REGULAR NYS EIC instead: transfer that amount to line 65 and submit IT-209, NOT Form IT-215
The New York City EIC is a separate credit and is not affected by this election.
## Notes
- The investment income limit applies here as it does to the regular EIC
- The child does not need to be your dependent and you do not need to have any parenting time
- Multiple children under one order do not multiply the credit
## Required Information
- SCU account number and full-year payment history
- The support order, its effective dates, and the annual amount due
- The child's name and date of birth
- Your earned income and federal AGI
- The federal EIC amounts under both scenarios, with and without a qualifying child
## Questions
- Is your child support order payable through a New York State Support Collection Unit?
- Was the order in effect for at least half the year?
- Did you pay the full amount due for the year?
- Is the child under 18?
- Were you a full-year New York State resident?
## Common Errors
- Claiming it when support is paid directly to the other parent instead of through an SCU
- Claiming it after paying most, but not all, of the year's obligation
- Claiming both this credit and the New York State EIC
- Submitting Form IT-215 when Form IT-209 is the correct form
- Assuming an out-of-state support collection unit qualifies
## Prompt
- I pay child support through the state and my kids live with their mother.
- Is there a New York credit for noncustodial parents?
- I got almost nothing from the federal EITC because my child does not live with me.
