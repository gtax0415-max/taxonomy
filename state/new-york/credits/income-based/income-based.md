# Income-Based Credits
## Description
Credits that scale with earned income. New York's earned income credit is a straight percentage of the federal EIC, so federal eligibility drives everything, and New York City stacks a second percentage on top.
## Credits in this folder
- Earned Income Credit (earned-income-credit.md) — Form IT-215 → Form IT-201, Line 65 (state) and Line 70 (city). Refundable
- Noncustodial Parent EIC (noncustodial-parent-eic.md) — Form IT-209 → Form IT-201, Line 66. Refundable
## Three credits, one federal input
| Credit | Rate | Line |
|---|---|---|
| New York State EIC | 30% of federal EIC for 2025, 45% for 2026 | 65 |
| New York City EIC | 10% to 30% of federal EIC, sliding by NYAGI | 70 |
| Noncustodial parent EIC | Alternative formula, see file | 66 |
A New York City resident claims the state and city credits TOGETHER on one Form IT-215. They stack.
## The mutual exclusion
You cannot claim the NYS EIC and the noncustodial parent EIC in the same year. Form IT-209 computes both and tells you which is larger. Claim the noncustodial version and you file IT-209 alone, not IT-215.
## What changes for 2026
The New York State EIC rate rises from 30% to 45% of the federal EIC for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2026 — a 50% increase in the credit with no change in federal law. The NYC rate is unchanged.
