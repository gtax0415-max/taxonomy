# Earned Income Credit (New York State and New York City)
## Description
Refundable credit computed as a percentage of the federal earned income credit. New York State's version is one of the most generous state EICs in the country, and New York City residents claim a second city credit on the same form.
## New York State amount
- 2025: 30% of the federal EIC allowed, REDUCED by the New York State household credit
- 2026: 45% of the federal EIC, under legislation passed in 2025
The household credit offset is the wrinkle people miss. Form IT-215 Worksheet B subtracts your household credit from the tentative EIC. Because the household credit is nonrefundable and the EIC is refundable, that offset can cost real money for a filer with no tax liability.
## New York City amount
Ranges from 10% to 30% of the federal EIC, sliding by New York adjusted gross income — the lowest incomes get the highest percentage. This was raised from a flat 5% beginning in 2022.
A city resident with a $4,328 federal EIC in 2025 could see roughly $1,298 from the state plus several hundred more from the city.
## The investment income cliff
2025 limit: $11,950. 2026 limit: $12,200. New York uses the federal figure.
This is a CLIFF, not a phase-out. One dollar over and BOTH the state and city credits are lost entirely, on top of losing the federal credit. A single year-end mutual fund capital gain distribution can do it.
## Eligibility
- You must have claimed the federal EIC, or could have claimed it based on your New York recomputed federal AGI
- Valid SSNs for you, your spouse, and each qualifying child, issued by the due date of the return including extensions. ITINs do NOT work here, unlike the Empire State child credit
- Married filing separately is barred, with the same narrow separated-spouse exception the federal rules allow
- You cannot also claim the noncustodial parent EIC
## City eligibility is independent
You may qualify for the NEW YORK CITY EIC EVEN IF YOU DO NOT QUALIFY FOR THE STATE EIC. A part-year state resident who lived in the city, for instance, may clear the city test and not the state one. Do not skip Worksheet C because the state credit came out at zero.
## Part-year residents
Compute the credit as if you were a full-year resident, then multiply by your New York resident fraction. Part-year state filers complete IT-215 lines 18 through 26; part-year city residents complete line 28.
## If the IRS is figuring your federal EIC
Write EIC in the box to the left of the money column on Form IT-201 line 65 and leave the amount blank. Complete IT-215 lines 1 through 9 and submit it. The Tax Department will compute the credit and either send the refund with an explanation or bill you, payable within 21 days or by April 15, whichever is later. Do not complete IT-201 lines 76 through 80.
## Required Information
- The federal EIC amount actually allowed
- Number of qualifying children and their SSNs and dates of birth
- Earned income, federal AGI, and New York AGI
- Investment income total
- Months of New York State and New York City residency
- Your New York household credit amount, for the Worksheet B offset
## Questions
- Did the IRS allow you a federal earned income credit, and how much?
- Is your investment income under $11,950 for 2025?
- Did you live in New York City, and for how many months?
- Do you and your children have SSNs, not ITINs?
- Are you a noncustodial parent, which would send you to Form IT-209 instead?
## Common Errors
- Forgetting the household credit offset on Worksheet B and overstating the state credit
- Skipping Worksheet C and losing the city credit entirely
- Assuming an ITIN works, when the EIC requires SSNs even though the child credit does not
- Claiming both the NYS EIC and the noncustodial parent EIC
- Missing that city eligibility survives when state eligibility fails
- Applying the 45% rate to a 2025 return — it starts with tax year 2026
## Prompt
- I claimed the EITC on my federal return.
- I work part-time in Manhattan and have one child.
- Does New York have its own earned income credit?
