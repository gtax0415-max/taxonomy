# New York City Income Tax Elimination Credit
## Description
NEW FOR TAX YEARS BEGINNING ON OR AFTER JANUARY 1, 2025. Tax Law Section 1310(h). A credit that can ELIMINATE the entire remaining New York City personal income tax liability for a low- and moderate-income city resident with dependents. It is not a fixed dollar amount — it is sized to whatever city tax is left after all other credits.
## How it works
The credit equals the NYC tax due REMAINING after all other applicable credits are applied. If your federal AGI is at or below your income threshold, the credit is FULL and your city tax goes to zero. If your FAGI is up to $5,000 ABOVE the threshold, you get a PARTIAL credit. More than $5,000 above and you get nothing.
## Four eligibility gates, all required
1. Full-year or part-year NEW YORK CITY RESIDENT
2. You claimed AT LEAST ONE DEPENDENT at Form IT-201 Item H
3. You did NOT claim a New York City or New York State PASS-THROUGH ENTITY TAX credit
4. Your INVESTMENT INCOME is $10,000 or less
Plus the income threshold test below.

## The four stop questions on the form
Part 1 of Form IT-270 is a screen. Answer wrong on any of them and the form stops:
- A: at least one dependent? NO stops you
- B: is Form IT-201 line 54 zero? YES stops you — there must be city tax left to eliminate
- C: does FAGI exceed the threshold by more than $5,000? YES stops you
- D: is investment income over $10,000? YES stops you
## Investment income here
Taxable and tax-exempt interest, ordinary dividends, and net capital gains above zero, plus rental income, royalties, and passive activity income. Use Worksheet 1 in federal Publication 596 or IRC Section 32(i). Note this is the EIC definition of investment income but with a $10,000 threshold rather than the EIC's own limit.
## The PTET conflict
Claiming a New York State OR New York City PTET credit disqualifies you entirely. For a small business owner in the city whose entity elected PTET, the two benefits are mutually exclusive and need comparing before the entity-level election is even made — by filing season it is too late.
## Notes
- Applies to both full-year and part-year city residents
- Because it is sized to remaining tax rather than fixed, it is claimed LAST, after the household credit, the city child care credit, the city EIC, and the school tax credits
- It appears at line 70a, inside the payments and credits block, though it functions as a liability offset rather than a refundable payment
## Required Information
- Number of dependents at Item H
- Federal AGI from line 19
- Investment income components from the federal return
- NYC tax remaining after other credits, from line 54
- Whether any PTET credit was claimed
## Questions
- Were you a New York City resident during the year?
- How many dependents did you claim?
- Is your federal AGI within $5,000 of your applicable threshold?
- Is your investment income $10,000 or less?
- Did you or your business claim a PTET credit?
## Common Errors
- Overlooking the credit entirely because it is new for 2025
- Claiming it with no dependents
- Claiming it alongside a PTET credit
- Computing it before other city credits, rather than last
- Missing that a partial credit exists within $5,000 above the threshold
- Using the wrong threshold row for the number of dependents
