# Real Property Tax Credit (Homeowners and Renters)
## Description
Refundable credit for low-income New Yorkers who pay property taxes OR RENT. One of only three New York credits you can claim WITHOUT FILING AN INCOME TAX RETURN — Form IT-214 stands on its own.
## Amount
- $375 maximum if you or your spouse were 65 OR OLDER during the year
- $75 maximum otherwise
The amounts are modest, but the credit is fully refundable and reaches people with no tax liability at all.
## Eligibility, all of which must be met
- Full-year New York State resident
- Your household gross income was $18,000 OR LESS
- You occupied the same New York residence for at least SIX MONTHS
- You cannot be claimed as a dependent on another taxpayer's federal return
- The residence was not completely exempt from real property taxes
FOR HOMEOWNERS: the market value of all real property you owned was $85,000 or less.
FOR RENTERS: average monthly rent was $450 OR LESS, excluding any charges for heat, gas, electricity, furnishings, and board.
## Household gross income is broader than AGI
This is the trap. Household gross income includes income of EVERY MEMBER of the household, and it includes items that never appear on a tax return:
- Social Security and railroad retirement benefits, including the nontaxable portion
- Pensions and annuities, including nontaxable portions
- Workers' compensation and disability payments
- Unemployment compensation
- Cash public assistance and relief
- Support money and child support received
- Veterans' benefits, and GI Bill benefits
A household that looks well under $18,000 on Form IT-201 line 19 can be over the line once nontaxable Social Security is added.
## The rent exclusion that helps
The $450 monthly test excludes charges for heat, gas, electricity, furnishings, and board. A $525 all-utilities-included rent may test under $450 once those are backed out. Get the landlord to break out the components if the lease does not.
## Notes
- If you lived in more than one residence, apply the six-month test to the one you are claiming
- If you moved during the year, prorate
- Renters and owners use the same form; the tests differ but the credit amounts do not
- The credit is available for prior years — you can file Form IT-214 for up to three years back
## Required Information
- Total household gross income from all members and all sources, taxable and not
- Age of each household member
- Property tax paid, or total rent paid and the monthly amount excluding utilities
- Market value of the residence, for homeowners
- Months of occupancy
## Questions
- What was the TOTAL income of everyone in the household, including nontaxable Social Security?
- Do you rent or own?
- If renting, what is your average monthly rent excluding heat, gas, electricity, and furnishings?
- Was anyone in the household 65 or older?
- Did you occupy the residence at least six months?
## Common Errors
- Counting only taxable income and missing nontaxable Social Security, disability, or public assistance
- Counting only the filer's income instead of the whole household's
- Assuming renters do not qualify
- Testing rent including utilities instead of net of them
- Not filing at all because no tax return is required — Form IT-214 can be filed by itself
- Missing prior-year claims, which are still open for three years
## Prompt
- I rent an apartment and my income is very low.
- I am 70 and own my home in upstate New York.
- Do I have to file a tax return to get the property tax credit?
