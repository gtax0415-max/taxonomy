# Historic Homeownership Rehabilitation Credit
## Description
Credit for rehabilitating an OWNER-OCCUPIED historic home in New York State. Distinct from the rehabilitation of historic properties credit (Form IT-238), which covers income-producing certified historic structures.
## Amount
- 20% of qualified rehabilitation expenditures
- Capped at $50,000 of credit per year
- The rehabilitation must cost MORE THAN $5,000, and at least 5% of the total must be spent on the EXTERIOR of the building
## The location requirement
The home must sit in a QUALIFYING CENSUS TRACT — generally one at or below the state median family income. This is the gate that disqualifies most applicants, and it is checkable in advance. Confirm the tract BEFORE any work begins, because there is no relief for finding out afterward.
## The approval sequence
Approval from the New York State Office of Parks, Recreation and Historic Preservation is required, and the order matters:
1. Part 1 — certify that the building is a historic structure
2. Part 2 — get the proposed work approved BEFORE STARTING IT
3. Part 3 — certify the completed work
Work performed before Part 2 approval is generally not creditable, even if it would have qualified. This is the most expensive mistake available in this credit.
## Refundability
The credit is refundable for taxpayers below a New York AGI threshold; above that threshold, unused credit carries forward instead. Confirm the current threshold and carryforward period on the Form IT-237 instructions for the filing year, since this provision has been amended more than once.
## Notes
- The property must be your PRIMARY RESIDENCE, though the credit has applied to buildings with limited rental units in some configurations
- The federal rehabilitation credit is for income-producing property only and does not cover owner-occupied homes, so there is usually no federal parallel claim
- Recapture applies if the property is sold or converted within the applicable period
## Required Information
- OPRHP Part 1, 2, and 3 certifications
- Census tract determination for the property address
- Itemized qualified rehabilitation expenditures with invoices
- Exterior work amount, to prove the 5% test
- Placed-in-service or completion date
- Your New York AGI, for the refundability test
## Questions
- Is the property in a qualifying census tract?
- Is it a certified historic structure, and is it your primary residence?
- Did you get OPRHP approval BEFORE starting the work?
- Did the rehabilitation exceed $5,000, with at least 5% on the exterior?
- What is your New York AGI, which determines whether the credit is refundable?
## Common Errors
- Starting work before OPRHP Part 2 approval
- Failing the census tract test, discovered after the money is spent
- Missing the 5% exterior requirement on an interior-only renovation
- Confusing this credit with the Form IT-238 credit for income-producing historic property
- Assuming refundability without checking the income threshold
## Prompt
- I am restoring an old house in Buffalo.
- Is there a New York credit for fixing up a historic home?
- My house is on the historic register and needs a new roof.
