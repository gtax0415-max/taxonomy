# STAR Credit (School Tax Relief)
## Description
School property tax relief for owner-occupied primary residences. STAR is INCLUDED IN THIS FOLDER BUT IS NOT AN INCOME TAX CREDIT in the ordinary sense — it never touches Form IT-201. It arrives as a separate check or direct deposit, usually before the school tax bill is due.
## Two delivery mechanisms
- STAR EXEMPTION: a reduction applied directly to the school tax bill. Closed to new applicants. Only homeowners who have had it continuously since 2015 still receive it this way
- STAR CREDIT: a check or direct deposit from the Tax Department. This is what everyone who bought a home after 2015 receives
If you bought a home recently, the exemption is not available to you and you must register for the credit. Nobody will do it for you.
## Two benefit levels
- BASIC STAR: primary residence, income below the statutory ceiling. No age requirement
- ENHANCED STAR: at least one owner age 65 or older, with a much lower income ceiling that is adjusted annually
Enhanced STAR is worth substantially more. Owners turning 65 should check eligibility in the year they turn 65 rather than waiting to be told.
## Income tests
The income limits and the definition of income for STAR are set separately from the income tax rules and are ADJUSTED ANNUALLY. Verify the current figures on tax.ny.gov before advising anyone near a threshold — do not rely on a prior year's number.
The income test generally uses the year TWO YEARS PRIOR to the benefit year, so a recent drop in income does not qualify someone immediately.
## Why the credit is often worth more than the exemption
The STAR credit amount can grow by up to 2% per year, while the exemption savings are frozen. Homeowners still holding the old exemption may be better off SWITCHING to the credit. The switch is voluntary and irreversible.
## Registration, not filing
Register through the Homeowner Benefit Portal or by phone. Registration is ONE TIME for the Basic credit — you do not reapply annually. Enhanced STAR recipients must have income verified each year, which the Tax Department does automatically once enrolled in the Income Verification Program.
## Notes
- One STAR benefit per primary residence. Vacation homes and rental properties do not qualify
- Trusts, life estates, and co-ops have specific ownership rules
- If you receive the check and also somehow the exemption, the Tax Department reconciles it
- STAR is not reported as income on your New York return
## Required Information
- Property address and school district
- All owners' names, ages, and Social Security numbers
- Income from the applicable prior year for all owners and their spouses
- Whether the property is your primary residence
- Purchase date, which determines exemption versus credit
## Questions
- Is this your primary residence?
- When did you buy the home?
- Is any owner 65 or older?
- Have you registered for STAR, or are you receiving it as an exemption on your tax bill?
- What was your income two years ago?
## Common Errors
- Assuming STAR is claimed on the income tax return
- Buying a home and never registering, then wondering why no check arrived
- Missing Enhanced STAR in the year an owner turns 65
- Keeping a frozen exemption when the credit would now be worth more
- Using current-year income for a test that looks back two years
## Prompt
- I bought a house in New York last year, how do I get STAR?
- I just turned 65, does my STAR benefit change?
- Why did I get a check from the Tax Department for school taxes?
