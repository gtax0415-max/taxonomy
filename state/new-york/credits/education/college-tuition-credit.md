# College Tuition Credit
## Description
Refundable credit for undergraduate tuition paid to an eligible institution, available to full-year New York State residents. Small in dollar terms but refundable, so it pays out even with no tax owed.
## Amount
Qualified tuition is capped at $10,000 PER ELIGIBLE STUDENT.
- If qualified expenses for a student are $5,000 OR MORE: the credit is 4% of expenses, which at the $10,000 cap means a MAXIMUM OF $400 per student
- If qualified expenses are LESS THAN $5,000: the credit is the lesser of the expenses paid or $200 per student
The cap is per student, not per return, so a family with three students in college can reach $1,200.
## What counts as qualified tuition
Tuition only. This is a narrower definition than the federal credits use.
NOT qualified:
- Room and board
- Books and supplies, even when required
- Student activity fees, athletic fees, insurance, or health fees
- Application, admission, or registration fees
- Any expense for a course not part of a degree program
- Tuition paid with tax-free scholarships, grants, or the earnings portion of a 529 distribution
## Eligible student and institution
- The student must be YOU, YOUR SPOUSE, or a DEPENDENT you claim
- UNDERGRADUATE degree program only. Graduate tuition does not qualify
- The institution must be an eligible one under the federal rules, in or outside New York State
## The credit-or-deduction election
You cannot take both. The alternative is the college tuition itemized deduction on Form IT-196, which allows up to $10,000 of tuition per student as a New York itemized deduction. Complete Form IT-272 either way — the form calculates both and shows which is larger.
The deduction is only usable if your total New York itemized deductions exceed your New York standard deduction. For 2025 that standard deduction is $8,000 single and $16,050 married filing jointly.
## Who is left out
- Part-year residents and nonresidents file Form IT-203 and use the IT-203-B worksheet instead; the credit itself is limited
- If a student is claimed as a dependent, only the person claiming them may take the credit, regardless of who actually paid
## Required Information
- Each student's name and SSN
- The institution's name and address
- Tuition paid per student, separated from fees, room, and board
- Scholarships, grants, and 529 amounts applied to tuition
- Whether the student is you, your spouse, or a dependent
- Your New York itemized deduction total, to test the alternative
## Questions
- Was the tuition for an undergraduate degree program?
- How much was tuition alone, excluding fees, books, room, and board?
- Was any of it paid with scholarships, grants, or a 529 withdrawal?
- Is the student your dependent, and are you the one claiming them?
- Do your New York itemized deductions exceed your New York standard deduction?
## Common Errors
- Including fees, books, or room and board in qualified expenses
- Claiming graduate tuition
- Failing to subtract tax-free scholarships and 529 earnings before computing the credit
- Claiming both the credit and the itemized deduction for the same student
- A dependent student claiming the credit when the parent claims the dependent
- Missing the per-student structure and capping the whole family at $400
## Prompt
- I paid tuition for my son at a state school.
- I am taking undergrad classes and live in New York.
- Should I take the tuition credit or the deduction?
