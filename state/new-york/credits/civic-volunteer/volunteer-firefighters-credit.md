# Volunteer Firefighters' and Ambulance Workers' Credit
## Description
Flat refundable credit for active volunteer firefighters and volunteer ambulance workers. Simple to claim and frequently missed, because volunteers do not expect a tax benefit for volunteering.
## Amount
- $200 per qualifying volunteer
- $400 on a joint return where BOTH spouses qualify
Flat amount, no computation, no income test, no phase-out.
## Eligibility
- Full-year New York State resident
- An ACTIVE volunteer firefighter or volunteer ambulance worker
- For the ENTIRE TAX YEAR
The entire-year requirement is absolute. Someone who joined in February or resigned in November gets nothing — there is no proration. Someone who transferred between two departments mid-year but was continuously active generally still qualifies, since the service was uninterrupted.
## The property tax exemption conflict
You CANNOT claim this credit if you received a REAL PROPERTY TAX EXEMPTION for the same volunteer service. Many New York municipalities offer volunteers a 10% assessment exemption. You must choose one.
Which is worth more depends entirely on the local exemption. On a home with a $6,000 annual tax bill, a 10% exemption saves roughly $600 — three times the credit. On a modest home or for a volunteer who rents, the $200 credit wins by default. Do the arithmetic before choosing; the choice is made annually.
## Notes
- Refundable, so it pays out with no tax liability
- The employer certification is the substantiation; the Tax Department does request it
- Paid firefighters and paid EMTs do not qualify. Someone paid in one municipality and volunteering in another qualifies for the volunteer service
- A volunteer who dies during the year is treated under the general rules for decedents; the entire-year test is applied to the shortened year
## Required Information
- Name of the fire company or ambulance service
- Confirmation of active status for the full year
- Whether you received a property tax exemption for the service
- Whether both spouses qualify, on a joint return
## Questions
- Were you an active volunteer for the entire year?
- Which department or squad?
- Did you receive a property tax exemption for your volunteer service?
- Does your spouse also volunteer?
- Were you a full-year New York State resident?
## Common Errors
- Claiming after joining or leaving mid-year
- Claiming both this credit and the local property tax exemption
- Taking the $200 credit when a local exemption would have been worth more
- Missing the $400 amount when both spouses volunteer
- Claiming for paid service
## Prompt
- I am a volunteer firefighter in upstate New York.
- I volunteer with the ambulance corps.
- Should I take the volunteer credit or the property tax exemption?
