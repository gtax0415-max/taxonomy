# Organ Donation Credit
## Description
Credit for the unreimbursed costs of donating an organ while living. New York was among the first states to offer this, on the reasoning that a live donor should not lose money for donating.
## Amount
Up to $10,000 of UNREIMBURSED expenses:
- Travel expenses
- Lodging expenses
- LOST WAGES during recovery
Lost wages are the important inclusion and the largest component for most donors. A live kidney donation typically means four to six weeks away from work; for many donors that alone exceeds the $10,000 cap.
## Eligibility
- FULL-YEAR New York State resident
- A LIVE donation of a qualifying organ — all or part of a liver, pancreas, kidney, intestine, lung, or bone marrow
- Expenses must be UNREIMBURSED
- ONCE PER LIFETIME. This is a one-time credit, not per donation
## What reimbursed means
Anything covered by insurance, by the recipient, by the recipient's insurer, or by a donor assistance program is out. The National Living Donor Assistance Center and similar programs cover travel and lodging for many donors, which leaves LOST WAGES as the piece that is usually still unreimbursed and still claimable — and the piece donors most often fail to document.
## Documenting lost wages
This is where claims fail. You need something showing what you would have earned: pay stubs bracketing the absence, an employer letter stating dates and rate, or for the self-employed, a reasonable computation from prior income. Sick leave or PTO that was PAID is not lost wages. Unpaid leave is.
## Notes
- The credit is claimed for the tax year in which the donation occurred
- Deceased donation does not qualify; this is for live donors only
- The recipient gets no credit
- Expenses for a companion or caregiver traveling with the donor are generally not covered — the credit is for the donor's own costs
## Required Information
- Date and type of the donation
- Hospital or transplant center documentation
- Itemized travel and lodging receipts
- Employer statement or pay records showing unpaid time and rate
- Documentation of any reimbursement received
## Questions
- Did you donate an organ as a living donor, and when?
- How much unpaid time did you take from work?
- Were your travel and lodging costs reimbursed by anyone?
- Have you claimed this credit in a prior year?
- Were you a full-year New York State resident that year?
## Common Errors
- Claiming expenses that a donor assistance program reimbursed
- Failing to document lost wages, which is usually the largest component
- Counting paid sick leave as lost wages
- Claiming it a second time
- Claiming for a deceased donation or as the recipient
## Prompt
- I donated a kidney to my brother.
- I was a living liver donor last year.
- Is there a tax credit for organ donation in New York?
