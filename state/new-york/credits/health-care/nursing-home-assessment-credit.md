# Nursing Home Assessment Credit
## Description
Refundable credit for the New York assessment on residential health care facilities, when that assessment is passed through to you and you pay it directly. One of the most OVERLOOKED credits in the New York system, because the assessment is buried inside a nursing home bill and nobody thinks to ask about it.
## Amount
The credit equals the assessment amount you actually paid. New York imposes an assessment on residential health care facilities, currently around 6% of revenue, which facilities pass through on residents' bills. You get 100% of the portion you paid back as a refundable credit.
For someone paying $120,000 a year privately, the assessment portion can run into thousands of dollars. This is not a trivial credit.
## Who can claim it
- The RESIDENT, or
- ANOTHER PERSON WHO PAID the assessment on the resident's behalf — commonly an adult child paying a parent's bill
You do NOT need to be related to the resident, and you do not need to claim them as a dependent. This is unusual and worth flagging: a child paying a parent's nursing home bill claims the credit on their OWN return.
## What does not count
- Amounts paid by MEDICAID
- Amounts paid by long-term care INSURANCE
- Amounts paid by anyone other than the person claiming the credit
- The base cost of care itself — only the ASSESSMENT portion qualifies
## Getting the number
The facility must tell you. Request a statement showing the assessment portion of what you paid during the calendar year. Many facilities issue this automatically in January; many do not, and will produce one on request. Without the statement there is nothing to claim.
The facility must be a RESIDENTIAL HEALTH CARE FACILITY located in New York State. Assisted living facilities and adult homes are generally NOT subject to the assessment and produce no credit.
## Notes
- Refundable, so it pays out even with no tax liability, which matters for elderly filers with mostly nontaxable Social Security
- Available for prior years within the statute of limitations — three years from filing or two from payment. Families discovering this credit late can often amend
- If more than one person paid, each claims only what they paid
## Required Information
- The facility's statement of the assessment portion paid
- The calendar-year total you personally paid
- The resident's name and the facility's name and address
- Confirmation that Medicaid or insurance did not cover the amount claimed
## Questions
- Did you pay a New York nursing home bill directly during the year?
- Was any of it covered by Medicaid or long-term care insurance?
- Have you asked the facility for a statement of the assessment portion?
- Is the facility a residential health care facility, not an assisted living residence?
- Did you pay for a parent or relative rather than yourself?
## Common Errors
- Never asking the facility for the assessment figure and skipping the credit entirely
- Claiming the full nursing home cost rather than the assessment portion
- Claiming amounts Medicaid paid
- Assuming only the resident can claim it, when the payer claims it
- Missing amendable prior years
## Prompt
- I pay my father's nursing home bill.
- Is there a credit for nursing home costs in New York?
- My mother is in a skilled nursing facility upstate.
