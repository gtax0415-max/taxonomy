# Long-Term Care Insurance Credit
## Description
Nonrefundable credit for premiums paid on a qualifying long-term care insurance policy. The credit was narrowed substantially in 2020 and now carries both a dollar cap and an income ceiling.
## Amount
- 20% of premiums paid during the tax year
- CAPPED AT $1,500 of credit, reached at $7,500 of premiums
- ONLY IF your New York AGI is LESS THAN $250,000
- Nonrefundable, with a carryforward of unused amounts
## The 2020 narrowing
Before 2020 the credit was 20% of premiums with NO DOLLAR CAP and NO INCOME LIMIT. Both restrictions were added at once. Anyone working from older guidance, an old software carryover, or a prior-year return will overstate this credit. Check the year of any source before relying on it.
## The income cliff
The $250,000 New York AGI test is a CLIFF, not a phase-out. At $249,999 you get the full credit; at $250,001 you get nothing. For a household near the line, a deferral or an acceleration of income decides whether $1,500 exists.
## Qualifying policy
The policy must be:
- A QUALIFIED long-term care insurance contract under IRC Section 7702B(b), and
- APPROVED BY the New York Superintendent of Financial Services
Both conditions. A federally qualified policy issued in another state to someone who later moved to New York may fail the second test. Confirm with the insurer rather than assuming.
## What counts as premiums paid
- Premiums you paid for yourself, your spouse, or a dependent
- Amounts paid through a partnership or S corporation and passed through to you
- NOT premiums paid by an employer or with pre-tax dollars
- NOT the long-term care portion of a hybrid life or annuity product, unless separately stated and qualifying
## Notes
- Long-term care premiums may ALSO be a medical expense itemized deduction on Form IT-196, subject to the age-based federal limits. Claiming the credit does not bar the deduction, but the same dollars are being used twice in different systems, so check the current instruction language before doubling up
- Both spouses' premiums count on a joint return, against one shared $1,500 cap
## Required Information
- Total premiums paid during the year, per person
- Insurer name and policy number
- Confirmation the policy is qualifying and New York approved
- New York AGI from Form IT-201, line 33
- Any carryforward from a prior year
## Questions
- How much did you pay in long-term care premiums this year?
- Is your New York AGI under $250,000?
- Is the policy approved by the New York Superintendent of Financial Services?
- Did you pay with after-tax dollars?
- Do you have unused credit carried forward?
## Common Errors
- Using pre-2020 rules with no cap and no income limit
- Missing the $250,000 cliff entirely
- Claiming premiums on a hybrid life or annuity product without a separately stated qualifying LTC premium
- Claiming employer-paid or pre-tax premiums
- Expecting a refund from a nonrefundable credit
## Prompt
- I pay long-term care insurance premiums.
- Is there a New York credit for long-term care?
- My LTC credit was bigger a few years ago, what changed?
