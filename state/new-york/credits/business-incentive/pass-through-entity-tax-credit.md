# Pass-Through Entity Tax (PTET) Credit
## Description
Refundable credit for your share of the New York pass-through entity tax paid by a partnership or S corporation that ELECTED into the regime. PTET is New York's workaround to the federal state and local tax deduction cap: the entity pays the state tax and deducts it federally as a business expense, and the owners take a credit for it on their personal returns.
The highest-dollar credit on most New York returns that has one.
## Two separate credits
- NEW YORK STATE PTET, available to any direct partner, member, or shareholder in an electing entity
- NEW YORK CITY PTET, a separate election and a separate credit for city resident owners
Both are claimed on Form IT-653 and both land on line 71.
## Who can claim it
DIRECT partners, members, or shareholders only. A tiered structure where an upper-tier entity holds the interest requires tracing, and the credit does not automatically flow to the ultimate individual owner. Trusts other than grantor trusts have their own treatment.
## The mandatory add-back
This is the mechanical step that is missed most often. Because the entity DEDUCTED the PTET in computing the income reported to you, you must ADD IT BACK on Form IT-225 for New York purposes. Claiming the credit without the add-back understates New York income and produces a notice.
The add-back and the credit are two halves of one transaction. Software usually handles it; a manually prepared return often does not.
## Timing
The credit is claimed in the individual's tax year in which the ENTITY'S PTET year ENDS. For a calendar-year entity that is the same year. For a fiscal-year entity it is not, and the mismatch strands the credit in the wrong year if you follow the K-1 date rather than the entity's year end.
## Estimated tax interaction
PTET paid by the entity is NOT your withholding and NOT your estimated tax. It does not appear on Form IT-201 line 75. Owners relying on entity PTET payments to cover personal liability can still incur an estimated tax penalty at line 81, because the credit arrives only at filing.
## The NYC elimination credit conflict
Claiming EITHER a New York State or a New York City PTET credit DISQUALIFIES you from the New York City income tax elimination credit. For a small city business with modest owner income, the elimination credit can be worth more than the PTET benefit. The comparison has to happen before the entity makes its annual election, since the election is irrevocable for the year.
## Notes
- The election is made annually by the entity, by a deadline early in the year, and cannot be revoked
- Nonresident owners get a credit too, against their New York nonresident tax
- The resident credit rules for OTHER states' PTET regimes are separate and state-specific; see other-jurisdiction/resident-credit.md
- The federal deductibility that motivates the whole structure depends on federal guidance that has been revisited more than once
## Required Information
- The entity's name, EIN, and PTET year end
- Your share of PTET paid, state and city separately
- Confirmation that you are a DIRECT owner
- The add-back amount for Form IT-225
- Whether you might otherwise qualify for the NYC income tax elimination credit
## Questions
- Did your partnership or S corporation elect into PTET for the year?
- What is your share of the PTET paid, and is any of it city PTET?
- Are you a direct owner or do you hold through another entity?
- Did you make the Form IT-225 add-back?
- When does the entity's tax year end?
## Common Errors
- Claiming the credit without the required Form IT-225 add-back
- Treating entity PTET payments as personal estimated tax and underpaying, then incurring a penalty
- Claiming the credit in the wrong year for a fiscal-year entity
- Claiming as an indirect owner in a tiered structure
- Claiming PTET alongside the NYC income tax elimination credit
- Missing the annual election deadline, which cannot be cured later
