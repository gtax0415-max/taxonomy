# Solar Energy System Equipment Credit
## Description
Nonrefundable credit for solar equipment installed at a principal residence in New York State. With the federal Section 25D credit terminated after 2025, THIS IS NOW THE PRIMARY TAX BENEFIT for a New York solar installation.
## Amount
- 25% of qualified solar energy system equipment expenditures
- MAXIMUM CREDIT $5,000, which is reached at $20,000 of expenditures
- Nonrefundable, with a FIVE-YEAR CARRYFORWARD of any unused amount
The $5,000 cap is per residence, and it applies across all qualifying installations at that residence — you cannot reset it by installing in stages.
## What qualifies
- Solar ELECTRIC generating equipment (photovoltaic)
- Solar THERMAL equipment for domestic hot water
- Installation costs, including labor and wiring or plumbing to connect the system
The residence must be your PRINCIPAL RESIDENCE in New York State. Second homes and rentals do not qualify, which is narrower than the old federal 25D rule.
## Three ways to acquire the system
All three qualify, which is unusual:
1. PURCHASE — the credit is 25% of the total expenditure, claimed in the year placed in service
2. LEASE of at least ten years — the credit is based on the lease payments
3. POWER PURCHASE AGREEMENT of at least ten years — the credit is based on the payments under the agreement
For a lease or PPA, the credit is claimed over the term rather than all at once, still subject to the $5,000 lifetime cap.
## The nonrefundability problem
This credit can only offset tax you actually owe. A retired couple with low New York tax may take years to absorb $5,000, and after five years the remainder is lost. Estimate the absorption before assuming the full $5,000 is real money. A large Roth conversion or capital gain in the installation year can be the difference between using the credit and losing it.
## Timing
The credit is claimed for the year the equipment is PLACED IN SERVICE, not the year it was ordered or paid for. A system paid for in December and energized in January belongs to the later year. Utility interconnection delays routinely push this across a year boundary.
## Notes
- New York State also exempts residential solar from sales tax, and many localities offer a real property tax exemption — separate benefits, claimed separately
- NYSERDA incentives reduce the cost basis for the credit, so subtract them before computing 25%
- Battery storage on its own is not covered by this credit
## Required Information
- Total system cost, net of NYSERDA and utility incentives
- Placed-in-service date and interconnection date
- Whether purchased, leased, or under a PPA, and the term
- Proof the property is your principal residence
- Any prior-year solar credit claimed on the same residence
## Questions
- Is this your principal residence in New York State?
- What was the total cost after NYSERDA and utility rebates?
- When was the system placed in service and interconnected?
- Did you buy the system, lease it, or sign a power purchase agreement?
- What is your New York tax liability, since the credit is nonrefundable?
## Common Errors
- Computing 25% on the gross cost without subtracting NYSERDA incentives
- Using the payment date rather than the placed-in-service date
- Claiming it for a second home or rental property
- Assuming refundability and expecting a check
- Losing the carryforward by not tracking it for five years
- Expecting the federal credit too, for a 2026 installation
## Prompt
- I put solar panels on my house in New York.
- I leased a solar system for 20 years.
