# Geothermal Energy System Credit
## Description
Nonrefundable credit for ground-source heat pump systems at a principal residence in New York State. The federal Section 25D credit that covered geothermal ended after 2025, leaving this as the main tax benefit for a New York installation.
## Amount
- 25% of qualified geothermal energy system equipment expenditures
- MAXIMUM CREDIT $5,000
- Nonrefundable, with a five-year carryforward
Same structure and same cap as the solar credit, and the two caps are SEPARATE — a home with both solar and geothermal can reach $10,000 total.
## What qualifies
- GROUND-SOURCE HEAT PUMP systems that use the ground or groundwater as a thermal energy source
- The system must meet the ENERGY STAR requirements in effect when installed
- Installation costs including the ground loop, drilling, and trenching, which are typically the largest part of the bill
Air-source heat pumps do NOT qualify. This is the most common misunderstanding — an air-source heat pump is a different technology and gets no New York income tax credit at all, only NYSERDA and utility rebates.
## Eligibility
- Must be installed at your PRINCIPAL RESIDENCE in New York State
- Available for systems placed in service on or after January 1, 2022
- Purchase or a written lease agreement both qualify
## Notes
- Drilling and excavation are usually 40% to 60% of a geothermal job, and they count toward the credit
- NYSERDA offers a separate ground-source heat pump rebate; it reduces the basis for this credit
- Because the cap is hit at $20,000 of cost and most residential geothermal jobs cost more, nearly every claimant lands at exactly $5,000
- The nonrefundability and five-year carryforward work exactly as they do for solar
## Required Information
- Total system cost net of NYSERDA rebates
- Placed-in-service date
- ENERGY STAR compliance documentation
- Confirmation that the system is ground-source, not air-source
- Proof of principal residence
## Questions
- Is the system GROUND-source, using loops in the ground or groundwater?
- Does it meet ENERGY STAR requirements?
- Is this your principal residence in New York State?
- What was the cost after NYSERDA rebates?
- When was it placed in service?
## Common Errors
- Claiming an air-source heat pump, which does not qualify
- Computing the credit before subtracting NYSERDA rebates
- Missing drilling and excavation costs, which do qualify and are substantial
- Claiming for a vacation home
- Expecting a refund from a nonrefundable credit
## Prompt
- I installed a geothermal heat pump.
- Does New York give a credit for ground source heat?
- I put in a heat pump, do I get the geothermal credit?
