# Other Jurisdiction Credits
## Description
Relief from double taxation when a New York resident's income is also taxed somewhere else. This is New York's structural parallel to the federal foreign tax credit — same problem, different boundary. The federal credit relieves tax paid to another COUNTRY; this one relieves tax paid to another STATE.
## Credits in this folder
- Resident Credit (resident-credit.md) — Forms IT-112-R and IT-112-C → Form IT-201, Line 41. Nonrefundable
## Related credits filed elsewhere
- Resident credit against the separate tax on lump-sum distributions — Form IT-112.1, nonrefundable, line 42
- Claim of right credit — Form IT-257, refundable, line 71, in four flavors: NYS, NYC, Yonkers, and MCTMT
- Pass-through entity tax credit — see business-incentive/, which interacts heavily with the resident credit
## The gap worth knowing
The resident credit covers other STATES, the District of Columbia, local governments of other states, and CANADIAN PROVINCES. It does NOT cover other foreign countries. A New York resident taxed by the United Kingdom gets a FEDERAL foreign tax credit and NO New York credit — that income is taxed by New York with no offset. Canada is the sole foreign exception, and it uses its own form.

## Questions
- Did you earn income in another state while living in New York?
- Did you pay income tax to a Canadian province?
- Did you file a nonresident return anywhere else?
- Were you a full-year New York resident?
## Prompt
- I live in New York and work in New Jersey.
- I paid tax to Connecticut on consulting income.
- I have rental property in Pennsylvania.
