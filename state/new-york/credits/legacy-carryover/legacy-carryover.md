# Legacy and Carryover-Only Credits
## Description
Credits that GENERATE NOTHING NEW but still absorb unused amounts from prior years. They exist in the current instructions only so that a carryforward has somewhere to land. If a client mentions one of these, the question is never whether they qualify now — it is whether an unused balance is still sitting in an old return.
Also collected here: the two accumulation distribution credits, which are current-year credits but are computed only on instruction worksheets with no claim form.

## The two credits with no form at all
Solar and wind energy carryover (code 052) and residential fuel oil storage tank carryover (code 054) have NO CLAIM FORM. You enter the amount and the code on one of the open lines between IT-201-ATT lines 6a and 6n, and you must SUBMIT A SCHEDULE showing how the carryforward amount was calculated. Without that schedule the credit is disallowed.
Note that the solar and wind carryover is a DIFFERENT credit from the current solar energy system equipment credit on Form IT-255. The old one no longer generates anything; the new one does. Do not merge them.
## Accumulation distribution credits
Not carryovers, but likewise formless:
| Credit | Jurisdiction | Line | Where computed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accumulation distribution | NYS | 42, via IT-201-ATT line 1 | IT-201-I worksheet |
| Accumulation distribution | NYC | 53, via IT-201-ATT line 9 | IT-201-I worksheet |
Available to a BENEFICIARY OF A TRUST who received an accumulation distribution, for their share of tax the trust already paid. Two limits apply: the credit cannot exceed the proportion of tax attributable to the doubly taxed income, and it cannot reduce your tax below what it would have been if the distribution were excluded entirely. Submit your computation.
## How to work a carryforward
1. Find the year the credit was originally generated and the amount
2. Trace every intervening return to see how much was absorbed
3. Check the carryforward PERIOD — some expire, some do not
4. Reconstruct the schedule, because the Tax Department will ask for it
The most common outcome is discovering a carryforward that quietly expired several returns ago. The second most common is discovering one that is still live and has never been used.

- Do you have unused credit from a prior New York return?
- Which year did it originate, and how much has been used since?
- Do you have the original computation schedule?
- Was your Empire Zone or START-UP NY certification still active?
