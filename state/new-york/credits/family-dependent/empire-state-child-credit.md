# Empire State Child Credit
## Description
Fully refundable credit for full-year New York residents with at least one qualifying child under 17. For tax year 2025 New York DECOUPLED this credit from the federal child tax credit and set its own amounts, tripling the credit for the youngest children.
## Amounts for 2025
| Child's age on December 31 | Credit per child |
|---|---|
| Under 4 | $1,000 |
| 4 through 16 | $330 |
Children 17 and older do not qualify at all.
## Amounts for 2026
| Child's age on December 31 | Credit per child |
|---|---|
| Under 4 | $1,000 |
| 4 through 16 | $500 |
## Income phase-out
The credit is reduced by $16.50 for every $1,000 of federal AGI above the threshold for your filing status:
| Filing status | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Married filing jointly | $110,000 |
| Single, head of household, qualifying surviving spouse | $75,000 |
| Married filing separately | $55,000 |
The reduction applies to the TOTAL credit, not per child. Note that the joint threshold is not double the single threshold.
## No income floor
There is no minimum income requirement. A family with zero income can claim the full credit as cash back, but ONLY BY FILING a New York return. Someone below the $4,000 filing threshold who does not file gets nothing. This is the single largest source of unclaimed New York credit dollars.
## Qualifying child tests
- Your child, sibling, stepsibling, or a descendant of any of them — including stepchild, eligible foster child, adopted child, or a child placed with you for legal adoption
- Lived with you more than half the year
- Under 17 years old on December 31
- Did not provide over half of their own support
- Did not file a joint return, except to claim a refund of withholding or estimated tax
## Identification requirement
A correct and valid SSN OR ITIN is required for each child listed. Unlike the federal child tax credit, which now requires a work-eligible SSN, New York accepts an ITIN. This is a meaningful difference for mixed-status families — a household can be barred from the federal credit and still qualify here.
## Notes
- You must be a full-year New York State resident, or married to a full-year resident
- Unused credit is refunded or can be applied to next year's estimated tax. The Tax Department pays no interest on it
- The credit does not count as income and does not affect eligibility for Medicaid, SNAP, SSI, cash assistance, or housing assistance. Saved refunds are excluded from asset limits for 12 months
- Part-year and nonresident filers do not qualify
## Required Information
- Each child's name, SSN or ITIN, date of birth, and relationship
- Months the child lived with you
- Federal AGI from Form IT-201, line 19
- Filing status
## Questions
- Were you a full-year New York State resident?
- How old was each child on December 31, 2025?
- Does each child have a valid SSN or ITIN?
- What is your federal AGI relative to the threshold for your filing status?
- Did anyone else claim the same child?
## Common Errors
- Assuming the credit still tracks the federal child tax credit — it decoupled for 2025
- Using the wrong age band, since the difference between under-4 and 4-through-16 is $670 per child for 2025
- Not filing at all because income was below the filing threshold, forfeiting a fully refundable credit
- Claiming a child who turned 17 during the year
- Filing Form IT-203 as a part-year resident and claiming this credit anyway
## Prompt
- I have a toddler and live in New York.
- Do I qualify for the Empire State child credit?
- I had no income last year but I have two kids.
