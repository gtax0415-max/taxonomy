# Child and Dependent Care Credit
## Description
Refundable credit for care expenses that let you work or look for work. Unlike most New York credits it is computed as a PERCENTAGE OF THE FEDERAL CREDIT, so federal law changes flow through automatically. New York City residents with very young children may claim a second, stacked city credit on the same form.
## Eligibility hinges on the federal credit
You qualify if you WOULD QUALIFY for the federal child and dependent care credit — whether or not you actually claimed it. Someone with no federal tax liability who skipped Form 2441 can still claim the New York credit, and often should, because the New York version is refundable while the federal one is not.
## New York State amount
The credit is a percentage of the federal credit, sliding by New York AGI. The percentage runs from 110% at the lowest incomes down to 20% at higher incomes, with the step-down occurring across roughly $25,000 to $65,000 of NYAGI. Above that range it holds at 20%.
The consequence worth noticing: at low income the NEW YORK CREDIT EXCEEDS THE FEDERAL ONE, and it is refundable while the federal credit is not.
## New York City amount
A separate city credit for full- or part-year NYC residents:
- Federal AGI of $30,000 or less
- At least one qualifying child UNDER AGE FOUR on December 31
- Worth up to 75% of the New York State credit, phasing down as income approaches $30,000
The under-four age limit is much tighter than the state credit's under-13 rule.
## Refundability differs by residency
The state credit is refundable for everyone. The city credit is refundable for FULL-YEAR city residents (line 64) but NONREFUNDABLE for PART-YEAR city residents, who route it through Form IT-201-ATT Section C to line 53 instead. A part-year resident with no city tax gets nothing.
## Federal changes that flow through
Because the New York credit is a percentage of the federal amount, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act raising the federal top rate from 35% to 50% beginning in 2026 raises the New York credit too, with no change to New York law. The federal dependent care FSA limit rising to $7,500 cuts the other way — FSA dollars reduce the expenses eligible for the credit at both levels.
## Required Information
- A completed federal Form 2441, even if the federal credit was not claimed
- Care provider's name, address, and TIN or SSN
- Each qualifying person's name, SSN, and date of birth
- Total care expenses and any employer dependent care benefits
- New York AGI from Form IT-201, line 33
## Questions
- Did you pay someone to care for a child under 13 or a disabled dependent so you could work?
- Would you have qualified for the federal credit, even if you did not claim it?
- Do you have the provider's taxpayer identification number?
- Are you a New York City resident with a child under four?
- Were you a city resident all year or only part of the year?
## Common Errors
- Skipping the credit because no federal credit was claimed, when New York eligibility only requires that you COULD have claimed it
- Missing the stacked NYC credit for a child under four at incomes at or below $30,000
- Treating the part-year NYC credit as refundable when it is nonrefundable and routes to line 53
- Failing to reduce expenses by employer-provided dependent care benefits
- Missing the provider's TIN, which invalidates the claim
## Prompt
- I paid for daycare so I could work.
- I live in Queens and have a two-year-old in childcare.
- I did not claim the federal childcare credit, can I still claim New York's?
