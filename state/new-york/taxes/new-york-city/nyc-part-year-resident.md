# Part-Year New York City Resident
## Description
If you were a New York City resident for only part of the year, city tax is computed on FORM IT-360.1, Change of City Resident Status, and entered at Form IT-201 line 50 rather than at lines 47 and 47a.
## What Form IT-360.1 does
It computes city tax on the income attributable to your CITY RESIDENT PERIOD. Income earned while a city nonresident is not subject to city tax at all — remember that the city has no nonresident earnings tax.
The form allocates income, additions, subtractions, deductions, and exemptions between the resident and nonresident periods, then applies the city rate schedule to the resident-period amount.
Submit Form IT-360.1 with your return. See Form IT-360.1-I for the detailed instructions.
## Special accruals apply
A part-year city resident is subject to the SPECIAL ACCRUAL RULES if they have accrued income. You have accrued income for the year if you have an item of:
- Income FIXED AND DETERMINABLE in your 2025 city RESIDENT period but not reportable federally in that period, or
- NON-NEW YORK SOURCE income fixed and determinable in your city NONRESIDENT period but not reportable federally in that period
The addition modification is A-115 and the subtraction is S-129, both on Form IT-225.
This catches deferred compensation, installment sale gains, and stock compensation that vests around a move. Anyone moving into or out of the city with equity compensation should be screened for it specifically.
## Full-year state residents can still be part-year city residents
Moving from Manhattan to Westchester in June makes you a FULL-YEAR NEW YORK STATE RESIDENT and a PART-YEAR NEW YORK CITY RESIDENT. You file Form IT-201, not Form IT-203, and attach Form IT-360.1.
This is the most common part-year city situation and it does not change which main return you file.
## Credits are prorated
Several city credits prorate by MONTHS OF CITY RESIDENCE, taken from Item F on Form IT-201:
- NYC school tax credit, fixed amount: a per-month table from $5 to $63 (single) and $10 to $125 (joint)
- NYC household credit
- NYC earned income credit: allocated by the city residency fraction
Item F must be completed. Leaving it blank stalls all of them.
Note one reversal: the NYC child and dependent care credit is REFUNDABLE for a full-year city resident at line 64, but NONREFUNDABLE for a part-year resident, routed through Form IT-201-ATT Section C to line 53.
## Married couples with different city periods
- BOTH part-year: for the school tax credit, use the months of the spouse with the LONGER city residence period
- One FULL-YEAR city resident, one PART-YEAR: compute each credit separately and ADD them. The full-year spouse uses Table 1, the part-year spouse uses Table 2
- One PART-YEAR, one FULL-YEAR CITY NONRESIDENT: only the part-year spouse claims, using Table 2
For the tax itself, the part-year spouse files Form IT-360.1 and the amounts are combined at line 51 along with any separately computed full-year city resident tax.
## Determining the change date
City residency changes when DOMICILE changes, or when the statutory residency test stops being met. Moving your belongings is not by itself a change of domicile. Keep records establishing the date: lease start and end, deed, closing statement, utility transfers, voter and vehicle registration, and where your family lived.
The 184-day statutory test can make you a city resident for the FULL YEAR even after moving out, if you kept a permanent place of abode in the city and spent 184 days or more there. Moving out in November after 300 days in the city does not produce part-year status under that test.
## Required Information
- Exact move date and supporting documentation
- Income and deductions allocated between the two periods
- Months of city residence for Item F
- Accrued income items fixed in one period but reported federally in another
- Spouse's city residency dates, if different
## Questions
- When exactly did you move into or out of New York City?
- Did you keep an apartment in the city after moving?
- How many days were you physically in the city after the move?
- Do you have deferred compensation or unvested equity that spans the move?
- Did your spouse have a different city residency period?
## Common Errors
- Filing Form IT-203 when a move within New York State keeps you a full-year state resident
- Omitting Form IT-360.1 and computing city tax at line 47a instead of line 50
- Leaving Item F blank, which damages every prorated city credit
- Missing special accruals on deferred compensation around the move date
- Claiming the part-year NYC child care credit as refundable
- Assuming part-year status when the 184-day test makes you a full-year city resident
## Prompt
- I moved from Brooklyn to Long Island in June.
- I moved into Manhattan halfway through the year.
- Do I still owe New York City tax after I moved out?
