# New York Standard Deduction
## Description
A flat amount subtracted from New York adjusted gross income when you do not itemize. Set by New York statute, unrelated to the federal standard deduction, and not indexed annually in the way federal amounts are — the New York table has been stable across 2025 and 2026.
## Amount (2026)
| Filing status | Standard deduction |
|---|---|
| Single AND you can be claimed as a dependent on another return | $3,100 |
| Single AND you cannot be claimed as a dependent | $8,000 |
| Married filing jointly | $16,050 |
| Married filing separately | $8,000 |
| Head of household with a qualifying person | $11,200 |
| Qualifying surviving spouse | $16,050 |
Confirmed for 2026 from Form IT-2105-I (2026). Identical to the 2025 table.
## The dependent reduction
A single filer who CAN be claimed as a dependent on someone else's federal return gets $3,100 instead of $8,000 — a $4,900 difference. Item C on Form IT-201 controls this, and you must mark Yes even if the other taxpayer chose not to claim you.
This same Item C answer also disqualifies you from the household credit and the New York City school tax credit, so one checkbox drives several outcomes.
## Choosing between standard and itemized
1. Find your standard deduction in the table above
2. Complete Form IT-196 to get your New York itemized total (line 49)
3. Enter the LARGER of the two on Form IT-201 line 34
4. Mark the Standard or Itemized box accordingly
5. If you itemize, submit Form IT-196 with the return
The federal choice does not bind you. You may take the federal standard deduction and itemize for New York, or the reverse.
## Married filing separately
Both spouses must take the standard deduction UNLESS BOTH elect to itemize on their New York returns. You cannot have one spouse itemizing and the other taking the standard deduction. This requires coordination between two separately filed returns.
## What New York does NOT have
- NO additional standard deduction for age 65 or older
- NO additional standard deduction for blindness
- NO charitable deduction for non-itemizers
All three exist federally in some form. A New York senior gets no age-based bump, which further tilts the calculation toward itemizing for retirees with property taxes.
## Required Information
- Filing status
- Whether you can be claimed as a dependent (Item C)
- Your Form IT-196 line 49 total, for the comparison
## Questions
- What is your filing status for New York?
- Can anyone claim you as a dependent on their federal return?
- Did you add up your potential New York itemized deductions before defaulting to the standard?
- If married filing separately, is your spouse itemizing?
## Common Errors
- Assuming the federal standard deduction applies to New York
- Defaulting to the standard deduction because the federal standard deduction was taken
- Missing the $3,100 dependent reduction, or claiming $8,000 while marked as a dependent at Item C
- One spouse itemizing while the other takes the standard deduction on separate returns
- Expecting an age-65 or blindness addition that New York does not provide
## Prompt
- What is the New York standard deduction for my filing status?
- I am claimed as a dependent by my parents.
- Can I take the standard deduction federally and itemize in New York?
