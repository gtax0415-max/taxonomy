# Casualty and Theft Losses
## Description
Losses from theft, fire, storm, vandalism, accidents, and similar causes. THE MOST VALUABLE SINGLE DIFFERENCE in Form IT-196: federal law allows a casualty loss deduction only for FEDERALLY DECLARED DISASTERS, while New York allows it for ordinary casualties and thefts under 2017 rules.
## What New York allows that federal law does not
A house fire, a burglary, a car accident, vandalism, or a bank failure produces NO federal deduction unless a federal disaster was declared. For New York, all of these can be deductible.
This is worth checking with any client who had a significant uninsured loss. They will often have been told, correctly, that there is no federal deduction and will not think to ask about New York.
## The two floors
1. Each separate casualty or theft loss must exceed $100
2. Total losses for the year, after the $100 reductions, must exceed 10% OF FEDERAL AGI from Form IT-201 line 19
Both floors are 2017 rules. The 10% floor makes this a deduction for large losses only — a $4,000 uninsured theft produces nothing for a household with $100,000 of AGI.
## Computing the loss
For each property: the deductible amount is the SMALLER of the adjusted basis or the decline in fair market value, reduced by insurance or other reimbursement.
Reimbursement counts even if you DID NOT FILE A CLAIM. The instructions give the example directly: a $2,000 car destroyed, insured with a $500 deductible, no claim filed to avoid a policy cancellation — you must still enter $1,500 as reimbursement. Deciding not to claim does not convert an insured loss into a deductible one.
## Three worksheet sections
- SECTION A: personal use property. Result goes to line 20
- SECTION B: business and income-producing property. Result goes to line 30 (income-producing) or line 24 (property used as an employee)
- SECTION C: Ponzi-type investment schemes under IRS Revenue Procedure 2009-20
Property used partly for business and partly personally, such as a home with a rental unit, is split between Sections A and B.
## Ponzi-type losses
Section C replaces Appendix A of IRS Revenue Procedure 2009-20. The safe harbor deducts 95% of the qualified investment if you have no potential third-party recovery, or 75% if you do. Qualified investment is initial plus subsequent investments plus income previously reported, minus withdrawals.
There is a related item on the main return: Form IT-201 special condition code 56 is entered when reporting a New York theft loss deduction from a Ponzi-type arrangement using the federal safe harbor. Submit a copy of the statement made under Revenue Procedure 2009-20.
## Corrosive drywall
Amounts paid to repair a personal residence or household appliances damaged by corrosive drywall may be deductible under the special procedure in 2017 IRS Publication 547.
## Costs of proving the loss
Appraisal fees and photographs used to establish the amount of the loss are NOT part of the casualty loss itself. They go on Form IT-196 line 24 as other expenses.
## Required Information
- Description, location, and acquisition date of each damaged or stolen item
- Cost or other basis, including improvements
- Fair market value immediately before and immediately after
- Insurance or other reimbursement received OR EXPECTED, whether or not claimed
- Federal AGI, for the 10% floor
## Questions
- What was lost or damaged, and how?
- Was it insured, and did you file a claim?
- What did the property cost, and what was it worth before and after?
- Was the property personal, business, or income-producing?
- Was a federal disaster declared, which changes the routing?
## Common Errors
- Assuming no deduction exists because there is none federally
- Failing to reduce the loss by insurance you chose not to claim
- Applying the $100 and 10% floors in the wrong order
- Putting income-producing property losses on line 20 instead of line 30
- Including appraisal costs in the loss rather than on line 24
- Deducting a loss already claimed on an estate tax return
## Prompt
- My house was burglarized and insurance did not cover everything.
- I had a fire and lost furniture.
- I lost money in an investment fraud.
