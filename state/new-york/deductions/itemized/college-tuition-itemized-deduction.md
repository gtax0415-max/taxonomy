# College Tuition Itemized Deduction
## Description
An alternative to the college tuition CREDIT. Same expenses, same form, two mutually exclusive outcomes. Up to $10,000 of qualified undergraduate tuition PER STUDENT as a New York itemized deduction.
## The election
| | Credit | Deduction |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum | $400 per student | $10,000 of tuition per student |
| Refundable | Yes | No — it is a deduction |
| Requires itemizing | No | YES |
| Where it lands | Form IT-201, line 68 | Form IT-196, line 48 |
Complete Form IT-272 either way. The form computes both and shows which is larger.
## Which one wins
The deduction only helps if your total New York itemized deductions EXCEED your New York standard deduction. If you would otherwise take the $8,000 or $16,050 standard deduction and tuition is your only itemized item, the deduction is worth nothing and the credit is worth up to $400 cash.
For a filer already itemizing, $10,000 of deduction at a 5.40% marginal rate is roughly $540 of state tax, plus city tax — more than the $400 credit. Add New York City tax and the gap widens.
Rough guide: low and middle income, or not itemizing, take the CREDIT. Already itemizing with substantial tuition, take the DEDUCTION. Always run both.
## The high-income squeeze
For a Form IT-201 filer, the tuition deduction enters at line 48, AFTER the line 46 high-income adjustment. It therefore escapes the cutback that strips other itemized deductions at high income.
But the practical value still falls as income rises, and above $1,000,000 of New York AGI the surrounding deduction has largely been eliminated, which can push a high earner back toward the credit — except that the credit itself is unavailable at those income levels in practice. Run Form IT-272 rather than assuming.
## Qualified expenses
TUITION ONLY, for an UNDERGRADUATE degree program, for you, your spouse, or a dependent you claim.
Excluded: room and board, books and supplies, activity and athletic fees, insurance, application and registration fees, and anything paid with tax-free scholarships, grants, or the earnings portion of a 529 distribution.
## Residency routing
- IT-201 filers (full-year residents): the deduction goes on Form IT-196 line 48
- IT-203 filers (nonresidents and part-year residents): use Form IT-203-B line 2 and enter it on Form IT-196 line 43, which sits BEFORE the line 46 adjustment
The different placement means the same deduction is treated differently depending on residency status.
## Required Information
- Each student's name and SSN
- Institution name and address
- Tuition paid per student, separated from all other charges
- Scholarships, grants, and 529 amounts applied to tuition
- Your New York itemized total and standard deduction, for the comparison
## Questions
- Was the tuition for an undergraduate degree program?
- How much was tuition alone?
- Are you itemizing for New York?
- Do your New York itemized deductions exceed your standard deduction?
- How many students are you claiming?
## Common Errors
- Claiming both the credit and the deduction for the same student
- Taking the deduction while claiming the standard deduction, producing no benefit
- Including fees, books, or room and board
- Failing to reduce tuition by scholarships and 529 earnings
- Using line 43 as a full-year resident, or line 48 as a nonresident
## Prompt
- Should I take the New York tuition credit or the deduction?
- I paid $22,000 in tuition for two kids.
- I itemize in New York and my daughter is in college.
