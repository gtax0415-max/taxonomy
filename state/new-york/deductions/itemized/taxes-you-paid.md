# Taxes You Paid
## Description
State and local taxes on Form IT-196 lines 5 through 9. The headline is that NEW YORK DOES NOT APPLY THE FEDERAL SALT CAP. The qualification, which matters just as much, is that New York then removes income taxes at line 41.
## No federal cap
For federal purposes the deduction for state and local taxes is limited to $40,000 ($20,000 married filing separately) for 2025, with the limit reduced as modified AGI rises. Your New York deduction is NOT subject to that limit.
New York also allows FOREIGN REAL ESTATE TAXES on line 8, which the federal system eliminated entirely.
## The line 41 giveback
Lines 5 through 9 let you enter state and local income taxes. Line 41 then requires you to SUBTRACT them.
Net effect on a New York return:
| Tax paid | Deductible for New York? |
|---|---|
| New York real property tax | YES, uncapped |
| Personal property tax | YES |
| General sales tax | YES |
| Foreign real estate tax | YES |
| State and local INCOME tax | NO — removed at line 41 |
| Foreign income tax | NO — removed at line 41 |
So a New York City resident with $30,000 of state and city income tax and $12,000 of property tax deducts the $12,000, not the $42,000. Describing New York as having "no SALT cap" without this qualification overstates the benefit substantially.
## Property tax credits reduce the deduction
If you claimed a New York property tax credit or received a property tax REBATE CHECK, and you itemize, you must REDUCE the real estate taxes reported on line 6 by the total credit claimed and rebate received.
This catches STAR recipients. A homeowner receiving a STAR check who itemizes for New York must net that check against the property taxes deducted here. Non-itemizers do nothing.
## Which year's rules
If you claimed the SALT deduction federally, use the amounts from federal Schedule A lines 5a, 5b, and 5c. If you did NOT itemize federally, compute lines 5 through 8 as if you had, using the 2017 federal Schedule A instructions.
## Paid, not assessed
The deduction is for taxes PAID during the calendar year. A December bill paid in January belongs to the later year. Prepaying a following year's installment in December accelerates the deduction, subject to the usual rule that the tax must have been assessed.
## Required Information
- Real property taxes paid during the year, by parcel
- Any STAR credit check or property tax credit received
- Personal property taxes paid
- General sales tax, if elected instead of income tax federally
- Foreign real estate taxes
- State and local income taxes, needed for the line 41 subtraction
## Questions
- How much New York property tax did you pay during the calendar year?
- Did you receive a STAR check or any property tax rebate?
- Do you own real estate outside the United States?
- Did you pay income tax to another state, which comes back out at line 41?
## Common Errors
- Applying the federal $40,000 cap to the New York return
- Forgetting the line 41 subtraction and deducting state and local income taxes for New York
- Failing to reduce property taxes by a STAR or rebate check while itemizing
- Deducting taxes assessed but not yet paid
- Omitting foreign real estate taxes, which New York still allows
## Prompt
- Does New York have a SALT cap?
- I pay $18,000 in property taxes on Long Island.
- Can I deduct my New York City income tax on my New York return?
