# Job Expenses and Certain Miscellaneous Deductions
## Description
Form IT-196 lines 21 through 26, subject to a 2% of federal AGI floor. ELIMINATED FEDERALLY by the TCJA and fully alive for New York. For an employee with real out-of-pocket work costs, this is often what makes New York itemizing worthwhile on its own.
## The four lines
- Line 21: unreimbursed employee business expenses, from the worksheet in the IT-196 instructions
- Line 22: job-related education expenses
- Line 23: tax preparation fees
- Line 24: other expenses, including investment and custodial fees
Line 25 totals them; line 26 applies the 2% of federal AGI floor; line 27 is the excess.
## Line 21: unreimbursed employee expenses
Computed on the Unreimbursed employee business expenses worksheet, which reconstructs 2017-era federal Form 2106. Vehicle expenses, parking and tolls, overnight travel, business meals at 50% (80% for employees under Department of Transportation hours-of-service limits), and other job costs, reduced by any employer reimbursement not already in Form W-2 box 1.
The 2025 standard mileage rate used on the worksheet is 70 cents per business mile. COMMUTING MILES NEVER COUNT.
Four groups deduct these expenses ABOVE the line federally and enter only the EXCESS here: Armed Forces reservists traveling over 100 miles, qualified performing artists, fee-basis state or local government officials, and individuals with impairment-related work expenses. The last group enters the amount on line 36 instead, where the 2% floor does not apply.
## Line 22: job-related education
Deductible if the education MAINTAINS OR IMPROVES skills required in your present work, or is required by your employer or by law to keep your salary, status, or job.
NOT deductible if it meets the minimum requirements to qualify you for your trade, or is part of a program leading to a NEW trade or business — even if you never intend to enter that new trade. Law school for a paralegal fails. A continuing education course for a licensed professional passes.
## Line 23: tax preparation fees
Fees paid DURING the year, so 2025 fees for preparing the 2024 return go on the 2025 form. Includes tax software, tax publications, and e-filing fees. Exclude anything already deducted on Schedule C, E, or F.
## Line 24: other expenses
- Legal and accounting fees to produce or collect taxable income
- Clerical help and office rent
- Custodial and trust account fees
- Your share of a regulated investment company's investment expenses
- Losses on non-federally-insured deposits in a failed institution
- Casualty and theft losses of property used as an employee
- Claim of right repayments of $3,000 or less
- The convenience fee charged for paying income tax by credit or debit card
Keep a written list of the type and amount of each item claimed on line 24.
## The 2% floor
Total these lines and subtract 2% of federal AGI. At $100,000 of AGI, the first $2,000 produces nothing. This makes the category meaningful mainly for people with substantial unreimbursed costs — tradespeople, outside salespeople, performers, and employees with large investment accounts.
## Required Information
- Mileage log with business, commuting, and total miles by vehicle
- Receipts for tools, uniforms, dues, publications, and supplies
- Employer reimbursements, including Form W-2 box 12 code L amounts
- Tax preparation and software costs paid during the year
- Investment, custodial, and advisory fees
- Federal AGI, for the 2% floor
## Questions
- Did you pay for work expenses your employer did not reimburse?
- Do you drive for work, excluding your commute?
- Did you pay professional dues, licensing fees, or buy tools or uniforms?
- What did you pay to have last year's return prepared?
- Do you pay investment advisory or custodial fees?
## Common Errors
- Skipping the category because it no longer exists federally
- Including commuting miles as business miles
- Deducting education that qualifies you for a new trade or business
- Failing to subtract employer reimbursements
- Including expenses already deducted on Schedule C, E, or F
- Missing that impairment-related work expenses go to line 36, free of the 2% floor
## Prompt
- I bought my own tools for work and my boss did not reimburse me.
- Can I deduct union dues in New York?
- I paid an accountant to do my taxes.
