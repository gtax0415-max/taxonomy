# New York Itemized Deductions
## Description
Computed on Form IT-196 using the federal rules AS THEY EXISTED FOR TAX YEAR 2017 — before the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act. This freeze is why New York itemizing is worth checking even when the federal return takes the standard deduction: several categories the TCJA eliminated are still fully deductible for New York.

## The four categories the TCJA killed that New York kept
| Deduction | Federal after 2017 | New York |
|---|---|---|
| Unreimbursed employee expenses | Gone | ALLOWED |
| Tax preparation fees | Gone | ALLOWED |
| Investment and custodial fees | Gone | ALLOWED |
| Casualty and theft loss, non-disaster | Gone | ALLOWED |
Plus two more where New York is simply more generous: state and local taxes are NOT capped, and foreign real estate taxes ARE deductible.
## Form IT-196 structure
```
Lines 1-4     Medical and dental
Lines 5-9     Taxes you paid
Lines 10-15   Interest you paid
Lines 16-19   Gifts to charity
Line 20       Casualty and theft losses
Lines 21-26   Job expenses and certain miscellaneous (2% floor)
Lines 29-39   Other itemized deductions (no 2% floor)
Line 40       Total, after the 2017 federal overall limitation
Line 41       New York SUBTRACTION adjustments
Line 44       New York ADDITION adjustments
Line 46       New York itemized deduction adjustment (high income)
Line 49       New York itemized deduction → Form IT-201, Line 34
```
Lines 41 through 46 have no federal counterpart at all. They are where New York takes back some of what it gave.
## Line 41: the giveback that surprises people
New York requires you to SUBTRACT the state, local, and foreign INCOME taxes you deducted on lines 5 through 9. New York will not let you deduct its own income tax against itself.
What survives line 41: real property taxes, personal property taxes, general sales tax, and foreign real estate taxes. What does not: income taxes to any jurisdiction.
So "no SALT cap for New York" is true but incomplete. The uncapped part is the PROPERTY and SALES tax piece.
## Line 44: addition adjustments
New York permits deductions federal law does not, principally expenses connected to income that is exempt federally but TAXABLE in New York. Amortizable bond premium on out-of-state municipal bonds is the common case: New York taxes that interest, so New York allows the offsetting premium deduction.
## What you cannot double-count
You cannot claim a New York itemized deduction for anything already deducted on federal Schedule C, Schedule E, or Schedule F. Business expenses belong on those schedules, not here.
## Married filing separately
If both spouses file separate New York returns, BOTH must itemize or BOTH must take the standard deduction. Table 1 in the Form IT-196 instructions allocates jointly paid expenses — generally half of medical expenses paid from a joint account, and for property held as tenants by the entirety, only the portion you alone paid.
