# Gifts to Charity
## Description
Charitable contributions on Form IT-196 lines 16 through 19. Unlike most of Form IT-196, this category uses CURRENT-YEAR federal rules, not 2017 rules. It is also the category that survives longest as income rises — at the top of the New York limitation scale, charitable contributions are the only itemized deduction left standing.
## Lines
- Line 16: gifts by cash or check, from federal Schedule A line 11
- Line 16a: the portion of line 16 you elected to treat as QUALIFIED CONTRIBUTIONS
- Line 16b: contributions included on line 16a made to the New York Charitable Gifts Trust Fund
- Line 17: gifts other than by cash or check, from federal Schedule A line 12
- Line 18: carryover from a prior year, from federal Schedule A line 13
## Why line 16a matters
Qualified contributions are treated differently in the Form IT-196 line 40 limitation worksheet — they sit in the protected group that is not subject to the overall reduction. Reporting them on line 16a rather than leaving them buried in line 16 preserves deduction value for high-income filers.
## The New York Charitable Gifts Trust Fund
Line 16b captures contributions to two accounts: the Health Charitable Account and the Elementary and Secondary Education Account. These were created as a SALT-cap workaround, letting a taxpayer contribute and claim both a federal charitable deduction and a New York credit.
If you contributed AND claimed a New York itemized deduction for it, the amount feeds the Line 47 worksheet in the Form IT-201 instructions, which adds it back in computing New York City taxable income. Flag any line 16b entry as having downstream city consequences.
## Charitable survives the high-income limitation
Above roughly $525,000 of New York AGI, the Form IT-196 line 46 adjustment strips out non-charitable deductions, leaving charitable contributions as the effective remainder. Above $1,000,000, only a portion of charitable survives; above $10,000,000, less still. See itemized-deduction-limitations.md.
The planning point: for a very high income New York filer, charitable giving is close to the only itemized deduction that still moves the New York number.
## The food pantry conflict
You may NOT use the same qualified donation to a food pantry to claim BOTH a charitable contribution itemized deduction AND the farm donations to food pantries credit on Form IT-649. Pick one.
## Required Information
- Cash and check contributions by organization
- Noncash contributions with fair market value and basis
- Written acknowledgments for gifts of $250 or more
- Whether any contributions were elected as qualified contributions
- Contributions to the New York Charitable Gifts Trust Fund, by account
- Prior-year carryover
## Questions
- What did you give to charity in cash and by check?
- Did you donate property, and was it appraised?
- Did you contribute to the New York Charitable Gifts Trust Fund?
- Do you have a charitable carryover from a prior year?
- Is your New York AGI high enough that charitable is your only surviving deduction?
## Common Errors
- Using 2017 rules for this category, which follows current-year federal rules
- Failing to break out qualified contributions on line 16a, losing protection in the line 40 worksheet
- Missing the New York City add-back triggered by a line 16b entry
- Claiming both the food pantry credit and a deduction for the same donation
- Omitting a prior-year carryover
## Prompt
- I donated to charity last year.
- I gave a car to a nonprofit.
- I contributed to the New York Charitable Gifts Trust Fund.
