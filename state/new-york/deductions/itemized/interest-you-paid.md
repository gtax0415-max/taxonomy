# Interest You Paid
## Description
Home mortgage interest, points, and investment interest, on Form IT-196 lines 10 through 15. Computed using the federal rules that applied to TAX YEAR 2017, which are more generous than current federal law in two specific ways.
## The 2017 rules that still apply for New York
| Item | 2017 rules, which New York uses | Current federal |
|---|---|---|
| Acquisition debt limit | $1,000,000 ($500,000 MFS) | $750,000 ($375,000 MFS) |
| Home equity debt | $100,000 ($50,000 MFS) deductible REGARDLESS OF USE | Only if used to buy, build, or improve |
Both differences favor New York taxpayers, and both matter most in high-cost downstate markets where mortgages routinely exceed $750,000.
## Home equity interest is the bigger gap
Under current federal law, interest on a home equity loan used to pay off credit cards, buy a car, or pay tuition is NOT deductible at all. Under the 2017 rules New York uses, up to $100,000 of such debt IS deductible no matter what the money was spent on.
A homeowner with a $100,000 HELOC used for debt consolidation deducts nothing federally and deducts the full interest for New York.
## Acquisition debt over $750,000
A $900,000 mortgage taken out to buy a home: federal deduction limited to the interest on $750,000; New York allows interest on the full $900,000, since it is under the $1,000,000 2017 ceiling.
## Debt dated on or before October 13, 1987
Mortgages predating October 14, 1987 are grandfathered with no dollar limit. If you REFINANCED such a mortgage, treat the new loan as pre-October 1987 debt up to the balance of the old loan; only the excess is treated as newer debt.
## What lines to use
- Line 10: mortgage interest and points reported to you on federal Form 1098
- Line 11: mortgage interest paid to someone who did NOT give you a Form 1098, including seller financing. Write the recipient's name, identifying number, and address
- Line 12: points not reported on Form 1098
- Line 14: investment interest. Uses CURRENT-year federal rules, from federal Schedule A line 9, not 2017 rules
## Points
Points paid purely to borrow money are generally deducted over the life of the loan. Points on a REFINANCE must be amortized over the loan term, except that the portion attributable to improving your main home may be deducted in the year paid.
## Seller-financed mortgages
If you paid interest to the person who sold you the home and received no Form 1098, report it on line 11 and provide that person's name, SSN or EIN, and address. You must also give the recipient your own SSN.
## Required Information
- Form 1098 from each lender
- Origination date of each mortgage, which determines which limit applies
- Original loan amount and current balance
- How home equity proceeds were used, for the federal comparison
- Points paid and whether the loan was a purchase or a refinance
- Fair market value of the home, if total mortgages exceed it
## Questions
- What is the current balance on each mortgage secured by your home?
- When was each loan taken out?
- Do you have a home equity loan or HELOC, and what was the money used for?
- Did you refinance, and if so did you take cash out?
- Did you pay points at closing?
## Common Errors
- Applying the current federal $750,000 limit to the New York return
- Omitting home equity interest for New York because it was not deductible federally
- Deducting refinance points all at once instead of amortizing
- Treating a full refinance of a pre-1987 mortgage as grandfathered, when only the old balance is
- Using 2017 rules for INVESTMENT interest on line 14, which follows current-year rules
## Prompt
- I have a $900,000 mortgage on my house in Westchester.
- I took out a HELOC to pay off credit cards.
- Can I deduct more mortgage interest in New York than federally?
