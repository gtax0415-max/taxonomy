# Medical and Dental Expenses
## Description
Unreimbursed medical and dental expenses above a percentage-of-AGI floor. Form IT-196 lines 1 through 4. This is the one major itemized category where New York follows CURRENT federal rules rather than 2017 rules.
## How it works
- Line 1: total medical and dental expenses
- Line 2: federal AGI from Form IT-201, line 19
- Line 3: the AGI floor
- Line 4: line 1 minus line 3, the deductible amount
## Which year's rules apply
CURRENT-YEAR federal rules, not 2017 rules. The Form IT-196 instructions direct you to the 2025 federal Schedule A instructions and 2025 IRS Publication 502. This is unusual within Form IT-196, where most categories are frozen at 2017 — worth noting so you do not reach for the 2017 publication out of habit.
## If you did not itemize federally
Compute the amount AS IF you had, using the current-year federal Schedule A instructions, and enter the result on line 1. You do not need to have filed a federal Schedule A. This is the general pattern throughout Form IT-196.
## What counts
The federal definition applies: payments for diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment, or prevention of disease, and for treatments affecting any part or function of the body. Health insurance premiums paid with AFTER-TAX dollars, long-term care premiums within age-based limits, prescription drugs, and medical travel all qualify.
What does not: premiums already paid pre-tax through an employer plan, amounts reimbursed by insurance or an HSA or FSA, and cosmetic procedures.
## The long-term care overlap
Long-term care insurance premiums can appear in TWO places on a New York return — here as a medical expense, and as the basis for the long-term care insurance credit on Form IT-249. Confirm the current instruction language before using the same premium dollars in both places.
## Married filing separately
If expenses were paid from a joint checking account in which both spouses have an equal interest, each may deduct HALF the total medical expenses, unless you can show you alone paid them.
## Required Information
- Total unreimbursed medical and dental expenses for the year
- Federal AGI from Form IT-201, line 19
- Insurance reimbursements received
- Whether premiums were paid pre-tax or after-tax
## Questions
- What were your total out-of-pocket medical costs, net of reimbursement?
- Were your health insurance premiums paid with after-tax dollars?
- Did you pay long-term care premiums, which may also support a credit?
- Did you pay for a dependent's medical care?
## Common Errors
- Using 2017 federal rules here, when this category follows current-year rules
- Including premiums already excluded from income pre-tax
- Failing to subtract insurance and HSA reimbursements
- Missing the deduction entirely because no federal Schedule A was filed
- Deducting the full expense rather than the amount above the floor
## Prompt
- Can I deduct medical bills on my New York return?
- I had major surgery last year and paid a lot out of pocket.
- My health insurance premiums are expensive.
