# Other Itemized Deductions
## Description
Form IT-196 lines 29 through 39. These are NOT subject to the 2% floor, which makes them more valuable dollar for dollar than the line 21 through 26 group.
## What is here
| Line | Deduction |
|---|---|
| 29 | Gambling losses |
| 30 | Casualty and theft losses of income-producing property |
| 31 | Federal estate tax on income in respect of a decedent |
| 32 | Amortizable bond premium |
| 34 | Claim of right repayment over $3,000 |
| 35 | Unrecovered investment in a pension or annuity |
| 36 | Impairment-related work expenses of a disabled person |
| 37 | Federal qualified disaster loss |
| 38 | Other itemized deductions from partnerships, type not specified |
Do not enter an amount on line 39 without amounts on lines 29 through 38 — a bare line 39 entry may be disallowed.
## Gambling losses (line 29)
New York limits gambling loss deductions to the amount of GAMBLING INCOME REPORTED ON YOUR RETURN. Federal rules for gambling losses changed; New York uses the 2017 rules.
Three requirements:
- You cannot deduct losses exceeding winnings for the year
- You cannot net losses against winnings and report only the difference
- You must report the FULL winnings as income on Form IT-201 line 16 and claim losses separately here
A filer with $20,000 of winnings and $20,000 of losses reports $20,000 of income and a $20,000 deduction. If they take the standard deduction for New York, they pay tax on the full $20,000 of winnings with no offset. This is the single most punitive interaction in the New York return for casual gamblers, and it is a strong reason to itemize.
Form 1040NR filers cannot claim New York gambling losses at all.
### The diary requirement
Keep a record showing the date and type of each wager, the name and address of the establishment, the names of others present, and amounts won or lost. Supporting documentation by game type: keno tickets validated by the house, slot machine numbers with dates and times, table numbers or pit credit records, bingo ticket counts, racing tickets and payment records, lottery ticket purchase records.
## Impairment-related work expenses (line 36)
Attendant care at your place of work and other workplace expenses that let you work, for a person with a physical or mental disability. NOT subject to the 2% limit, which is why these come here rather than to line 21.
## Amortizable bond premium (line 32)
For FEDERALLY TAXABLE bonds. Premium on bonds issued by other states — which New York taxes even though the federal government does not — goes to line 44 as an ADDITION adjustment instead, if not attributable to a trade or business. If it is attributable to a trade or business, it becomes a Form IT-225 subtraction modification instead.
## Claim of right (line 34)
Repayment of more than $3,000 included in income in an earlier year. Repayments of $3,000 or less go to line 24 and are subject to the 2% floor. There is also a claim of right CREDIT on Form IT-257, in state, city, Yonkers, and MCTMT versions — compare the deduction and the credit.
## Required Information
- Gambling winnings from Form W-2G and the corresponding diary of losses
- Estate tax attributable to income in respect of a decedent
- Bond premium computations and whether the bond is federally taxable
- Repayment amounts and the year the income was originally reported
- Partnership statements for line 38 amounts
## Questions
- Did you have gambling winnings, and do you have records of losses?
- Did you inherit income in respect of a decedent from a taxable estate?
- Did you repay income you reported in an earlier year?
- Do you have impairment-related work expenses?
## Common Errors
- Netting gambling losses against winnings instead of reporting both separately
- Deducting gambling losses exceeding winnings
- Claiming gambling losses without a contemporaneous diary
- Putting impairment-related expenses on line 21, where the 2% floor would consume them
- Entering an amount on line 39 with nothing on lines 29 through 38
- Using line 32 for tax-exempt bond premium, which belongs on line 44
## Prompt
- I won money at a casino and lost some too.
- I had to pay back a bonus from a prior year.
- I inherited an IRA from an estate that paid estate tax.
