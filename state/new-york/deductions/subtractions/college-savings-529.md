# New York's 529 College Savings Program Deduction
## Description
A subtraction for contributions to New York's own 529 plan. One of the few New York tax benefits available for a current-year action taken before December 31, which makes it a live year-end planning item.
## Amount
- Up to $5,000 of contributions per year
- Up to $10,000 for MARRIED TAXPAYERS FILING A JOINT RETURN
- $5,000 for individuals, heads of household, married filing separately, and qualifying surviving spouses
2025 figures, carried forward as the 2026 proxy.
The cap is PER ACCOUNT OWNER, not per beneficiary. Contributing to accounts for three children does not triple it.
## You must be the ACCOUNT OWNER
Only the account owner may take the subtraction. A grandparent contributing to an account owned by the parent gets NOTHING — the parent, who owns the account, would claim it. To get the deduction, the grandparent must open their own account.
## New York plans only
Only New York's 529 College Savings Program Direct Plan and the Advisor Guided College Savings Program qualify. Contributions to another state's 529 produce no New York subtraction, and TRANSFERRING an account to another state's plan is treated as a NONQUALIFIED WITHDRAWAL requiring an add-back.
## The line 30 worksheet
- Line 1: contributions made during the year, up to the cap
- Line 2: the portion of any Qualified Tuition Program distribution included in your federal AGI
- Line 3: add them; this is the line 30 amount
Line 2 exists because certain distributions land in federal AGI and New York removes them.
## Beneficiaries
If you received a withdrawal AS A BENEFICIARY and included part of it in federal AGI, and did not already include it as an account owner on worksheet line 2, enter that amount directly on line 30.
## The add-back trap: line 22
Nonqualified withdrawals are ADDED BACK at line 22 using a separate worksheet, and the add-back recaptures prior years' subtractions cumulatively. The line 22 worksheet requires TOTAL CURRENT AND PRIOR YEARS' contributions, subtractions, and additions — so the worksheets must be retained year after year. The instructions say so explicitly: keep the line 30 worksheet for future-year calculations of the line 22 worksheet.
What counts as NONQUALIFIED for New York, even where federal law is more permissive:
- TRANSFER TO ANOTHER STATE'S PROGRAM, for the same or a different beneficiary
- K-12 TUITION. New York limits qualified use to post-secondary institutions, so elementary and secondary tuition is nonqualified even though federal law permits it
- Student loan principal or interest paid BEFORE September 5, 2024
- Roth IRA rollovers made BEFORE September 5, 2024
- Postsecondary credentialing expenses not paid to an eligible educational institution
The K-12 divergence is the one that costs families money without warning. A New York family using 529 funds for private school tuition gets federal treatment as qualified and a New York add-back.
## What is qualified
- Qualified higher education expenses at an eligible institution under Education Law 695-b(5)
- Qualified postsecondary credentialing expenses on or after July 4, 2025
- Student loan principal and interest for the beneficiary or a sibling, on or after September 5, 2024
- Roth IRA rollovers under IRC 529(c)(3)(E), on or after September 5, 2024
- Any withdrawal resulting from the death or disability of the beneficiary
- Rollovers to a qualified ABLE program
Transfers between family members' accounts WITHIN New York's program, not disbursed in cash or in kind, are not distributions and require no add-back.
## Refund direct deposit
Form IT-195 lets you deposit part of a New York refund directly into up to three NYS 529 accounts. You need not be the account owner to do that — but only an account owner gets the subtraction. Routing number for all NYS 529 accounts is 011001234; plan code 552 for the Direct Plan, 553 for the Advisor Guided Program.
## Required Information
- Contributions made during the calendar year, by account
- Whether you are the account owner
- Distributions and how the funds were used
- Cumulative prior-year contributions, subtractions, and additions, for the line 22 worksheet
## Questions
- Did you contribute to a New York 529 plan, and are you the account owner?
- Is the plan New York's, or another state's?
- Did you take any withdrawals, and what were they used for?
- Did you use 529 funds for K-12 tuition, which New York treats as nonqualified?
- Do you have prior-year worksheets?
## Common Errors
- A grandparent claiming the subtraction on an account owned by the parent
- Claiming for another state's 529 plan
- Multiplying the cap by the number of children
- Using 529 funds for private K-12 tuition and missing the New York add-back
- Discarding the line 30 worksheet, making the line 22 computation impossible later
- Rolling to another state's plan without recognizing the add-back
## Prompt
- I put money in a 529 for my daughter.
- Can I deduct 529 contributions in New York?
- I used 529 money for my son's private high school.
