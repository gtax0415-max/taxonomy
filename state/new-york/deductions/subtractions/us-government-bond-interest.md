# U.S. Government Bond Interest
## Description
Interest on obligations of the United States is exempt from state income tax by FEDERAL LAW (31 U.S.C. 3124), not by New York's choice. New York implements the exemption as a subtraction at line 28.
## What qualifies
- U.S. Treasury bills, notes, and bonds
- U.S. Savings Bonds, Series EE, I, and HH
- Obligations of federal agencies where the enabling statute grants the exemption
Interest appears in federal Form 1099-INT BOX 3, separate from box 1. Box 3 is the starting point.
## The mutual fund rule
Dividends from a regulated investment company that invests in U.S. government obligations qualify ONLY IF the fund meets the 50% ASSET REQUIREMENT EACH QUARTER.
If it does, you may subtract the portion of the dividend derived from federal obligations — not the whole dividend. Funds publish this percentage annually, usually in January, in a "government obligations" or "state tax information" supplement. Multiply your total dividend by that percentage.
Contact the fund if the statement is not available. Without the percentage, the subtraction cannot be substantiated.
## What does NOT qualify
- GINNIE MAE, FANNIE MAE, and FREDDIE MAC securities. These are not direct U.S. obligations and their interest is fully taxable by New York. This is the most common error in the category
- Interest on federal tax REFUNDS
- Repurchase agreements collateralized by Treasuries
- Gains from selling Treasury securities. Only the INTEREST is exempt
## Where the income came from
The subtraction is available for amounts included on Form IT-201 lines 2 (interest), 6 (business income), or 11 (rental, royalty, partnership, S corporation, and trust income). If you are subtracting amounts from MORE THAN ONE of those lines, submit a schedule showing the breakdown by line.
## Do not double-count
The same interest cannot appear on both line 28 and line 31. Form IT-225 subtraction modification codes S-121 and S-123 cover related government-obligation subtractions; use one route or the other, not both.
## Required Information
- Form 1099-INT box 3 amounts by payer
- Mutual fund government obligations percentage for each fund
- Total dividends from each qualifying fund
- Which Form IT-201 line the income was originally reported on
## Questions
- Do you hold Treasury bills, notes, bonds, or U.S. Savings Bonds?
- Do you own bond mutual funds, and do you have their government obligations percentage?
- Does the fund meet the 50% asset test each quarter?
- Are any holdings agency securities such as Ginnie Mae, which do not qualify?
## Common Errors
- Subtracting Ginnie Mae, Fannie Mae, or Freddie Mac interest
- Subtracting an entire bond fund dividend instead of the government obligations portion
- Missing the subtraction entirely because box 3 was not noticed
- Claiming the same interest on both line 28 and line 31
- Subtracting capital gains on Treasuries rather than only interest
## Prompt
- I have Treasury bonds, does New York tax the interest?
- I own a government bond fund.
- I cashed in savings bonds last year.
