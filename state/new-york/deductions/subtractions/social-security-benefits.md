# Social Security Benefits
## Description
New York does NOT TAX SOCIAL SECURITY BENEFITS AT ALL. Whatever portion is taxable federally is subtracted in full at Form IT-201 line 27. Also covers Tier 1 and Tier 2 railroad retirement benefits.
## How it works
- Federal Form 1040 line 6a: total benefits received
- Federal Form 1040 line 6b: the TAXABLE portion, up to 85% of benefits
- Line 6b flows into federal AGI, and therefore into Form IT-201 line 19
- Line 27 subtracts the SAME line 6b amount, removing it from the New York base
Enter the taxable amount, not the gross benefit. Entering the gross figure from Form SSA-1099 overstates the subtraction.
## No conditions
Unlike the $20,000 pension exclusion, there is no age test, no dollar cap, and no income phase-out. A high-income retiree with 85% of a large benefit taxable federally subtracts the entire taxable amount.
## Railroad retirement uses TWO routes
Only SOCIAL SECURITY EQUIVALENT TIER 1 railroad benefits come out here at line 27. Tier 2 benefits, supplemental annuities, and railroad unemployment insurance are exempt too, but they use Form IT-225 code S-122 and land at line 31 instead.
Subtracting only the Tier 1 amount leaves the rest taxed. See state/new-york/deductions/subtractions/railroad-retirement-benefits.md.
## Interaction with the federal calculation
The federal taxable portion depends on COMBINED INCOME — AGI plus tax-exempt interest plus half of benefits. Because New York removes the whole taxable amount regardless, federal planning to reduce the taxable portion produces no additional New York benefit. It is already fully excluded.
This cuts the other way for a common piece of advice: shifting income to municipal bonds to reduce the taxable portion of Social Security helps the federal return and does nothing for New York, where the benefit is already exempt.
## Not covered here
- Social Security DISABILITY benefits follow the same treatment
- Supplemental Security Income (SSI) is not taxable federally, so nothing reaches line 19 and no subtraction is needed
- A lump-sum retroactive payment reported under the federal lump-sum election flows through at whatever amount landed in line 6b
## Required Information
- Form SSA-1099 or RRB-1099
- The TAXABLE amount from federal Form 1040 line 6b
- Whether benefits are Social Security or railroad retirement
## Questions
- Did you receive Social Security or railroad retirement benefits?
- What amount appears on federal Form 1040, line 6b?
- Did you receive a lump-sum retroactive payment?
## Common Errors
- Entering gross benefits from Form SSA-1099 instead of the taxable amount from line 6b
- Leaving line 27 blank because the return preparer assumed New York follows federal treatment
- Missing railroad retirement benefits, which get the same treatment
- Reducing the subtraction for high income, when no phase-out exists
## Prompt
- Does New York tax Social Security?
- I am retired and collect Social Security and a small pension.
- My railroad retirement benefits show as taxable on my federal return.
