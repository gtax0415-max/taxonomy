# Long-Term Residential Care Deduction
## Description
A subtraction for residents of a QUALIFIED CONTINUING-CARE RETIREMENT COMMUNITY (CCRC), for the portion of annual fees attributable to LONG-TERM BENEFITS under the continuing care contract. Rarely claimed, because the resident has to know to ask the community for the figure.
## What is deductible
Only the part of your fees attributable to the cost of providing LONG-TERM BENEFITS under the contract — not the full monthly fee, which also covers housing, meals, and services.
The amount of the subtraction is determined by the fees paid for long-term benefits AND YOUR AGE, using the same age-banded structure that governs qualified long-term care insurance premiums. Older residents deduct more.
## Qualified community
The community must hold a CERTIFICATE OF AUTHORITY from the NEW YORK STATE DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH to operate as a continuing care retirement community. A retirement community, assisted living residence, or independent living facility WITHOUT that certificate produces no subtraction.
There are a limited number of certified CCRCs in New York. Confirm the community's status rather than assuming — the word "continuing care" in a marketing brochure is not the test.
## Married couples
If you file jointly and BOTH spouses qualify, EACH may claim the subtraction. You may NOT claim any unused part of your spouse's subtraction. Same structure as the pension and annuity exclusion.
## Getting the number
The community must tell you. Request an annual statement identifying the long-term benefit portion of fees paid during the calendar year. Many certified CCRCs issue this automatically for tax purposes; some do not and will produce one on request.
Without that allocation the subtraction cannot be substantiated, and estimating a percentage of total fees is not defensible.
## Relationship to other long-term care items
Three separate New York provisions touch long-term care and they are frequently confused:
| Item | Form | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Long-term care INSURANCE PREMIUMS | IT-249 | Nonrefundable CREDIT, 20% capped at $1,500 |
| CCRC fees for long-term benefits | IT-225 | SUBTRACTION from income |
| Nursing home ASSESSMENT | IT-258 | Refundable CREDIT |
A CCRC resident may qualify for more than one. They address different payments and none excludes the others, though the same dollars cannot support two benefits.
## Required Information
- Annual statement from the CCRC showing the long-term benefit portion
- Your age at year end, which determines the deductible amount
- Confirmation of the community's Department of Health certificate
- Whether your spouse also resides there and qualifies
## Questions
- Do you live in a continuing-care retirement community?
- Does it hold a certificate of authority from the New York State Department of Health?
- Has the community given you a statement of the long-term benefit portion of your fees?
- Does your spouse also live there?
- Are you also paying long-term care insurance premiums, which support a separate credit?
## Common Errors
- Deducting the entire monthly fee rather than the long-term benefit portion
- Claiming it for an assisted living residence or independent living facility without CCRC certification
- Missing the subtraction entirely because the community never volunteered the figure
- Using one spouse's unused subtraction on the other's income
- Confusing this subtraction with the long-term care insurance credit
## Prompt
- I live in a continuing care retirement community.
- Can I deduct my CCRC fees in New York?
- My mother moved into a life-care community.
