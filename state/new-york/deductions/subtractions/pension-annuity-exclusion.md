# Pension and Annuity Income Exclusion
## Description
Up to $20,000 of PRIVATE pension and annuity income excluded from New York income, for taxpayers who reached age 59½. Separate from, and much narrower than, the government pension exclusion at line 26.
## The $20,000 cap
- Maximum $20,000 PER TAXPAYER, regardless of the number of sources
- $20,000 for 2025, carried forward as the 2026 proxy. The cap has been $20,000 for many years and is not indexed
- You must have been 59½ BEFORE JANUARY 1 of the tax year to exclude a full year's income
- If you TURNED 59½ during the year, exclude only amounts received AFTER you reached 59½, still capped at $20,000
## Married couples
Each spouse who qualifies may exclude up to $20,000 of THEIR OWN pension income. A couple can reach $40,000 combined.
NEITHER SPOUSE MAY USE THE OTHER'S UNUSED PORTION. The instructions give the example: Taxpayer A with $30,000 of qualifying income excludes $20,000; Taxpayer B with $15,000 excludes $15,000. Total $35,000, not $40,000. B's unused $5,000 does not transfer to A.
## What qualifies
- Periodic payments for services performed as an EMPLOYEE before retirement
- Periodic AND lump-sum payments from an IRA, excluding amounts from contributions made after retirement
- PERIODIC distributions from government IRC 457 deferred compensation plans
- Periodic distributions from an IRC 403(b) annuity purchased by a corporation, community chest, fund, foundation, or public school
- Periodic payments from an HR-10 (Keogh) plan
- Lump-sum HR-10 payments, but ONLY if federal Form 4972 is not used
- Periodic distributions from a cafeteria plan (IRC 125) or a 401(k) or deferred profit-sharing or stock bonus plan
## What does NOT qualify
- Distributions received as a NONEMPLOYEE SPOUSE under a QDRO or a New York DRO. These are excluded at line 26 for government plans and get NOTHING here for private plans
- Distributions from an annuity you bought with YOUR OWN FUNDS from an insurance company. The payments trace to your premium payments, not to personal services
- Employer NONELECTIVE and MATCHING contributions designated to a ROTH account under the SECURE 2.0 Act of 2022
- Any portion derived from contributions made AFTER you retired
## Beneficiaries
If you received a decedent's pension and annuity income, you may take the exclusion IF THE DECEDENT WOULD HAVE BEEN ENTITLED to it — regardless of YOUR OWN AGE. A 30-year-old inheriting a pension from a 70-year-old qualifies.
Two limits:
- Reduce your claim by any amount already subtracted on the DECEDENT'S return. The decedent and all beneficiaries together cannot exceed $20,000
- With multiple beneficiaries, the decedent's $20,000 is ALLOCATED by the value each beneficiary inherited, as a fraction of the total inherited
The instructions' example: a decedent's $24,000 of pension income split equally among four beneficiaries gives each a $5,000 share of the exclusion. A beneficiary who also has $14,000 of their own qualifying pension income excludes $19,000 total.
## Disability income interaction
If you also claim the disability income exclusion (Form IT-225, S-124), the COMBINED total of the pension exclusion and the disability exclusion cannot exceed $20,000. One shared ceiling.
## Required Information
- Form 1099-R for each distribution
- Your date of birth and the date you reached 59½
- Whether payments are periodic or lump-sum
- Whether federal Form 4972 was used for any lump sum
- For beneficiaries: the decedent's date of birth, the total value of inherited pensions, the number of beneficiaries, and amounts the decedent already excluded
## Questions
- What is your date of birth, and had you reached 59½ before January 1?
- Which pensions are private and which are government?
- Did you and your spouse each receive pension income?
- Are you receiving a pension as a beneficiary of someone who died?
- Are you also claiming the disability income exclusion?
## Common Errors
- Applying the $20,000 cap to a government pension that is fully exempt at line 26
- Transferring one spouse's unused exclusion to the other
- Excluding a full year when the taxpayer turned 59½ mid-year
- Claiming it for a QDRO share of a private pension
- Claiming it for a commercial annuity bought with personal funds
- Beneficiaries claiming a full $20,000 without allocating among co-beneficiaries
- Exceeding $20,000 combined with the disability income exclusion
## Prompt
- I get a pension from my old company, is it taxed in New York?
- My husband and I both have pensions, can we each exclude $20,000?
- I inherited my mother's IRA.
