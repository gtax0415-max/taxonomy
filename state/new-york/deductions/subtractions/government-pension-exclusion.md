# Government Pension Exclusion
## Description
Pensions from New York State, New York local governments, and the FEDERAL government are 100% EXEMPT from New York income tax. No dollar cap and no age requirement. Materially larger than the $20,000 private pension exclusion and frequently confused with it.
## No cap, no age test
The $20,000 limit on line 29 does NOT apply here. A retired New York City police officer with a $95,000 pension excludes all $95,000. A federal retiree at 52 excludes the full amount with no age requirement.
## Which employers qualify
- NEW YORK STATE, including State and City University of New York employees and New York State Education Department employees in the Optional Retirement Program
- LOCAL GOVERNMENTS within New York State
- THE UNITED STATES, its territories and possessions, any agency or instrumentality including the MILITARY, and the District of Columbia
- CERTAIN PUBLIC AUTHORITIES, specifically the Metropolitan Transit Authority Police 20-Year Retirement Program, the Manhattan and Bronx Surface Transit Operating Authority (MABSTOA), and the Long Island Railroad Company
The public authority list is exhaustive as written. Other authorities do not qualify under this line.
## What does NOT qualify
- Pensions from a PRIVATE employer, including a private university. These may qualify for the $20,000 exclusion on line 29 instead
- The portion attributable to contributions you made to a SUPPLEMENTAL ANNUITY plan funded through salary reduction
- PERIODIC DISTRIBUTIONS from government IRC 457 deferred compensation plans. These get line 29 treatment, not line 26
- Government pensions from ANOTHER STATE. A retired New Jersey teacher living in New York does not qualify here, though the $20,000 exclusion may apply
That last point is the most common misreading. The exclusion is for NEW YORK public employers and the FEDERAL government — not for public employment generally.
## Optional Retirement Program members
May subtract only the portion attributable to employment with the State or City University of New York or the New York State Education Department. Service elsewhere does not qualify, so the pension must be allocated.
## Return of contributions before retirement
Distributions representing a return of contributions in a year PRIOR to retirement also qualify, as an officer, employee, or beneficiary of an officer or employee of a qualifying entity.
## Nonemployee spouses
You may exclude distributions received as a NONEMPLOYEE SPOUSE under a court-issued qualified domestic relations order meeting IRC 414(p)(1)(A), or a domestic relations order issued by a New York court.
Note the asymmetry: QDRO distributions are EXCLUDED here at line 26, but are specifically DISQUALIFIED from the $20,000 exclusion at line 29. A divorced spouse receiving a share of a government pension does better than one receiving a share of a private pension.
## Required Information
- Form 1099-R for each distribution, with payer name and EIN
- The employing entity and whether it is New York State, New York local, or federal
- For ORP members, the allocation between qualifying and non-qualifying service
- Any QDRO or DRO, if receiving as a nonemployee spouse
- Whether any portion came from a supplemental annuity funded by salary reduction
## Questions
- Who was the employer that provided the pension?
- Was it New York State, a New York local government, or the federal government?
- Are you receiving it as a retiree, a beneficiary, or a divorced spouse under a court order?
- Did any portion come from a supplemental annuity you funded by salary reduction?
- If you were in the Optional Retirement Program, how much service was with SUNY, CUNY, or NYSED?
## Common Errors
- Applying the $20,000 cap to a fully exempt government pension
- Claiming the exclusion for another state's government pension
- Claiming it for a private university or nonprofit pension
- Including a supplemental annuity portion funded by salary reduction
- Putting a government 457 periodic distribution on line 26 instead of line 29
- Excluding a QDRO share of a PRIVATE pension, where it does not qualify at all
## Prompt
- I retired from the NYPD, is my pension taxed by New York?
- I am a federal retiree living in New York.
- I get part of my ex-husband's state pension.
