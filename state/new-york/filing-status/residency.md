# New York Residency
## Description
The determination that governs everything else. A RESIDENT pays New York tax on ALL income wherever earned. A NONRESIDENT pays only on New York source income. There is no federal equivalent and no federal answer to borrow.
## Two independent paths to residency
You are a New York State resident if EITHER:
1. YOUR DOMICILE IS NEW YORK STATE, or
2. You maintain a PERMANENT PLACE OF ABODE in New York for SUBSTANTIALLY ALL of the taxable year AND spend 184 DAYS OR MORE in New York during the year
The second path is the STATUTORY RESIDENT test. It is purely mechanical — a dwelling plus a day count — and it makes people residents who have never considered themselves New Yorkers.
## Domicile
Your permanent and primary residence: the place you intend to return to or remain in after being away, whether for vacation, business assignment, educational leave, or military assignment.
You may have SEVERAL RESIDENCES. YOU HAVE ONLY ONE DOMICILE. Living somewhere for an extended period does not make it your domicile if it is not the place you ultimately attach yourself to.
### Changing domicile is hard
YOUR NEW YORK DOMICILE DOES NOT CHANGE until you demonstrate with CLEAR AND CONVINCING EVIDENCE that you abandoned it and established a new one outside New York. This means SHIFTING THE FOCUS OF YOUR LIFE.
Filing a certificate of domicile or registering to vote elsewhere IS NOT ENOUGH. The instructions say so explicitly: easily controlled factors such as where you vote, where your driver's license and registration are issued, and where your will is located are NOT primary factors.
### The five primary factors
When comparing an old and a new location, New York weighs:
1. The SIZE, VALUE, AND NATURE OF USE of your first residence against the newly acquired one
2. Your EMPLOYMENT AND BUSINESS CONNECTIONS in both locations
3. The AMOUNT OF TIME spent in both locations
4. The PHYSICAL LOCATION OF ITEMS WITH SIGNIFICANT SENTIMENTAL VALUE
5. Your CLOSE FAMILY TIES in both locations
A change is clear and convincing only when your PRIMARY TIES are clearly greater in the new location. Some factors weigh more heavily than others depending on your overall lifestyle. The burden of proof is on the taxpayer.
### Temporary moves do not change domicile
If you move somewhere intending to stay only for a limited time, no matter how long, your domicile does not change. The instructions give the example of an employee assigned from Newburgh to Atlanta for two years who then returned: domicile remained New York throughout.
Going abroad for business, study, or research does not change domicile unless you show you DEFINITELY DO NOT INTEND TO RETURN to New York.
## Permanent place of abode
A residence — a building or structure where a person can live — that you PERMANENTLY MAINTAIN and that is SUITABLE FOR YEAR-ROUND USE. IT DOES NOT MATTER WHETHER YOU OWN IT. A residence your spouse owns or leases usually counts.
### The undergraduate student exception
A residence maintained by a FULL-TIME STUDENT enrolled at an institution of higher education in an UNDERGRADUATE DEGREE PROGRAM LEADING TO A BACCALAUREATE DEGREE, and occupied by that student while attending, is NOT a permanent place of abode with respect to that student. See TSB-M-09(15)I.
Note the limits: undergraduate only, full-time only, baccalaureate programs only. A graduate student's apartment IS a permanent place of abode.
## Counting days
ANY PART OF A DAY IS A DAY. You do NOT need to be present at the permanent place of abode for the day to count. A two-hour layover, a dinner, a doctor's appointment — each is a New York day.
184 days is the threshold. In a leap year the arithmetic is unchanged.
IF YOU MAINTAIN A PERMANENT PLACE OF ABODE IN NEW YORK AND CLAIM NONRESIDENT STATUS, YOU MUST BE ABLE TO PROVIDE ADEQUATE RECORDS proving you did not spend more than 183 days here. The burden is yours. Contemporaneous records — calendars, tolls, card transactions, phone records — are what survive an audit. Reconstruction afterward rarely does.
## Two escape routes for New York domiciliaries
Even if your domicile IS New York, you are NOT a resident if you meet ALL THREE conditions in either group.
### GROUP A
- You did NOT maintain any permanent place of abode in New York State during the tax year, AND
- You DID maintain a permanent place of abode OUTSIDE New York for the ENTIRE tax year, AND
- You spent 30 DAYS OR LESS in New York State during the tax year
### GROUP B
- You were in a FOREIGN COUNTRY for at least 450 DAYS during any period of 548 CONSECUTIVE DAYS, AND
- You, your spouse (unless legally separated), and your minor children spent 90 DAYS OR LESS in New York during that 548-day period, AND
- During the nonresident portion of the tax years in which the 548-day period begins and ends, your New York presence did not exceed a proportional limit:
```
(days in the nonresident portion ÷ 548) × 90 = maximum New York days allowed
```
Group B is the route for expatriate assignments. The 450-of-548 arithmetic is unforgiving and the spouse-and-children day count catches families who visit New York over holidays.
## City and Yonkers residency
The SAME TESTS, with the locality substituted. You are a New York City resident if your domicile is the city, or you have a permanent place of abode there and spend 184 days or more IN THE CITY. Same for Yonkers.
City domicile rules are identical to state rules: if your permanent and primary residence in one of the five boroughs is the place you intend to return to, you are a city domiciliary, and that does not change until you abandon it with clear and convincing evidence.
All city residents' income is subject to NYC tax no matter where earned. NONRESIDENTS OF NEW YORK CITY ARE NOT LIABLE FOR NYC PERSONAL INCOME TAX.
## Sales tax residency is broader
For SALES AND USE TAX purposes, a resident includes anyone with a permanent place of abode in the state — no day count. You can be a nonresident for income tax and a resident for sales tax. See state/new-york/taxes/sales-use-tax.md.
## Required Information
- Every dwelling maintained during the year, where, and whether owned, leased, or provided
- A day-by-day presence log for New York State and, separately, the city
- Employment and business locations
- Where family members lived
- Where items of sentimental value are kept
- For Group B, foreign presence dates across the 548-day period
## Questions
- Where is the place you intend to return to and remain?
- Did you maintain a dwelling in New York suitable for year-round use, whether or not you owned it?
- How many days were you physically present in New York State? In the city?
- Can you document the day count contemporaneously?
- Did you move abroad, and for how long?
## Common Errors
- Believing a driver's license, voter registration, or a domicile affidavit changes domicile
- Keeping a New York apartment after moving and crossing 184 days on visits
- Counting only overnight stays, when any part of a day counts
- Assuming a graduate student's apartment falls under the undergraduate exception
- Failing to keep contemporaneous day records while claiming nonresident status
- Meeting two of three Group A or Group B conditions and assuming the exception applies
## Prompt
- Am I still a New York resident if I moved to Florida?
- I kept my apartment in Manhattan but I live in Connecticut now.
- I was working overseas for two years.
