# Selecting Your New York Filing Status
## Description
Item A on Form IT-201. IN MOST CASES YOU MUST USE THE SAME FILING STATUS YOU USED ON YOUR FEDERAL RETURN. If you did not have to file a federal return, use the status you would have used.
There are exactly three exceptions, all involving married couples who filed a joint federal return.
## Exception 1: one spouse is a New York resident and the other is not
If one spouse was a New York State RESIDENT and the other a NONRESIDENT or PART-YEAR RESIDENT, you must either:
- File SEPARATE New York returns using the married-filing-separately status; OR
- File JOINTLY as if BOTH were New York State residents
Choosing to file jointly makes the nonresident spouse's ENTIRE income taxable by New York. Run both ways before electing.
## Exception 2: your spouse's whereabouts are unknown
You may file a SEPARATE New York return if ALL of the following are true:
- The address or whereabouts of your spouse is unknown, AND
- You can demonstrate that REASONABLE EFFORTS have been made to locate your spouse, AND
- GOOD CAUSE exists for the failure to file a joint New York return
## Exception 3: your spouse refuses to sign
You may file a SEPARATE New York return if ALL of the following are true:
- Your spouse REFUSES TO SIGN a joint New York return, AND
- Reasonable efforts have been made to have your spouse sign, AND
- There is OBJECTIVE EVIDENCE OF ALIENATION, meaning one of:
  - A judicial order of protection
  - Legal separation under a decree of divorce or separate maintenance
  - Living apart for the TWELVE MONTHS immediately preceding the application to file separately, or commencement of a divorce action or certain family court proceedings
- AND good cause exists for the failure to file jointly
These two exceptions matter in domestic violence and abandonment situations, where the federal rules would otherwise force a filer into a joint return they cannot obtain.
## Consequences of married filing separately for New York
- BOTH spouses must take the standard deduction UNLESS BOTH elect to itemize. One cannot itemize while the other does not
- The household credit uses a separate table and tests the COMBINED federal AGI from both returns
- The NYC school tax credit and household credit have their own married-filing-separately treatment
- Dependents cannot be claimed on both returns
## Same-sex married couples
Same-sex married couples have the same New York State tax benefits and requirements as different-sex married couples. Determine filing status using the general married rules. Throughout the New York instructions, SPOUSE is gender-neutral and MARRIAGE includes a marriage between same-sex spouses.
## Form 1040-SR filers
If you filed federal Form 1040-SR, U.S. Tax Return for Seniors, read every reference in the New York instructions to Form 1040 and its schedules as a reference to Form 1040-SR.

## Questions
- What filing status did you use federally?
- Were you and your spouse both New York residents for the full year?
- Is your spouse's whereabouts unknown, or refusing to sign a joint return?
- If filing separately, is your spouse itemizing?
## Common Errors
- Using a New York filing status different from the federal one without qualifying for an exception
- Electing a joint New York return with a nonresident spouse without comparing the alternative
- One spouse itemizing and the other taking the standard deduction on separate returns
- Using only one spouse's AGI for the married-filing-separately household credit test
