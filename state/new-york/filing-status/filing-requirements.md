# New York Filing Requirements
## Description
Who must file a New York return, and which one. Determine RESIDENCY FIRST — the filing thresholds and the form both depend on it.
## Full-year residents: file Form IT-201 if ANY of these apply
- YOU MUST FILE A FEDERAL RETURN
- You did NOT have to file a federal return, but your FEDERAL AGI PLUS NEW YORK ADDITIONS exceeded $4,000 — or $3,100 if you are SINGLE AND CAN BE CLAIMED AS A DEPENDENT on another taxpayer's federal return
- You want to CLAIM A REFUND of New York State, New York City, or Yonkers tax withheld from your pay or from unemployment insurance benefit payments
- You want to claim any REFUNDABLE OR CARRYOVER CREDITS
## The threshold uses additions, not just AGI
The $4,000 test is federal AGI PLUS NEW YORK ADDITIONS. Someone with $3,500 of federal AGI and $800 of out-of-state municipal bond interest is over the line at $4,300 even though their federal AGI is not. See state/new-york/income/additions/.
## Filing when not required is often the right move
The last two triggers are permissive, not mandatory, and they are where money is left behind. Several New York credits are REFUNDABLE and pay out with no tax liability at all:
- Empire State child credit, up to $1,000 per child under four, WITH NO INCOME MINIMUM
- Earned income credit
- Real property tax credit
- College tuition credit
A family with no income and two young children owes nothing and is owed up to $2,000 — but only if they file.
Three benefits can be claimed WITHOUT a full return: the real property tax credit on Form IT-214 alone, the NYC school tax credit on Form NYC-210 alone, and STAR outside the income tax system entirely.
## Nonresidents and part-year residents
File FORM IT-203 if you were a nonresident or part-year resident and you received income from New York sources during the year.
DO NOT FILE FORM IT-201 if you moved into New York on any day other than January 1, or out on any day other than December 31.
## Married couples: when separate New York returns are REQUIRED
If you filed a JOINT FEDERAL return but one spouse was a New York State RESIDENT and the other a NONRESIDENT or PART-YEAR RESIDENT, you must EITHER:
- File SEPARATE New York returns — the resident on Form IT-201, the nonresident or part-year resident on Form IT-203 if required to file; OR
- File JOINTLY on Form IT-201, in which case BOTH SPOUSES' INCOME IS TAXED AS FULL-YEAR RESIDENTS
The joint election is a real trap. It subjects the nonresident spouse's entire out-of-state income to New York tax. It only makes sense when the nonresident spouse has little income.
Form IT-203-C, Nonresident or Part-Year Resident Spouse's Certification, is used when only one spouse has New York source income.
## Deceased taxpayers
If a taxpayer died and did NOT have to file a federal return but had New York tax withheld, the spouse or personal representative MUST file a New York return to claim the refund. Enter the date of death near the top of the return.
## Children with investment income
If your child has investment income over $2,700, it is generally to your advantage to file a NEW YORK RETURN FOR THE CHILD, because there is no New York tax on the first $3,100 of that income. Report the child's investment income on federal Form 8615 rather than Form 8814. If you use Form 8814, the child's investment income over $2,700 lands in YOUR federal AGI and is taxed at YOUR New York rate.

- Were you a resident, nonresident, or part-year resident?
- Did you have to file a federal return?
- Was New York, New York City, or Yonkers tax withheld from your pay?
- Do you have qualifying children, which could make filing worthwhile even with no income?
- Did you and your spouse have different residency statuses?
## Common Errors
- Not filing when income is below the threshold, forfeiting refundable credits
- Testing only federal AGI without adding New York additions
- Filing Form IT-201 after a mid-year move into or out of New York
- Electing to file jointly when one spouse is a nonresident, exposing their income to New York tax
- Assuming a child's investment income is better reported on the parent's return
