# Public Employee 414(h) Retirement Contributions
## Description
Mandatory retirement contributions made by NEW YORK public employees are excluded from federal wages but are TAXABLE BY NEW YORK. Added at Form IT-201 line 21. This affects hundreds of thousands of teachers, police officers, firefighters, transit workers, and civil servants, and it is invisible on the federal return.
## Where the number comes from
Form W-2 BOX 14, usually labeled 414(H) or 414H. Box 1 federal wages have already been reduced by this amount; New York adds it back.
If box 14 shows nothing, there is nothing to add. Not every public employee has 414(h) contributions.
## Which systems trigger it
You must add back 414(h) contributions if you are a member of:
- The NEW YORK STATE AND LOCAL RETIREMENT SYSTEMS, which include the Employees' Retirement System (ERS) and the Police and Fire Retirement System (PFRS)
- The NEW YORK STATE TEACHERS' RETIREMENT SYSTEM
- The OPTIONAL RETIREMENT PROGRAM, if you are an employee of the State or City University of New York
- The NEW YORK CITY EMPLOYEES' RETIREMENT SYSTEM
- The NEW YORK CITY TEACHERS' RETIREMENT SYSTEM
- The NEW YORK CITY BOARD OF EDUCATION RETIREMENT SYSTEM
- The NEW YORK CITY POLICE PENSION FUND
- The NYC FIRE DEPARTMENT PENSION FUND
- The MANHATTAN AND BRONX SURFACE TRANSIT OPERATING AUTHORITY (MABSTOA) PENSION PLAN
## What is NOT added back
DO NOT include contributions to:
- A section 401(k) deferred arrangement
- A section 403(b) annuity
- A section 457 deferred compensation plan
These are VOLUNTARY deferrals and remain excluded for New York as well. The distinction is mandatory pension contributions (added back) versus voluntary supplemental deferrals (not added back). A teacher with both a 414(h) and a 403(b) adds back only the 414(h).
## Federal employees and other states
This addition is for NEW YORK public employers only. A federal employee's FERS or CSRS contributions are not 414(h) amounts and are not added back here. A New York resident working for another state's government is likewise outside this line.
## The offsetting benefit
New York taxes these contributions now and exempts the resulting PENSION ENTIRELY when it is paid, at Form IT-201 line 26, with no cap and no age test. The federal system does the reverse — excludes the contribution, taxes the pension.
Over a career the two systems collect on the same dollars once each, at different times. A public employee objecting to line 21 is usually reassured by the line 26 treatment.
## Nonresidents and part-year residents
A nonresident who works for a New York public employer adds back the 414(h) amount attributable to New York-source wages on Form IT-203. Residency does not exempt anyone from this addition.
## Required Information
- Form W-2 box 14 amount, per employer
- Which retirement system you belong to
- Whether any box 14 amount is a 403(b), 457, or 401(k) deferral rather than 414(h)
## Questions
- Do you work for New York State, a New York local government, or New York City?
- Does box 14 of your W-2 show a 414(h) amount?
- Which retirement system are you a member of?
- Do you also contribute to a 403(b) or 457 plan, which is not added back?
## Common Errors
- Omitting the addition because it never appears on the federal return
- Adding back 403(b), 457, or 401(k) deferrals along with the 414(h) amount
- Adding back a federal employee's FERS or CSRS contributions
- Missing it on a second W-2 from a different public employer
- Assuming it makes the future pension taxable, when the pension is fully exempt
## Prompt
- My W-2 has a 414(h) amount in box 14.
- I am a teacher in New York, why is my state income higher than my federal?
- I work for the MTA.
