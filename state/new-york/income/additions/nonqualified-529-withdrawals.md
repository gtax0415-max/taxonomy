# Nonqualified 529 Withdrawals
## Description
Recapture of New York 529 contribution subtractions claimed in earlier years, when funds come out for a purpose New York does not treat as qualified. Added at Form IT-201 line 22.
## The recapture is cumulative
The line 22 worksheet does not look at this year alone:
```
1  Total CURRENT AND PRIOR years' nonqualified withdrawals
2  Total CURRENT AND PRIOR years' contributions
3  Current year subtraction (line 30 worksheet) plus PRIOR years' subtractions
4  Line 2 minus line 3
5  Total PRIOR years' addition modifications
6  Line 4 plus line 5
7  Line 1 minus line 6 → Form IT-201, Line 22
```
If line 7 is zero or less, no entry is required.
The practical consequence: YOU MUST KEEP EVERY YEAR'S WORKSHEET. Without prior-year figures the computation cannot be done, and reconstructing a decade of contributions from account statements is painful. The Form IT-201 instructions say so explicitly on both worksheets.
Note the ORDER: you must complete the line 30 subtraction worksheet BEFORE the line 22 addition worksheet, because line 3 draws from it.
## What New York treats as nonqualified
Including but not limited to:
- TRANSFER TO ANOTHER STATE'S 529 PROGRAM, for the same beneficiary or another family member
- K-12 TUITION at public, private, or religious elementary or secondary schools. New York limits qualified higher education to POST-SECONDARY institutions
- Student loan principal or interest for the beneficiary or a sibling paid BEFORE September 5, 2024
- Roth IRA rollovers under IRC 529(c)(3)(E) made BEFORE September 5, 2024
- Postsecondary credentialing expenses NOT paid to an eligible educational institution
## The K-12 divergence
Federal law permits 529 funds for K-12 tuition. NEW YORK DOES NOT. A family paying private elementary school tuition from a 529 gets clean federal treatment and a New York add-back that recaptures every prior subtraction they claimed.
This is the single most expensive surprise in the New York 529 rules, and it usually surfaces years after the account was opened.
## What is qualified, so no add-back
- Qualified higher education expenses at an eligible institution under Education Law 695-b(5), even if reinvested within the IRC 60-day rollover window
- Qualified postsecondary credentialing expenses at an eligible institution, on or after July 4, 2025
- Qualified education loan principal and interest for the beneficiary or a SIBLING, on or after September 5, 2024
- Roth IRA rollovers on or after September 5, 2024
- Any withdrawal resulting from the DEATH OR DISABILITY of the designated beneficiary
- Rollover to a qualified ABLE program
TRANSFERS BETWEEN FAMILY MEMBERS' ACCOUNTS within New York's program, not disbursed in cash or in kind, are not distributions at all and require no add-back.
## Required Information
- Form 1099-Q for each distribution
- What the funds paid for, and the payee
- Cumulative prior-year contributions, subtractions, and additions from retained worksheets
- The current-year line 30 worksheet, completed first
## Questions
- Did you withdraw from a New York 529 plan?
- What did the money pay for?
- Was any of it used for K-12 tuition or moved to another state's plan?
- Do you have your prior-year line 30 and line 22 worksheets?
- Did you claim New York subtractions for contributions in earlier years?
## Common Errors
- Using 529 funds for private K-12 tuition without recognizing the New York add-back
- Rolling the account to another state's plan, which is a nonqualified withdrawal
- Computing line 22 before completing the line 30 worksheet
- Treating the add-back as current-year only rather than cumulative
- Discarding prior worksheets, making the computation impossible
## Prompt
- I used 529 money for my son's private high school.
- I moved my 529 to another state.
- I took money out of my 529 for something other than college.
