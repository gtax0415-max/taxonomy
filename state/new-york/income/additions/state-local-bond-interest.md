# Interest Income on State and Local Bonds and Obligations
## Description
Interest on municipal bonds issued by states OTHER THAN NEW YORK, and by their local governments, is taxable by New York even though it is exempt federally. Added at Form IT-201 line 20.
## The rule in one line
New York exempts NEW YORK bonds. Everything else municipal is taxable by New York.
| Issuer | Federal | New York |
|---|---|---|
| New York State or a New York local government | Exempt | Exempt |
| Any other state or its localities | Exempt | TAXABLE |
| Puerto Rico, Guam, U.S. Virgin Islands | Exempt | Exempt by federal law |
## What to include
Interest received or credited during the year that was NOT in your federal AGI, from:
- State and local bonds of other states
- Interest and dividend income from TAX-EXEMPT BOND MUTUAL FUNDS
- TAX-EXEMPT MONEY MARKET FUNDS that invest in obligations of states other than New York
## The mutual fund problem
A national municipal bond fund holds bonds from many states. Only the New York portion is exempt for New York purposes; the rest is an addition.
Funds publish a state-by-state breakdown annually, usually in January, in a "tax information" supplement. Multiply the exempt-interest dividend by the NON-New York percentage. Without that breakdown the addition cannot be computed accurately, and reporting zero is not a defensible default.
A New York investor who wants a genuinely tax-free result needs a NEW YORK municipal fund, not a national one. This is the practical planning point and it is frequently missed by advisors outside New York.
## Bonds bought or sold between interest dates
- BOUGHT between interest dates: include the interest you received, MINUS the seller's accrued interest (accrued from the interest date preceding your purchase to the purchase date)
- SOLD between interest dates: include the interest you received, PLUS the accrued interest from the preceding interest date to the sale date
Your broker provides these figures at the time of purchase or sale.
## Bond premium is not an offset here
For purposes of this addition, bond premium amortization may NOT be netted directly against the interest. It must be reported separately:
- If the premium is attributable to a TRADE OR BUSINESS: as a subtraction modification on Form IT-225
- If NOT attributable to a trade or business: as an itemized deduction ADDITION ADJUSTMENT on Form IT-196 line 44, and only if you itemize
A non-itemizer with premium on out-of-state municipal bonds gets no relief at all. New York taxes the interest and gives nothing back for the premium.
## Partnerships and S corporations
If your entity reports this modification, use CODE EA-113 and enter it directly on line 20 — not on Form IT-225. Routing it through Form IT-225 double-counts it.
## Required Information
- Tax-exempt interest by issuer or, for funds, the state-by-state percentage breakdown
- Total exempt-interest dividends from each fund
- Accrued interest paid or received on mid-period purchases and sales
- Bond premium amortization, and whether it relates to a trade or business
## Questions
- Do you hold municipal bonds, and which states issued them?
- Do you own a national or out-of-state municipal bond fund?
- Do you have the fund's state-by-state breakdown for the year?
- Did you buy or sell any bonds between interest payment dates?
- Do you have amortizable bond premium on out-of-state bonds?
## Common Errors
- Reporting zero because the interest was tax-exempt federally
- Adding back the entire fund dividend when part came from New York issuers
- Adding back Puerto Rico or other U.S. territory interest, which is exempt by federal law
- Netting bond premium directly against the interest
- Routing an EA-113 entity modification through Form IT-225 instead of line 20
## Prompt
- I own municipal bonds from several states.
- My tax-exempt fund shows income on my New York return.
- Does New York tax muni bond interest?
