# Household Employment Taxes (Schedule 4, Line 2; Schedule IN-H)
## Description
If you paid cash wages to someone who worked in and around your home as a baby-sitter, nanny, health aide, private nurse, maid, caretaker, yard worker or similar domestic worker, that person may be your household employee. Indiana state and county income taxes you withheld from household employees are paid with your Form IT-40 on Schedule 4, line 2, using Schedule IN-H.
## Who is not your household employee (for this rule)
- Your spouse,
- Your child under age 21,
- Your parent,
- An employee under age 18.
See federal Publication 926, Household Employer's Tax Guide, to determine whether a worker is your employee.
## Thresholds
The booklet (Schedule 5 instructions) says: if you paid cash wages of $2,200 or more to a household worker who is your employee, or total cash wages of $1,000 or more in any calendar quarter of the current or prior year to all household employees, you may have withheld state and county income taxes; get Schedule IN-H from DOR to pay them on your Indiana return. These are 2025 amounts (2025 amount; confirm against the 2026 form). The $2,200 figure printed in the 2025 booklet should be checked against the federal household employee threshold for 2026 in Publication 926.
## Source note
The Schedule IN-H form text is not among the source files for this taxonomy; this file is based on the booklet (Schedule 4, line 2 and Schedule 5, lines 1-2 instructions). Confirm the Schedule IN-H line flow against the 2026 schedule.
## Example
A family paid a nanny $26,000 in cash wages in 2026 and withheld $767 of Indiana state tax and $525 of Marion County tax (2025 amount; confirm against the 2026 form). They complete Schedule IN-H and report $1,292 on Schedule 4, line 2.
## Questions
- Did you pay anyone to work in your home (nanny, housekeeper, caregiver, yard worker)? How much in cash wages?
- Is the worker your spouse, child under 21, parent or under 18?
- Did you withhold Indiana state and county tax from the worker's pay?
## Common Errors
- Treating a household employee as an independent contractor
- Omitting withheld household employee taxes from Schedule 4
- Claiming the withheld household tax as your own withholding credit
