# Renter's Deduction (Schedule 2, Line 1)
## Description
A deduction for rent paid on your principal place of residence in Indiana, if the rented place was subject to Indiana property tax. The deduction is the lesser of the rent paid or $3,000 ($1,500 if married filing separately) (2025 amount; confirm against the 2026 form). Your "principal place of residence" is where you have your true, fixed, permanent home and where you intend to return after being absent.
## What qualifies
- Rent on your principal residence that was subject to Indiana property tax.
- Rent for a manufactured home, or rent for a manufactured home lot, if the above requirements are met.
- Rent at more than one place during the year (list each landlord and address; enclose additional pages).
## What does not qualify
| Item | Reason |
|---|---|
| Summer homes or vacation homes | not a principal residence |
| Government owned housing | not subject to Indiana property tax |
| Property owned by a nonprofit organization | not subject to Indiana property tax |
| Student housing | not subject to Indiana property tax |
| Property owned by a cooperative association | not subject to Indiana property tax |
| Property located outside Indiana | not subject to Indiana property tax |
## How to report
Complete the information area on line 1: the address where rented if different from the front of the return (leave blank if the same), the landlord's name and address, the total rent paid and the number of months you lived there. Repeat for each place rented. Enter the lesser of $3,000 ($1,500 MFS) or the rent paid.
## Examples (booklet)
- Emily paid $4,800 in rent on her principal residence. Line 1 = $3,000.
- Bill paid $400 for his first apartment and $2,800 at a second one, $3,200 in all. Line 1 = $3,000.
## County tax split
On a joint return where spouses lived in different counties on January 1, the deduction is divided between Columns A and B of Schedule CT-40 (booklet example: $3,000 split $1,500 and $1,500).
## Questions
- Did you rent your main home in Indiana in 2026? How much rent, and for how many months?
- Who is the landlord, and is the property government, nonprofit, student or cooperative housing?
- Did you rent at more than one address?
## Common Errors
- Claiming rent on student housing or a nonprofit- or government-owned unit
- Using $3,000 on a married filing separately return
- Claiming rent for a vacation home or an out-of-state residence
- Leaving the landlord information blank

- How much of my rent can I deduct in Indiana?
- I live in university housing. Can I take the renter's deduction?
