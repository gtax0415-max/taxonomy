# Homeowner's Residential Property Tax Deduction (Schedule 2, Line 2)
## Description
A deduction of up to $2,500 ($1,250 if married filing separately) (2025 amount; confirm against the 2026 form) of Indiana property taxes (residential real estate taxes) paid on your principal place of residence. Your "principal place of residence" is where you have your true, fixed home and where you intend to return after being absent. Property tax on summer or vacation homes is not deductible.
## How to claim
- Enter the address where the property tax was paid if different from the front of the return. List any additional principal residence on a separate sheet.
- Enter the number of months you lived there (other residences on a separate sheet).
- Enter the combined Indiana property tax paid on all principal residences.
- Enter the smaller of $2,500 ($1,250 MFS) or the tax paid.
## Amount paid
Add the spring and fall installments paid during the year for the property taxes billed that year (the 2025 booklet says: add the 2025 spring and fall installments if you paid both; if you received only one installment statement, use the amount paid for that installment). If a mortgage company paid from escrow, use Form 1098 or its equivalent, or contact the county treasurer or mortgage company. Keep proof of payment (Form 1098, the property tax statement, canceled checks).
## Lake County credit interaction
You cannot claim this deduction for property tax paid in the year if you claim the Lake County (Indiana) residential income tax credit on Schedule 5, line 7. The credit requires Lake County property tax on your residence and Modified Indiana AGI under $18,600 (2025 amount; confirm against the 2026 form). If you claim the credit, remove any amount on Schedule 2, line 2 (the "Final Step" after Worksheet B of the credit instructions).
## No double benefit
If any portion of the property tax was deducted as an expense on federal Schedule C, E or F, do not deduct that portion here.
## Examples (booklet)
- Jean paid $1,200 of Indiana property tax and deducted $200 of it on federal Schedule C for a home office. Line 2 = $1,000.
- Jamie and Ella each owned a home, married during the year, sold both and began renting. Filing jointly they may claim the combined property tax on both homes, limited to $2,500 in total.
## County tax split
Spouses filing jointly who lived in different counties on January 1 divide the deduction on Schedule CT-40 (booklet example: $2,500 split $1,250 and $1,250).
## Questions
- Do you own your main home in Indiana, and how much property tax did you pay during the year?
- Was any of it deducted as a business or rental expense?
- Is the home in Lake County, and are you claiming the Lake County residential income tax credit?
- Did you live in more than one owned home during the year?
## Common Errors
- Claiming both this deduction and the Lake County residential income tax credit
- Deducting property tax on a vacation home or on a home outside Indiana
- Deducting the business-use portion already taken on Schedule C, E or F
- Using $2,500 on a married filing separately return
## Prompt
- Can I deduct my Indiana property taxes?
- I live in Lake County. Should I take the property tax deduction or the Lake County credit?
