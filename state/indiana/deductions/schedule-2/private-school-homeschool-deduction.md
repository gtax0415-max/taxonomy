# Private School/Homeschool Deduction (Schedule 2, Line 8)
## Description
A deduction of $1,000 (2025 amount; confirm against the 2026 form) for each dependent child enrolled in a private school or homeschooled in Indiana for whom you made an unreimbursed education expenditure during the year.
## Dependent child qualifications
- The child must be eligible to receive a free elementary or high school education (K-12) in an Indiana school corporation;
- You must be eligible to claim the child as a dependent on your federal return; and
- The child must be your natural or adopted child, or you must have been awarded custody in a court proceeding as the court-appointed guardian or custodian.
## Education expenditure
Any expenditure made in connection with enrollment, attendance or participation in a private elementary or high school education program: tuition, fees, computer software, textbooks, workbooks, curricula, school supplies (other than personal computers), and other written materials used primarily for academic instruction or tutoring. It does not include instruction in a home setting for a child enrolled in a school corporation or charter school, or expenditures that are reimbursed (for example by a scholarship or grant).
A "private elementary or high school education program" is attendance at a nonpublic school (private, parochial or homeschool) in Indiana that satisfies compulsory attendance, which means at least 180 days in a calendar year. No deduction for a child enrolled less than 180 days.
## Example (booklet)
Greg and Constance have children aged 7, 9 and 11. The two oldest attend private school; the youngest attends public school. They bought schoolbooks for all three. Line 8 = $2,000.
## Married filing separately
A child may be claimed only once per year. The parent eligible to claim the child as a dependent for exemption purposes claims this deduction. See Income Tax Information Bulletin #107.
## Questions
- How many of your dependent children attended an Indiana private school or were homeschooled for at least 180 days?
- Did you pay any unreimbursed education expense for each of them?
- Were any of the expenses paid by a scholarship or grant?
## Common Errors
- Counting a child who attends a public or charter school
- Claiming the deduction for a personal computer purchase as the only expense
- Both separately filing parents claiming the same child
