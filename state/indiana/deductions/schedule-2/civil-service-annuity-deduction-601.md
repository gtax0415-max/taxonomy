# Civil Service Annuity Deduction (Schedule 2, Line 11, Code 601)
## Description
If federal AGI includes federal civil service annuity income, you may take a deduction if you were at least 62 years of age by the end of the tax year and/or are a surviving spouse of a civil service annuitant. Keep Form CSA 1099-R with your records.
## Amount (per annuitant)
- The lesser of the taxable civil service annuity included in federal AGI or $16,000 (2025 amount; confirm against the 2026 form),
- minus all Social Security income and tier 1 and tier 2 Railroad Retirement income (issued by the Railroad Retirement Board) received by that annuitant.
If you receive a civil service annuity both for yourself and as a surviving spouse, the combined deduction cannot exceed $16,000.
## Surviving spouse
A surviving spouse may claim the deduction with no age requirement, using the same computation with the surviving spouse's own Social Security and railroad retirement.
## Examples (booklet)
- Age 67, taxable annuity $6,000, Social Security $1,200: $6,000 - $1,200 = $4,800.
- Matthew and Claire, both 68, file jointly. Matthew: taxable annuity $13,700, Social Security $17,500, so no deduction. Claire: taxable annuity $21,900, limited to $16,000, minus Social Security $6,300 = $9,700. Code 601 = $9,700.
- Marie, a surviving spouse: taxable annuity $14,500, Social Security $1,200 = $13,300.
## Questions
- Do you receive a federal civil service annuity? How old were you at year end, or are you a surviving spouse?
- How much Social Security or Railroad Retirement Board income did each annuitant receive?
## Common Errors
- Subtracting a spouse's Social Security from the other spouse's annuity
- Exceeding $16,000 when receiving both an own and a survivor annuity
- Claiming before age 62 when not a surviving spouse

- I'm a retired federal employee in Indiana. Is my civil service pension deductible?
