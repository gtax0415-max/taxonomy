# Qualified Tips Deduction, 2026 Only (Schedule 2, Line 11)
## Description
For the 2026 tax year only, Indiana allows a deduction equal to the qualified tips deducted federally under IRC section 224 (IC 6-3-2-31, SEA 243, effective January 1, 2026). "Qualified tips" has the meaning in IRC 224(d). The Indiana deduction starts from the federal deduction reported on federal Schedule 1-A, line 13 (line per Income Tax Information Bulletin #128, which refers to the 2025 version of the federal form), including federal income phaseouts and the federal disallowance for married individuals filing separately. It can never exceed the federal deduction. The Schedule 2 code number is not yet published: code to be assigned on the 2026 form.
## Full-year residents
For Indiana residents the state deduction equals the federal deduction. For county tax the deduction is also the same, except in isolated cases of Perry County residents and individuals domiciled in Indiana but residing outside Indiana.
## When an exemption applies
If an exemption removes some tips from Indiana tax, the deduction is the federal deduction multiplied by: qualified tips subject to Indiana state or local tax (after exemptions but before the tips and overtime deductions) divided by qualified tips included in federal AGI. Exemptions include the 30-day nonresident compensation deduction (IC 6-3-2-27.5), the reciprocal-state exemption (IC 6-3-5), the county exclusion for nonresidents and the Perry County exclusion for certain Kentucky locality income (IC 6-3.6-8-7). Personal and dependent exemptions are not "exemptions" for this purpose. Where state and county amounts differ, the ratio is applied separately.
## Married filing jointly
Spouses may allocate the federal deduction based on their circumstances, but neither may be allocated more than that spouse's own qualified tips otherwise allowable as if unmarried. If the spouses are taxed in different counties, the same allocation is used on Schedule CT-40.
## Credit for taxes paid to other states
For the Indiana credit for taxes paid elsewhere, Indiana tax on the tips is figured after this deduction, and the other state's tax after any deduction that state allows (Bulletin #128, Example 12: Ripley County tax on $45,000 of post-deduction compensation at .0238 = $1,071, which limits the credit for $1,260 of Cincinnati tax).
## Example
A full-year Marion County resident received $14,000 (2025 amount; confirm against the 2026 form) of qualified tips and claimed a $14,000 federal Schedule 1-A tips deduction. Schedule 2, line 11: qualified tips, code to be assigned on the 2026 form, $14,000. The same $14,000 reduces Indiana AGI used for county tax on Schedule CT-40.
## Questions
- Did you receive qualified tips in 2026 and claim the federal tips deduction on Schedule 1-A? What amount?
- Were you an Indiana resident all year, and if married filing jointly, how much of the tips were each spouse's?
## Common Errors
- Deducting total tips received instead of the federal deduction allowed after the phaseout
- Claiming the deduction on a married filing separately return where the federal deduction is disallowed
- Allocating to one spouse more than that spouse's own tips
- Carrying the deduction into a year other than 2026
