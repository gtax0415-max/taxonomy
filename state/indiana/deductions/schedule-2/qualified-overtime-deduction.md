# Qualified Overtime Compensation Deduction, 2026 Only (Schedule 2, Line 11)
## Description
For the 2026 tax year only, Indiana allows a deduction equal to the qualified overtime compensation deducted federally under IRC section 225 (IC 6-3-2-32, SEA 243, effective January 1, 2026). "Qualified overtime compensation" has the meaning in IRC 225(c). The Indiana deduction starts from the federal deduction on federal Schedule 1-A, line 21 (line per Income Tax Information Bulletin #128, which refers to the 2025 version of the federal form), including federal phaseouts and the federal disallowance for married filing separately, and cannot exceed it. The Schedule 2 code number is not yet published: code to be assigned on the 2026 form.
## Rules
The rules are the same as for qualified tips (Bulletin #128 says the tips examples also apply to overtime):
- Full-year residents: the state deduction equals the federal deduction; county tax uses the same amount except in isolated Perry County and domiciled-outside-Indiana cases.
- If an exemption applies, multiply the federal deduction by overtime subject to Indiana state or local tax (after exemptions, before the tips and overtime deductions) divided by overtime in federal AGI, separately for state and county where they differ.
- Married filing jointly: allocate the deduction between spouses, never more to a spouse than that spouse's own qualified overtime; the allocation carries to Schedule CT-40.
## Credit for taxes paid to other states (Bulletin #128, Example 11)
Tom, an Indiana resident working in Illinois, earned $10,000 (2025 amount; confirm against the 2026 form) of qualified overtime and $50,000 of other Illinois compensation, and claimed the $10,000 Indiana overtime deduction. Illinois has no such deduction and taxed $60,000 ($2,840). Indiana tax on the Illinois compensation after the deduction is $50,000 x .0295 = $1,475, so the Indiana credit for out-of-state taxes is $1,475.
## Example
A full-year Allen County resident claimed a $6,200 federal overtime deduction on Schedule 1-A. Schedule 2, line 11: qualified overtime, code to be assigned on the 2026 form, $6,200.
## Questions
- Did you receive qualified overtime in 2026 and claim the federal overtime deduction? What amount?
- Did you work in another state or claim a reciprocal-state exemption?
## Common Errors
- Deducting the full overtime pay rather than the federal deduction amount
- Claiming it on a married filing separately return where the federal deduction is disallowed
- Ignoring the effect on the credit for taxes paid to other states
