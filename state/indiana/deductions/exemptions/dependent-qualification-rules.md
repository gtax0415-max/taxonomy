# Dependent Qualification Rules (Schedule 3, Lines 2-3; booklet pages 24-28)
## Description
Indiana uses the federal-style dependency tests, set out in the IT-40 booklet and Income Tax Information Bulletin #117. A dependent is either a qualifying child or a qualifying relative.
## Step 1: Qualifying child
A child who is your son, daughter, stepchild, foster child, brother, sister, stepbrother, stepsister, half brother, half sister, or a descendant of any of them, AND who was:
- Under age 19 at year end and younger than you (or your spouse if joint), or under 24, a student and younger than you, or any age and permanently and totally disabled;
- Did not provide over half of his or her own support;
- Is not filing a joint return (or files one only to claim a refund of withholding or estimated tax);
- Lived with you more than half the year (see exceptions).
## Step 2: Is the qualifying child your dependent?
1. U.S. citizen, U.S. national, U.S. resident alien, or resident of Canada or Mexico? If no, stop (see the adopted child exception).
2. Married? See Married person.
3. Could you, or your spouse if filing jointly, be claimed as a dependent on someone else's return? If yes, you cannot claim any dependents.
## Step 3: Qualifying relative
A person who is your child, stepchild, foster child or their descendant; brother, sister, half brother, half sister or their child; father, mother, an ancestor or sibling of either; or any other person (not your spouse) who lived with you all year as a household member where the relationship does not violate local law; AND who:
- Was not a qualifying child of any taxpayer,
- Had gross income of less than $4,400 (2025 amount; confirm against the 2026 form) (disabled sheltered workshop exception),
- Received over half of his or her support from you (subject to the divorced parents, multiple support and kidnapped child rules).
Then the same citizenship, marriage and "could you be claimed" questions apply.
## Special rules
| Rule | Summary |
|---|---|
| Children of divorced or separated parents | Treated as the noncustodial parent's child if the parents are divorced, separated or lived apart the last 6 months, the child got over half of support from the parents, was in their custody over half the year, and the custodial parent signed Form 8332 (or a qualifying pre-2009 decree, or a pre-1985 decree plus $600 of support). Post-2008 decrees cannot replace Form 8332 |
| Custodial parent | Parent with whom the child lived the greater number of nights; if equal, the parent with higher federal AGI |
| Release revoked | Custodial parent keeps a copy of the revocation |
| Exception to citizen test | Adopted child who lived with a U.S. citizen or national all year meets the citizenship test |
| Exception to time lived with you | Temporary absences (school, vacation, business, medical care, military service, juvenile detention) count as time lived with you; a child born or who died during the year meets the test if your home was the child's home for more than half the time alive |
| Foster child | Placed by an authorized placement agency or by court order |
| Kidnapped child | May still count; see Bulletin #117 |
| Married person | Cannot be claimed if filing jointly, unless the joint return is only a refund claim |
| Multiple support agreements | A group providing over half of support may designate one member; see Bulletin #117 |
| Permanently and totally disabled | Cannot engage in substantial gainful activity because of a condition expected to last a year or more or to lead to death |
| Public assistance | TANF payments used to support someone; see Bulletin #117 |
| Student | Full-time student during any part of 5 calendar months, or in a full-time on-farm training course; not on-the-job training, correspondence or internet-only schools |
## Qualifying child of more than one person (tie-breakers)
- Only one is a parent: the parent.
- Parents file jointly: the parents.
- Parents file separately and both claim: the parent the child lived with longer; if equal, the parent with higher federal AGI.
- No parent can claim: the person with the highest federal AGI.
- A parent can claim but does not: the person with the highest federal AGI, but only if higher than any parent who could claim.
Booklet example: you, your daughter and your mother live together. You can claim your daughter; your mother cannot unless you do not claim her and your mother's federal AGI is higher than yours.
## SSN, ATIN and ITIN
Each dependent needs a 9-digit SSN in Box C matching the card. Use an ATIN (Form W-7A) for a child placed for adoption, or an ITIN (Form W-7) for a non-citizen non-resident. A child born and died in the year without an SSN: enter "Died".
## Questions
- How is each person related to you, how old at year end, and how long did they live with you?
- Did they provide over half their own support, or did you provide over half?
- Are you divorced or separated, and is there a signed Form 8332?
- Could someone else claim you or your spouse?
## Common Errors
- Both separated parents claiming the same child
- Noncustodial parent claiming without Form 8332 under a post-2008 decree
- Claiming a relative whose gross income is $4,400 or more
- Claiming dependents when you are yourself someone's dependent
