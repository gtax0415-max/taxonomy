# County Tax (Form IT-40, Line 9; Schedule CT-40)
## Description
If you live in an Indiana county on January 1 of the tax year, you will probably owe county tax. It is computed on Indiana adjusted gross income (IT-40, line 7) at the rate for the county where you lived on January 1, 2026, using Schedule CT-40. Enclose Schedule CT-40.
## County codes on the front of Form IT-40
Enter the two-digit codes (from the back of Schedule CT-40) for the county where you lived and the county where you worked on January 1, 2026, and the same for your spouse. If your mailing address differs from your principal residence on January 1, list the principal residence address on Schedule CT-40.
- County where you lived: where you maintained your home on January 1. With more than one Indiana home: where you were registered to vote; if not applicable, where your personal automobile was registered; if not applicable, where you spent the majority of your time in Indiana during the year.
- County where you worked: your main place of business or main work activity on January 1. With more than one job, the county where you worked the most hours and earned the most income. Changes after January 1 take effect next year.
- Worked outside Indiana on January 1: code 00, except Illinois 94, Kentucky 95, Michigan 96, Ohio 97, Pennsylvania 98, Wisconsin 99.
- Retired, homemaker or unemployed on January 1: enter the county of residence code in both the lived and worked boxes. Do not write "Retired", "Homemaker" or "Unemployed" over the boxes.
## Moving during the year
Booklet example: William lived in Scott County until moving to Martin County on March 15. He figures Scott County tax for that year and Martin County tax the next year if he still lives there on January 1. Jessie worked in Marion County on January 1 and started a job in Johnson County on February 10; she enters Marion County code 49 as her work county.
## Military personnel
If stationed in Indiana, your county of residence is the county where you lived on January 1 of the year you entered military service. If on January 1 you were single and stationed outside Indiana, or stationed outside Indiana with your family, enter 00 in all county boxes (no county tax). If you kept a home in an Indiana county, or your spouse and family lived in an Indiana county on January 1, you are a resident of that county and owe county tax.
## Principal residence address (Schedule CT-40)
Enter the street address (or rural route) where you lived on January 1, even if the same as the mailing address; for a joint return also the spouse's. Do not enter a P.O. box, a non-residential address, or "Same", "See page 1" or similar. Use the 2-character code for a foreign country.
## Schedule CT-40 lines
```
Line 1A/1B  IT-40 line 7 (all in 1A unless spouses lived in different counties)
Line 2A/2B  County tax rate for the county where each lived on Jan. 1
Line 3A/3B  Line 1 x line 2 (leave blank if less than zero)
Line 4      3A + 3B
Line 5      Perry County residents: income taxed by certain Kentucky localities
Line 6      Line 5 x Perry County rate (.0140)
Line 7      Line 4 - line 6 → Form IT-40, line 9
```
## Spouses in different counties
Each spouse's share of IT-40 line 7 goes in Columns A and B. Each spouse uses his or her own personal $1,000 exemption, age 65 or older exemption and additional age 65 exemption (2025 amount; confirm against the 2026 form). Dependent exemptions are assigned in whole to whichever spouse gives the most benefit, usually the spouse with the higher county rate. Exemptions cannot be split (a $1,000 exemption cannot be divided $700/$300). Total exemptions used cannot exceed Schedule 3, line 7 (the booklet says "Schedule 3, line 6", which is the adopted child line; line 7 is the total). Exception: if one spouse has negative income after exemptions, it may reduce the other spouse's positive income; only then can one spouse's exemptions reduce the other's income.
Booklet example 1: Jacob (Greene County) and Becca (Clay County) married during the year. Wages $23,000 and $21,000, joint interest $40 each, renter's deduction $1,500 each, exemption $1,000 each. Line 1A = $20,540; line 1B = $18,540; total $39,080 = IT-40 line 7.
Booklet example 2: Sam (County A, rate .01) and Molly (County B, rate .025), son Sebastian age 3. Total exemptions $4,500. Wages $49,000 and $45,000; $2,500 property tax deduction split $1,250 each. Molly uses all exemptions except Sam's $1,000. Line 1A = $46,750; line 1B = $40,250; total $87,000. County tax: $46,750 x .01 = $468 (rounded); $40,250 x .025 = $1,006 (rounded); line 4 = $1,474.
## 2026-only deductions and county tax
The 2026 tips, overtime and vehicle loan interest deductions reduce IT-40 line 7 and so reduce county tax. For full-year residents the county deduction equals the state deduction except in isolated Perry County and domiciled-outside-Indiana cases. Spouses in different counties use the same allocation of those deductions on Schedule CT-40 as for the state return (Income Tax Information Bulletin #128).
## Perry County residents, lines 5 and 6
If you were a Perry County resident on January 1, worked in the Kentucky counties of Breckinridge, Hancock and/or Meade, and that income was subject to a Kentucky county income tax or a local income tax of a locality in those counties: enter that income on line 5, multiply by .0140 on line 6, and subtract on line 7. Example: line 4 county tax $700 on $50,000 of income; $30,000 was taxed by a Meade County locality; line 6 = $420; line 7 = $280.
## 2026 rates
Use the rate for the county where you lived on January 1, 2026, from the 2026 Schedule CT-40 chart. Six counties changed rates effective January 1, 2026 (Carroll, Grant, Greene, Howard, Shelby, Union) and Boone changed from .017 to .0171 effective October 1, 2026. See county-tax-rates-2026.md.
## Out of scope
Nonresidents who work in an Indiana county, and part-year residents, figure county tax on Schedule CT-40PNR with Form IT-40PNR.
## Questions
- Where did you (and your spouse) live and work on January 1, 2026? Did you move or change jobs after that date?
- If married filing jointly, did you live in different counties on January 1, and how should exemptions be assigned?
- Were you retired, a homemaker, unemployed or in the military on January 1?
- If you live in Perry County, did you have income taxed by a Breckinridge, Hancock or Meade County, Kentucky locality?
## Common Errors
- Using the county you moved to during the year instead of the January 1 county
- Writing "Retired" over the county boxes instead of the residence code
- Splitting a $1,000 exemption between spouses
- Leaving the principal residence address blank or writing "Same"
- Using the 2025 rate for one of the counties that changed on January 1, 2026
