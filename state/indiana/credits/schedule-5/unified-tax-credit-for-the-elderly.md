# Unified Tax Credit for the Elderly (IT-40 Schedule 5, Line 5)
## Description
A small refundable credit for low-income individuals age 65 or older. The amount is read from Table A (joint filers both 65 or older) or Table B (only one person 65 or older) according to the income on IT-40, line 1 (federal AGI), which must be less than $10,000 (2025 amount; confirm against the 2026 form). The credit may be claimed during the same time period as any other refundable credit: the return must be filed and the credit claimed within three years of the filing due date (including extensions) to be eligible for a refund.
## Who qualifies (all must be met)
- You and/or your spouse were age 65 or older by Dec. 31 of the tax year (Dec. 31, 2026).
- If married and living together at any time during the year, you file a joint return.
- The amount on IT-40, line 1 is less than $10,000.
- You were an Indiana resident for at least six months during the year.
- You were not in prison for 180 days or more during the year.

Disabled persons under age 65 do not qualify. If a spouse died after Jan. 1, the survivor claims the credit on a joint return. If a person dies with no surviving spouse, no one can claim the credit on behalf of the deceased person.
## Table A - Joint filers both age 65 or older
Use if filing jointly, lived with your spouse during the year, both were Indiana residents for at least six months and both were 65 or older by Dec. 31; or both met all the requirements and the spouse died after Jan. 1.

| IT-40, line 1 | Credit |
|---|---|
| less than $1,000 | $140 |
| between $1,000 and $2,999 | $90 |
| between $3,000 and $9,999 | $80 |
## Table B - Only one person age 65 or older
Use if you are 65 or older and single or widowed; filing jointly and only one is 65 or older; filing jointly and only one was an Indiana resident for at least six months; or married, did not live with your spouse during the year, are 65 or older and file separately.

| IT-40, line 1 | Credit |
|---|---|
| less than $1,000 | $100 |
| between $1,000 and $2,999 | $50 |
| between $3,000 and $9,999 | $40 |

Table amounts are 2025 amounts; confirm against the 2026 form. The booklet tables are in whole dollars ("between $1,000 and $2,999"); amounts such as $2,999.50 fall between the printed bands, and Form SC-40 prints the same bands to the cent ($1,000-$2,999.99).
## Form SC-40 alternative
A filer who is not required to file Form IT-40 may claim the credit on the simplified Form SC-40 if they have no other credits (such as withholding or estimated tax) and:
- single or widowed with income under $2,500; or
- married with only one person 65 or older and income under $3,500; or
- married with both 65 or older and income under $5,000.

No double benefit: file either Form SC-40 or Form IT-40, not both, and only once. Form SC-40 cannot be filed with an extension of time to file. SC-40 measures income on its own line E (wages, dividends and interest, business and rental, pensions; Social Security, veteran's disability pensions and life insurance proceeds excluded), not IT-40 line 1, and prints a chart that stops at those lower limits (for example single $1,000-$2,499.99 gives $50; $2,500 or over must file IT-40). SC-40 itself is out of scope for this taxonomy; this file covers the IT-40 claim.
## Examples
- Single, age 72, federal AGI $4,200, lived in Indiana all year: Table B, between $3,000 and $9,999 → $40 on line 5.
- Married filing jointly, both 66, federal AGI $850: Table A, less than $1,000 → $140.
- Married filing jointly, one spouse 67, the other 60, federal AGI $9,999: Table B → $40. At $10,000 no credit.
## Questions
- What were your and your spouse's dates of birth?
- How many months did each of you live in Indiana during the year?
- Did you live with your spouse at any time during the year?
- Were either of you in prison for 180 days or more?
- Did a spouse die during the year, and on what date?
- Are you filing Form SC-40 for this year?
## Common Errors
- Using Indiana AGI (IT-40, line 7) or SC-40 line E instead of IT-40, line 1
- Claiming the credit at exactly $10,000 of federal AGI
- Using Table A when only one spouse is 65 or older or only one was a six-month resident
- Claiming on both Form SC-40 and Form IT-40
- Claiming for a disabled filer under 65
- Claiming for a deceased person with no surviving spouse
