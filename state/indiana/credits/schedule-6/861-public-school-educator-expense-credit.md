# Public School Educator Expense Credit (IT-40 Schedule 6, Line 6, Code 861)
## Description
An eligible educator working for an Indiana school corporation may take a credit for qualified expenses paid for certain classroom supplies. The credit is the lesser of the amount paid for qualified supplies (less reimbursements not included in W-2 box 1) or $100 (2025 amount; confirm against the 2026 form); up to $200 on a joint return when both spouses qualify, but not more than $100 each.
## Eligible educator
During the taxable year you are employed as a kindergarten through grade 12 Indiana public school teacher, counselor, superintendent, librarian or principal. Public school means a school maintained by an Indiana school corporation and includes charter schools. Private schools, parochial schools and homeschools are not public schools.
## Qualified and non-qualified expenses
- Qualified: amounts paid or incurred during the year for books, supplies, computer equipment (including related software and services), other equipment, and supplementary materials used in the classroom. For health and physical education, supplies count only if related to athletics.
- Not qualified: certain professional development course expenses; protective items such as face masks, disinfectant, hand soap, hand sanitizer, disposable gloves, tape, paint or chalk for social distancing, physical barriers, air purifiers and other items recommended by the CDC to prevent the spread of disease.
- Reduce the expenses by reimbursements received that were not included in W-2 box 1.
## How to claim
Enter the credit name, code 861 and the amount under Schedule 6, line 6 (rows 6a-6d; enclose additional sheets if needed).
## Limits
Combined Limitation: Schedule 6, lines 4 through 7 together cannot be greater than the state adjusted gross income tax on IT-40, line 8 (2.95% of Indiana AGI for 2026). The credit does not reduce county tax.
## Example
- Booklet Example 1: Jonah spent $40 and was reimbursed $30 from petty cash (not on his W-2) → credit $10.
- Booklet Example 2: Quincy, an 8th grade public school teacher, spent $314 → credit $100.
- Booklet Example 3: Chris spent $74 and Pat spent $214, both public high school teachers filing jointly → $74 + $100 = $174.
## Questions
- Do you (or your spouse) work for an Indiana public or charter school in a K-12 teacher, counselor, superintendent, librarian or principal position?
- How much did each of you spend on classroom supplies?
- Were any of the costs reimbursed outside your W-2 wages?
- Were any purchases protective items or professional development?
## Common Errors
- Claiming as a private or parochial school teacher, an aide or a college instructor
- Combining spouses' expenses to let one spouse exceed $100
- Including protective items or reimbursed amounts
- Thinking the federal educator deduction rules it out
## Prompt
- I am an Indiana teacher who buys classroom supplies. Is there a state credit?
