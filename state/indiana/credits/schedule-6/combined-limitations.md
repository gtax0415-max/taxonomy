# Combined Limitations and Order of Application (IT-40 Schedule 6)
## Description
Three rules control how much of the Schedule 6 offset credits can be entered. They are applied in this order: (1) the restriction that limits certain credits to one per project; (2) the Combined Limitation for the county-tax credits on lines 1 through 3; (3) the Combined Limitation for the state-tax credits on lines 4 through 7. Schedule 5 credits are not limited.
## 1. Restriction for Certain Tax Credits - Limited to One per Project
A taxpayer may not be granted more than one credit for the same project. The credits subject to the restriction are:
- alternative fuel vehicle manufacturer credit (845)
- community revitalization enhancement district credit (Schedule 6 line 2; 808)
- enterprise zone investment cost credit (813)
- Hoosier business investment credit (820)
- industrial recovery credit (824)
- venture capital investment credit (835)

See Income Tax Information Bulletin #59. Apply this restriction first, then the Combined Limitation.
## 2. Combined Limitation - lines 1 through 3 (county tax)
If you claim more than one credit on lines 1 through 3, together they cannot be greater than the county tax on IT-40, line 9; if they are, adjust the amounts before entering them.

Order of Application: first use the credits that cannot be carried over and applied against county tax in another year: the credit for local taxes paid outside Indiana first, then the community revitalization enhancement district credit.

Example (booklet): Megan has a $100 credit for local taxes paid outside Indiana and a $200 CRED credit, $300 in total (2025 example amounts). Her county tax (IT-40, line 9) is $160. She reduces the last entry, the CRED credit, by the $140 excess to $60. She enters $100 on line 1 and $60 for CRED. She may use the remaining $140 CRED credit against state tax (IT-40, line 8) under line 6 with code 808.

The booklet's example says the $60 CRED amount goes "on line 3a"; the 2025 Schedule 6 prints CRED on line 2 and line 3 as "Other Local Credits." Enter CRED on line 2 per the form; confirm on the 2026 booklet.
## 3. Combined Limitation - lines 4 through 7 (state tax)
If you have more than one credit on lines 4 through 7, together they cannot be greater than the state adjusted gross income tax on IT-40, line 8, including credits from Schedule IN-OCC carried to line 7. Adjust the amounts before entering them.

Example (booklet): Miranda has a $200 college credit and a $300 credit for taxes paid to other states, $500 in total. Her state tax (IT-40, line 8) is $360. She reduces the last entry (other states) by the $140 excess to $160: line 4 $200, line 5 $160.

Example (booklet): Matthew has a $500 Indiana529 credit and a $600 industrial recovery credit. His state tax is $700. He reports $500 for Indiana529 on line 6a and $200 of industrial recovery on line 6b, and carries the $400 unused industrial recovery credit to next year.

The examples keep whole the credit that cannot be carried over (Indiana529, college, other states) and cut the one that can (industrial recovery). Schedule IN-ABLE's instructions similarly warn that when other current-year-only Schedule 6 state credits are claimed, a lower ABLE amount may have to be reported. Unused current-year credits are reported on Schedule IN-OCC, Part B.
## 2026 note
IT-40, line 8 for 2026 is Indiana AGI x 2.95%, not the 3% printed on the 2025 form, so the state-tax ceiling for lines 4-7 is lower for the same income.
## Questions
- What are your county tax (IT-40, line 9) and state tax (IT-40, line 8)?
- Which of your credits can be carried forward, and which expire this year?
- Do any two of your credits relate to the same project?
## Common Errors
- Entering every credit in full and letting Schedule 6 exceed the tax
- Reducing a credit that cannot be carried over while keeping a carryforward credit whole
- Forgetting that CRED cut off against county tax can still be used against state tax (808)
- Claiming two of the listed project credits for the same project
## Prompt
- My Indiana credits are more than my tax. Which one do I reduce?
- Can my CRED credit offset state tax?
