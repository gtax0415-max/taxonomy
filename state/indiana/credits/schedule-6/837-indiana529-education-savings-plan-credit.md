# Indiana529 Education Savings Plan Credit (IT-40 Schedule 6, Line 6, Code 837)
## Description
A credit for contributions to the Indiana529 Education Savings Plan (formerly "CollegeChoice 529"), governed by the Indiana Education Savings Authority. Only contributions to this specific Indiana plan qualify. The credit is 20% of contributions, up to $1,500 ($750 married filing separately) (2025 amount; confirm against the 2026 form), and not more than the state tax on IT-40, line 8. It is available on single, joint and married filing separately returns. Schedule IN-529 must be enclosed.
## 2026 change (SEA 453)
For taxable years from January 1, 2026, the credit is 20% of the total contributions made by the taxpayer to Indiana529 accounts during the year. The earlier law (and the 2025 Schedule IN-529) split contributions into higher education (Column C) and K-12 (Column D) and required a designation when contributing; SEA 453 removes the split and the designation requirement, and the Indiana Education Savings Authority tracks designations by subaccount. The 2026 Schedule IN-529 is expected to have one contribution column; confirm its layout. The $1,500 / $750 caps are 2025 figures.
## Schedule IN-529 (2025 layout)
- Lines 1-9: Column A mark if you or your spouse do not own the account; Column B account number (11 digits beginning 61 or 62, 10 digits beginning 200, 201, 208 or 209, or 16 digits beginning 200 or 201); Column C higher education contributions; Column D K-12 contributions.
- Line 10 total of Column C; line 11 total of Column D; line 12 = line 10 + line 11; line 13 = line 12 x .20; line 14 = lesser of line 13, 1500, or 750 if married filing separately; line 15 = IT-40, line 8; line 16 = lesser of line 14 or line 15 → under Schedule 6, line 6.
- Complete IT-40 through line 8 first.
## Contributions after year end
A contribution made before the regular due date of the return may be elected for the prior year (the 2025 booklet: by April 15, 2026, for 2025). Include a letter with the contribution or ask the plan administrator to count it for the prior year. A contribution claimed for one year cannot be claimed again for the next. Prior-year-elected contributions may take up to 45 days to be verifiable.
## Not eligible
- Contributions to an account that will be transferred to an ABLE account
- Money credited because of bonus points or other consideration
- Money transferred from any other qualified tuition program under IRC 529 or a similar plan
## Recapture
An account owner who makes a non-qualified withdrawal (not for qualified higher education expenses, death or disability of the beneficiary, a scholarship, or a custodian transfer; a withdrawal from an account closed within 12 months of opening; or a rollover to another 529 plan or ABLE account) repays the credit on Schedule IN-CR, code 837: 20% of the non-qualified withdrawal (10% if from funds contributed to a 2018 K-12 account, Column B box). The total goes to Schedule 4, line 3. See Income Tax Information Bulletin #98.
## How to claim
Enter the credit name, code 837 and the amount under Schedule 6, line 6 (rows 6a-6d; enclose additional sheets if needed).
## Limits
- Any unused credit cannot be carried back or forward, sold, assigned or transferred. In the booklet's Combined Limitation example the Indiana529 credit is kept whole and the carryforward credit is reduced.
- Combined Limitation: Schedule 6, lines 4 through 7 together cannot be greater than the state adjusted gross income tax on IT-40, line 8 (2.95% of Indiana AGI for 2026). The credit does not reduce county tax.
## Example
Married filing jointly; $5,000 contributed to their child's Indiana529 account and $1,000 by the couple to a niece's account they do not own. 2026: $6,000 x 20% = $1,200; cap $1,500; IT-40 line 8 $4,100 → credit $1,200. With $8,000 contributed: $1,600, limited to $1,500.
## Questions
- Did you or your spouse contribute to an Indiana529 account during the year, and to which account numbers?
- Do you own each account?
- Did you make a contribution after year end that you want counted for this year?
- Are you married filing separately?
- Did you, as account owner, make any non-qualified withdrawals?
## Common Errors
- Using the 2025 higher education / K-12 split for 2026
- Claiming contributions to a non-Indiana 529 plan
- Using $1,500 on a married filing separately return
- Claiming rollovers or transfers as contributions
- Claiming the same contribution in two years
- Carrying unused credit forward
