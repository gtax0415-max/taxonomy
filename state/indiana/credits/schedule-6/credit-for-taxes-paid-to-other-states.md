# Credit for Taxes Paid to Other States (IT-40 Schedule 6, Line 5)
## Description
An Indiana resident reports all income, including income from other states, on the Indiana return. A credit is allowed for income tax paid to another state (or a foreign country or U.S. possession) on income Indiana also taxes, depending on which group the other state is in. The credit offsets state adjusted gross income tax only.
## State groups
| Group | Rule | States |
|---|---|---|
| A - No Agreement (credit on the Indiana resident return) | Group A Worksheet | Alabama, Arkansas, California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Georgia, Hawaii, Idaho, Illinois, Iowa, Kansas, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Minnesota, Mississippi, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, New Jersey, New Mexico, New York, North Carolina, North Dakota, Oklahoma, Rhode Island, South Carolina, Utah, Vermont, Virginia, Washington (capital gains only), West Virginia; any foreign country or U.S. possession |
| B - Reciprocal Agreement (wages, salaries, tips and commissions only) | No Indiana credit for wages; the employer should withhold Indiana tax; if it withheld for the other state, claim a refund from that state. Other income from these states uses the Group A Worksheet | Kentucky, Michigan, Ohio, Pennsylvania, Wisconsin |
| C - Reverse Credit (credit on the nonresident return) | Pay Indiana tax on all income; file the other jurisdiction's nonresident return and claim the credit there | Arizona, Oregon, Washington D.C. |
| D - No State Income Tax | No credit | Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Wyoming |

Winnings from Indiana riverboats and lotteries are not covered by the reciprocal agreement. If a Group B employer does not withhold enough Indiana tax, estimated payments may be needed.
## Group A Worksheet
- A. Tax paid to the other state: the actual tax figured on the other state's return, not the tax withheld from wages.
- B. Income from the other state that is subject to Indiana tax x the state rate. The 2025 booklet prints 3% (.03); for 2026 use the 2026 state rate of 2.95% (.0295).
- C. Indiana state income tax on IT-40, line 8.

The credit is the lesser of A, B or C.
## Special rules
- District of Columbia: a resident personally subject to the D.C. Unincorporated Business Franchise Tax (D-30) may claim the credit for it. Do not claim a credit for tax paid to D.C. on Form D-40.
- Pass-through entity taxes: an owner or beneficiary of a partnership, S corporation, estate, trust or similar entity subject to another state's pass through entity tax enacted after 2017 and substantially similar to Indiana's PTET may claim a credit for that tax as if paid directly. Composite or withholding taxes paid by the entity on your behalf also qualify.
- Exception 1, gambling winnings: if no other-state return is required, enclose the W-2G issued for that state and use the state tax withheld on line A.
- Exception 2, deferred foreign tax: income from a non-U.S. country or territory not yet taxed there but that will be; enclose the country, type of income, amount, reason for deferral, the tax due on recognition, and the credit computation.
- Foreign tax: include federal Form 1116, Canadian Form NR4 or a qualified payee statement (Form 1099-DIV, 1099-INT, Schedule K-1, Schedule K-3 or similar substitute).
## Records
Enclose a copy of each other state's return. See Income Tax Information Bulletin #28 for acceptable documentation when no return is filed with the other state.
## Examples
- Booklet (2025 rate): Ryan has $10,000 of Illinois wages and paid $300 to Illinois; IT-40 line 8 is $870. A $300; B $10,000 x .03 = $300; C $870 → $300.
- Same facts at the 2026 rate: B = $10,000 x .0295 = $295 → credit $295.
- Booklet (2025 rate): Sarah's partnership share is $100,000 plus $10,000 bonus depreciation add-back; 40% from Illinois; she paid $2,000 to Illinois; her state tax is $5,000. A $2,000; B ($110,000 x .4 x .03) = $1,320; C $5,000 → $1,320. At 2.95%, B = $1,298.
## Combined Limitation
Lines 4 through 7 together cannot exceed IT-40, line 8.
## Questions
- Which states (or countries) taxed your income, and how much tax did each return show?
- What kind of income was it (wages, business, capital gains, gambling)?
- Did a pass-through entity pay another state's PTET, composite or withholding tax for you?
- Do you have the other state's return, W-2G, Form 1116 or K-1?
## Common Errors
- Using other-state withholding instead of the tax on the other state's return
- Claiming a credit for wages from Kentucky, Michigan, Ohio, Pennsylvania or Wisconsin
- Claiming a credit for Arizona, Oregon or D.C. (Form D-40) tax
- Multiplying line B by 3% for 2026
- Including Washington tax other than capital gains
- Reporting other-state withholding on Schedule 5, line 1
## Prompt
- I live in Indiana and work in Illinois. How do I get credit for Illinois tax?
- I work in Kentucky. Do I get a credit for Kentucky tax?
