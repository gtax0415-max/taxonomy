# Gifts and Inheritance
## Description
A gift or inheritance is NOT income and is not reported. Income the gifted or inherited property LATER produces is taxable.

## Income that can follow a gift or inheritance
- Interest
- Dividends
- Rent
- Pension or retirement distributions
- Gain on a sale of real property

## Example
Inherited cash is deposited and earns interest. Only the interest is income; the inherited cash is not.

## Notes
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- California has no inheritance or gift tax
- Property held by an estate or trust is reported through the estate or trust; beneficiaries report their share from Schedule K-1
- Inherited retirement accounts are taxable when distributed

## Required Information
- Description and value of the gift or inheritance
- Income produced after receipt
- Any K-1 from an estate or trust

## Questions
- Did you receive a gift or inheritance in 2026?
- Has it earned interest, dividends or rent since you received it?
- Did you inherit a retirement account, or sell inherited property?

## Common Errors
- Reporting the inheritance itself as income
- Forgetting income produced by inherited assets
- Treating inherited IRA distributions as tax-free

## Prompt
- I inherited $100,000 from my aunt.
- My parents gave me money for a down payment. Is that taxable?
