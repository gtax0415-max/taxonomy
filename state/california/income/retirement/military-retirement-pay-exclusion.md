# Military Retirement Pay and Survivor Benefit Plan Exclusion
## Description
Available for 2025 through 2029 (so for the 2026 return): California lets qualified taxpayers exclude up to $20,000 a year of federal military (uniformed services) retirement pay, or of U.S. Defense Department Survivor Benefit Plan annuity payments. Before 2025, residents paid full California tax on military retirement pay.

## Amount
- Up to $20,000 per year
- Applies to tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2025 and before January 1, 2030

## Income limits (federal AGI)
- Individual: $125,000 or less
- Married filing jointly or surviving spouse: $250,000 or less

## Related military benefits
- Military retirement pay ABOVE the exclusion stays taxable for residents, wherever the person served or was domiciled
- VA disability benefits, including lump-sum retroactive payments: tax-exempt, not income, not reported on a 1099-R
- Concurrent Retirement and Disability Pay (CRDP): taxable, reported on a 1099-R, even with a VA disability determination
- Combat-Related Special Compensation (CRSC): tax-exempt
- California follows federal law on VA disability treatment

## Notes
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- The exclusion is a subtraction on Schedule CA; confirm the line in the 2026 Schedule CA (540) instructions when released
- Nonresident military retirees generally owe California nothing on retirement pay

## Required Information
- 1099-R amounts and payer
- Type of payment (retirement, SBP annuity, CRDP, CRSC, VA disability)
- Federal AGI and filing status

## Questions
- Do you receive military retirement pay or a Survivor Benefit Plan annuity?
- What is your federal AGI?
- Do you also receive VA disability, CRDP or CRSC payments?

## Common Errors
- Missing the exclusion, which first applied in 2025
- Claiming it with federal AGI above $125,000 / $250,000
- Excluding CRDP as if it were VA disability
- Reporting VA disability as income

## Prompt
- I'm a retired Army veteran living in California and receive military retirement pay.
- I get a Survivor Benefit Plan annuity from my late husband's service.
