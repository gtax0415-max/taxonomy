# Pensions and Annuities
## Description
Pension and annuity income generally follows federal law. Three California differences appear on Schedule CA line 5.

## California differences
- RAILROAD RETIREMENT (Tier 2, Form RRB-1099-R): NOT taxed by California. Subtract the taxable amount in column B. (Tier 1 is treated like Social Security; see income/government-benefits/social-security-benefits.md)
- THREE-YEAR RULE: a filer who began receiving an annuity between July 1, 1986 and January 1, 1987 and used the three-year rule for California but the annuity rules federally adds back the federally excluded amount in column C
- MILITARY RETIREMENT and Survivor Benefit Plan annuities: up to $20,000 excluded (2025–2029; see income/retirement/military-retirement-pay-exclusion.md)

## Notes
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- Early distributions before 59½ may owe California's additional tax (FTB 3805P)
- Nonqualified deferred compensation and nongovernmental 457 plan payments are reported on Schedule CA, line 8t (see income/other-income/other-income-items.md)
- FTB Publication 1005 has pension and annuity guidance

## Required Information
- 1099-R and RRB-1099-R
- Annuity start date and method used

## Questions
- Do you receive railroad retirement benefits?
- When did your pension annuity start?
- Is any of your pension military retirement pay?

## Common Errors
- Paying California tax on Tier 2 railroad retirement
- Missing the three-year rule adjustment

## Prompt
- I get a railroad retirement pension. Is it taxable in California?
