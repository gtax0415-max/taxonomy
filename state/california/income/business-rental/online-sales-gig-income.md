# Online Sales, Gig and 1099 Income
## Description
Income from online selling, auctions, app-based work and other nonemployee services. California taxes residents on ALL income from every source, and the income is taxable even if no 1099 is received.

## 1099 thresholds
- 1099-K (third-party settlement organizations): generally issued only when gross payments exceed $20,000 AND there are more than 200 transactions (retroactive to 2022). The previously announced phased-in thresholds ($5,000 for 2024, $2,500 for 2025) were withdrawn and do not apply
- 1099-K for app-based drivers: $600 or more, regardless of the number of transactions (since January 1, 2021)
- 1099-NEC and 1099-MISC: $2,000 or more for 2026 (was $600 through 2025)

## Hobby or business
An activity is a business if carried on continuously with the expectation of profit. Factors:
1. Businesslike operation and complete books and records
2. Time and effort showing intent to profit
3. Dependence on the income
4. Losses beyond your control or from start-up
5. Changed methods to improve profit
6. Knowledge to run it as a business
7. Past profit in similar activities
8. Profit in most of the past 5 years
9. Expected profit from asset appreciation

## Online sales
- Occasional garage-sale-type sales of personal items: generally not reported (sold at a loss)
- A sale above cost plus improvements is a reportable gain
- Continuous sales or buying items for resale suggests a business; self-employment tax may apply

## Notes
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- Part-year residents are taxed on all income received while a California resident
- Estimated tax payments may be required
- Business expenses are deductible with good records
- Sellers making 3 or more sales in 12 months generally need a seller's permit from the CDTFA (a sales tax matter, not income tax)
- Buyers may owe use tax when an out-of-state seller does not collect tax

## Required Information
- All 1099-K, 1099-NEC and 1099-MISC forms
- Gross sales and cost of items sold
- Business expenses
- Hobby or business determination

## Questions
- Do you sell online regularly, or only occasional personal items?
- Do you drive or deliver for an app?
- Did you receive any 1099 forms?
- What expenses did you have?

## Common Errors
- Not reporting income because no 1099 arrived
- Reporting the gross 1099-K amount without subtracting cost of goods or refunds
- Treating a business as a hobby, or the reverse
- Skipping estimated payments

## Prompt
- I sell vintage clothes on an online marketplace.
- I drive for a rideshare app and got a 1099-K.
- I received a 1099-NEC for freelance design work.
