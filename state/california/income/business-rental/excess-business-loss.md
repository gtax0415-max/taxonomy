# Excess Business Loss Limitation
## Description
Noncorporate taxpayers cannot deduct net business losses above a threshold in one year. California applies its own version: the disallowed loss carries forward as an EXCESS BUSINESS LOSS carryover, not as an NOL.

## Thresholds
- 2026: pending — FTB said in October 2026 that it is still finalizing the 2026 threshold amounts
- 2025: $313,000; $626,000 married/RDP filing jointly
- File FTB 3461 when net losses from all trades or businesses exceed the threshold

## California differences
- California did NOT adopt the CARES Act suspension for 2018–2020, nor the ARPA and Inflation Reduction Act extensions
- The disallowed amount carries to the next year as an excess business loss carryover (federally it becomes an NOL)
- A prior-year carryover offsets current business income first (FTB 3461 instructions, lines 14b and 15)
- For 2025 and later, California conforms to the CARES Act clarification that wages and employee-related income and deductions are excluded when figuring the excess business loss (SB 1435)

## Notes
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)

## Required Information
- Net income or loss from each trade or business
- Prior-year California carryover

## Questions
- Do you have large business losses from partnerships, S corporations or a sole proprietorship?
- Do you have an excess business loss carryover from last year's California return?

## Common Errors
- Treating the California disallowed loss as an NOL
- Using the federal threshold or federal carryover
- Including wages in the business loss computation

## Prompt
- My partnership losses this year were over $700,000.
