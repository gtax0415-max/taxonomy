# Rental Income
## Description
Income from the occupancy of real estate or the use of personal property, less ordinary and necessary expenses. For California, rental activity is ALWAYS a PASSIVE activity.

## What counts as rental income
- Monthly rent received from tenants
- Payments by a tenant for canceling a lease

## Notes
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- All ordinary and necessary expenses of maintaining the rental are deductible
- RESIDENTS are taxed on rental income from property ANYWHERE; NONRESIDENTS only on California property
- Because rental is always passive in California, losses may be limited differently than federally (for example, California does not conform to the federal real estate professional exception). Passive losses are figured on the California passive activity loss form (FTB 3801), with California's own $25,000 rental allowance and carryover, and differences flow to Schedule CA
- Depreciation can differ between federal and California, creating basis differences

## Required Information
- Rents received and lease cancellation payments
- Expenses by category
- Depreciation schedule
- Property location
- Residency status

## Questions
- Where is the rental property located?
- What rent did you receive, and what expenses did you pay?
- Did the rental produce a loss?
- Are you a California resident, nonresident or part-year resident?

## Common Errors
- Treating rental income as nonpassive in California
- Missing California depreciation differences
- A nonresident reporting out-of-state rental income to California

## Prompt
- I rent out a condo in San Diego.
- My rental property had a loss this year.
