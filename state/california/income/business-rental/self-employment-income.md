# Self-Employment Income
## Description
A self-employed person is in business for themselves, as a sole proprietor, independent contractor or partner in a trade or business. Business income or loss is reported on the individual California return (Form 540), and quarterly estimated tax payments may be required.

## Who is self-employed
- In business for yourself, including a part-time business
- A sole proprietor or independent contractor
- A partner in a partnership carrying on a trade or business
- Often: no W-2 was received

## Common self-employed jobs
- Rideshare and food delivery drivers
- Gardeners
- Childcare, homecare and domestic workers
- Street vendors
- Professional house cleaners

## Notes
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- All business income and losses go on the individual return
- Estimated tax may be due quarterly
- California has no self-employment tax of its own; the federal SE tax deduction and self-employed health insurance deduction generally carry through federal AGI (but see income/business-rental/employee-or-independent-contractor.md when California treats the worker as an employee)
- App-based drivers can be independent contractors in California under Proposition 22 if its conditions are met

## Required Information
- All 1099 forms and other business receipts
- Business expenses by category
- Partnership K-1s
- Estimated payments made

## Questions
- Did you work for yourself or receive 1099s instead of a W-2?
- What expenses did your business have?
- Did you make estimated tax payments?
- Are you a partner in a business?

## Common Errors
- Leaving out income that had no 1099
- Skipping estimated payments
- Not keeping records for expenses
