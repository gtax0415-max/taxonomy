# 529 Plan and Coverdell ESA Distributions
## Description
California follows the older federal rules for 529 tuition plans and does not adopt several federal expansions, so some distributions that are tax-free federally are TAXABLE in California.

## Distributions taxable in California (column C)
- K-12 TUITION and other elementary or secondary school expenses (TCJA expansion; OBBBA added more K-12 expenses and raised the federal cap to $20,000 starting 2026)
- Qualified POSTSECONDARY CREDENTIALING expenses (OBBBA)
- 529-TO-ROTH IRA ROLLOVERS (SECURE 2.0, for 529 accounts open 15 years): the rollover is taxable in California AND subject to a 2.5% additional tax
- Amounts above the federal maximum distribution rules that California does not follow

## Coverdell ESA
- Enter the difference between the federal and California taxable amount on line 8z (column B or C)
- A taxable distribution may owe California's additional tax on FTB 3805P

## Notes
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- The ScholarShare (California 529) contributions are not deductible for California
- See other-taxes/retirement-account-penalties.md for FTB 3805P

## Required Information
- 1099-Q and what the money paid for
- Any 529-to-Roth rollover

## Questions
- Did you use 529 funds for K-12 tuition or a credential program?
- Did you roll a 529 account into a Roth IRA?
- Did you take a Coverdell distribution?

## Common Errors
- Treating K-12 529 withdrawals as tax-free in California
- Missing the 2.5% additional tax on a 529-to-Roth rollover

## Prompt
- I used my 529 plan to pay private high school tuition.
- I rolled my child's leftover 529 money into a Roth IRA.
