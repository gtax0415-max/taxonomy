# Cancellation of Debt (including Mortgage Debt Forgiveness)
## Description
When a lender cancels or forgives a debt, the forgiven amount is generally income. California follows most federal rules, but it does NOT conform to the federal exclusion for qualified principal residence (mortgage) debt for recent years.

## Common forgiven debts
- Credit cards
- Mortgages
- Personal loans

## Mortgage debt forgiveness
- FEDERAL: the qualified principal residence exclusion covered discharges from January 1, 2014 through December 31, 2025, plus later discharges under a written arrangement entered into before January 1, 2026. For other 2026 discharges it has EXPIRED unless Congress extends it (an extension bill, H.R. 917, has been introduced)
- CALIFORNIA: out of conformity; mortgage debt excluded federally for discharges on or after January 1, 2014 (including 2026 discharges under a pre-2026 written arrangement) may have to be ADDED to California income
- For a 2026 discharge with no pre-2026 written arrangement, the debt is generally taxable both federally and in California, so no California adjustment is needed unless the insolvency or bankruptcy exclusion applies
- Earlier California relief existed for discharges from January 1, 2007 through December 31, 2013

## Exceptions that still apply in California
- NONRECOURSE debt forgiveness generally does not create cancellation of debt income
- RECOURSE debt forgiveness can create income unless an exclusion applies, most commonly:
  1. Discharge in a Title 11 bankruptcy
  2. The taxpayer was insolvent at the time of discharge (to the extent of insolvency)

## Notes
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- The 2025 Schedule CA instructions state California does not conform to the federal exclusion for discharge of principal residence debt occurring after December 31, 2017; enter the federally excluded amount on Section B, line 8c, column C

## Required Information
- 1099-C amount and date of cancellation
- Whether the debt was recourse or nonrecourse
- Whether the debt was on the main home
- Assets and liabilities just before the discharge (insolvency)
- Bankruptcy case information, if any

## Questions
- Did a lender forgive or cancel any debt in 2026?
- For mortgage debt, was the arrangement put in writing before January 1, 2026?
- Was it mortgage debt on your main home?
- Was the loan recourse or nonrecourse?
- Were you insolvent or in bankruptcy when it was forgiven?

## Common Errors
- Following the federal mortgage forgiveness exclusion on the California return
- Missing the insolvency or bankruptcy exclusion
- Reporting nonrecourse foreclosure debt as cancellation income

## Prompt
- My lender forgave part of my mortgage in a short sale.
- I got a 1099-C for a credit card that was written off.
