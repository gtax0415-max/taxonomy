# State Income Tax Refund (Form 1099-G)
## Description
The FTB issues Form 1099-G to report a California income tax refund, credit or offset. The amount may be taxable on the FEDERAL return, but a California refund is NEVER income on the California return, so any amount included in federal AGI is subtracted.

## When the FTB issues a 1099-G
Both of the following happened:
- State income tax was claimed as an itemized deduction on federal Schedule A for the year in box 3
- A refund, credit or offset of $10 or more was received

## What it reports
- Refunds by direct deposit or check
- Amounts offset against tax, penalties or interest
- Amounts credited to estimated tax or applied to use tax
- Amounts intercepted by other state or county agencies or the IRS

## Notes
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- A 1099-G is NOT a bill and NOT a notice of an additional refund
- If the filer did not itemize (or took the sales tax deduction) for the box 3 year, nothing needs to be reported federally
- A 1099-G from EDD is unemployment or Paid Family Leave, not a refund

## Required Information
- 1099-G box 2 and box 3
- Whether state income tax was itemized federally in that year

## Questions
- Did you itemize state income tax on your federal return last year?
- Did you receive a California refund or apply it to estimated tax?

## Common Errors
- Paying California tax on a California refund
- Reporting the refund federally when the filer did not itemize
- Confusing the FTB 1099-G with an EDD 1099-G

## Prompt
- I got a 1099-G from the Franchise Tax Board.
- Is my California tax refund taxable?
