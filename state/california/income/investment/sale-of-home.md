# Sale of Your Home (Principal Residence)
## Description
California CONFORMS to the federal exclusion of gain on the sale of a main home: up to $250,000, or $500,000 for married/RDP filing jointly, when the ownership and use tests are met.

## Amounts
- Up to $250,000 excluded for an individual
- Up to $500,000 excluded for married/RDP filing jointly
- Gain above the exclusion is taxable as ordinary income in California

## Ownership and use test
In the 5 years before the sale:
- At least 2 years of ownership AND
- At least 2 years of use as the main home
- The two periods do not have to overlap
- No exclusion used on another home in the prior 2 years

## Married/RDP $500,000 rules
- File jointly for the year of sale
- EITHER spouse meets the ownership test
- BOTH spouses meet the use test
- Neither excluded gain on another home in the last 2 years

## What can be a home
- House, houseboat, mobile home, trailer, cooperative apartment or condominium
- Only one main home at a time

## Notes
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- No reporting is needed if the gain is fully excluded (unless a 1099-S was issued)
- Use IRS Publication 523 to figure gain and the excludable amount
- File Schedule D (540) only if federal and California taxable amounts differ (for example, a different California basis)

## Required Information
- Sale price and selling costs
- Purchase price and improvements (basis)
- Ownership and residence dates
- Any prior home sale exclusion in the last 2 years

## Questions
- When did you buy and sell the home, and how long did you live in it?
- Are you married and filing jointly?
- Did you exclude gain on another home sale in the past 2 years?
- Did you rent out or use the home for business?

## Common Errors
- Claiming $500,000 when only one spouse meets the use test
- Not reporting gain above the exclusion
- Leaving improvements out of basis

- I sold my house in Sacramento this year.
- We sold our home for a big gain. How much is taxable?
