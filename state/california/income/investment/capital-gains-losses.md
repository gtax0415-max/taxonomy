# Capital Gains and Losses
## Description
Gain or loss from selling a capital asset for more or less than its purchase price. California taxes ALL capital gains as ORDINARY income; there is no lower long-term rate.

## Notes
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- Assets whose California basis commonly differs: investments sold inside an HSA (taxable for California), qualified small business stock (IRC 1045 and 1202; California does not follow the OBBBA expansion), installment sales (FTB 3805E), a personal residence on which depreciation was allowable, pass-through gains, property inherited before 1987, and children's gains reported by a parent (FTB 3803)
- California capital loss carryovers come from the prior-year California Schedule D (540)
- Every sale or exchange of a capital asset must be reported
- File California Schedule D (540) only when federal and California amounts differ (for example, different basis from California depreciation, non-conformity items, or a California capital loss carryover that differs from the federal one)
- The $3,000 annual capital loss limit applies in California as well; the excess carries forward
- The full capital loss, not just the $3,000 used, counts toward the YCTC net-loss test

## Required Information
- Every 1099-B and 1099-S
- Cost basis and dates acquired and sold
- Any basis difference between federal and California
- California capital loss carryover from prior years

## Questions
- Did you sell stock, crypto, real estate or other assets?
- Is your California basis different from your federal basis?
- Do you have a capital loss carryover?

## Common Errors
- Applying the federal long-term capital gains rate in California
- Missing Schedule D (540) when basis differs
- Using the federal capital loss carryover when the California carryover differs

## Prompt
- I sold stocks at a gain this year.
- I have a capital loss carryover from last year.
