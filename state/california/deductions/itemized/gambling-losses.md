# Gambling Losses
## Description
Itemized deduction for gambling losses, LIMITED TO GAMBLING WINNINGS. Losses cannot create a net loss.

## Amounts
- California (2026): losses deductible up to 100% of winnings
- FEDERAL (2026 and later): losses deductible only up to 90% of winnings
- Example: $10,000 won and $15,000 lost → California deducts $10,000; federal deducts $9,000
- Because California's conformity date (January 1, 2025) predates the federal change, California does NOT follow the 90% limit

## Notes
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- California Lottery winnings are not taxed by California, so California Lottery LOSSES are also not deductible: remove them in Schedule CA, Part II, line 16, column B
- California does not conform to the federal rule that also caps a professional gambler's other wagering expenses; that difference goes on Schedule CA, Part I, Section B, line 3
- Must itemize to deduct; losses are never netted against winnings in income
- NEW for 2026: the federal 90% limit creates a federal/California difference. Add the extra California-allowed loss (the gap between the federal amount and 100% of winnings) on Schedule CA, Part II, line 16, column C

## Required Information
- Total winnings and losses by type
- California Lottery losses
- Supporting records

## Questions
- How much did you win and lose in 2026?
- Were any losses on California Lottery tickets?
- Are you a professional gambler?
- Are you itemizing in California?

## Common Errors
- Deducting losses above winnings
- Copying the federal 90%-limited amount to California instead of adding back the difference
- Deducting California Lottery losses
- Netting losses against winnings in income

## Prompt
- I won $3,000 at a casino but lost $5,000 over the year.
