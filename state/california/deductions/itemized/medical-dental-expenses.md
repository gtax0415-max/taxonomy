# Medical and Dental Expenses
## Description
Itemized deduction for unreimbursed medical and dental expenses. California uses the SAME 7.5% floor as federal law, measured against FEDERAL AGI.

## Amount
- Expenses above 7.5% of federal AGI

## Notes
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- Self-employed health insurance of a worker reclassified as a California employee can be added to medical expenses (line 4, column C)
- Medical expenses paid tax-free from an HSA can be deducted for California above 7.5% of federal AGI (line 4, column C)
- Starts from federal Schedule A; Schedule CA adjusts only California differences (for example, premiums or expenses tied to accounts California treats differently, such as health savings accounts)
- Health savings account contributions are not deductible for California and HSA distributions used for medical care do not get the federal tax-free treatment, which can change what counts as unreimbursed
- Reimbursed expenses and pre-tax premiums are not deductible

## Required Information
- Medical and dental expenses paid
- Insurance reimbursements
- Federal AGI

## Questions
- What unreimbursed medical and dental costs did you pay?
- Were any premiums paid pre-tax through work?
- Did you pay medical costs from a health savings account?

## Common Errors
- Using California AGI instead of federal AGI for the 7.5% floor
- Deducting pre-tax or reimbursed costs
- Missing the deduction because the federal return used the standard deduction

## Prompt
- We had large medical bills this year.
