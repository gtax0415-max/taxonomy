# Casualty and Theft Losses
## Description
Federal law limits personal casualty and theft losses to federally declared disasters. California does NOT conform: personal casualty and theft losses from any sudden, unexpected or unusual event (fire, storm, theft, accident) remain deductible as an itemized deduction.

## Rules
- Complete a second federal Form 4684 using California amounts
- Enter the difference between the federal and California amount on line 15, column B or C
- Each loss is reduced by insurance or other reimbursement received or expected
- The pre-2018 federal limits apply: $100 per event, then 10% of AGI for the total of personal losses

## Notes
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- Losses from a President- or Governor-declared disaster have extra options (prior-year election, carryover); see deductions/itemized/disaster-loss.md
- Casualty losses are excluded from the itemized deduction limitation
- Losses of property used as an employee go on the miscellaneous line (line 21) instead

## Required Information
- Description and date of the event
- Basis and decline in value
- Reimbursements

## Questions
- Was your property damaged, destroyed or stolen in 2026?
- Was there a disaster declaration?
- What did insurance pay?

## Common Errors
- Assuming a non-disaster theft or fire loss is not deductible in California
- Not reducing the loss by insurance proceeds

## Prompt
- My car was stolen and insurance didn't cover it all.
- A kitchen fire damaged my house.
