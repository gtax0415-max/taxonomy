# Disaster Loss Deduction
## Description
Deduction for property losses from a disaster in California declared by the PRESIDENT OR the GOVERNOR. California generally follows federal casualty rules, but a Governor-only declaration also qualifies, which federal law does not recognize for personal losses.

## Qualifying events
- Earthquake, fire, flood or a similar sudden, unexpected or unusual event
- Property damaged or lost and NOT repaid by insurance
- Loss suffered in California on or after January 1, 2014 and before January 1, 2029 (earlier years: FTB Publication 1034)

## Timing election
- Claim in the year the disaster occurred, OR
- Elect to claim it in the year immediately BEFORE the disaster (by original or amended return) to get a refund sooner

## How to claim
- E-file: use the disaster code from the FTB list of California disasters
- Paper: print the disaster name and year in blue or black ink across the top of the return (for example, "Disaster: Camp Fire 2018")
- Attach a statement with the disaster date, location (city, county, state), and any election to claim in the prior year
- Attach federal Form 4684 (with California amounts), a copy of the federal return and supporting schedules
- Business property may need Schedule D-1 (540); excess losses use FTB 3805V

## Postponed deadlines
- California may postpone filing, payment and IRA contribution deadlines after a Governor emergency or presidential major disaster, up to 1 year
- Interest and penalties are canceled for the postponement period; write the disaster name on the return so FTB stops interest
- Lost or damaged California returns are replaced free (FTB 3516, with the disaster name written at the top)

## Notes
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- Federal law lets standard-deduction filers add net qualified disaster losses; California allows disaster losses ONLY to filers who itemize
- A disaster loss carryover deduction from FTB 3805V goes on Schedule CA, Section B, line 9b1, column B
- Disaster loss carryovers are NOT affected by the 2024–2026 NOL suspension
- Reduce the loss by insurance received or expected

## Required Information
- Disaster name, declaration and date
- Property basis and decline in value
- Insurance reimbursement
- Year elected for the deduction

## Questions
- Was the disaster declared by the President or the Governor?
- What was damaged, and what did insurance pay?
- Do you want to claim the loss this year or last year?
- Was it personal or business property?

## Common Errors
- Assuming a Governor-only disaster does not qualify
- Not writing the disaster name or code on the return
- Not reducing the loss by insurance
- Missing the prior-year election

## Prompt
- Our house was damaged in the Los Angeles County fires.
- Can I claim my wildfire loss on last year's return?
