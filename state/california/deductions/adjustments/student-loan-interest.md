# Student Loan Interest Deduction
## Description
California generally follows the federal deduction for interest on qualified student loans, but computes it with its own worksheet. The adjustment arises mainly when the California amount is larger than the federal amount.

## California worksheet (2025 figures; 2026 worksheet pending)
1. Federal line 21 amount; if zero, no California deduction
2. Interest paid on qualified student loans, up to $2,500
3. Federal AGI plus the federal student loan interest deduction
4. Phase-out start: $85,000 single, HOH or QSS; $170,000 MFJ
5. Excess of line 3 over line 4, divided by $15,000 ($30,000 MFJ), capped at 1.000
6. Deduction = line 2 reduced by that fraction
7. If the federal amount is less than the California amount, enter the difference on line 21, column C

## Exception
- A spouse/RDP of a non-California-domiciled military servicemember living in a community property state (FTB Publication 1032)

## Notes
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- Married/RDP filing separately cannot claim the deduction

## Required Information
- 1098-E interest
- Federal AGI and filing status

## Questions
- How much student loan interest did you pay in 2026?
- What is your federal AGI?

## Common Errors
- Assuming the federal amount always equals the California amount
- Claiming when filing separately

## Prompt
- I paid $1,800 of student loan interest.
