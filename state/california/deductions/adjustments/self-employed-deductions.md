# Self-Employed Deductions (SE Tax, Retirement Plans, Health Insurance)
## Description
The three federal above-the-line deductions for the self-employed generally carry to California unchanged:
- Line 15: deductible part of self-employment tax
- Line 16: self-employed SEP, SIMPLE and qualified plan contributions
- Line 17: self-employed health insurance

## When California differs
- If the worker is an independent contractor federally but an EMPLOYEE for California, the SE tax and SE health insurance deductions are not allowed for California (column B). The health insurance premiums may instead be itemized as medical expenses (see income/business-rental/employee-or-independent-contractor.md)
- Contribution limits can differ when California self-employment income differs from federal, which can also change later IRA and plan basis

## Notes
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)

## Required Information
- Federal SE tax, plan contributions and health premiums
- Worker classification for California

## Questions
- Are you self-employed for both federal and California purposes?
- Did you contribute to a SEP, SIMPLE or solo 401(k)?
- Did you deduct health insurance premiums as self-employed?

## Common Errors
- Removing these deductions when no reclassification applies
- Keeping them when California treats the worker as an employee

## Prompt
- I'm a freelancer and contribute to a SEP-IRA.
