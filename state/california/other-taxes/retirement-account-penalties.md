# Retirement Account Penalties (Early Distribution Additional Tax)
## Description
Distributions taken before age 59½ from pensions, annuities, profit-sharing plans or IRAs are income (they flow in through federal AGI), and they also trigger a California ADDITIONAL TAX that is separate from, and much smaller than, the federal one.

## Additional tax rates
- California: 2.5%
- California, SIMPLE plan distribution within the first 2 years of participation: 6%
- Federal, for comparison: 10%, or 25% for SIMPLE plans in the first 2 years

## Notes
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- FTB 3805P also covers other tax-favored accounts: Coverdell ESAs, 529 plans (including the 2.5% tax on 529-to-Roth rollovers), and Archer MSAs (12.5% additional tax, including an Archer MSA rolled into an HSA)
- Report the additional tax on Form 540, line 63 and write "FTB 3805P" to the left of the amount
- The distribution itself flows to California through federal AGI
- The California additional tax is figured on FTB 3805P and attached to the return
- The exceptions generally follow federal exceptions; an exception claimed on federal Form 5329 usually applies in California
- Retirement plan basis can differ for California (for example, contributions not deductible in California in some years)

## Required Information
- 1099-R and distribution code
- Age at distribution
- Any exception claimed
- Plan type (SIMPLE and date first participated)

## Questions
- Were you under 59½ when you took the distribution?
- What type of plan was it?
- Does an exception apply (disability, medical, first-time home, education)?
- Did you roll any of it over?

## Common Errors
- Applying the 10% federal rate in California
- Forgetting FTB 3805P and the California 2.5% additional tax
- Missing the 6% rate on early SIMPLE distributions

## Prompt
- I took money out of my 401(k) at age 45.
- I cashed out an IRA early. What does California charge?
