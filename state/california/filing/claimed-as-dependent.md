# Filer Who Can Be Claimed as a Dependent (Line 6)
## Description
Check line 6 if someone else CAN claim the filer (or spouse/RDP) as a dependent, even if they choose not to. This changes several parts of the California return.

## Effects
- PERSONAL EXEMPTION CREDIT: enter 0 (single, MFS, HOH, or MFJ when both can be claimed); enter 1 for MFJ when only one spouse can be claimed
- BLIND and SENIOR exemption credits: not allowed
- STANDARD DEDUCTION: use the California Standard Deduction Worksheet for Dependents. The deduction is the larger of earned income plus $450 or $1,350 (2026 federal minimum under Rev. Proc. 2025-32, unchanged from 2025; California's worksheet has used the federal amount — confirm in the 2026 Form 540 booklet), but no more than the regular standard deduction ($5,900 / $11,800 for 2026)
- FORM 540 2EZ: not available in several dependent situations (see filing/residency/residents.md)
- A married couple filing a joint return only to get a refund of withholding can still be claimed as dependents

## Notes
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)

## Required Information
- Whether anyone can claim the filer or spouse
- Earned income

## Questions
- Can a parent or anyone else claim you as a dependent?
- Are you filing a joint return only to get a refund of withholding?

## Common Errors
- Claiming the full personal exemption credit when someone can claim the filer
- Using the regular standard deduction instead of the dependent worksheet

## Prompt
- I'm a college student and my parents claim me.
