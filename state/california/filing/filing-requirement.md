# California Filing Requirement
## Description
A California return is generally required for a resident, part-year resident or nonresident who is required to file a federal return, has California-source income, or whose California GROSS income OR California ADJUSTED GROSS income exceeds the threshold for their filing status, age and number of dependents. Residents are taxed on all income; part-year residents on all income while a resident plus California-source income while a nonresident. Filing can also be worthwhile when it is not required.

## Gross income thresholds
2026 thresholds are pending FTB release (expected late December 2026). 2025 amounts shown for reference:
(Part-year residents and nonresidents use total WORLDWIDE gross income)
- Single or HOH, under 65: $22,941 (0 dependents), $38,774 (1), $50,649 (2+)
- Single or HOH, 65+: $30,591 / $42,466 / $51,966
- MFJ or MFS, both under 65: $45,887 / $61,720 / $73,595
- MFJ or MFS, one 65+: $53,537 / $65,412 / $74,912
- MFJ or MFS, both 65+: $61,187 / $73,062 / $82,562*
- Qualifying surviving spouse/RDP, under 65: N/A / $38,774 / $50,649; 65+: N/A / $42,466 / $51,966

## Notes
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- A person whose 65th birthday is January 1, 2027 is treated as 65 on December 31, 2026
- A filer who can be claimed as a dependent has a lower standard deduction: the larger of earned income plus $450 or $1,350 (2026 federal minimum under Rev. Proc. 2025-32, unchanged from 2025; California's worksheet has used the federal amount — confirm in the 2026 Form 540 booklet), but no more than the regular standard deduction ($5,900 / $11,800 for 2026)
- California-source income includes services performed in California, rent from California property, sales of California real property, a California business, and (for residents) gains on stocks and bonds
- Military filers follow special residency rules (see filing/residency/military-servicemember-residency.md)
- Filers without a Social Security Number can file with an IRS-issued ITIN, including to claim refundable credits or a refund

## Should you file anyway?
- Qualify for CalEITC: 2026 maximum pending FTB release (2025: up to $3,756)
- Had California tax withheld: a refund may be due
- Also YCTC and FYTC, which can be claimed with an ITIN

## Required Information
- Residency status
- Filing status, age and number of dependents
- California gross income and AGI
- California withholding
- SSN or ITIN for everyone on the return

## Questions
- Were you a California resident, part-year resident or nonresident?
- What is your filing status and age?
- How many dependents do you have?
- What was your total income?
- Was California tax withheld, or do you qualify for CalEITC or YCTC?

## Common Errors
- Checking only one of the two income tests (gross income and AGI)
- Not filing below the threshold when a refund or refundable credit is available
- A nonresident using only California income for the threshold instead of worldwide income
- Using the wrong table for qualifying surviving spouse
- Not filing because there is no SSN (an ITIN can be used)

## Prompt
- Do I need to file a California return? I made $20,000.
- I only made $15,000 but had state tax withheld.
- I'm 70 and only have a small pension.
