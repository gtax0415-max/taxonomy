# Qualifying Surviving Spouse/RDP
## Description
Lets a surviving spouse or RDP with a dependent child keep the benefits of the joint rates and standard deduction for the TWO years after the year of death.

## Who qualifies for 2026
All of the following:
- Spouse/RDP died in 2024 or 2025
- Did not remarry or enter another RDP in 2026
- Has a child, stepchild, adopted child, or foster child claimed as a dependent
- The child lived in the home ALL of 2026 (temporary absences for vacation or school count as time at home)
- Paid over half the cost of keeping up the home for the child
- Could have filed a joint return in the year of death, even if not actually filed

## Notes
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- Year of death (2026 deaths): file married/RDP jointly, not this status
- Standard deduction for 2026: $11,800; personal exemption credit $316
- Without a qualifying child, the survivor files single (or HOH if another qualifying person applies)
- A survivor 65 or older who loses HOH status may qualify for the senior head of household credit

## Required Information
- Date of spouse's death
- Remarriage or new RDP status
- Child's relationship, dependency and residence
- Household costs

## Questions
- When did your spouse pass away?
- Have you remarried or entered a new domestic partnership?
- Does a dependent child live with you all year?
- Did you pay more than half the household costs?

## Common Errors
- Using this status in the year of death instead of joint
- Using it a third year after death
- Claiming it without a dependent child living in the home all year

## Prompt
- My husband died in 2025 and I have two kids at home.
