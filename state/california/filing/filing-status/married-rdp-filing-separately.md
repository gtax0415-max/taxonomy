# Married/RDP Filing Separately
## Description
Each spouse or RDP files their own return. Because California is a COMMUNITY PROPERTY state, each separate return must include ONE-HALF of the community income plus all of that spouse's own separate income.

## Effects of filing separately
- Higher tax rates than filing jointly
- If one spouse ITEMIZES, the other must also itemize
- Generally NO CalEITC and NO child and dependent care credit
- Standard deduction is half the joint amount ($5,900 for 2026)

## Community property
- Each spouse reports one-half of community income and all of their own separate income
- Community property rules decide how income is divided

## Notes
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- A spouse who lived apart from the other spouse for the entire last six months of the year, with a child living with them, may qualify for HEAD OF HOUSEHOLD instead
- Some credits require MFS, such as the dependent parent credit

## Required Information
- Each spouse's income, split between community and separate
- Whether the other spouse itemizes
- Living arrangements during the last six months of the year

## Questions
- Why are you filing separately?
- Do you and your spouse live in California?
- Does your spouse itemize deductions?
- Did you live apart for the last six months of the year with a child?

## Common Errors
- Reporting only one's own wages instead of half of community income
- Taking the standard deduction when the other spouse itemizes
- Claiming CalEITC or the child care credit
- Missing head of household eligibility

## Prompt
- My husband and I want to file separately in California.
- We're separated but not divorced.
