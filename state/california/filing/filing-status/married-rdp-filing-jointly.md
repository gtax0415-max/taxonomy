# Married/RDP Filing Jointly
## Description
Married couples and registered domestic partners combine income and deductions on one return. Both are liable for the full tax.

## Who qualifies
At the end of the year:
- Married or in an RDP, OR
- Living apart but NOT legally separated, OR
- The spouse/RDP died during the year and the survivor has not remarried or entered another RDP

## Exception: separate California returns after a federal joint return
Spouses who file jointly federally may file SEPARATELY for California if either spouse was:
- An active member of the U.S. armed forces or an auxiliary military branch during the year, OR
- A nonresident for the ENTIRE year with NO California-source income

## Notes
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- Joint liability: each spouse is responsible for all tax, penalties and interest (an innocent joint filer may request relief from FTB on FTB 705)
- California has no injured spouse rule; a joint refund can be offset for either spouse's debts
- If one spouse is a resident and the other is not, a joint return must be filed on Form 540NR
- Standard deduction for 2026: $11,800; personal exemption credit $316

## Required Information
- Marital or RDP status on December 31, 2026
- Each spouse's residency and income sources
- Military status

## Questions
- Were you married or in an RDP at the end of 2026?
- Did you file jointly federally?
- Is either spouse in the military or a nonresident all year with no California income?
- Did your spouse pass away during 2026?

## Common Errors
- Filing jointly when legally separated under a decree
- Not using the military or nonresident exception when separate California returns would help
- Filing Form 540 instead of 540NR when one spouse is a nonresident

## Prompt
- My spouse and I file jointly federally. Do we have to file jointly in California?
- My wife lives out of state and has no California income.
