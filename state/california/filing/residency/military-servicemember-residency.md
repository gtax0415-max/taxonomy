# Military Servicemembers: Residency, Combat Pay and Filing
## Description
A servicemember's California tax status depends on DOMICILE and where they are permanently stationed. Generally a servicemember stays a resident of the state they entered the military from. Military pay is NOT California-source income for a nonresident.

## Residency rules
- DOMICILED in California and permanently stationed in California: RESIDENT
- Domiciled in California and leaving on TDY: STILL a resident
- Home port in California: resident while on sea duty, wherever the ship is
- Domiciled in California but stationed OUTSIDE California under PCS orders: NONRESIDENT while stationed out of state; taxed only on California-source income
- Coming to California on PCS orders does NOT make someone a resident
- National Guard and reservists on active duty follow the same rules based on their assignment
- Domicile is the one true, fixed, permanent home a person intends to return to

## Nonresident servicemembers
- Military pay is NOT California-source income
- A return may still be required for California-source income such as non-military wages, California sales or rental property

## Combat and hazard zones
- Up to 100% of combat pay can be excluded (follows federal law)
- Deadline extended: 180 days plus the days served in a combat zone or qualified hazardous duty area

## Filing thresholds (California gross income)
2026 thresholds are pending FTB release (expected late December 2026). 2025 amounts shown for reference:
- Single or HOH, under 65: $22,941 (0 dependents), $38,774 (1), $50,649 (2+)
- Single or HOH, 65+: $30,591 / $42,466 / $51,966
- MFJ or MFS, both under 65: $45,887 / $61,720 / $73,595
- MFJ or MFS, one 65+: $53,537 / $65,412 / $74,912
- MFJ or MFS, both 65+: $61,187 / $73,062 / $82,562
- Qualifying surviving spouse/RDP, under 65: $38,774 (1 dependent) / $50,649 (2+); 65+: $42,466 / $51,966
(California AGI thresholds are lower; see filing/filing-requirement.md)

## Notes
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- Military pay and combat pay rules (including the Sinai Peninsula difference): income/earned/military-pay.md
- A resident and a nonresident spouse who want to file jointly must use Form 540NR
- California is a community property state: when one spouse is a resident and the other is not, some income earned outside California may have to be reported
- Free filing help is available on most bases through VITA
- An Individual Status Letter (FTB 4148) shows no outstanding California tax, for VA financing or student loans

## Required Information
- State of domicile when entering service
- Duty station, PCS or TDY orders, home port
- Combat zone service dates
- Non-military income and its source

## Questions
- What state were you living in when you joined the military?
- Where are you permanently stationed, and under what orders?
- Did you serve in a combat zone in 2026?
- Do you have non-military income from California?

## Common Errors
- Treating a servicemember stationed in California on PCS orders as a resident
- Reporting a nonresident's military pay to California
- A California-domiciled servicemember on TDY filing as a nonresident
- Missing the combat zone filing extension

## Prompt
- I'm in the Navy, from Texas, stationed in San Diego.
- I'm from California but stationed in Germany.
