# Military Spouses Residency Relief Act (MSRRA)
## Description
A civilian spouse of a servicemember stationed in California under PCS orders may be exempt from California tax on their own wages if they keep a domicile in another state. Since 2023, the spouse can choose among several residences for state tax purposes.

## Eligibility
All of the following:
- Not in the military
- Legally married to the servicemember
- Living with the servicemember spouse/RDP
- The servicemember has PCS orders to California
- The spouse's domicile is a state other than California

## Residence election
- 2018 through 2022: could elect the servicemember's residence
- 2023 and later: may elect ANY of:
  1. The servicemember's residence or domicile
  2. The spouse's own residence or domicile
  3. The servicemember's permanent duty station

## Notes
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- The exemption covers the spouse's own service income; other California-source income (for example, California rental property) can still be taxable
- California is a community property state, which can bring a resident spouse's share of income into play when spouses have different residency

## Required Information
- PCS orders and duty station
- Spouse's state of domicile
- Spouse's wages and other income by source

## Questions
- Is your spouse on active duty with PCS orders to California?
- Do you live with your spouse in California?
- Which state is your domicile?
- Do you have any other California-source income?

## Common Errors
- Paying California tax on wages that qualify under MSRRA
- Claiming MSRRA when not living with the servicemember
- Assuming MSRRA covers California rental or business income

## Prompt
- My husband is stationed at Camp Pendleton and I work at a local store. We're from Florida.
