# Part-Year Residents and Nonresidents
## Description
A PART-YEAR resident pays California tax on all worldwide income received while a resident, plus California-source income while a nonresident. A NONRESIDENT pays only on California-source income. Both file Form 540NR, and California uses its own method to compute their tax.

## Who is a nonresident
Someone who is not a resident. Generally:
- Simply passing through
- Here briefly for rest or vacation
- Here for a short time to complete a job, transaction or contract work
(applies only if domiciled outside California)

SAFE HARBOR: someone domiciled in California but outside the state under an employment-related contract may qualify as a nonresident (FTB Publication 1031).

## California-source income includes
- Services performed in California
- Rent from California real property
- Sale or transfer of California real property
- Income from a California business, trade or profession

## Leaving California: scenarios
1. Moved away, works remotely for a California employer, travels back periodically: YES, file. California-source wages = total wages × (California workdays ÷ total workdays)
2. Moved away and performs ALL services outside California: MAYBE. Generally not California-source, but deferred or equity-based compensation can still be (FTB Publication 1004)
3. Temporarily relocated for work, plans to return: may STILL be a resident, taxed on worldwide income; the Other State Tax Credit may apply. Safe harbor may apply
4. Independent contractor or sole proprietor who moved but still serves California customers: YES, file. Source is where the CUSTOMER receives the benefit, not where the work is done

## Filing requirement
- Same thresholds as residents (2026 amounts pending FTB release), using TOTAL WORLDWIDE gross income or California AGI (see filing/filing-requirement.md)

## Notes
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- California computes the tax on all income, then prorates it by the California share (FTB Publication 1100)
- Community property: a resident spouse may need to report income earned outside California, and a nonresident spouse may need to report income earned by the resident spouse
- Nonresidents may be subject to withholding on California-source payments
- Tax paid to another state may qualify for the Other State Tax Credit

## Required Information
- Dates of residence in California
- Income by period (resident vs. nonresident) and by source
- California workdays vs. total workdays
- Equity compensation details
- Spouse's residency

## Questions
- When did you move into or out of California?
- Did you work in California after moving away? How many days?
- Do you have stock options or RSUs from a California job?
- Do you own California rental property?
- Do you have California clients as an independent contractor?

## Common Errors
- Not filing after moving away while still working days in California
- Ignoring equity compensation earned while in California
- Sourcing contractor income by where the work was done instead of where the customer benefits
- Filing Form 540 instead of 540NR

## Prompt
- I moved from California to Texas in June 2026.
- I live in Nevada but travel to California for work a few days a month.
