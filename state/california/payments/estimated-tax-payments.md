# Estimated Tax and Other Payments
## Description
Payments made toward the 2026 California tax before filing: quarterly estimates, an extension payment, and prior-year overpayments applied.

## What to include on line 72
- 2026 estimated payments (Form 540-ES, electronic funds withdrawal, Web Pay or credit card)
- The 2025 overpayment applied to 2026 estimated tax
- Payment sent with FTB 3519 (automatic extension)
- California estimated payments made on the filer's behalf by an estate, trust or S corporation

## California payment schedule (differs from federal)
- April 15, 2026: 30% | June 15, 2026: 40% | September: none | January 15, 2027: 30%

## Safe harbors
- 90% of the current-year tax, or 100% of the prior-year tax (110% if prior-year California AGI was over $150,000, or $75,000 MFS)
- Filers with California AGI of $1,000,000 or more ($500,000 MFS) must base estimates on the current-year tax
- Nonresidents or new residents of California in 2026 with no 2025 California tax liability need not make 2026 estimates

## Joint and separate payments
- Joint estimates but now filing separately: either may claim all, or they may split them; notify FTB before filing (allocation statement with notarized signatures, or divorce agreement)
- Separate estimates but now filing jointly: add them and attach a statement that payments were made under both SSNs

## Mandatory e-pay
- Once an estimate or extension payment exceeds $20,000, or an original return shows total tax over $80,000, all later payments must be electronic; otherwise a 1% noncompliance penalty applies (waiver available)

## Notes
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)

## Required Information
- Date and amount of each 2026 payment
- Overpayment applied from 2025
- Extension payment amount
- Prior-year California AGI (for the 110% rule)

## Questions
- What estimated payments did you make for 2026, and when?
- Did you apply last year's refund to 2026?
- Did you make an extension payment?
- Did you and your spouse pay estimates separately?

## Common Errors
- Using the federal 25%-per-quarter schedule
- Omitting the applied overpayment
- Splitting joint estimates without notifying FTB

## Prompt
- I'm self-employed. When are California estimated payments due?
