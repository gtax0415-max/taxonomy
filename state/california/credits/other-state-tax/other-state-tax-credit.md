# Other State Tax Credit
## Description
Nonrefundable credit that prevents double taxation when the same income is taxed by both California and another state.

## Who can claim
- Individuals, estates and trusts
- Resident beneficiaries of estates and trusts
- Partners of partnerships and members of LLCs taxed as partnerships
- Shareholders of S corporations

## Amount
- Offsets tax paid to the other state on income also taxed by California, figured on Schedule S

## Eligibility
- Income taxed by both California and another state
- NOT available if the other state gives you a credit for the same tax

## Notes
- Credit code: 187
- Schedule P (540) section: B3 (may reduce tax below tentative minimum tax)
- Business credit limitation: Not listed among the exempt credits in the Schedule P instructions; confirm treatment
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- Use a separate Schedule S for each state
- Taxes paid to a FOREIGN country do not qualify: California has no foreign tax credit and no itemized deduction for foreign income taxes
- Attach the other state's return(s)
- Which state grants the credit depends on residency and the source of the income; Schedule S instructions set the rules

## Required Information
- The other state's return and the tax paid
- The income taxed by both states
- Residency status in each state

## Questions
- Did you pay income tax to another state this year?
- What income was taxed by both states?
- Does the other state already give you a credit for California tax?
- How many states are involved?

## Common Errors
- Claiming when the other state is the one that grants the credit
- Combining several states on one Schedule S
- Not attaching the other state's return
- Using tax withheld instead of the final tax liability

## Prompt
- I live in California but worked in Oregon for part of the year.
- I'm a partner in an S corp that paid tax in another state.
