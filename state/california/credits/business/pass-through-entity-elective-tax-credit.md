# Pass-Through Entity (PTE) Elective Tax Credit
## Description
When a partnership, S corporation or LLC elects to pay California's PTE elective tax on its owners' share of income, each owner gets a credit against personal income tax. The election lets owners work around the federal SALT deduction cap.

## Status for 2026
- The original PTE tax covered tax years 2021 through 2025
- SB 132 EXTENDED the PTE elective tax and credit to tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2026 and before January 1, 2031, so it is available for 2026

## Amount
- Credit equals the owner's pro rata share of qualified net income × 9.3%
- Unused credit carries forward up to 5 years

## 2026 changes
- An entity that misses the June 15 payment may STILL elect, but the owners' credit is reduced by 12.5% of their pro rata share of the June 15 shortfall
- The credit is allowed for 2026 when a fiscal-year electing entity's year differs from the owner's year
- Ordering with the Other State Tax Credit: owners increase "net tax" by the PTE credit when figuring the Other State Tax Credit

## Notes
- Credit code: 242
- Schedule P (540) section: B4 (may reduce tax below tentative minimum tax; carryover); applied after the Other State Tax Credit
- Business credit limitation: Not subject to the 2024–2026 business credit limitation
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- Schedule P, Section B4: applied after the Other State Tax Credit, and it can reduce regular tax below tentative minimum tax
- A disregarded entity and its owners generally cannot receive the credit, except a single-member LLC owned by an individual, fiduciary, estate or trust subject to personal income tax
- EXEMPT from the 2024–2026 $5 million business credit cap
- Claimed on FTB 3804-CR, usually through Schedule P (540)

## Required Information
- Entity's election and payment confirmation
- Owner's share of qualified net income and elective tax
- Any June 15 shortfall
- Carryovers from prior years

## Questions
- Did your partnership, S corporation or LLC elect the California PTE tax for 2026?
- Did the entity make its June 15 payment?
- Do you have unused PTE credit from earlier years?
- Do you also claim the Other State Tax Credit?

## Common Errors
- Assuming the PTE tax ended after 2025
- Not reducing the credit for a missed June 15 payment
- Not carrying forward unused credit
