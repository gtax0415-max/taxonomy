# Child and Dependent Care Expenses Credit
## Description
Nonrefundable credit for paying someone to care for a child, spouse/RDP or dependent so the filer could work or look for work. California runs its own test on FTB 3506 rather than simply copying federal Form 2441, and the care must be provided IN CALIFORNIA.

## Amount
- Qualifying expenses capped at $3,000 for one qualifying person, $6,000 for two or more
- Credit is a percentage of the amount paid, with the percentage set by federal AGI (see FTB 3506 instructions)
- FEDERAL AGI must be $100,000 or less; above that, $0

## Qualifying person
- CHILD under 13 who is the filer's son/daughter, stepchild, adopted child, foster child, sibling, half- or step-sibling, niece/nephew, or a descendant of any of these; lived with the filer more than half the year; did not provide over half of own support; did not file a joint return; is a U.S. citizen, national, or resident of Canada or Mexico
- SPOUSE/RDP who was physically or mentally incapable of self-care, or a full-time student for any 5 months
- DEPENDENT incapable of self-care, or who would have been a dependent except their gross income exceeded the dependent gross income limit (adjusted yearly; the FTB page shows $5,200, the 2025 figure; confirm the 2026 amount in the FTB 3506 instructions), they filed a joint return, or the filer could be claimed as someone else's dependent

## Eligibility
- File jointly if married
- Care provided in California
- Paid for care to work or look for work
- Have earned income
- Lived with the qualifying person more than half the year
- Federal AGI of $100,000 or less

## Notes
- Credit code: 232
- Schedule P (540) section: A1 (cannot reduce tax below tentative minimum tax; no carryover)
- Business credit limitation: Not subject to the 2024–2026 business credit limitation
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- NONREFUNDABLE: it only reduces tax; it does not create a refund
- Provider cannot be the filer's spouse/RDP or the child's parent; the filer's own child can be the provider only if 19 or older
- Care received outside California does not count even when it qualifies federally
- Employer dependent care benefits (W-2 box 10) reduce the expenses that can be used

## Required Information
- Provider name, address, identifying number and amount paid
- Each qualifying person's name, identifying number and age or disability status
- Earned income of filer and spouse
- Federal AGI
- Dependent care benefits received

## Questions
- Did you pay for care so you could work or look for work?
- Was the care provided in California?
- Is your federal AGI $100,000 or less?
- Did your employer provide dependent care benefits?
- Who provided the care?

## Common Errors
- Claiming with federal AGI over $100,000
- Including care provided outside California
- Expecting a refund from a nonrefundable credit
- Paying a spouse, the child's parent, or a child under 19 as the provider
- Not reducing expenses by employer-provided benefits

## Prompt
- I pay for daycare so I can work.
- I claimed the federal child care credit. Does California have one?
