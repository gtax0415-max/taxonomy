# Dependent Parent Credit
## Description
Nonrefundable credit for a married/RDP filer who files separately, lives apart from the spouse, and pays more than half the household expenses for an elderly parent. The parent does not have to live with the filer.

## Amount
- 30% of net tax (Form 540, line 35)
- Maximum for 2026: pending FTB release (expected late December 2026); 2025 was $610

## Eligibility
- Married/RDP filing SEPARATELY
- Spouse/RDP did not live with the filer during the last 6 months of the year
- Paid over half the household expenses for the parent

## Notes
- Credit code: 173
- Schedule P (540) section: B1 (may reduce tax below tentative minimum tax; no carryover)
- Business credit limitation: Not subject to the 2024–2026 business credit limitation
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- Filing status is narrow: NOT available to single, head of household, qualifying surviving spouse/RDP, or married/RDP filing jointly
- Cannot be claimed together with the Joint Custody Head of Household credit
- Computed on line 35 tax, so a filer with no tax gets nothing

## Required Information
- Filing status and where the spouse lived the last 6 months
- Parent's name and household expenses paid
- Form 540, line 35 tax

## Questions
- Are you married and filing separately?
- Did your spouse live apart from you for the last 6 months of the year?
- Did you pay more than half the cost of your parent's household?
- Are you also claiming the joint custody head of household credit?

## Common Errors
- Claiming while filing single, head of household or jointly
- Claiming both this credit and the joint custody head of household credit
- Using 30% of taxable income instead of 30% of line 35 tax

## Prompt
- I'm separated, filing separately, and I support my elderly mother.
