# Nonrefundable Renter's Credit
## Description
Small flat nonrefundable credit for California renters with moderate income who rented a home in California for at least half the year.

## Amount
- $60: single or married/RDP filing separately
- $120: head of household, married/RDP filing jointly, or qualifying surviving spouse/RDP

## Income limits (California AGI, 2026 — official FTB indexing)
- $55,830 or less: single or married/RDP filing separately (2025: $53,994)
- $111,660 or less: married/RDP filing jointly, head of household, or qualifying surviving spouse/RDP (2025: $107,988)

## Eligibility
- Paid rent in California for at least half the year
- The property was not tax-exempt
- Did not live with someone who can claim you as a dependent
- Neither you nor your spouse/RDP received a property tax exemption during the year

## Notes
- Schedule P (540) section: B1 (may reduce tax below tentative minimum tax; no carryover)
- Business credit limitation: Not subject to the 2024–2026 business credit limitation
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- NONREFUNDABLE despite its name being familiar; it only offsets tax
- Has its own line (46), not claimed with a credit code
- Keep the qualification record in the Form 540 booklet with your records

## Required Information
- Months rented in California and address(es)
- Landlord information
- Filing status and California AGI
- Whether a homeowner's property tax exemption was received

## Questions
- Did you rent in California for at least six months?
- What is your filing status and California AGI?
- Did you live with someone who claims you as a dependent?
- Did you or your spouse own a home with a property tax exemption?

## Common Errors
- Claiming with income over the limit
- Claiming while living with a parent who claims you
- Claiming in a year you also had a homeowner's exemption
- Claiming for rent in tax-exempt housing

## Prompt
- I rent an apartment in Los Angeles. Is there a renter's credit?
