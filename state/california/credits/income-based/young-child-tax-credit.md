# Young Child Tax Credit (YCTC)
## Description
Refundable credit for California families with a qualifying child under age 6 who also qualify for CalEITC. Since tax year 2022 it has a special exception that allows filers with zero earned income or a net loss to claim it.

## Amount
- Maximum credit for 2026: pending FTB release (expected late December 2026); 2025 was $1,189 per return (not per child)
- Earned income limit for 2026: pending FTB release (expected late December 2026); 2025 was $32,900
- Phases out as earned income rises (see FTB 3514, Part VII)

## Eligibility
- A qualifying child under 6 years old at the end of the tax year
- Qualify for CalEITC, OR meet the zero-or-negative earned income exception
- ZERO / NEGATIVE EARNED INCOME EXCEPTION (2022 forward): YCTC is allowed with earned income of zero or less if:
  1. Total wages, salaries, tips and other employee compensation (whether or not subject to California withholding) do not exceed the annual limit (2026: pending FTB release (expected late December 2026); 2025 was $35,640)
  2. Total net loss does not exceed the same limit (2026: pending; 2025 was $35,640)
  3. All other CalEITC and YCTC requirements are met

## Notes
- Schedule P (540) section: none (refundable; Form 540, line 76)
- Business credit limitation: Not subject to the 2024–2026 business credit limitation
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- REFUNDABLE
- NET LOSS counts ALL current-year losses, not only the deductible portion. Example: a $50,000 capital loss counts as $50,000 toward the net-loss limit (2025: $35,640), even though only $3,000 reduces taxable income
- Net losses commonly come from gig work, self-employment, investments or rentals
- A filer without at least $1 of earned income does NOT get CalEITC but can still get YCTC under the exception
- For 2021 only, earned income of $1 to $30,000 was required; zero or negative earned income did not qualify
- Can be claimed for 2021 forward by filing or amending

## Required Information
- Child's name, identifying number and date of birth
- Earned income, total wages and total net loss from all sources
- CalEITC eligibility

## Questions
- Do you have a child who was under 6 on December 31?
- What was your total earned income for the year?
- Did you have a business, investment or rental loss? What was the full loss before limitations?

## Common Errors
- Treating the credit as per child (it is per return)
- Using only the deductible portion of a capital loss to test the net-loss limit
- Assuming no earned income means no credit
- Claiming for a child who turned 6 before year-end

## Prompt
- I have a 3-year-old and earned about $20,000.
- My self-employment business had a loss this year and I have a toddler.
