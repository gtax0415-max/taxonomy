# Personal Exemption Credit
## Description
California has no personal exemption DEDUCTION. Instead, it gives a flat exemption CREDIT that directly reduces tax. It is nonrefundable and phases out at high income.

## Amounts for 2026 (official FTB indexing)
- Single, married/RDP filing separately, head of household: $158 (enter 1 in the box)
- Married/RDP filing jointly, qualifying surviving spouse/RDP: $316 (enter 2 in the box)
- 2025 was $153 per person

## Can be claimed as a dependent (line 6 checked)
- Single, MFS or HOH: enter 0
- MFJ with both spouses claimable as dependents: enter 0
- MFJ with only one spouse claimable: enter 1

## AGI limitation (line 32)
- When federal AGI (Form 540, line 13) exceeds the threshold, each exemption credit is reduced by $6 for every $2,500 ($1,250 MFS) of the excess, or fraction of it
- Thresholds for 2026: pending FTB release (expected late December 2026). 2025 thresholds: $252,203 single or MFS, $378,310 HOH, $504,411 MFJ or QSS
- RDPs use recalculated (pro forma) federal AGI

## Notes
- Schedule P (540) section: none (exemption credits reduce tax on Form 540, line 32)
- Business credit limitation: Not subject to the 2024–2026 business credit limitation
- Line numbers follow the 2025 Form 540 and schedules; confirm them on the 2026 forms (expected December 2026)
- The exemption credits are applied before the special credits (lines 40–46)
- Line 32 combines personal, blind, senior and dependent exemption credits

## Required Information
- Filing status
- Whether anyone can claim the filer or spouse as a dependent
- Federal AGI

## Questions
- What is your filing status?
- Can a parent or anyone else claim you or your spouse as a dependent?
- Is your federal AGI above the phase-out threshold?

## Common Errors
- Claiming the credit when someone else can claim the filer as a dependent
- Skipping the AGI limitation worksheet at high income
- Treating the exemption as a deduction from income

## Prompt
- How much is the California personal exemption credit?
- My parents claim me as a dependent. Do I get the exemption credit?
