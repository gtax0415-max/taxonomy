# Taxable Social Security Benefits
## Description
Ohio does not tax Social Security. The federally taxable portion on Form 1040 or 1040-SR line 6b is subtracted in full on Schedule of Adjustments line 16.
## How it works
- Federal Form 1040 line 6a: total benefits received
- Federal Form 1040 line 6b: the taxable portion, up to 85% of benefits
- Line 6b is in federal AGI and therefore in IT 1040 line 1
- Schedule of Adjustments line 16 subtracts that same line 6b amount

Enter the taxable amount, not the gross benefit.
## No conditions
No age test, no dollar cap, and no MAGI phase-out. A high-income retiree subtracts the entire federally taxable amount.
## Railroad retirement
Railroad benefits reported as taxable Social Security stay on this line. Other railroad retirement, disability, and unemployment benefits that are in federal AGI and exempt from state tax go to line 17. Do not put the same dollars on both lines.
## Interaction with credits
Amounts deducted here are not retirement income for the line 2 retirement income credit or the line 3 lump-sum retirement credit.
## Required Information
- Form SSA-1099
- Federal Form 1040 line 6b
## Questions
- What amount is on federal line 6b?
- Are some benefits railroad retirement that belong on line 17?
## Common Errors
- Entering gross benefits from the SSA-1099
- Leaving the line blank because the federal return taxed part of the benefit
- Using the deducted amount in the retirement-credit table
## Prompt
- Does Ohio tax Social Security?
- I am retired and collect Social Security and a small pension.
