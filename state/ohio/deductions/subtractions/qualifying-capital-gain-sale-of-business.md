# Qualifying Capital Gain From the Sale of a Business
## Description
NEW for taxable years beginning in or after 2026. Deduct qualifying capital gain from the sale of an ownership interest in a business, subject to a payroll-based cap. R.C. 5747.01(A)(34) and R.C. 5747.79.

Enacted in H.B. 110 (2021) with a five-year delayed effective date, the same delay that held back the VCOC deduction. It is the sibling of vcoc-capital-gain.md and is easy to miss because neither has a printed 2026 line yet. Unlike the VCOC deduction, this one needs no advance certification of the business.

If the gain is business income, take this deduction BEFORE the $250,000 business income deduction in R.C. 5747.01(A)(28). R.C. 5747.01(A)(34) says so expressly.
## Amount
For each entity, the lesser of:
- the taxpayer's qualifying capital gain from the sale of that entity, or
- the deductible payroll attributable to that entity

Then aggregate the per-entity amounts to get the total deduction. R.C. 5747.79(C)(2). Deductible payroll does not include compensation paid to the selling owner or to relatives.
## Eligibility
- Taxable year beginning in or after 2026
- Sale of an ownership interest — an equity sale, not an asset sale
- The business is Ohio-headquartered
- The owner either materially participated in the business for the five years preceding the sale, or invested at least $1,000,000 in it

Confirm each element against R.C. 5747.79's own definitions before claiming. The statutory definitions govern, and Ohio has not yet published booklet guidance.
## Required Information
- Sale documents showing an equity sale and the gain
- Entity payroll records supporting deductible payroll, net of owner and relative compensation
- Material participation evidence for the five preceding years, or proof of the $1,000,000 investment
- On request of the tax commissioner, whatever is needed to establish the deducted amount. R.C. 5747.79(D)
## Questions
- Was this an equity sale or an asset sale? An asset sale does not qualify
- Is the business headquartered in Ohio?
- Did you materially participate for five years, or invest at least $1,000,000?
- What is the entity's deductible payroll, excluding your own and relatives' compensation?
- Is the gain business income that should come off before the $250,000 deduction?
## Common Errors
- Claiming it on an asset sale
- Claiming the full gain without applying the deductible-payroll cap
- Counting the seller's own compensation toward deductible payroll
- Taking the $250,000 business income deduction first and then this one
- Missing the deduction entirely because no 2026 line number is printed yet
