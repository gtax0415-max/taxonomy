# Medical Savings Account
## Description
Deduct deposits you and your spouse (if filing jointly) made to an Ohio medical savings account. R.C. 5747.01(A)(13) and R.C. 3924.66. Also deduct investment income or interest earned on those deposits to the extent included in federal AGI. R.C. 3924.66(C).

Do not include federal health savings account contributions deducted on the federal return. An Ohio MSA is a separate creature from an HSA.

Use the worksheet on page 42 of the 2025 booklet until the 2026 worksheet is issued; complete it and include a copy with the return. Nonmedical withdrawals can flip the result into an addition on Schedule of Adjustments line 6.
## The 2026 cap
$6,003 per taxpayer was the 2025 cap. For 2026:

- R.C. 3924.66(B) has the Department of Taxation adjust the cap annually for the increase in the CPI for all urban consumers, Midwest region, measured over the preceding calendar year
- The statute has ODT determine the adjustment in SEPTEMBER of each tax year for the succeeding tax year. The 2026 cap was therefore set in September 2025, before the 2026 booklet ships. Look it up rather than assuming the 2025 number carries over
- The statute forbids a decrease, so $6,003 is a floor. If no 2026 figure can be confirmed, $6,003 is the safe amount to claim
## Required Information
MSA deposit and earnings records; completed worksheet; the 2026 cap as determined by ODT.
## Questions
- Is this an Ohio MSA, or a federal HSA already deducted on the federal return?
- Have you confirmed the 2026 cap, or are you defaulting to $6,003?
## Common Errors
- Deducting federal HSA contributions again
- Assuming the 2025 cap is the 2026 cap without checking the September determination
- Exceeding the published cap
- Skipping the worksheet
