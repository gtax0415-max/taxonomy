# Ohio Federal Conformity
## Description
Ohio starts from federal AGI as defined in the Internal Revenue Code, then applies its own additions and subtractions. When Ohio has not adopted a federal change, the difference lands on Schedule of Adjustments line 11 (addition) or line 31 (deduction). Do not put federal Schedule A items on those lines.

Check tax.ohio.gov/conformity before filing. The 2026 IT 1040 booklet is not published; use the current conformity release plus R.C. 5701.11.
## The structural rule that answers most conformity questions
Ohio's starting point is federal ADJUSTED GROSS INCOME, not federal taxable income. A federal break that reduces AGI flows into Ohio automatically. A federal break taken below the line — the standard deduction, itemized deductions, and any deduction allowed to non-itemizers under IRC 63(b) — never touches IT 1040 line 1 and gives no Ohio benefit unless Ohio separately enacts one.
## OBBBA items worth flagging for 2026
- Qualified tips (IRC 224) and qualified overtime (IRC 225): these are below-the-line deductions, allowed whether or not the taxpayer itemizes. They do not reduce federal AGI, so the income stays in Ohio AGI. Ohio has not enacted its own tip or overtime exclusion. Expect taxpayers to arrive believing "no tax on tips" applies to their Ohio return — it does not. The senior deduction and auto-loan-interest deduction sit below the line the same way
- 100% bonus depreciation made permanent: this DOES reduce federal AGI, and it enlarges Ohio's 5/6 add-back on Schedule of Adjustments line 9 with the matching 1/5 recovery on line 27. More federal expensing means a bigger Ohio add-back, not a bigger Ohio deduction

Confirm all of this against the current ODT conformity release and any 2026 Ohio conformity legislation before relying on it.
## What Ohio does not follow automatically
- Bonus depreciation and excess IRC 179 — add back on line 9, recover on line 27
- Other states' municipal-bond interest — add on line 1 even when federally exempt
- U.S. obligation interest — subtract on line 26 even when federally taxable
- Social Security — subtract the federally taxable portion on line 16
- Railroad retirement — subtract on line 17
## Questions
- Did a federal law change this year that Ohio has not adopted?
- Is the federal break above the line or below it? Below-the-line breaks do not reach Ohio
## Common Errors
- Assuming the federal tips and overtime deductions reduce Ohio income
- Putting federal Schedule A items on line 11 or line 31
- Treating a bigger federal depreciation deduction as an Ohio benefit
## Prompt
- Does Ohio conform to the latest federal tax bill?
- Do I pay Ohio tax on my tips in 2026?
