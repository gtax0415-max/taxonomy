# Personal and Dependent Exemptions
## Description
Ohio allows an exemption for you (if not claimed as a dependent), your spouse on a joint return (if not claimed as a dependent), and dependents claimed on the federal return. The dollar amount phases by MAGI. A dependent who files their own return claims $0.

H.B. 96, Section 757.120(A), suspends the Tax Commissioner's annual inflation adjustment of the brackets and the personal exemption for taxable years 2025 and 2026, so the dollar tiers hold at the 2025 amounts. The MAGI cliff drops from $750,000 to $500,000 beginning in 2026. R.C. 5747.025(A).

Note that the dollar amounts printed in R.C. 5747.025(A) itself ($2,350 / $2,100 / $1,850) are the unindexed statutory base. The figures below are the indexed amounts in force, frozen by the suspension.
## Amount
| MAGI | Exemption per person (2026) |
|---|---|
| $40,000 or less | $2,400 |
| $40,001 – $80,000 | $2,150 |
| $80,001 – $499,999 | $1,900 |
| $500,000 or greater | $0 |
## Notes
- Complete the Ohio Schedule of Dependents when you claim dependents
- An ITIN or ATIN may be entered in the SSN boxes
- The $20 exemption CREDIT is a separate MAGI-less-exemptions cliff at $30,000 — see state/ohio/credits/family-dependent/exemption-credit.md
## Required Information
- Number of exemptions
- MAGI (Ohio AGI plus the business income deduction)
- Dependent names and SSNs, ITINs, or ATINs
## Questions
- Can you be claimed as a dependent on someone else's return?
- Is MAGI at or above $500,000? The statute requires MAGI to be LESS THAN $500,000, so exactly $500,000 gets nothing
## Common Errors
- Using the 2025 $750,000 cliff on a 2026 return
- A child claimed as a dependent also taking an exemption on the child's Ohio return
- Using federal AGI instead of MAGI

- How much is the Ohio exemption for 2026?
- My MAGI is $520,000. Do I still get an Ohio exemption?
