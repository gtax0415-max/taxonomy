# Joint Filing Credit
## Description
Nonrefundable credit for joint filers when EACH spouse has at least $500 of qualifying income and MAGI is under $500,000. The credit is a percentage of tax remaining after the line 2–9 credits, capped at $650. H.B. 96 dropped the MAGI cliff from $750,000 to $500,000 beginning in 2026.

## Amount
Percentage of Schedule of Credits line 11, based on MAGI less exemptions:

| MAGI less exemptions | Credit |
|---|---|
| $0 – $25,000 | 20% of line 11 |
| $25,001 – $50,000 | 15% of line 11 |
| $50,001 – $75,000 | 10% of line 11 |
| $75,001 or more | 5% of line 11 |

Maximum credit per return is $650.

Two different numbers run this credit. Eligibility turns on plain MAGI, which must be LESS THAN $500,000. The rate tier above turns on MAGI LESS EXEMPTIONS. Do not apply the $500,000 figure to the table or the table's brackets to eligibility.

## Qualifying income
Any amount included in Ohio AGI except:
- Interest
- Dividends and distributions
- Capital gains
- Rents and royalties

Amounts already deducted on the Ohio Schedule of Adjustments are not in Ohio AGI, so they are not qualifying income. That list includes business income taken on the business income deduction, state and local tax refunds, Social Security and railroad retirement benefits, and certain military compensation and retirement.

## The example the booklet wants you to see
Kevin has $50,000 of wages and $5,000 of interest. Krysten has $40,000 of business income and $20,000 of taxable Social Security. Kevin has $50,000 of qualifying income. Krysten has none, because both of her items were deducted in computing Ohio AGI. No joint filing credit.

## Eligibility
- Married filing jointly
- Each spouse has at least $500 of qualifying income
- MAGI under $500,000
- Attach a separate statement listing each spouse's qualifying income

## Required Information
- Filing status
- Each spouse's Ohio AGI items, after Schedule of Adjustments deductions
- MAGI less exemptions
- Tax on Schedule of Credits line 11

## Questions
- Are you filing jointly?
- Does EACH spouse have $500 of wages or other Ohio AGI that was not deducted on the Schedule of Adjustments?
- Did one spouse's only income get stripped out as business income, Social Security, or military retirement?

## Common Errors
- Counting deducted business income or Social Security as qualifying income
- Applying the percentage to line 1 (tax before the first credit stack) instead of line 11
- Skipping the qualifying-income statement
- Claiming it on a married-filing-separately return

## Prompt
- We file jointly and both work.
- Why did we lose the Ohio joint filing credit when my spouse only has Social Security and a small business?
