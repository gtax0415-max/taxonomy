# School District Senior Citizen Credit
## Description
$50 credit against school district income tax for each taxing district in which you were a resident, if you were 65 or older at year-end. This is not the IT 1040 senior citizen credit. It lives only on Form SD 100.

## Amount
- $50 per district
- Available on both the traditional tax base schedule (line 26) and the earned-income tax base schedule (line 36)
- You can claim it even if you were ineligible for the $50 credit on the Ohio IT 1040

That last point is the trap. The IT 1040 senior credit dies at $100,000 of MAGI less exemptions, and dies forever after a lump sum distribution credit. The SD 100 credit does not use those tests. Age 65 is enough.

## Eligibility
- Age 65 or older at the end of the tax year
- Resident of a taxing school district for at least part of the year
- Filing Form SD 100 because you have (or are reporting) school district tax

Not every Ohio school district levies an income tax. Confirm the district and rate on booklet pages 49–56 or at tax.ohio.gov/finder.

## Required Information
- Date of birth
- School district number and tax base (traditional or earned income)
- Residency dates on the Schedule of School District Residency

## Questions
- Were you 65 or older on December 31?
- Did you live in a taxing school district?
- Were you told you could not take the IT 1040 senior credit because of income or a prior lump-sum election?

## Common Errors
- Skipping SD 100 line 26 or 36 because IT 1040 line 4 was $0
- Treating $50 as per return across all districts rather than per district
- Claiming it in a district with no school district income tax
- Putting this amount on the Ohio Schedule of Credits

## Prompt
- I am 70 and I live in a school district that has its own income tax.
- Can I still take the $50 senior credit on my school district return if Ohio denied it?
