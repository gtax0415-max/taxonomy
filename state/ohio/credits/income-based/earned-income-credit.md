# Earned Income Credit (Ohio)
## Description
Nonrefundable credit equal to 30% of the federal earned income credit allowed on Form 1040 or 1040-SR, line 27a. Eligibility is borrowed from the federal credit. There is no separate Ohio qualifying-child test and no Ohio investment-income recomputation on this line.

## Amount
- 30% of the federal EIC actually allowed. R.C. 5747.71, unchanged for 2026
- H.B. 96 did not touch this credit. There is no Ohio rate increase the way New York moved from 30% to 45%
- Nonrefundable. Unused credit does not carry forward and does not generate a refund

A taxpayer with a $4,328 federal EIC has a $1,298 Ohio EIC. If Ohio tax after earlier credits is $200, only $200 is used. The other $1,098 vanishes.

## Why it is smaller than it looks
Ohio tax on the first $26,050 of taxable nonbusiness income is 0% in 2026, and the threshold is frozen. Many EIC households have little or no Ohio tax left after the retirement, senior, exemption, and joint-filing credits that print ABOVE line 13. The 30% figure is the ceiling, not the check.

## Eligibility
- You must have a federal EIC on Form 1040 / 1040-SR, line 27a
- Ohio does not recompute the federal EIC from Ohio AGI
- Married filing separately follows the federal EIC rules
- There is no Ohio noncustodial-parent alternative
- There is no Ohio city EIC

## Investment income
Ohio does not impose its own investment-income cliff on this line. If federal EIC is lost because investment income exceeds the federal limit, the Ohio credit is also zero because the federal input is zero. That limit is indexed annually by the IRS — it was $11,950 for 2025; confirm the 2026 figure rather than reusing it.

## Part-year residents and nonresidents
The credit is computed on the full federal EIC, then sits in the nonrefundable stack. The nonresident credit on line 38 and the resident credit on line 39 apply later and can zero out remaining tax. The EIC itself is not multiplied by a residency fraction.

## Required Information
- Federal EIC from Form 1040 / 1040-SR, line 27a
- Confirmation the federal credit was allowed, not merely estimated
- Ohio tax remaining on Schedule of Credits, line 11, because the credit cannot exceed remaining tax after later stacking

## Questions
- Did the IRS allow a federal earned income credit, and what amount is on line 27a?
- Is your Ohio tax already wiped out by the retirement, senior, exemption, or joint-filing credits that come first?
- Are you expecting a refund from this credit? It will not create one.

## Common Errors
- Treating the Ohio EIC as refundable because New York or the federal credit is refundable
- Using 45% on an Ohio return (that is a New York rate, not Ohio)
- Entering the federal EIC instead of 30% of it
- Looking for an Ohio Form IT-215 equivalent — Ohio has no separate EIC form
- Claiming a city add-on. Ohio municipalities have their own income taxes; they do not piggyback this credit

## Prompt
- I claimed the EITC on my federal return.
- I work part-time in Columbus and have one child.
- Does Ohio have its own earned income credit?
