# Lump Sum Distribution Credit
## Description
One-time alternative to the $50 senior citizen credit. Available only if you were 65 or older and received a total lump-sum distribution from a qualified plan. Taking it bars the $50 senior citizen credit this year and every later year. R.C. 5747.055(G).

## Amount
Worksheet, booklet page 44:
- Look up the recipient's year-end age on the expected-return-multiple table
- Multiply that multiple by $50
- The credit cannot exceed $1,000

The multiples come from the unisex ordinary life annuity table, so they carry decimals and fall as age rises. Age 65 is 20.0, which produces exactly $1,000 — the cap and the maximum coincide, because younger than 65 cannot claim it at all. Age 75 is 12.5, so $625.

On a joint return, compute each qualifying spouse in a separate column. Confirm whether the $1,000 ceiling applies per person or per return before claiming more than $1,000 on one return; the worksheet states the limit on the per-recipient line.

## Careful with the table names
The booklet and the standalone LS WKS form number their two tables in OPPOSITE order:

| | Retirement income credit amounts | Age multiples |
|---|---|---|
| Booklet (page 44) | Table 2 | Table 1 |
| Ohio LS WKS form | Table 1 | Table 2 |

This file and its siblings use the booklet's numbering. If you are working from the standalone worksheet, the table names flip.

## Eligibility
All of the following must be true:
- MAGI less exemptions is less than $100,000
- You were 65 or older at the end of the tax year
- You received a total lump-sum distribution from a qualified pension, retirement, or profit-sharing plan
- You have not previously claimed this credit

## The permanent trade
If you take this credit, you cannot take the $50 senior citizen credit on this year's return or any future return to which you are a party. This election does not, by itself, kill the line 2 retirement income credit — that trade lives on line 3, and both lump-sum credits can be claimed in the same year if each qualifies.

## Required Information
- 1099-R showing a total distribution
- Recipient's age on December 31
- Worksheet from booklet page 44
- MAGI less exemptions

## Questions
- Are you 65 or older?
- Is box 2b a total distribution?
- Is the age multiple × $50 worth giving up the $50 senior credit for the rest of your filing life?

## Common Errors
- Claiming this credit before age 65
- Claiming line 4 and line 5 on the same return
- Omitting the $1,000 ceiling
- Confusing this worksheet with the lump sum RETIREMENT worksheet on page 43
- Using a partial distribution

## Prompt
- I am 68 and I took my entire pension as a lump sum.
- What is the difference between the Ohio lump sum retirement credit and the lump sum distribution credit?
