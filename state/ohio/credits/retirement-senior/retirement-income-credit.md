# Retirement Income Credit
## Description
Nonrefundable credit of $25 to $200 based on retirement income included in Ohio AGI. One credit per return, not per spouse. This is the annual credit most retirees want. The lump-sum alternative on line 3 permanently replaces it.

## Amount
Table 2, booklet page 44:

| Retirement income in Ohio AGI | Credit |
|---|---|
| $0 – $500 | $0 |
| $501 – $1,500 | $25 |
| $1,501 – $3,000 | $50 |
| $3,001 – $5,000 | $80 |
| $5,001 – $8,000 | $130 |
| $8,001 or more | $200 |

Maximum per return is $200. Age does not change the table.

## Eligibility
All of the following must be true:
- MAGI less exemptions is less than $100,000
- You received income from a pension, profit-sharing, or retirement plan (traditional IRA, 401(k), and similar)
- The income was received on account of retirement
- The income is included in Ohio AGI
- You have not previously taken the Ohio lump sum retirement credit

## What does not count
- Social Security benefits deducted on the Schedule of Adjustments
- Uniformed services retirement income deducted on the Schedule of Adjustments
- Rollovers not included in federal AGI
- Amounts deducted on Schedule of Adjustments lines 16, 17, 34, 42, or 43

## Required Information
- 1099-R forms for the year
- Total qualifying retirement income included in Ohio AGI, combined for both spouses on a joint return
- MAGI less exemptions
- Confirmation the lump sum retirement credit was never claimed

## Questions
- How much pension, IRA, or 401(k) income is actually in Ohio AGI after the Schedule of Adjustments?
- Is MAGI less exemptions under $100,000?
- Have you ever claimed the lump sum retirement credit?

## Common Errors
- Counting deducted Social Security or military retirement toward the table
- Claiming $200 per spouse on a joint return — it is $200 per return
- Claiming it after a prior-year lump sum retirement credit election
- Missing the $100,000 MAGI-less-exemptions cliff

## Prompt
- I have a pension and my Ohio income is under $100,000.
- Does Ohio give a credit for retirement income?
- Why didn't my Social Security count toward the Ohio retirement credit?
