# Scholarship Donation Credit
## Description
Nonrefundable credit for a cash donation made directly to a certified Ohio scholarship-granting organization. R.C. 5747.73.

SGOs are certified by the Ohio ATTORNEY GENERAL, not by the Department of Taxation. The certified list lives at charitable.ohioago.gov; ODT mirrors it at tax.ohio.gov. Donations to a school, a church, or a donor-advised fund do not land on this line.

## Amount
- Lesser of the amount donated during the taxable year or the cap
- Cap is $1,500 for spouses filing a JOINT RETURN, $750 for everyone else

## The joint-return rule changed in 2023 — do not use the old version
Before April 3, 2023, the statute used singular language and a couple had to make two separate $750 donations, one in each spouse's name, to reach $1,500. That is no longer the law. R.C. 5747.73(B) now caps the credit at $1,500 for spouses filing a joint return, full stop. A single $1,500 donation on a joint return reaches the full $1,500.

Many SGO fundraising pages still advertise the old two-donations rule. They are out of date. Follow the statute.

## Pass-through entities: $750 across ALL investors
If a pass-through entity donates, investors claim their proportionate or distributive share — but the statute caps the share claimable by ALL such investors combined at $750 for any taxable year. It is not $750 per investor. R.C. 5747.73(B).

## Timing — treat the "April 15" rule as unsupported
Ohio SGOs widely advertise that a donation received by April 15 can be claimed on the prior year's return. Neither R.C. 5747.73 nor ODT's guidance says that. The statute allows the credit for cash donated "during the taxable year," and ODT describes it as the total donated during the tax year.

Unless ODT issues guidance to the contrary, claim the donation in the year the payment was actually made. Do not carry an April 2027 gift back onto a 2026 return on the strength of a school's fundraising page.

## Eligibility
- Monetary donation made directly to a certified SGO during the taxable year
- Receipt or other document from the SGO acknowledging the contribution and the amount must be provided to the tax commissioner. The credit is not allowed without it. R.C. 5747.73

## Required Information
- SGO name, confirmed against the Attorney General's certified list
- Receipt from the SGO and proof the payment cleared
- Filing status, since the cap turns on it
- IT K-1 or ownership records if the donation came through a pass-through entity

## Questions
- Is the organization on the Attorney General's current certified SGO list?
- Are you filing jointly? If so the cap is $1,500 regardless of which spouse paid
- Did the donation actually clear during the tax year?

## Common Errors
- Using the pre-2023 rule and limiting a joint return to $750 because only one spouse donated
- Treating the pass-through $750 as a per-investor cap
- Claiming an April donation on the prior year's return
- Donating to a school instead of a certified SGO
- Skipping the receipt, which the statute makes a condition of the credit
