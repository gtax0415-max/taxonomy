# Home School Expenses Credit
## Description
Nonrefundable credit for educational expenses paid for a student who is home educated in Ohio and excused from compulsory attendance. The cap is $250 per qualifying student, not $250 per return.

The per-student basis took effect for tax year 2025 and continues for 2026. Anyone working from pre-2025 guidance will have the per-return version.

## Amount
- Lesser of $250 per qualifying student or the educational expenses paid for that student
- No MAGI cap in the booklet instructions

## What counts as educational expenses
Books, subscriptions, school supplies, supplementary materials, computer software, or applications used directly for home education.

## What does not count
Expenses or fees for computers or similar electronic devices or accessories. Hardware is out. Software used directly for home education is in.

## Eligibility
- The student is exempt from compulsory attendance for the purpose of home education
- Expenses were paid for that student
- Attach the documentation listed by the Department

Only students excused for home education qualify. Teacher-led online school is not home education for this credit.

## Required Information
- Copy of the Ohio school district superintendent's letter excusing the student from in-class attendance for a school year that includes the tax year
- Receipts for up to $250 of qualifying expenses per student
- Proof of payment (bank or card statements)
- Number of qualifying students

## Questions
- Is each child formally excused from compulsory attendance for home education?
- How much did you spend on books, supplies, and educational software — not on a laptop?
- How many students are formally excused, since the cap runs per student?

## Common Errors
- Claiming $250 once for the whole household, which was the pre-2025 rule
- Including a computer, tablet, or printer
- Claiming expenses for a student in an online public or chartered program
- Missing the superintendent excuse letter

## Prompt
- I home-school two children in Ohio.
- Did the Ohio home school credit go up this year?
