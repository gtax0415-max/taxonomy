# Nonresident Credit
## Description
Credit for the portion of Ohio AGI that was not earned while an Ohio resident and is not Ohio-sourced. Full-year Ohio residents cannot take it. Part-year residents and full-year nonresidents must use Form IT NRC.

## Amount
Computed on Form IT NRC. In general the credit is the Ohio tax attributable to the nonresident portion of Ohio AGI. You cannot estimate it as "the tax the other state charged."

## What goes in IT NRC column B
Nonbusiness income is sourced first:
- Compensation for work performed in Ohio, or paid while you were an Ohio resident
- Nonbusiness capital gain from Ohio real property or Ohio-sited tangible property
- Rents and royalties from Ohio property or Ohio-utilized intangibles
- Lottery, casino, and sports-gaming winnings paid by Ohio sources or received while a resident

Business income is not dropped onto those lines. It is computed in IT NRC Sections II and III and enters on line 5.

Anything already deducted on the Schedule of Adjustments is out of Ohio AGI and therefore out of the IT NRC entirely. For compensation that means reciprocity wages (line 14), nonresident disaster work (line 21), military pay for residents stationed outside Ohio (line 32), and nonresident military and civilian spouse wages (line 33). Check the current IT NRC instructions for the exact list before relying on it.

## Joint-filer trap
A full-year resident who files jointly with a nonresident or part-year resident must include ALL income in IT NRC column B, even income that is not Ohio-sourced. The booklet is explicit. Do not strip the resident spouse's out-of-state income off the form.

## Eligibility
- Part-year resident or full-year nonresident, or a joint return that includes one
- Form IT NRC included with the IT 1040
- Residency dates entered for part-year residents

## Required Information
- Residency dates and the other state's two-letter code
- W-2s and 1099s showing work location and payer
- Business-income allocation from IT NRC Sections II and III
- Days-worked-in-Ohio ratio if that is the compensation method used

## Questions
- Were you a full-year Ohio resident? If yes, stop
- Did you work in Ohio while a nonresident?
- Are you filing jointly with a spouse who has a different residency status?

## Common Errors
- Full-year residents claiming this credit
- Skipping Form IT NRC and putting a guessed number on line 38
- Treating all wages as non-Ohio because the employer is out of state
- Leaving Ohio lottery or casino winnings out of column B
- On a mixed-residency joint return, omitting the resident spouse's income from column B

## Prompt
- I moved out of Ohio in June.
- I live in Kentucky and work a few days a year in Cincinnati.
- Does Ohio tax my whole salary if I am a nonresident?
