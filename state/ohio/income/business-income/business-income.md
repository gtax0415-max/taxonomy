# Ohio Business Income
## Description
Ohio lets you deduct the first $250,000 of business income ($125,000 married filing separately) and taxes any leftover business income at a flat 3%. You must complete one Ohio Schedule of Business Income per return. Do not copy federal schedule totals blindly. Ohio's definition of business income is narrower in some places and broader in others.

## Schedule of Business Income map
| Line | Source |
|---|---|
| 1 | Federal Schedule B interest and ordinary dividends that are business income |
| 2 | Federal Schedule C net profit or loss, netted across businesses |
| 3 | Federal Schedule D capital gain or loss on business property |
| 4 | Federal Schedule E net business income, excluding guaranteed payments |
| 5 | Guaranteed payments and/or W-2 compensation from a PTE you own at least 20% of |
| 6 | Federal Schedule F farming |
| 7 | Portion of Schedule of Adjustments line 2 that is IT 4738 tax on business income |
| 8 | Portion of Schedule of Adjustments line 3 that is Notice 2020-75 tax on business income |
| 9 | Other business income, including federal Form 4797 business amounts |
| Part 2 | Computes the deduction that goes to Schedule of Adjustments line 13 |
| Part 3 | Taxable leftover business income at 3%, IT 1040 line 8b |
| Part 4 | FEIN, ownership %, and name of each PTE or sole proprietorship |

Do not complete Part 3 if Ohio taxable income (IT 1040 line 5) is zero. Taxable business income on IT 1040 line 6 cannot exceed line 5 — the cap is on the base, not on the 3% tax.
## New for 2026: capital gain deductions come first
Two capital-gain deductions became available for taxable years beginning in or after 2026. When the gain is business income, both come off BEFORE the $250,000 business income deduction in R.C. 5747.01(A)(28):

- R.C. 5747.01(A)(34) and R.C. 5747.79 — qualifying capital gain on the sale of an ownership interest in a business, limited to the lesser of the gain from each entity or that entity's deductible payroll
- R.C. 5747.01(A)(35)(a) — Ohio VCOC capital gain, 100% of the Ohio-business slice and 50% of the other-business slice

Only what survives those deductions enters the $250,000 business income deduction, and only what survives that is taxed at 3%. Running the order backwards overstates the deduction. See state/ohio/deductions/subtractions/vcoc-capital-gain.md.
## 20% ownership rule
Compensation and guaranteed payments from a pass-through entity, or a PEO paying on its behalf, are business income only if you directly or indirectly own at least 20%. Indirect ownership does not include constructive ownership under federal attribution. A spouse who works at your S corporation but owns 0% does not convert those wages to business income.
## Tests
Transactional test: nature, frequency, and regularity. Functional test: whether the property was integral to the trade or business. See Kemppel v. Zaino, 2001-Ohio-92.
## Questions
- Is this income from the regular course of a trade or business, or a personal investment?
- Do you own at least 20% of the entity that paid the wages or guaranteed payments?
- Is any of the gain a 2026 qualifying or VCOC capital gain that comes off first?
## Common Errors
- Listing personal bank-account interest on line 1
- Putting guaranteed payments on line 4
- Treating a spouse's constructive ownership as indirect ownership
- Taking the $250,000 deduction before the 2026 capital-gain deductions
- Skipping Part 4 entity listing
## Prompt
- I own 25% of an LLC that pays me a guaranteed payment.
- I sold farm equipment. Is that Ohio business income?
