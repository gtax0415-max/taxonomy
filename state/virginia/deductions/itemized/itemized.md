# Itemized Deductions (Form 760, Line 10; Virginia Schedule A, Lines 1-19)
## Description
Virginia itemized deductions start from the federal Schedule A categories and are recomputed on Virginia Schedule A. The main Virginia differences:
- **Medical floor:** 10% of federal AGI.
- **SALT cap:** none on real estate and personal property taxes.
- **Income taxes:** state, local and foreign income taxes are removed on Line 18.
- **Overall limitation:** Virginia limits itemized deductions for higher-income taxpayers (Lines 17-18).

The result on Schedule A, Line 19 goes to Form 760, Line 10.
## 2026 federal changes
Virginia conforms to the Internal Revenue Code as of December 31, 2025 (HB 29, Chapter 7; Tax Bulletin 26-1). That includes federal changes to itemized deductions, so these 2026 changes carry to Virginia Schedule A:
- SALT cap amount of $40,400 ($20,200 married filing separately), which matters only for the sales tax election and the overall limitation
- Mortgage insurance premiums deductible again
- 0.5% floor on charitable contributions
- Gambling losses limited to 90%
- Casualty losses from state-declared disasters

Virginia does not follow:
- The federal 2/37 limitation; it keeps the Pease limitation
- The 7.5% medical floor
- The federal charitable deduction for non-itemizers, which is not an itemized deduction

The 2026 federal Schedule A draft renumbers several lines. The 2026 Virginia Schedule A has not been released; line numbers in this folder follow the 2025 form.
## Who itemizes
- You must claim the same type of deduction (standard or itemized) on the Virginia return as on the federal return. A federal itemizer must itemize for Virginia; a taxpayer who took the federal standard deduction may not itemize for Virginia.
- If one spouse claims itemized deductions, the other spouse must also claim itemized deductions.
- If state and local income tax is the only federal itemized deduction claimed, enter zero on Form 760, Line 10.
- Filing Status 3 after a joint federal return: itemized deductions that cannot be accounted for separately are allocated between spouses by each spouse's share of the combined federal adjusted gross income.
- At the top of Schedule A, enter the filing status claimed on the FEDERAL return: 1 = Single, 2 = Joint, 3 = Married filing separately, 4 = Head of household, 5 = Qualifying surviving spouse. The Line 17 threshold is based on this federal status.
- Enclose Virginia Schedule A with the return.
## How the lines flow
- **Conformity Worksheet (page 2):** FAGI + conformity additions - conformity subtractions = Conformity FAGI. It is used for the medical floor, the charitable and casualty limits and the Line 17 test when there are conformity adjustments.
- **Line 17:** Line 4 medical + Line 7 taxes + Line 10 interest + Line 14 gifts + Line 15 casualty + Line 16c other = total itemized deductions, limited if income is over the threshold.
- **Line 18:** state and local income tax (Line 5a) plus foreign income tax (Line 6), or the Part B amount from the Limited Itemized Deduction Worksheet.
- **Line 19:** Line 17 - Line 18, not below zero, → Form 760, Line 10.
## Line 17 thresholds (Form 760, Line 1, federal filing status)
| Federal filing status | 2025 (confirmed) | 2026 (estimated) |
|---|---|---|
| Married filing jointly or qualifying surviving spouse | $399,200 | $408,900 |
| Head of household | $365,950 | $374,800 |
| Single | $332,700 | $340,750 |
| Married filing separately | $199,600 | $204,450 |

The 2026 thresholds have not been published by Virginia Tax; confirm against the 2026 Schedule A instructions.
