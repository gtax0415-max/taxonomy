# Medical and Dental Expenses (Virginia Schedule A, Lines 1-4)
## Description
Unreimbursed medical and dental expenses are deductible for Virginia only to the extent they exceed 10 percent of federal adjusted gross income. The federal floor is 7.5 percent. Virginia deconformed from the lower federal floor for taxable years beginning on or after January 1, 2019. Tax Bulletin 26-1 lists this as a continuing exception for 2025 and later. So the Virginia figure is recomputed on Schedule A rather than copied from the federal Schedule A. When the taxpayer has conformity additions or subtractions, the floor uses Conformity FAGI from the Conformity Worksheet.
## How to compute
| Line | Entry |
|---|---|
| 1 | Medical and dental expenses (do not include expenses reimbursed or paid by others) |
| 2 | Federal adjusted gross income from Line 5 of the Conformity Worksheet; part-year residents use only income attributable to Virginia residency; if negative enter 0 |
| 3 | Line 2 x 10% (0.10) |
| 4 | Line 1 - Line 3; if Line 3 is more than Line 1, enter 0 |

Example: expenses of $9,000 and Conformity FAGI of $70,000. Line 3 = $7,000; Line 4 = $2,000. The federal deduction for the same taxpayer is $9,000 - $5,250 = $3,750.
## Conformity Worksheet (Schedule A, page 2)
Complete it BEFORE Schedule A.
1. Federal adjusted gross income from the federal return
2. Conformity additions to FAGI (Schedule ADJ, Line 2a)
3. Line 1 + Line 2
4. Conformity subtractions from FAGI (Schedule ADJ, Line 6a)
5. Conformity FAGI = Line 3 - Line 4

Use Line 5 instead of federal AGI wherever a federal AGI-based limit applies: the medical floor (Line 4), gifts to charity (Line 14), casualty and theft loss (Line 15), and the Line 17 limitation test (in place of Form 760, Line 1). With no conformity additions or subtractions, Line 5 equals federal AGI. Virginia's 2026 conformity deconforms mainly from business depreciation and expensing items, so these adjustments usually arise only for business owners.
## Interaction with other lines
- Line 4 is included in Lines 1 and 2 of the Limited Itemized Deduction Worksheet, so medical expenses are NOT reduced by the overall limitation.
- Part-year residents use only the portion of federal AGI adjusted for conformity and earned while a Virginia resident.
- Long-term care premiums deducted on Schedule ADJ (code 106) and organ donor expenses (code 110) cannot also be counted here, and vice versa.
## Required Information
- Total medical and dental expenses paid in 2026, including insurance premiums paid with after-tax dollars
- Reimbursements from insurance or others, and amounts paid from an HSA or FSA
- Federal adjusted gross income
- Any business-related conformity adjustments, such as Virginia depreciation differences
- Residency dates, for part-year residents
- Long-term care premiums or organ donor costs already claimed on Schedule ADJ
## Questions
- What medical and dental expenses did you pay that insurance or others did not reimburse?
- Did you pay any of these from an HSA or FSA?
- Do you have any business depreciation or other conformity differences between federal and Virginia?
- Were you a Virginia resident all year?
## Common Errors
- Copying the federal Schedule A medical deduction, which uses 7.5%, instead of recomputing at 10%
- Using federal AGI when there are conformity adjustments (use Conformity FAGI)
- Including reimbursed amounts or amounts paid from an HSA or FSA
- Claiming the same long-term care premiums or organ donor costs here and on Schedule ADJ

- Why is my Virginia medical deduction different from my federal one?
