# Age 65 or Older and Blind Exemptions (Form 760, Line 12, Section B)
## Description
Additional $800 exemptions for a taxpayer or spouse who is 65 or older, and for a taxpayer or spouse who is blind. They are entered in Exemption Section B at the top of Form 760 and added to the Section A total on Line 12. A person who is both 65 or older and blind gets two $800 exemptions. These exemptions are separate from, and in addition to, the age deduction on Form 760, Line 4.
## Section B boxes
| Box | Qualifies if |
|---|---|
| You 65 or over | age 65 or older on or before January 1, 2027 (born on or before January 1, 1962) |
| Spouse 65 or over | same test for the spouse |
| You Blind | considered blind for federal income tax purposes |
| Spouse Blind | same test for the spouse |
| Total Section B | count x $800 |

Example: a joint return where both spouses are over 65 and one is blind counts 3; 3 x $800 = $2,400.
## Mutually exclusive with the Credit for Low-Income Individuals
You cannot claim the 65 or older or Blind exemptions if you also claim a Credit for Low-Income Individuals on Form 760, Line 23. Compare the two before choosing. The low-income credit computation on Schedule ADJ, Line 12 counts only personal and dependent exemptions, never these.
## Interaction with other lines
- The age deduction (Form 760, Line 4, up to $12,000 each, income-based for those born January 2, 1939, to January 1, 1962) is a different item with its own restrictions; claiming the $800 exemption does not depend on it.
- Spouse Tax Adjustment Worksheet, Part 1, Line 2: each spouse's 65 or over and Blind boxes are multiplied by $800 and added to $930.
## Questions
- What are your and your spouse's birth dates?
- Is either of you considered blind for federal tax purposes?
- Are you claiming the Credit for Low-Income Individuals instead?
## Common Errors
- Using an age-65 date other than January 1, 2027 (someone born January 1, 1962 qualifies)
- Claiming these exemptions together with the Credit for Low-Income Individuals
- Counting a 65-and-blind person only once
- Treating the $800 exemption and the $12,000 age deduction as the same benefit
