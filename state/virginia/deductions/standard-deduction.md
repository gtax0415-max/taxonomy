# Standard Deduction (Form 760, Line 11)
## Description
A flat deduction from Virginia adjusted gross income for taxpayers who do not itemize. Virginia follows the federal choice: if you claimed the standard deduction on your federal return, you must claim the Virginia standard deduction, and if you itemized federally you must itemize on Virginia Schedule A instead. The Virginia amount is a fixed figure set by Virginia, not the federal amount. Age 65 or older and blindness are recognized through the $800 exemptions on Line 12.
## Amounts for 2026
| Filing status | Line 11 |
|---|---|
| 1 = Single (including a federal head of household) | $8,750 |
| 2 = Married filing jointly | $17,500 |
| 3 = Married filing separately | $8,750 |

A single filer who is head of household for federal purposes still uses Filing Status 1 and the $8,750 figure.
## Dependent on another's return
If you can be claimed as a dependent on another taxpayer's federal return, your standard deduction is limited to the amount of your earned income. Enter the SMALLER of:
1. Your earned income, or
2. The standard deduction for your filing status.

Fill in the "Dependent on another's return" oval at the top of Form 760. Example: a student claimed by a parent who earned $4,200 in wages enters $4,200 on Line 11, not $8,750. A dependent with $9,000 of wages enters $8,750.
## Interaction with other lines
- Do not complete Line 10 when claiming the standard deduction.
- If one spouse claims itemized deductions, the other spouse must also claim itemized deductions, so married filing separately spouses cannot split methods.
- The standard deduction does not affect Schedule ADJ deductions (Line 13) or exemptions (Line 12); both are still allowed.
- The sunset noted in the 2025 booklet no longer applies. The 2026 Appropriation Act (HB 30, Special Session I, Chapter 1) keeps $8,750 and $17,500 for 2026 and raises them to $9,200 and $18,400 for 2027, then $9,300 and $18,600 for 2028 and 2029. They revert to $3,000 and $6,000 only after Taxable Year 2029 (Va. Code § 58.1-322.03, subdivision 1 b).
## Required Information
- Whether the federal standard deduction was taken
- Virginia filing status
- For a dependent, earned income for 2026
## Questions
- Did you take the standard deduction on your federal return?
- What is your Virginia filing status (1, 2 or 3)?
- Can anyone claim you as a dependent on their federal return? If so, how much did you earn from work?
- If married filing separately, is your spouse itemizing?
## Common Errors
- Entering the federal standard deduction amount instead of the Virginia amount
- Itemizing on the Virginia return after taking the federal standard deduction (not allowed)
- A dependent entering the full $8,750 when earned income is lower
- Entering $17,500 on a married filing separately return (each spouse uses $8,750)
- Completing both Line 10 and Line 11
## Prompt
- What is the Virginia standard deduction for 2026?
- My parents claim me. How much standard deduction do I get in Virginia?
- I'm head of household federally. Do I get a bigger Virginia standard deduction?
