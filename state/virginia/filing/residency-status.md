# Residency Status and Choosing the Right Form
## Description
Virginia has two kinds of residents: domiciliary and actual. Residency can be full-year or part-year, a domiciliary resident of another state can also be an actual resident of Virginia, and a nonresident may still have to file. Form 760 is only for residents; this file decides whether a taxpayer belongs on Form 760 at all.
## Step 1: residency status
- Domiciliary (legal) resident: your permanent home is in Virginia, the place you intend to return to whenever absent. Everyone has one domicile at a time; actual presence is not required, and you stay a domiciliary resident until you establish domicile in another state. Armed forces members who claim Virginia as home of record are domiciliary residents even if stationed elsewhere. A domiciliary resident working outside Virginia (even outside the U.S.) for many years remains one unless Virginia is abandoned as domicile.
- Actual resident: you maintained an abode in Virginia or were physically present more than 183 total days in the year, even if domiciled elsewhere. You may have to file as a resident in both states; claim the credit on the domicile state's return for tax paid to Virginia.
- Students: same rules as anyone else.
- Members of the U.S. Congress domiciled in another state are not actual residents even with an abode here over 183 days, but may need Form 763 for other Virginia source income. The exemption does not cover spouses, dependents or congressional staff.
## Step 2: which return
- Form 760: actual or domiciliary resident for the entire year, or for part of the year with all income for the whole year from Virginia sources.
- Form 760PY: moved into Virginia and became an actual or domiciliary resident, or moved out and became domiciled elsewhere (not moving back within six months).
- Form 763: nonresident with Virginia source income other than personal savings interest, individual stock investment interest or dividends, or pension payments from a Virginia payor. Virginia source income includes:
  - income from Virginia real or tangible property
  - income from a business or occupation carried on in Virginia
  - Virginia Lottery prizes and gambling winnings from wagers placed or paid in Virginia
  - intangible income employed in a Virginia business
- Married couples with one resident and one nonresident spouse may file jointly only if both elect to be taxed as Virginia residents; otherwise the resident files Form 760 (Filing Status 3) or 760PY and the nonresident Form 763 if required. A full-year and a part-year resident may file jointly on Form 760PY.
## Military and spouses (SCRA)
A servicemember serving under military orders, and the spouse, may each elect for tax purposes one of three states: the member's residence or domicile, the spouse's residence or domicile, or the member's permanent duty station. The two need not elect the same state, and no form is filed.
- Electing a state other than Virginia exempts the member's active duty pay and the spouse's Virginia wages for services ("SCRA protected income").
- Tax withheld on that income can be refunded on Form 763-S, and Form VA-4 can stop future withholding.
- Other Virginia source income goes on Form 763.
- Electing Virginia (or being unable to elect another state) makes you a resident filing Form 760 with no exemption.
## Nonresident exceptions
- Residents of Kentucky or the District of Columbia do not file Form 763 if they commuted daily, had no Virginia abode, had only wages from Virginia and were taxed at home.
- Residents of Maryland, Pennsylvania or West Virginia also do not file if they had only Virginia wages, were present 183 days or less, and were taxed at home.
- Either group may need Form 763-S to recover Virginia withholding. Tax withheld in error by an employer is also refunded on Form 763-S.
## Required Information
- State of domicile and the evidence for it
- Days present in Virginia and any Virginia abode in 2026
- Dates of any move into or out of Virginia
- Military status and the state elected under the SCRA, for the member and spouse
## Questions
- Where is your permanent home, the place you intend to return to?
- Did you keep a home in Virginia or spend more than 183 days here?
- Did you move into or out of Virginia in 2026, and on what date?
- Are you or your spouse in the military, and which state have you elected?
- Did you live in Kentucky, DC, Maryland, Pennsylvania or West Virginia and work in Virginia?
## Common Errors
- Filing Form 760 for a part-year resident who had non-Virginia income
- Filing jointly on Form 760 when one spouse is a nonresident and no joint election was made
- Assuming a student away at school has changed domicile
- Forgetting Form 763 for Virginia source income received while a nonresident
