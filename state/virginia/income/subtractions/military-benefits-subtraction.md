# Military Benefits Subtraction (Schedule ADJ, Lines 6b-6d, code 60)
## Description
For taxable years beginning on and after January 1, 2024, certain military benefits received by an individual may be subtracted from Virginia taxable income, using code 60. The maximum subtraction is $40,000 for Taxable Year 2025 and after, so $40,000 for 2026.
## What counts as military benefits
- Military retirement income received for service in the Armed Forces of the United States
- Qualified military benefits received under IRC 134
- Benefits paid to the surviving spouse of a veteran of the Armed Forces under the Department of Defense Survivor Benefit Plan
- Military benefits paid to the surviving spouse of a veteran of the Armed Forces
## Amount
- Up to $40,000 per individual
- On a joint return by two married members of the military who both receive qualifying benefits, each spouse may use the maximum for his or her own benefits
## Limitation
Not allowed if a credit, exemption, subtraction or deduction is claimed for the same income under any other provision of Virginia or federal law (for example, Medal of Honor military retirement subtracted under code 44).
## What to enter
- Qualifying military benefits included in federal AGI, up to $40,000 per individual
- Enter code 60 on Line 6b, 6c or 6d, followed by the amount
## Questions
- Do you receive military retirement pay, or Survivor Benefit Plan payments as a surviving spouse?
- Are both spouses military retirees with their own benefits?
- Is any of the same income already subtracted or deducted elsewhere?
## Common Errors
- Subtracting more than $40,000 for one person
- Combining spouses' limits when only one spouse receives qualifying benefits
- Claiming the same income under code 60 and code 44 or another subtraction
## Prompt
- I'm a retired Air Force officer drawing a pension.
- I receive Survivor Benefit Plan payments since my husband died.
