# Age Deduction (Form 760, Line 4)
## Description
Virginia gives older taxpayers a deduction of up to $12,000 each for 2026 (Va. Code § 58.1-322.03, subdivision 5). Taxpayers born on or before January 1, 1962 may qualify, based on birth date, filing status and income. It is entered on Form 760, Line 4 with separate You and Spouse amounts. Birth dates must be provided on the return.
## Amounts
- Born on or before January 1, 1939: $12,000 (each spouse so born may claim $12,000)
- Born January 2, 1939 through January 1, 1962: income-based. The $12,000 is reduced $1 for every $1 that AFAGI exceeds $50,000 (Filing Status 1) or that the married couple's joint AFAGI exceeds $75,000 (all married taxpayers, joint or separate)
- AFAGI = federal adjusted gross income, modified for conformity adjustments, minus taxable Social Security and Tier 1 Railroad benefits
- The $12,000 amount and the $50,000 and $75,000 thresholds are fixed in statute and are not indexed
## Age 65 and Older Deduction Worksheet
Required only for those born January 2, 1939 through January 1, 1962. Married taxpayers always use combined income of both spouses, regardless of filing status.
1. Number claiming an income-based deduction: single 1; married, 1 if one spouse claims, 2 if both are eligible and both file Virginia returns (joint or separate)
2. FAGI (married: combined)
3. Conformity addition (married: combined)
4. Line 2 + Line 3
5. Conformity subtraction (married: combined)
6. Line 4 - Line 5
7. Taxable Social Security and Tier 1 Railroad benefits (married: combined)
8. Line 6 - Line 7 = AFAGI
9. Income limit: $50,000 single; $75,000 married
10. If Line 8 is less than Line 9: $12,000 (married: $12,000 for each spouse claiming)
11. If Line 8 is greater than Line 9: Line 8 - Line 9
12. Line 1 x $12,000
13. If Line 11 exceeds Line 12: no age deduction (if both spouses computed, neither qualifies)
14. If Line 12 exceeds Line 11: Line 12 - Line 11. Single, or married with one spouse claiming: this is the deduction
15. Both spouses claiming: Line 14 divided by 2, entered in the You and Spouse columns
A calculator is also available at www.tax.virginia.gov.
## Mutually exclusive items
- A taxpayer who claims the age deduction may NOT claim the disability income subtraction (Schedule ADJ, Line 5), the Credit for Low-Income Individuals or the Refundable Virginia Earned Income Credit.
- Each eligible spouse may take either an age deduction or a disability subtraction. Claim whichever gives the greater benefit.
- Neither spouse may claim an age deduction if one spouse claimed the Credit for Low-Income Individuals or Refundable Virginia Earned Income Credit, even if filing separately.
## Required Information
- Date of birth of each spouse
- FAGI and taxable Social Security and Tier 1 benefits of each spouse (combined, even if filing separately)
- Each spouse's Virginia conformity additions and subtractions
- Whether either spouse claims the disability subtraction, the low-income credit or the Virginia EIC
## Questions
- What are your and your spouse's dates of birth?
- What is your combined federal AGI and taxable Social Security (both spouses, even if filing separately)?
- Do you have any Virginia conformity additions or subtractions?
- Are you claiming a disability subtraction, the low-income credit or the Virginia EIC?
## Common Errors
- Married filing separately using only one spouse's income instead of joint AFAGI
- Both spouses computing separately instead of computing a joint deduction and splitting it in half
- Claiming the age deduction and the disability subtraction for the same person
- Claiming the age deduction together with the low-income credit or refundable Virginia EIC
- Omitting birth dates on the return
## Prompt
- I turned 65 this year. Do I get the Virginia age deduction?
- We're retired, with pensions and Social Security. How much of the $12,000 can we each take?
