# Disability Income (Schedule ADJ, Lines 5a and 5b)
## Description
Virginia lets a person who is permanently and totally disabled subtract up to $20,000 of disability income reported as wages (or payments in lieu of wages) on the federal return. Disability income is as defined under IRC 22(c)(2)(B)(iii). On a joint return each spouse can qualify.
## Amounts
- Up to $20,000 per individual
- Enter YOUR subtraction on Line 5a and your SPOUSE'S subtraction on Line 5b
## Age deduction or disability subtraction, not both
A taxpayer cannot claim an age deduction on Form 760, Line 4 and a subtraction for disability income. Claim the one that benefits you most. On a joint return, each spouse may claim, if applicable, either an age deduction or a disability subtraction.
## Social Security disability
If the disability payments come from Social Security, do not take the subtraction twice on the same income. The taxable portion of Social Security is already subtracted on Form 760, Line 5.
## Retirement age
Once a taxpayer reaches mandatory retirement age (as defined by the IRS), disability income becomes retirement income and no longer qualifies.
## Questions
- Are you permanently and totally disabled?
- Is your disability pay reported as wages (or in lieu of wages) on your federal return?
- Is any of it Social Security disability?
- Were you born on or before January 1, 1962 (so the age deduction is an alternative)?
- Have you reached mandatory retirement age?
## Common Errors
- Claiming both the age deduction and the disability subtraction for the same person
- Subtracting more than $20,000 per person
- Subtracting Social Security disability here when it is already on Form 760, Line 5
- Continuing the subtraction after mandatory retirement age
## Prompt
- I'm on permanent disability from my employer.
- My spouse and I both receive disability pay.
