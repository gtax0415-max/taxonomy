# Credit for Tax Paid to Another State (Schedule OSC, Line 21; Form 760, Line 24)
## Description
A Virginia resident is taxed on all income wherever earned. If you received income from another state, had to file a return there and paid its income tax, you may claim a nonrefundable credit on Schedule OSC for that tax. The credit applies only to income taxes paid to other states (Va. Code § 58.1-332). When someone is an actual resident of one state and a domiciliary resident of another, the domiciliary state generally allows the credit for tax paid to the state of actual residence.
## How to compute (one state per computation)
- Line 1: filing status on the other state's return (1 Single, 2 Married Filing Jointly, 3 Married Filing Separately, 4 Unified, 5 Other; Other includes corporation income tax paid by an S corporation and eligible PTE tax).
- Line 2: who claims the credit (1 You, 2 Spouse, 3 You and Spouse, not for composite or corporation filing status).
- Line 3: qualifying taxable income taxed by both states: earned or business income; gain from the sale of a capital asset not used in a trade or business (including a residence); income on which corporation income tax was paid to a state that does not recognize the federal S election; income on which an eligible PTE tax was paid to another state for you. Remove nonqualifying income (for example lottery or gambling winnings, unemployment compensation, and other-state additions not in Virginia taxable income). If the other state reduces tax on total income by an allocation percentage, use the reduced taxable income.
- Line 4: Virginia taxable income, Form 760, Line 15.
- Line 5: net tax liability after nonrefundable credits on the other state's return, counting only state income tax on earned, business and capital gain income.
- Line 6: the state's 2-letter abbreviation.
- Line 7: Virginia income tax, Form 760, Line 18.
- Line 8: Line 3 divided by Line 4, to one decimal place, not over 100% and not under 0.1%.
- Line 9: Line 7 times Line 8.
- Line 10: lesser of Line 5 or Line 9. Lines 11-20 repeat for a second state; Line 21 totals Lines 10 and 20 → Form 760, Line 24.
## Special situations
- Border state computation (Kentucky, Maryland, North Carolina, West Virginia): if you file only in one of these states, that income is solely wages, salaries or federal Schedule C business income taxed there, and Virginia taxable income is at least the qualifying taxable income on that return, fill in the border state oval and enter 100.0 on Line 8. Each spouse on a joint return may use it if each filed in only one listed state.
- Married taxpayers: unless both returns are joint, adjust Line 4 or Line 3 to the filer whose income was taxed; spouses in the same composite return or S corporation compute separately.
- Elective PTE tax paid to another state under a law substantially similar to Virginia's qualifies; enclose the PTE's statement.
## What to enclose
A separate computation for each state, a copy of each other state's return, a Composite Filing Statement if included in a composite return, and the S corporation or PTE statement where applicable. W-2s, 1099s and Schedules K-1 are not acceptable documentation.
## Questions
- Did you file a return and pay income tax to another state for 2026?
- What kind of income was taxed there (wages, business income, capital gain, other)?
- Were you included in a composite return, or did a pass-through entity pay tax to another state for you?
- Did you work in Arizona, California, Oregon, DC, Kentucky, Maryland, Pennsylvania or West Virginia?
## Common Errors
- Claiming the credit for Arizona, California, Oregon or DC tax on the Virginia return
- Claiming credit for tax withheld on wages that Kentucky, Maryland, Pennsylvania or West Virginia exempts, instead of obtaining a refund from that state
- Using the other state's withholding instead of its net tax liability on Line 5
- Including lottery winnings or unemployment compensation in Line 3
- Sending W-2s or K-1s in place of the other state's return
## Prompt
- I live in Virginia but work in North Carolina and pay tax there.
- My partnership paid pass-through entity tax to Georgia for me.
