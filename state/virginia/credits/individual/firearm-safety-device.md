# Firearm Safety Device Tax Credit (Schedule CR, Section 1, Part 33, Lines 33D and 33E)
## Description
Under Va. Code § 58.1-339.14 as currently in force, for taxable years beginning on and after January 1, 2024, but before January 1, 2028, a nonrefundable individual income tax credit of up to $300 is available for the cost of buying one or more firearm safety devices (the credit was first enacted in 2023 and revised in 2024 and 2025). Only one credit is allowed per taxable year. A firearm safety device is (i) a device that, installed on a firearm, prevents it from operating until deactivated (trigger locks, chamber locks, cable locks), or (ii) a safe, gun safe, gun case, lock box or other storage device unlocked only by key, combination or similar means. Purchases must be from a commercial retailer registered as a Virginia sales tax dealer; firearm purchases do not count.
## Limits and carryover
- Up to $300 per individual per tax year (Line A)
- The credit claimed in a year cannot exceed the individual's liability; the excess carries over for the next 5 taxable years or until used
- Aggregate credits are capped at $5 million per taxable year, allocated by the Department first-come, first-served
## How to compute (SPOUSE and YOU columns)
- Line A: credit authorized by the Department of Taxation, not over $300 per individual
- Line B: carryover from prior years
- Line C: A + B
- Line 33D (you) and Line 33E (spouse): lesser of Line C or the balance of the maximum nonrefundable credit
- Line F: C minus D and/or E, within the 5-year carryover period
## Authorization (required)
Apply to the Department of Taxation (Form FSD), preferably electronically at www.tax.virginia.gov (fax or mail also accepted). Because credits are granted in order of receipt, apply early. Claim only the amount authorized, and submit the purchase receipts with the return.
## Required Information
- Date, retailer and price of each safety device bought in 2026, for each spouse
- Credit amount the Department authorized
- Carryover from prior years and its year of origin
## Questions
- Did you or your spouse buy a gun lock, gun safe or lock box from a retailer in 2026, and what did it cost?
- Did you apply to the Department, and what amount was authorized?
## Common Errors
- Claiming more than $300 per person or more than one credit per year
- Including the cost of a firearm
- Claiming without the Department's authorization, or without enclosing the receipt
