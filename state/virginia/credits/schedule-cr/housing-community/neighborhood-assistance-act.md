# Neighborhood Assistance Act Tax Credit (Schedule CR, Section 1, Part 3, Line 3D)
## Description
The Virginia Neighborhood Assistance Act provides credits to individuals and businesses that make qualified donations directly to pre-approved Neighborhood Assistance Program organizations whose primary function is providing educational or other qualified services for the benefit of low-income families. Individuals may receive a credit for donating money or marketable securities. Businesses may also receive credit for donations of property, limited professional services or contracting services.
## Professional services
Licensed veterinarians, physicians, dentists, nurses, nurse practitioners, physician assistants, chiropractors, optometrists, dental hygienists, pharmacists, professional counselors, clinical social workers, clinical psychologists, marriage and family therapists, physical therapists, physician specialists and mediators who donate services for an approved organization may be eligible for credits.
## How to compute
- Line A: authorized amount of Neighborhood Assistance Act credit
- Line B: carryover from prior years
- Line C: A + B
- Line 3D: lesser of C or the balance of the maximum nonrefundable credit
- Line E: C minus D, only within the 5-year carryover period
## Authorization and pass-through
The credit on Line A is the authorized amount; organizations are approved through the Virginia Department of Social Services, Neighborhood Assistance Program, or the Virginia Department of Education, Neighborhood Assistance Tax Credit Program for Education. Credit attributable to a partnership or S corporation must be allocated to partners and shareholders in proportion to ownership. Unused credits carry forward for the next 5 taxable years.
## Questions
- Did you donate money or securities to a Neighborhood Assistance Program organization?
- What credit amount was authorized for your donation?
- If you are a licensed professional, did you donate services to an approved organization?
## Common Errors
- Claiming the donation amount rather than the authorized credit amount
- Donating to an organization that is not pre-approved
- Individuals claiming credit for donated property or services (individual credit is for money or marketable securities; the professional services rule applies to the listed licensed professionals)
## Prompt
- I donated to a local charity that said it has Neighborhood Assistance tax credits.
