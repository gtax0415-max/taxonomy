# Historic Rehabilitation Tax Credit (Schedule CR, Section 1, Part 10, Line 10E)
## Description
Individuals, estates, partnerships, trusts and corporations that incur eligible expenses rehabilitating a certified historic structure may claim a credit equal to 25% of eligible rehabilitation expenses. To qualify, the cost of the rehabilitation must equal at least 50% (25% for an owner-occupied residence) of the building's assessed value for local real estate tax purposes in the year before the rehabilitation started.
## Amount and limits
- Credit = 25% of eligible rehabilitation expenses
- For taxable years beginning on and after January 1, 2025, each taxpayer may claim no more than $7.5 million in any taxable year, including carryover (it was $5 million for taxable years 2017 through 2024); the 2026 Appropriation Act continues this limit
- Unused credit carries forward 10 years
## How to compute
- Line A: eligible expenses
- Line B: Line A times 25%
- Line C: carryover from prior years
- Line D: B + C
- Line 10E: lesser of D or the balance of the maximum nonrefundable credit; each credit holder cannot claim more than $7.5 million
- Line F: D minus E, within the 10-year carryover period
## Certification (required)
The rehabilitation must be certified by the Virginia Department of Historic Resources as consistent with the Secretary of the Interior's Standards for Rehabilitation. The request for certification of the completed project must be submitted within 1 year of completion of the work. Attach the certificate to the return on which the credit is claimed.
## Required Information
- DHR certification of the completed work and its date
- Eligible rehabilitation expenses
- Prior-year assessed value of the building and whether it is owner-occupied
- Carryover from prior years and its year of origin
- Ownership share if received through a pass-through entity
- Any federal income or gain recognized in connection with the credit
## Questions
- Did you rehabilitate a certified historic structure, and was it your own residence?
- Did the rehabilitation cost at least 50% (25% for an owner-occupied home) of the prior-year assessed value?
- Has DHR certified the completed project?
- Did you recognize any federal income or gain from the credit?
## Common Errors
- Claiming before DHR certifies the completed project
- Missing the 1-year deadline for the completion certification request
- Forgetting the code 53 subtraction for income or gain tied to the credit
## Prompt
- We renovated our historic home and got DHR approval.
- I invested in a partnership that rehabbed an old warehouse.
