# Land Preservation Tax Credit (Schedule CR, Section 1, Part 18, Lines 18F and 18G)
## Description
A credit for conveying land, or an interest in land, located in Virginia to a public or private agency eligible to hold it for conservation or preservation. The conveyance must be in perpetuity. For conveyances in 2007 and later, the credit equals 40% of the land's fair market value, substantiated by a qualified appraisal prepared by a qualified appraiser as defined under federal charitable contribution rules.
## Limits and carryover
- The Code lists a higher annual limit, but budget language overrides it: for taxable years beginning on and after January 1, 2024 (continued in the 2026 Appropriation Act, Item 3-5.19), the claim is limited to $20,000 per taxpayer per taxable year, including carryovers
- The Department may issue no more than $75 million of credits per year
- Unused credit keeps its original carryforward period: 10 years for donations originating on or after January 1, 2007; a transferee may carry the credit forward up to 11 years from the date the credit was originally issued
- Credits originating on or after January 1, 2002 may be transferred to another taxpayer; transfers and pass-through allocations from donations recorded on or after January 1, 2007 are generally subject to a 2 percent transfer fee
## How to compute (SPOUSE and YOU columns)
- Line A: credit authorized or transferred to you or your spouse in the current year
- Line B: carryover from prior years
- Line C: A + B
- Line D: credit transferred to others in the current year
- Line E: C minus D
- Line 18F (you) and Line 18G (spouse): lesser of Line E or the balance of the maximum nonrefundable credit, not over the $20,000 per-taxpayer limit
- Line H: E minus F and/or G carried forward
## Certification (required)
The donor applies to the Department of Taxation (Form LPC-1; transfers on Form LPC-2) at least 90 days before filing the return. For conveyances on or after January 1, 2020, the application must be filed by December 31 of the 2nd year after the year of conveyance (3rd year for conveyances before 2020). Applicants for $1 million or more must obtain verification of conservation value from the Department of Conservation and Recreation; for conveyances on and after January 1, 2017, if that takes over 90 days the deadline may be extended. The Department issues a letter acknowledging the credit amount.
## Related Schedule ADJ subtraction (code 51)
Gain or income recognized on applying a Land Preservation Tax Credit against Virginia tax, to the extent included in and not otherwise subtracted from federal AGI, is subtracted. Where federal gain includes gain or loss on the sale or transfer of the credit, subtract the gain or add back the loss (Schedule ADJ, code 51). The transfer and its use create no gain or loss for transferor or transferee.
## Required Information
- Date of the conveyance or transfer, and the credit amount the Department acknowledged
- Credit held in each spouse's name, and any carryover with its year of origin
- Credits sold or transferred to others in 2026, and any federal gain or loss reported on them
- Ownership share if allocated through a pass-through entity
## Questions
- Did you donate land or an easement, or buy transferred credits, and when?
- What amount did the Department acknowledge, and how much is in each spouse's name?
- Did you sell or transfer any credit, and did you report a federal gain?
## Common Errors
- Claiming more than $20,000 per taxpayer
- Combining spouses' credits in one column
- Missing the application deadline or the 90-day rule
- Forgetting the code 51 subtraction for gain on the credit
## Prompt
- I placed a conservation easement on my land and received Virginia tax credits.
- I bought land preservation credits from a broker.
