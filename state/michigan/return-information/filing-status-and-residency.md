# Filing Status and Residency (Lines 7 and 8)
## Filing status (Line 7) — three choices only
| Federal status | Michigan status |
|---|---|
| Single | Single |
| Married filing jointly | MUST file married filing jointly |
| Married filing separately | Married filing separately OR jointly |
| Head of household | Single |
| Qualifying surviving spouse | Single |
- Anyone treated as married for federal purposes (under Michigan or another state's law) files MFJ or MFS
- MFS: enter the spouse's full name and SSN (Line 3)
- MFS consequences: no Michigan EITC unless federally allowed; homestead and home heating credits must include both spouses' income unless separate homesteads (Form 5049); retirement, standard deduction, 529, and First-Time Home Buyer limits use single amounts
- A joint return cannot be changed to separate returns after the due date
- Year of death: the surviving spouse is still married and may file jointly
## Residency (Line 8) — check all that apply, per spouse
- Resident: Michigan is the permanent home (domicile). Temporary absences (e.g., wintering in another state) do not change residency. Taxed on all income; claims the Line 18 credit for tax paid elsewhere
- Part-year resident: moved domicile into or out of Michigan during the year. Allocate income by the move date; bonuses, severance, and deferred income accrued while a resident are Michigan income; deferred compensation on 1099-R and nonbusiness interest and dividends follow residence when received
- Nonresident: taxed only on Michigan-source income. Out-of-state students living in Michigan for school are nonresidents
- Nonresident military spouse (Military Spouses Residency Relief Act): not a Michigan resident if present only to accompany the servicemember under orders and domiciled elsewhere
- Reciprocal states: IL, IN, KY, MN, OH, WI — wages exempt both ways; business income is not
Part-year residents and nonresidents complete Schedule NR (../income/sourcing/nonresident-part-year.md). Domicile disputes: Form 3799.
## Questions
- What filing status did you use federally?
- Where was your permanent home during 2026, and did it change?
## Common Errors
- Filing head of household on the Michigan return
- Filing separately in Michigan after a joint federal return
- Treating a winter home as a change of residency
