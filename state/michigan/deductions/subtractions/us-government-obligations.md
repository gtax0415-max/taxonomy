# Income from U.S. Government Obligations
## Description
States cannot tax interest on U.S. government obligations, so Michigan subtracts it (Series EE and I bonds, Treasury bills, notes, and bonds), including amounts received through a partnership, S corporation, or other pass-through.
## How it works
- Subtract U.S. obligation interest included in AGI
- Reduce the subtraction by related expenses used to arrive at AGI
- Mutual funds: only the fund's qualifying U.S. government portion (from the fund's annual statement)
- If the subtraction exceeds $5,000, include FEDERAL Schedule B and a statement of amounts, sources, and issuing agencies
- Gains on sale of U.S. obligations are adjusted on MI-1040D, not here
## What does NOT qualify
Interest from GNMA, FNMA, and Freddie Mac pass-through securities generally is not exempt federal obligation interest; confirm by security.
## Questions
- Do you own U.S. savings bonds, Treasury bills, notes, or bonds, or a Treasury fund?
## Common Errors
- Subtracting interest from agency mortgage-backed securities
- Missing Box 3 interest
- Not reducing for related expenses
## Prompt
- Is my Treasury bill interest taxed in Michigan?
