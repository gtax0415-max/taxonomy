# Retirement and Pension Benefits Subtraction (Form 4884)
## Description
Subtraction for qualifying retirement and pension benefits included in federal AGI. PA 4 of 2023 phased out the 2012 age-based limits over 2023-2026; 2026 is the first year of the full deduction.
## 2026 figures
| Parameter | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Private retirement maximum (indexed) | $65,897 single / $131,794 joint | $67,610 single / $135,220 joint |
| Phase-in percentage for those born after 1945 | 75% ($49,423 / $98,846) | 100% ($67,610 / $135,220) |
| Who may use the phase-in deduction | Born after 1945 and before 1967 | Everyone born after 1945, regardless of year |
Form 4884 for 2026 has not been released. The 2025 form's Section D (75% phase-in) should be replaced by the full deduction; confirm the new section structure.
## Rules by group (2026)
1. Born before 1946 (Tier 1): public benefits from federal, Michigan, and Michigan local government are fully exempt. Private benefits (and public benefits from other states, subject to reciprocity) are exempt up to $67,610 / $135,220, reduced by the public benefits subtracted
2. Born after 1945: combined public AND private benefits exempt up to $67,610 / $135,220. Unlike pre-2012 law, federal and Michigan public pensions are now inside this cap (RAB 2026-1, Issue 3)
3. Public safety retirees (subsection 11), any age: public police or fire department employees subject to 1969 PA 312 (including EMS and emergency telephone operators employed directly by the department), state police troopers and sergeants, county-sheriff corrections officers certified under the Local Corrections Officer Training Act, and comparable federal law enforcement retirees. Public benefits fully exempt; private benefits up to the remaining maximum. EDRO recipients of those benefits qualify too. Check Form 4884 Line 6a
4. All maximums are reduced by military retirement, Michigan National Guard retirement, and railroad retirement subtracted on Schedule 1 Line 11
Joint filers use the OLDER spouse's birth year. The provisions are elective: a taxpayer may instead use the older tiered rules or the standard deduction if more favorable.
## Qualifying benefits
Most 1099-R payments included in AGI: defined benefit pensions, IRA distributions, most defined contribution plan payments, 403(b) annuities from 501(c)(3) organizations or public schools, 401(k) amounts attributable to employer contributions or mandatory employee contributions, and senior citizen retirement annuities.
NOT qualifying (taxable at any age): § 457 deferred compensation, 401(k) amounts from employee elective contributions alone, other 403(b) plans, premature distributions paid on separation before the recipient could have retired under the plan, and early-retirement incentives not paid from a pension trust.
## Rollovers
Public benefits rolled into an IRA keep their public character when distributed (Magen v. Department of Treasury). New contributions, private rollovers, and earnings are private. Keep documentation of source, timing, and amount.
## Surviving spouses
A surviving spouse who has not remarried may compute the subtraction using the older deceased spouse's birth year if a joint return was filed for the year of death and a retirement or Social Security subtraction was claimed. Surviving spouses of subsection 11 retirees need not meet those conditions and remain eligible after remarriage.
## Not on this form
Social Security, military retirement, and railroad retirement go on Schedule 1 Lines 11 and 14 (see social-security-military-railroad.md).
## Required Information
- Every 1099-R: payer name and FEIN, distribution code, taxable amount, public or private source, deceased spouse flag
- Birth years of filer and spouse (and deceased spouse, if relevant)
- Public safety service history, if claiming subsection 11
## Questions
- What year were you (and your spouse) born?
- Are your pensions from government (federal, Michigan, other state) or private employers?
- Did you retire from police, fire, state police (trooper or sergeant), or county corrections?
- Do you have military or railroad retirement?
- Are any distributions from a 457 plan or from your own 401(k) deferrals?
## Common Errors
- Using the 2025 75% limit ($49,423 / $98,846) for 2026
- Treating federal or Michigan public pensions as fully exempt for someone born after 1945
- Subtracting § 457 or elective-deferral 401(k) distributions
- Not reducing the maximum by military or railroad retirement
- Not comparing with the standard deduction for those 67 or older
