# Dividend, Interest, and Capital Gains Deduction (born before 1946)
## Description
Taxpayers born before 1946 — or the unremarried surviving spouse of a decedent born before 1946 who died after reaching 65 — may subtract interest, dividends, and capital gains included in AGI up to a cap.
## Figures
| Parameter | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum | $14,688 single or MFS / $29,376 joint | Pending — PROJECTED $15,070 single or MFS / $30,140 joint (2.6% index, the same factor as the official 2026 retirement maximum) |
## How it works
1. Start with the maximum
2. Subtract military and railroad retirement (Line 11), public and private retirement (Line 27), and the amount used for the federal credit for the elderly and disabled (Schedule R Line 19, subtracted on Line 23)
3. Deduction = lesser of the remainder or interest, dividends, and capital gains in AGI
Because the cap is reduced by retirement subtractions, retirees with large pensions often get little or nothing here.
## Questions
- Were you (or your deceased spouse) born before 1946?
- How much interest, dividend, and capital gain income do you have?
## Common Errors
- Forgetting to reduce the cap by the retirement subtraction
- Claiming it for someone born in 1946 or later

- I am 82. Is my interest income taxed in Michigan?
