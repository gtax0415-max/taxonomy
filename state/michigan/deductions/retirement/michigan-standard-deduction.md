# Michigan Standard Deduction for Seniors (Tier 2 and Tier 3)
## Description
A deduction against ALL types of income, available to certain taxpayers age 67 or older, as an alternative to the retirement and pension subtraction. It cannot be combined with Line 27.
## Amounts
| Tier | Who (older spouse on a joint return) | Base amount | Increase for non-Social-Security-covered government retirees |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 2 (Line 25) | Born January 1, 1946 through December 31, 1952, and reached 67 | $20,000 single / $40,000 joint | +$15,000 per box 24C or 24G checked (+$30,000 if both) |
| Tier 3 (Line 26) | Born after 1952 and reached 67 by December 31, 2026 (considered 67 the day before the 67th birthday) | $20,000 single / $40,000 joint | Tier 3 filers retired as of January 1, 2013 from SSA-exempt employment (boxes 24C+24D / 24G+24H) use Tier 2 treatment |
These dollar amounts are statutory and not indexed.
## Reductions
- Both tiers: reduced by military pay (Line 14) and military, Michigan National Guard, and railroad retirement (Line 11)
- Tier 3 also: reduced by the personal and stillbirth exemptions (MI-1040 Lines 9a and 9d)
- Tier 3 and taxable Social Security — BIG 2026 CHANGE: for 2026 through 2028, 2025 PA 24 removes the reduction for taxable Social Security. The taxpayer subtracts Social Security in full AND keeps the full standard deduction (less exemptions). Before 2026 and after 2028, Social Security reduces it
## Example (RAB 2026-1, Example J)
Single, born 1954, 2026 income: $18,000 wages, $5,000 pension, $12,000 Social Security (AGI $35,000). Using the 2026 exemption of $5,900: standard deduction route: $20,000 − $5,900 = $14,100 residual; taxable income = $35,000 − $5,900 − $12,000 − $14,100 = $3,000. Retirement subtraction route: $35,000 − $5,900 − $12,000 − $5,000 = $12,100. (RAB 2026-1 Example J shows the same result using a $5,800 placeholder.) The standard deduction wins; in 2025 the same person was better off with the retirement subtraction.
## Choosing
Compare: (standard deduction + Social Security + exemptions) against (retirement subtraction + Social Security + exemptions). Form 4884's questionnaire "Which Section of Form 4884 Should I Complete?" walks through it. With the 2026 retirement maximum of $67,610 / $135,220, retirees with large pensions will usually prefer Line 27; those with little pension income will prefer the standard deduction.
## Surviving spouse exception
An unremarried surviving spouse of a decedent born before 1946 who died after age 65 may check the box below Line 28 to claim BOTH the standard deduction and the senior investment income deduction.
## Questions
- What year were you and your spouse born, and has the older spouse turned 67?
- How much of your income is pension versus wages, interest, and Social Security?
- Did either of you retire from government work not covered by Social Security?
## Common Errors
- Still reducing the Tier 3 deduction by taxable Social Security in 2026
- Claiming both Line 26 and Line 27
- Using the younger spouse's birth year
## Prompt
- I am 68 and mostly live on Social Security and part-time wages.
