# Retirement and Senior Deductions
## Description
Michigan's rules for retirees depend on birth year, the source of the benefit, and — through 2025 — a phase-in under PA 4 of 2023. For 2026 the phase-in is COMPLETE: every retiree born after 1945 can subtract qualifying public and private retirement benefits up to the full private-retirement maximum.

## 2026 at a glance
| Born | Retirement subtraction (2026) |
|---|---|
| Before 1946 | Public benefits (federal, Michigan, Michigan local): unlimited. Private benefits: up to $67,610 / $135,220, reduced by public benefits subtracted |
| 1946 or later | Combined public and private benefits up to $67,610 single / $135,220 joint (full phase-in) |
| Qualifying public safety retirees (any age) | Public benefits unlimited; private benefits up to the remaining maximum |
All maximums are reduced by military retirement, Michigan National Guard retirement, and railroad retirement subtracted on Line 11. Joint filers use the OLDER spouse's birth year.
## Choosing
Taxpayers eligible for the standard deduction (Tier 2 or Tier 3) choose between it and the retirement subtraction, whichever gives the lower taxable income. The 2026 Social Security change (PA 24) makes the standard deduction the better choice more often for people born after 1952 who are 67 or older.
