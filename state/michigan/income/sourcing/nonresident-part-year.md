# Nonresident and Part-Year Resident Schedule (Schedule NR)
## Description
Nonresidents and part-year residents pay Michigan tax only on income earned in or attributable to Michigan. Schedule NR splits each income line into Column A (total, as on the federal return), Column B (Michigan), and Column C (other states), and prorates the exemption allowance.
## Line guide
| Line | Income | Column B (Michigan) rule |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Wages, salaries, tips | Part-year: all wages while a resident plus Michigan wages while a nonresident. Nonresidents: wages earned in Michigan. Nonqualified stock options earned in Michigan are Michigan income |
| 6 | Interest and dividends | Nonbusiness: received while a resident. Business: attributable to Michigan |
| 7 | Business and farm (Schedules C, F) | Attributable to Michigan (MI-1040H if apportioned) |
| 8 | Gains/losses (Schedule D, Form 4797) | All-Michigan: enter the gain. Split: MI-1040D Line 12/13 Column G and MI-4797 Line 18b Column E |
| 9 | Schedule E | Nonbusiness rents and royalties from Michigan property; business income attributable to Michigan |
| 10 | Pensions, IRAs, annuities, taxable Social Security | Received while a resident |
| 11 | Other income | Received while a resident; nonresidents include Michigan lottery winnings (after 1996) and Michigan casino/racetrack winnings. The federal NOL deduction goes entirely in Column C |
| 13 | Adjustments (federal Schedule 1) | IRA/SEP/SIMPLE: ratio of Michigan wages or SE income; SE tax deduction: ratio of Michigan SE income (enter on Schedule 1 Line 2); SE health insurance and early-withdrawal penalty: state of residence when paid |
| 14 | Line 12 minus Line 13 | Column A must equal MI-1040 Line 10 |
## Exemption proration (Lines 15-19)
Exemption allowance × (Michigan income ÷ total income), capped at 100%. If one spouse was a full-year resident and the other was not, use Worksheet 6 in the MI-1040 booklet instead. For 2026 the base exemption is $5,900 per exemption.
## Special rules
- Reciprocal states: Illinois, Indiana, Kentucky, Minnesota, Ohio, and Wisconsin residents put Michigan wages in Column C
- Qualifying nonresident military spouses put Michigan wages in Column C
- Part-year Renaissance Zone residents also use Schedule NR (Column C = zone income → Schedule 1 Line 15)
- Out-of-state losses cannot reduce Michigan taxable income
- Complete MI-461 first if a federal Form 461 limit applies; report each item only once
- 2026 tips and overtime: nonresidents may deduct only tips and overtime for services performed in Michigan
## Questions
- What dates were you a Michigan resident?
- Which state do you live in (reciprocal states are exempt on wages)?
- Did you earn wages in Michigan while living elsewhere?
- Do you own Michigan rental property or a Michigan business?
## Common Errors
- Column A not matching federal AGI
- Reciprocal-state residents reporting Michigan wages in Column B
- Putting the federal NOL in Column B or on Schedule 1 Line 7
- Not prorating the exemption
## Prompt
- I moved to Michigan partway through the year.
- I live in Ohio and work in Michigan.
