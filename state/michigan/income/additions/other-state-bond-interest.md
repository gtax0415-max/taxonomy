# Interest and Dividends from Other States' Obligations
## Description
Interest on bonds issued by states OTHER than Michigan, and their cities, counties, and authorities, is exempt from federal tax but taxable in Michigan. Michigan adds it back. Interest on Michigan's own municipal bonds stays exempt.
## Who reports what
- Residents: nonbusiness gross interest, dividends, and income from obligations or securities of other states and their political subdivisions
- Residents AND nonresidents: non-Michigan municipal BUSINESS income from a partnership, S corporation, estate, or trust with Michigan business activity. If that business income is subject to apportionment, it is included on MI-1040H
- The addition may be reduced by related expenses that IRC § 265(a)(1) did not allow as a federal deduction
## Mutual funds
Exempt-interest dividends from a national municipal fund are partly Michigan-sourced and partly other-state. Only the non-Michigan portion is added. Funds usually publish a year-end state percentage table.
## Required Information
- Tax-exempt interest and exempt-interest dividends, split by issuing state
- K-1 notes for pass-through municipal income
## Questions
- Do you own municipal bonds or a municipal bond fund?
- Are the bonds from Michigan or another state?
## Common Errors
- Adding back Michigan municipal interest
- Adding back the whole national-fund dividend instead of the non-Michigan share
- Missing municipal income reported on a K-1
## Prompt
- My muni bond interest is tax-free federally. Is it taxable in Michigan?
