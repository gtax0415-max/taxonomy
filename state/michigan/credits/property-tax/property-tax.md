# Property Tax Credits (Michigan)
## Description
Michigan's "circuit breaker" credits. They refund part of the property tax (or rent) a household pays when that tax is high relative to the household's total income. All are refundable.
## Credits in this folder
- Homestead Property Tax Credit (homestead-property-tax-credit.md) — MI-1040CR → MI-1040, Line 26. Refundable. The main credit for most homeowners and renters
- Homestead Property Tax Credit for Veterans and Blind People (homestead-property-tax-credit-veterans-blind.md) — MI-1040CR-2 → MI-1040, Line 26. Refundable. An ALTERNATIVE calculation; compute both and file the larger
- Farmland Preservation Tax Credit (farmland-preservation-tax-credit.md) — MI-1040CR-5 + Schedule CR-5 → MI-1040, Line 27. Refundable. For land under a PA 116 Farmland Development Rights Agreement
## Scale
During the 2025 filing season nearly 1.1 million claimants received the Homestead Property Tax Credit, about $900 million in total, averaging $820 (Michigan Treasury, March 2026).
## How the three fit together
- A claimant files only ONE homestead claim: MI-1040CR or MI-1040CR-2, whichever gives the larger credit (Which Credit Do I Claim worksheet in the CR-2 booklet)
- Farmers should calculate the homestead credit on MI-1040CR
- A Farmland Preservation claim REQUIRES a completed MI-1040CR, MI-1040CR-2, or MI-1040CR-7 even when the filer does not qualify for that credit, because it supplies total household resources

## Questions
- Do you own or rent your Michigan home?
- Were you a Michigan resident at least six months of the year?
- Are you blind, a veteran, or a veteran's surviving spouse?
- Do you have farmland enrolled in a Farmland Development Rights Agreement?
