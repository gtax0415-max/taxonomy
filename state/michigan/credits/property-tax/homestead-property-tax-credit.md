# Homestead Property Tax Credit (MI-1040CR)
## Description
Refundable credit for Michigan homeowners and renters whose property taxes (or the 23% of rent treated as property tax) are high relative to total household resources. It can be filed WITH an MI-1040 or on its own if the claimant has no filing requirement.
## How it works
1. Property taxes levied on the homestead in the year (or 23% of rent) go on Line 10 / Line 12
2. Total household resources (THR) are built on Lines 14-33
3. 3.2% of THR is NOT refundable (Line 34). The excess of taxes over that amount is the base
4. The base is multiplied by a claimant percentage: 60% for general claimants; up to 100% for seniors (Table A) and for deaf, blind, disabled claimants
5. The result is capped at the maximum credit and phased out above the THR threshold (Table B)

## 2025 non-refundable percentage for "other claimants" (Table 2)
Seniors and deaf, blind, paraplegic, hemiplegic, quadriplegic, or totally and permanently disabled claimants: 0% of THR at $3,000 or less, rising 1% per $1,000 to 3.2% above $6,000. General claimants use 3.2% at every income.
## Total household resources (THR)
THR is AGI, EXCLUDING net business and farm losses, net rent and royalty losses, and NOL carryovers, PLUS all nontaxable income. Losses are floored at zero by category (Lines 16 and 17). THR must be sufficient to pay living expenses; Treasury may ask how a household lived on a very low reported THR.
INCLUDED (not all obvious): Social Security and SSI net of Medicare premiums, child support, unemployment, workers' comp, VA disability, MDHHS/FIP payments, gifts and support over $300, gains on sale of a residence even if federally excluded, inheritances (other than from a spouse), life insurance proceeds (other than from a spouse), forgiven debt even if excluded from AGI, Roth distributions in excess of contributions, the Farmland Preservation Tax Credit received in the year.
EXCLUDED: NOL deductions, food assistance, energy assistance grants, state and city income tax refunds and prior homestead credits, the first $300 of gifts and of gambling or prizes, loan proceeds, employer-paid insurance premiums, government payments made directly to third parties.
REDUCTIONS: Schedule 1 adjustments (Line 30) and health, dental, vision, prescription and medical-portion auto insurance premiums paid (Line 31).
## Federal interaction: the Premium Tax Credit
Line 31 premiums must be REDUCED by the federal premium tax credit: the annual premium from FEDERAL Form 8962 (Line 11(a), or the sum of Lines 12(a)-23(a)) less the premium tax credit on Form 8962 Line 24. For 2026 the federal enhanced premium tax credit has expired, so Marketplace enrollees will generally have a SMALLER federal credit, a LARGER net premium deduction, and a LOWER THR here. See federal/credits/health-insurance/premium-tax-credit.md.
## Eligibility
- Homestead is in Michigan and the claimant was a Michigan resident at least six months of the year
- Claimant owns and occupies the homestead with property taxes levied in the year, OR rents it under a lease or rental contract
- Must be a natural person (not a trust, LLC, or S corporation). Claims cannot be filed on behalf of minor children
- Homeowner's taxable value at or below the cap; renters are not subject to the cap
- THR at or below the ceiling
- Not eligible if 100% of THR came from MDHHS; partial MDHHS/FIP recipients prorate using Worksheet 4
- Housing exempt from property tax with no service fee paid (including most university housing) does not qualify
## Filing status rules
- A joint FEDERAL return requires a joint property tax credit
- Federal head of household or qualifying surviving spouse files the credit as single
- Married filing separately: include both spouses' THR unless separate homesteads were maintained; file Form 5049
## Special situations
- Moved, bought, or sold during the year: prorate taxes by days of occupancy (Part 3)
- Part-year residents: include income received while a resident; annualize THR to test the phase-out
- Deceased claimant: prorate taxes and the maximum credit to the date of death; include MI-1310 when filed by a representative
- Separated or divorced: prorate taxes and THR for the shared period; Form 5049 and Form 2105
- Owner-occupied rental or duplex: two computations, use the lower
- Home office: exclude the business portion of taxes or rent (include federal Form 8829)
- Adult care and assisted living: claim rent if itemized, otherwise the resident's share of the facility's taxes
- Seniors who rent: Alternate credit (Worksheet 5) if rent exceeds 40% of THR
- Farmers: special rules for including unoccupied farmland taxes; the taxable value cap does not apply to that farmland
## Notes
- Deadline: file with the 2026 MI-1040 by April 15, 2027. The last day to claim a 2026 credit is four years from the original due date (April 15, 2031)
- Amend by filing a new MI-1040CR with the Amended Return box checked; do NOT file a new MI-1040 or Schedule AMD
- Delinquent prior-year taxes, penalties, interest, association dues, most special assessments, and taxes on second homes are not eligible
- The business portion of the credit for farmers is federal business income
## Required Information
- Property tax statement(s): taxable value and 2026 taxes levied, or lease and total rent paid (landlord name and address)
- All income for both spouses, taxable and nontaxable (FEDERAL Form 1040 and schedules, W-2, 1099-R, SSA-1099, MDHHS Client Annual Statement DHS-1241)
- Health insurance premiums paid and FEDERAL Form 8962 if Marketplace coverage
- Age (65+ by December 31) and disability status
- Dates of residency and occupancy if moved or part-year
## Questions
- Do you own or rent your Michigan home, and for how many months?
- What is the taxable value on your property tax bill (homeowners)?
- What was your total household income from all sources, including nontaxable?
- Are you 65 or older, or deaf, blind, or disabled?
- Did you receive FIP or other MDHHS assistance?
- Did you buy Marketplace health insurance (Form 1095-A / Form 8962)?
## Common Errors
- Leaving out nontaxable income from THR
- Reporting two years of property taxes, or including delinquent taxes
- Omitting the taxable value or school district code
- Forgetting to reduce health insurance premiums by the federal premium tax credit
- Including special assessments that do not qualify
- Filing only one page of the three-page claim
- Using unofficial "2026" limits before Treasury publishes them
## Prompt
- Can I get a refund for my property taxes?
- I rent in Michigan. Is there a credit for that?
