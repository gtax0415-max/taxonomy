# Farmland Preservation Tax Credit (MI-1040CR-5)
## Description
Refundable credit under the Farmland and Open Space Preservation Act (PA 116 of 1974). Farmland owners who agree NOT to develop their land through an FDRA with the Michigan Department of Agriculture and Rural Development (MDARD) get back property tax on that land that exceeds 3.5% of household income.
## How it works
1. Schedule CR-5 lists each agreement: taxable value, whether taxes are paid, ownership type, ownership percentage, and the claimant's share of eligible taxes
2. MI-1040CR-5 Part 1 compares those taxes to household income
3. Household income = THR (from MI-1040CR, CR-2, or CR-7) PLUS a loss adjustment for net business/farm losses, net rent and royalty losses, and an allowed NOL (Parts 2-4). Unlike THR, household income ALLOWS these losses, which increases the credit
4. The credit is the eligible FDRA property taxes in excess of 3.5% of household income
5. If the homestead property tax credit already covers part of the same taxes, Lines 17-20 coordinate the two so taxes are not double-credited (if Line 17 is less than Line 7, use Line 15; otherwise complete Lines 18-20)
## 2026 figures
| Parameter | 2026 |
|---|---|
| Threshold | 3.5% of household income (statutory; not indexed) |
| Maximum credit | None stated — limited only by eligible taxes |
| New agreement approval deadline | Approved by local government by November 1, 2026 to claim for 2026 (2025 booklet rule rolled forward; confirm in 2026 booklet) |
| Recorded FDRA must reach MDARD by | April 15, 2027, or file without it and amend later |
## Eligible taxes
- Property taxes LEVIED in 2026 on enrolled land, regardless of when paid; the 1% collection fee may be included
- Exclude special assessments not based on value, penalties, and interest
- Land not eligible for the principal residence or qualified agricultural exemption generally does not qualify (exception: rental land where the tenant farms at least 1,040 hours a year)
- Where only part of the land qualifies: exclude school operating tax, then multiply by the exemption percentage (e.g., $2,000 − $350 = $1,650 × 60% = $990 claimable)
- Statements covering land outside the agreement or multiple agreements need an assessor breakdown on official letterhead
## Ownership and allocation
| Ownership | Column D code | Allocation |
|---|---|---|
| Individual or with spouse | I | 100% |
| Joint owners (not spouses) | J | Per signed statement / Form 5678; otherwise equally. Spouses count as one owner; a dependent owner gets no share |
| Partnership / LLC | P | Partner's income or ownership percent from FEDERAL Schedule K-1 (or Form 5678 if no Form 1065) |
| S corporation | S | Stock ownership percent from FEDERAL Form 1120S Schedule K-1 |
| Life estate | — | Life tenant may claim all taxes, or split per written agreement |
| Grantor trust / spousal income trust | — | Grantor or surviving spouse claims |
Land owned by an estate, a C corporation, or certain S corporations that held the agreement before 1989 is claimed by the entity on a business return, not on the MI-1040, and is outside this individual taxonomy.
## Federal interaction
- The Farmland credit received in 2026 must be included in FEDERAL AGI (and in 2026 THR on the homestead claim)
- The business portion of the Homestead Property Tax Credit (taxable value of farmland and buildings ÷ total taxable value × credit) is also federal business income
- If a prior-year Michigan refund exceeded the prior-year Farmland credit plus the business portion of the homestead credit, the excess refund included in federal AGI may be subtracted on Michigan Schedule 1, Line 16
- NOL: the NOL deduction allowed in household income is the lesser of the federal NOL deduction or Federal Modified Taxable Income (Part 4). A Michigan NOL cannot be used
## Eligibility
- Owns farmland covered by an FDRA or PDR with MDARD
- Ownership on the tax statements matches ownership on the agreement
- Farm purchased during the year: credit only after MDARD completes the transfer of the agreement; prorate taxes to the ownership period
- Bankruptcy: prorate to the petition date; the trustee or debtor in possession claims the remainder
## Payment rule
If taxes are not shown as paid (Schedule CR-5 Column C unchecked or no receipt), Treasury issues the credit as a check payable JOINTLY to the claimant and the county treasurer. Falsely certifying payment makes all future credits jointly payable.
## Required Information
- Current agreement numbers (county code, contract number, six-digit expiration date; enter the year as four digits; PDR expiration is 9999)
- Taxable value and taxes for each agreement (entire taxable value even if only a share is claimed)
- Proof of 2025 or 2026 tax payment
- Completed MI-1040CR, CR-2, or CR-7 with THR, even if not eligible for that credit
- FEDERAL returns and schedules showing farm income and expenses (Schedule F, K-1s, Forms 4797, 4835)
- Signed distribution statement (Form 5678) for joint owners
## Questions
- Is your farmland enrolled in a Farmland Development Rights Agreement or PDR, and what is the agreement number?
- Has the agreement expired or been extended?
- How is the land owned: individually, jointly, partnership, LLC, or S corporation?
- Were the property taxes paid?
- Did you buy or enroll land this year?
- Did you have business, farm, rental losses, or a federal NOL?
## Common Errors
- Using an expired agreement number
- Listing each parcel instead of totaling by agreement
- Reporting only the claimant's share in Column B (it must be the ENTIRE taxable value)
- Including special assessments, penalties, or interest
- Omitting the homestead / home heating claim that supplies THR
- Not reporting last year's Farmland credit in federal AGI and THR
