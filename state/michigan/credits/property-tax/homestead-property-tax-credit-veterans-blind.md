# Homestead Property Tax Credit for Veterans and Blind People (MI-1040CR-2)
## Description
Refundable ALTERNATIVE to the standard Homestead Property Tax Credit. Instead of subtracting 3.2% of income, it uses a Taxable Value Allowance (TVA). Eligible claimants must compute BOTH MI-1040CR and MI-1040CR-2 and file the one that gives the larger credit (Which Credit Do I Claim worksheet). Farmers should use MI-1040CR.
## How it works
1. Look up the TVA for the claimant's category (table below)
2. Divide the TVA by the homestead's taxable value. The result, capped at 100%, is the percentage of tax relief
3. Multiply that percentage by the property taxes levied
4. Apply the maximum credit and the same THR phase-out as MI-1040CR
Renters first convert rent to a taxable value: (rent × 23%) ÷ non-homestead millage rate. Blind people who RENT cannot use this form; they claim as blind on MI-1040CR.
## Taxable Value Allowance (Table 2) — statutory, same in 2026 unless amended
| Category | Percent of disability | TVA |
|---|---|---|
| A. Blind (if each spouse is blind, $7,000) | — | $3,500 |
| B. Veteran with service-connected disability, or surviving spouse | 10-50% | $3,500 |
| B. same | 60-80% | $4,000 |
| B. same | 90-100% | $4,500 |
| C. Surviving spouse of veteran deceased in service | — | $4,500 |
| D. Active military, pensioned veteran, or surviving spouse | — | $3,500 |
| E. Surviving spouse of non-disabled, non-pensioned veteran of Korean War, WWII, or WWI | — | $2,500 |
Categories D and E are limited to total household resources of $7,500 or less.
## 2026 — status of figures
The THR ceiling, phase-out threshold, homeowner taxable value cap, and maximum credit are shared with MI-1040CR and are NOT YET PUBLISHED for 2026. 2025 values: THR ceiling $71,500; phase-out begins at $62,500 at 10% per $1,000; taxable value cap $165,400; maximum credit $1,900. Projected 2026 (unofficial): ceiling $73,400, phase-out $64,100, taxable value cap $169,700; maximum credit $1,900 until published. See homestead-property-tax-credit.md.
## Worked example (from the 2025 booklet)
Homeowner, 90% disabled veteran, taxable value $15,000, taxes $750: $4,500 ÷ $15,000 = 30%; $750 × 30% = $225 credit.
Renter, pensioned veteran, $395/month, 56 mills: rent $4,740 × 23% = $1,090; $1,090 ÷ 0.056 = $19,464 taxable value; $3,500 ÷ $19,464 = 17.98%; $1,090 × 17.98% = $196 credit.
## Total household resources
Same definition as MI-1040CR (see homestead-property-tax-credit.md), on CR-2 Lines 13-32. Line 30 insurance premiums must be reduced by the FEDERAL premium tax credit from Form 8962 (premiums on Line 11(a) or Lines 12(a)-23(a), less Line 24).
## Eligibility
- Blind and OWN the homestead; or
- Veteran with service-connected disability, or that veteran's surviving spouse; or
- Surviving spouse of a veteran deceased in service; or
- Active military, pensioned veteran, or surviving spouse with THR of $7,500 or less; or
- Surviving spouse of a non-disabled, non-pensioned Korean War, WWII, or WWI veteran with THR of $7,500 or less
- Plus all general homestead rules: Michigan homestead, resident at least six months, owner-occupant or renter under contract
## Notes
- FIP/MDHHS recipients prorate using the FIP/MDHHS Benefits Worksheet
- Moved, deceased, separated, and married-filing-separately rules mirror MI-1040CR (Parts 1-2, Form 5049)
- The veteran's disability percentage comes from the Veterans Administration and goes on Line 7b
## Required Information
- Claimant category and, for disabled veterans, the VA disability percentage
- Taxable value and taxes levied, or rent paid and the non-homestead millage rate
- All household income (THR)
## Questions
- Are you blind, a veteran, active military, or a veteran's surviving spouse?
- What is your VA service-connected disability rating?
- Do you own or rent? (Blind renters must use MI-1040CR)
- Is your total household income $7,500 or less (categories D and E)?
## Common Errors
- Filing only MI-1040CR-2 without comparing to MI-1040CR
- Blind renters using MI-1040CR-2
- Missing the $7,500 THR limit for categories D and E
- Using the homestead millage rate instead of the non-homestead rate for renters
## Prompt
- I am a disabled veteran. Is there a bigger property tax credit for me?
- I am blind and own my home.
