# Credit for Organ Donation Expenses
## Description
One-time NONREFUNDABLE credit for unreimbursed expenses of a LIVE organ donation (e.g., kidney or part of a liver), created by PA 156 of 2024 and first available for 2025.
## Amount (statutory, 2026)
Lesser of unreimbursed qualifying expenses or $10,000. Once per taxpayer (lifetime).
## Timing
May be claimed in the tax year before the donation, the year of the donation, or the year after. A donation in 2026 may be claimed on the 2025, 2026, or 2027 return (only once). Donations on or after January 1, 2025 qualify.
## Qualifying expenses
Unreimbursed and directly related to the donation, incurred by the taxpayer or a qualifying dependent claimed on the return: travel, lodging, lost wages, child care (list is not exhaustive). Amounts reimbursed (e.g., by the recipient's insurance or a donor assistance program) do not count.
## Rules
- Nonrefundable: limited to Michigan tax on Line 17 after the Line 18 and 19 credits; unused credit is lost
- Supporting documentation must be submitted
- Separate from the Organ Donor Registry checkbox on Form 4642, which is for donation after death and has no tax effect
- Federal side: donation-related medical costs may be itemized medical deductions; Michigan does not follow itemized deductions, so this credit is the Michigan benefit
## Questions
- Did you (or a dependent) donate an organ as a living donor, and when?
- What expenses were not reimbursed?
- Have you claimed this credit before?
## Common Errors
- Claiming it more than once
- Including reimbursed amounts
- Expecting a refund of the unused portion
## Prompt
- I donated a kidney to my brother. Is there a Michigan tax break?
