# Michigan Credits — Index
## Description
Credits claimed on the Michigan Individual Income Tax Return (MI-1040). Michigan starts from federal AGI (MI-1040 Line 10), so several Michigan credits pull numbers directly from federal forms (Form 1040, Schedule EIC, Form 8962, Schedule K-1). Where a source document is federal, the file says so.
## Credit line map (MI-1040)
| MI-1040 line | Credit | Refundable | Supporting form | File |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18a / 18b | Income tax imposed by government units outside Michigan | No | Copy of other return; Form 777 (Canadian province) | other-nonrefundable/ |
| 19a / 19b | Historic Preservation Tax Credit | No | Form 5803 (plans approved after 2020) or Form 3581 (plans approved before 2012) | historic-preservation/ |
| 20a / 20b | Credit for organ donation expenses (one-time, up to $10,000) | No | Donation documentation | other-nonrefundable/ |
| 26 | Homestead Property Tax Credit | Yes | MI-1040CR or MI-1040CR-2 | property-tax/ |
| 27 | Farmland Preservation Tax Credit | Yes | MI-1040CR-5 + Schedule CR-5 | property-tax/ |
| 28a / 28b | Michigan Earned Income Tax Credit (30% of federal) | Yes | FEDERAL Schedule EIC → Form 1040 Line 27 | income-based/ |
| 29 | Historic Preservation Tax Credit (refundable portion, legacy only) | Yes | Form 3581 | historic-preservation/ |
| 30 | Credit for allocated share of tax paid by an electing flow-through entity | Yes | Form 6072 (Schedule FTE) + Form 6074 | business-flow-through/ |
| — (separate claim) | Home Heating Credit | Yes | MI-1040CR-7 + Form 4976 | energy-assistance/ |
Line numbers are from the 2025 MI-1040 (Rev. 06-25). Michigan has not yet released the 2026 MI-1040; confirm line numbers when it is published (typically December).
## Structure
Nonrefundable credits (Lines 18-20) reduce tax on Line 17 down to zero; any excess is lost or carried forward. Refundable credits (Lines 26-30) are added to payments on Line 34 and can produce a refund with zero tax owed.

## Common cross-credit rules
- Total household resources (THR) is computed ONCE, on MI-1040CR, MI-1040CR-2, or MI-1040CR-7, and reused by the Farmland Preservation Tax Credit
- The Homestead Property Tax Credit and Farmland Preservation Tax Credit received in the year are taxable income for FEDERAL purposes to the extent business-related, and the Farmland credit counts in THR the following year
- Married couples filing a joint FEDERAL return must file a joint Michigan property tax credit claim
