# Refund for a Deceased Taxpayer (MI-1310)
## Description
Required, and must be signed, before Treasury issues a refund or credit (including Homestead Property Tax and Home Heating credits) claimed for a deceased taxpayer by someone OTHER than a surviving spouse filing jointly.
## Who files
- Surviving spouse filing a JOINT return: do NOT file MI-1310. Write "DECD" after the decedent's name, sign "filing as surviving spouse", and enter the date of death
- Court-appointed personal representative: include the court certificate (Letters of Authority pages 1 and 2). Not the will, a power of attorney, or conservator/guardianship papers
- Other claimant (no probate): certify the refund will be distributed under the law of the decedent's state of residence and include a death certificate. If someone will petition the court, no refund is issued until the appointment is shown
## Filing details
Use the claimant's address, not the decedent's. For the MI-1040 enter the date of death in the Deceased Taxpayer box (2026 returns: deaths after December 31, 2025).
## Related 2026 rules
- Homestead credit for a decedent: prorate taxes and the maximum credit to the date of death; annualize total household resources
- Home Heating Credit: a personal representative may claim the standard credit but not the alternate credit
- Retirement subtraction: surviving spouses may use the older deceased spouse's birth year if the conditions in RAB 2026-1 are met
## Questions
- Are you the surviving spouse filing jointly, the court-appointed representative, or another claimant?
- Has a probate estate been opened?
## Common Errors
- Filing MI-1310 on a joint return with the surviving spouse
- Using Form 151 (power of attorney) for a decedent
- Sending the will instead of Letters of Authority
