# Community Service Contribution Credit
## Description
Credit for contributions to a community service organization whose program the Kansas Department of Commerce has approved (K.S.A. 79-32,194 to 79-32,201). It is one of the few Kansas contribution credits that accepts cash, securities, real property, other property and even services, and the part above the tax is refunded.

## Amount
- 50% of the contribution
- 70% when both the contributor and the organization are in a RURAL community (a city under 15,000 people in a county that is not part of a metropolitan statistical area, with some exceptions)
- Minimum contribution: $250
- Statewide cap: $4.13 million per fiscal year

## What counts as a contribution
- Cash
- Stocks and bonds, valued at market price on the transfer date
- Real estate with clean title, valued at the lower of two independent appraisals
- Other property, valued at the lower of fair market value or the donor's cost (NOT used clothing)
- Services

## Notes
- The contributor's own credit above the tax is refunded; credits bought from another taxpayer are not refundable and are carried forward
- Any part of the contribution deducted federally must be added back on Schedule S (other additions)
- A non-itemizer's gift of shares, property or services gets no federal deduction, so there is usually nothing to add back

## Required Information
- Organization name and approved credit certificate
- Date, type and value of the contribution
- Whether donor and organization are both rural
- Any amount deducted federally

## Questions
- Did you give to a Kansas organization with an approved community service credit program?
- Was the gift cash, shares, property, real estate or services?
- Are you and the organization both in a rural community?
- Did you deduct the gift on your federal return?

## Common Errors
- Claiming for used clothing
- Valuing property at fair market value when cost is lower
- Missing the add-back for a federally deducted gift
- Using 70% when only one party is rural

## Prompt
- I donated shares to a Kansas nonprofit with a tax credit program.
- I volunteered repair work for a community organization that gave me a K-60 certificate.
