# Child and Dependent Care Credit
## Description
Nonrefundable Kansas credit equal to 50% of the federal child and dependent care credit ALLOWED on Form 2441. Raised from 25% to 50% starting in 2024 (2024 Special Session SB 1, K.S.A. 79-32,111c). Residents only.

## Amount
- Kansas credit = federal credit allowed (Form 2441, line 11) × 50%
- Uses the federal credit AFTER the federal tax-liability limit, so a filer with no federal tax and no federal credit gets no Kansas credit
- Unchanged Kansas rate for 2026

## 2026 federal changes that raise the Kansas credit
OBBBA increased the federal credit for 2026:
- Credit rate starts at 50% (was 35%) for AGI of $15,000 or less, then drops 1 point for each $2,000 of AGI above $15,000, but not below 35%
- A second step-down applies only above $75,000 of AGI ($150,000 joint): 1 point per $2,000 ($4,000 joint), but not below 20%
- In 2025 the rate fell to 20% once AGI passed $43,000, so families between about $43,000 and $75,000 / $150,000 gain the most
- Expense limits stay $3,000 (one qualifying person) / $6,000 (two or more), reduced by excluded employer dependent care benefits
- Employer dependent care assistance exclusion rises to $7,500 for 2026, which reduces the expenses available for the credit
Because Kansas is 50% of the federal amount, most middle-income families see a larger Kansas credit in 2026.

## Eligibility
- Full-year Kansas resident (not part-year or nonresident)
- Claimed the federal credit for 2026
- Valid SSN for the filer, spouse and each qualifying person

## Required Information
- Federal Form 2441 line 11
- Residency status

## Questions
- Did you claim the federal child and dependent care credit for 2026?
- Were you a Kansas resident all year?

## Common Errors
- Using 25% (the pre-2024 rate)
- Using federal qualifying expenses or the tentative credit instead of the credit allowed
- Claiming it as a part-year resident or nonresident

## Prompt
- I paid for daycare so I could work.
- Does Kansas have a child care credit?
