# Foreign Tax Credit Worksheet
## Description
Kansas treats a foreign country (and its political subdivisions) as a "state" for the credit for taxes paid to other states. Kansas only gives credit for foreign tax NOT already used for the federal foreign tax credit.

## Amount
1. 2026 tax paid to the foreign country
2. Minus the federal foreign tax credit allowed
3. = Kansas foreign tax limitation → enter on line 1 of the Worksheet for Residents (see tax-paid-other-states.md) and finish that worksheet

## Notes
- If foreign tax was taken as a federal ITEMIZED DEDUCTION instead of a credit, NO Kansas credit is allowed
- Most small investors claim the full foreign tax as a federal credit (often without Form 1116), leaving $0 for Kansas
- The foreign income must be included in Kansas AGI
- Enclose federal Form 1116 if one was filed
- "State" for this credit includes U.S. territories and possessions, Puerto Rico and D.C. as well as foreign countries

## Required Information
- Foreign taxes paid in 2026
- Federal foreign tax credit allowed
- Foreign-source income

## Questions
- Did you pay foreign income tax (including on mutual fund dividends)?
- Did you claim a federal foreign tax credit, and was all of it allowed?
- Did you deduct foreign tax on federal Schedule A instead?

## Common Errors
- Claiming Kansas credit for foreign tax already fully credited federally
- Claiming it after deducting the foreign tax federally
- Using the foreign tax total directly on line 13 without the resident worksheet limit

## Prompt
- I paid foreign tax on my international mutual funds.
- I worked abroad and paid tax to another country.
