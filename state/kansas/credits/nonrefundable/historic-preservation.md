# Historic Preservation Credit
## Description
Credit for rehabilitating a qualified historic building in Kansas (K.S.A. 79-32,211). The building can be income-producing OR not, so an owner-occupied historic home qualifies. Unlike the federal rehabilitation credit, which needs an income-producing building, a personal residence earns the Kansas credit.

## Amount
For projects placed in service on or after July 1, 2025:
- 25% in a city over 50,000 people when spending is $5,000 to under $50,000
- 40% in a city over 50,000 when spending is $50,000 or more
- 40% in a city, township or unincorporated area of 50,000 or fewer when spending is $5,000 or more
- 40% for a non-income-producing 501(c)(3) building
- Minimum spending: $5,000; no per-project cap

## Eligibility
- Building listed on the National Register, individually on the Kansas Register, or contributing to a listed district
- Rehabilitation plan approved in advance
- Claimed by the owner

## Notes
- Carries forward until used, but not past the 10th year after the project is placed in service
- Transferable; the buyer can carry it forward five years within that window
- Projects placed in service before July 1, 2025 use the older 25%/30% rates

## Required Information
- Credit certificate and approved plan
- Rehabilitation spending
- City population and listing status

## Questions
- Is your home listed on a historic register or in a historic district?
- Was the rehabilitation plan approved before the work?
- How much did you spend, and when was the work placed in service?
- What is the population of your city?

## Common Errors
- Claiming without an approved plan
- Using the 40% rate in a large city when spending is under $50,000
- Spending under the $5,000 minimum

## Prompt
- We restored the porch and windows on our historic house in Lawrence.
- Our home is on the Kansas Register and we did a major rehab this year.
