# Kansas Tax Rates
## Description
Kansas has two individual income tax rates for 2026, set by K.S.A. 79-32,110 after 2024 Special Session SB 1: 5.20% and 5.58%. The brackets are not indexed for inflation.

## 2026 rates
- Married filing joint: 5.20% on taxable income up to $46,000; 5.58% over $46,000
- Single: 5.20% up to $23,000; 5.58% over $23,000
- Head of household: 5.20% up to $23,000; 5.58% over $23,000
- Married filing separate: 5.20% up to $23,000; 5.58% over $23,000

## No 2026 rate cut
2025 SB 269 cuts rates (toward 4%) only when general fund revenue beats an inflation-adjusted base. KDOR Notice 25-06 (October 2, 2025) found the FY 2025 test NOT met, so 2026 rates are unchanged. The next determination (for 2027) follows FY 2026 results.

## Example
Single, Kansas taxable income $40,000: $23,000 × 5.20% = $1,196 + $17,000 × 5.58% = $948.60 → $2,144.60 (the Tax Table result may differ by a few dollars).

## Notes
- Head of household uses the SINGLE bracket, not the joint one
- Since 2024, there is no zero-tax rule for small taxable incomes
- Lump-sum distribution tax (line 11) and the nonresident percentage (lines 9–10) are applied separately

## Required Information
- Filing status
- Kansas taxable income

## Questions
- What is your filing status?
- What is your Kansas taxable income?

## Common Errors
- Using the joint $46,000 bracket for head of household
- Applying the old three-bracket rates (3.10% / 5.25% / 5.70%)
- Assuming the SB 269 cut took effect for 2026

## Prompt
- What are the Kansas income tax rates for 2026?
- Did Kansas lower its income tax rate for 2026?
