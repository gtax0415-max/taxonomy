# Interest on U.S. Government Obligations
## Description
Federal law bars states from taxing interest on U.S. government obligations. Kansas subtracts this interest (and qualifying fund dividends) when it is included in federal AGI.

## Amount
- Interest or dividends from obligations of the United States and its authorities, commissions, instrumentalities and possessions that are in federal AGI
- Reduce by related expenses directly incurred to buy them (management or trustee fees)
- Mutual funds holding both exempt and taxable obligations: subtract only the part attributable to exempt federal obligations
- Unchanged for 2026

## What qualifies
- U.S. Savings Bonds (Series EE and I), Treasury bills, notes and bonds
- Federal Land Bank and other qualifying federal agency obligations

## What does NOT qualify (taxable in Kansas)
- Fannie Mae (FNMA), Ginnie Mae (GNMA), Freddie Mac (FHLMC)
- Interest on a federal TAX refund

## Notes
- Savings bond interest excluded federally for education is not in federal AGI and cannot be subtracted again
- Keep a schedule naming each obligation; KDOR may request it

## Required Information
- 1099-INT box 3 amounts
- Fund percentages from U.S. government obligations
- Related expenses

## Questions
- Did you cash savings bonds or earn interest on Treasury securities in 2026?
- Do your mutual funds report a U.S. government obligation percentage?

## Common Errors
- Subtracting GNMA, FNMA or FHLMC interest
- Subtracting the whole fund dividend instead of the government share
- Forgetting to reduce by related expenses

## Prompt
- I own Treasury bills. Are they taxed in Kansas?
- I cashed in savings bonds this year.
