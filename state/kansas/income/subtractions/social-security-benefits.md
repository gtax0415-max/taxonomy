# Social Security Benefits
## Description
Kansas fully exempts Social Security benefits. Whatever part of the benefits is taxable federally (and so is inside federal AGI) is subtracted. Since 2024 there is NO income limit (2024 Special Session SB 1 removed the former $75,000 federal AGI cap).

## Amount
- Enter the TAXABLE Social Security included in federal AGI (Form 1040, line 6b), not the gross benefit
- Includes SSI and Social Security disability benefits to the extent taxed federally
- No entry if none of the benefit is taxable federally
- No cap, no phase-out. Unchanged for 2026

## Notes
- The new federal $6,000 senior deduction (OBBBA, 2025–2028) is separate and does not affect this subtraction or reach Kansas
- Railroad Retirement tier 1 benefits taxed like Social Security: the tier 1 Social Security equivalent can be subtracted here, or all Railroad Retirement benefits as exempt retirement (A14) — do not subtract the same dollars on both lines
- A lump-sum back payment taxable federally in 2026 is subtracted in full for the amount included

## Required Information
- SSA-1099 for filer and spouse
- Taxable amount on the federal return

## Questions
- Do you or your spouse receive Social Security or SSDI?
- Was any of it taxable on your 2026 federal return?

## Common Errors
- Subtracting the gross benefit instead of the taxable amount
- Applying the old $75,000 AGI limit
- Subtracting Railroad Retirement on both A10 and A14

## Prompt
- Is my Social Security taxed in Kansas?
- My income is over $75,000. Can I still exclude Social Security in Kansas?
