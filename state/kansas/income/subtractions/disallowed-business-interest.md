# Disallowed Business Interest Deduction
## Description
Kansas does not follow the IRC §163(j) business interest limit. Interest paid or accrued in 2026 that §163(j) DISALLOWS federally is subtracted in Kansas in the year paid. When the federal carryforward is later used, Kansas adds it back (see income/additions/business-interest-carryforward.md).

## Amount
- Interest paid or accrued in 2026 and disallowed for 2026 under §163(j)
- Interest counts as paid or accrued only in the first year it would have been deductible without §163(j)
- For tax years after 2020; unchanged for 2026

## 2026 federal context
- OBBBA restored the EBITDA-based adjusted taxable income limit for tax years after 2024, so less interest is disallowed federally in 2026 than in 2022–2024
- Small businesses meeting the §448(c) gross-receipts test are exempt from §163(j)

## Notes
- Keep a year-by-year record of amounts subtracted, so the matching add-back is correct when the carryforward is released
- Partners: excess business interest is allocated on the K-1 and tracked by the partner

## Required Information
- Disallowed interest on the 2026 Form 8990 (or K-1)
- Year each amount was paid

## Questions
- Did your business interest deduction get limited on your 2026 federal return?
- Do you receive excess business interest expense on a K-1?

## Common Errors
- Subtracting the cumulative carryforward instead of only the 2026 disallowed amount
- Not tracking the amount for the later add-back
- Subtracting interest that was fully deductible federally

## Prompt
- My business interest was limited under 163(j) this year.
- My partnership K-1 shows excess business interest expense.
