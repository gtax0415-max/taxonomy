# 529 Plan Contributions
## Description
Subtraction for contributions to a qualified tuition program (IRC §529), whether Kansas's own plan or ANOTHER state's plan. Federal law gives no deduction for 529 contributions; Kansas does.

## Amount
- Up to $3,000 per beneficiary, or $6,000 per beneficiary for married filing joint
- Limit applies per beneficiary, so contributions for several children each get the limit
- Unchanged for 2026 (K.S.A. 79-32,117)

## Timing election
Contributions made from January 1, 2027 until the 2026 return's due date may be applied to 2026 if the election is made when filing. A contribution can be used in only ONE tax year.

## Notes
- Rollovers between plans are not new contributions
- A nonqualified withdrawal later is an ADDITION (see other-additions.md)
- OBBBA broadens federal qualified uses for 2026 (K-12 expenses up to $20,000, more K-12 and credential costs); the Kansas subtraction amount itself is unchanged
- A refund can be direct deposited into a Kansas 529 account

## Required Information
- Each contribution's date, amount and beneficiary
- Whether early-2027 contributions are elected for 2026

## Questions
- Did you contribute to a 529 plan in 2026, in any state?
- How many beneficiaries?
- Did you contribute between January 1 and April 15, 2027 and want it counted for 2026?

## Common Errors
- Applying the limit per account holder instead of per beneficiary
- Counting a 2027 contribution in both 2026 and 2027
- Subtracting rollovers

## Prompt
- I put money in my kids' 529 plans.
- Do I get a Kansas deduction for an out-of-state 529?
