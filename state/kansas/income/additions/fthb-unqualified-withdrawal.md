# Unqualified First Time Home Buyer Account Withdrawals
## Description
Addition that recaptures the Kansas subtraction previously taken for a first-time home buyer savings account when the money is NOT used as the law allows. Contributions (up to $3,000, or $6,000 married filing joint) and account earnings are subtracted when deposited or earned; a nonqualifying withdrawal reverses that benefit.

## Amount
Add back the contributions to, and earnings from, the account to the extent that:
- Distributions were not used for the expenses or transactions authorized by K.S.A. 58-4904, or
- The funds were not held for the minimum time K.S.A. 58-4904 requires, or
- The account holder did not designate a surviving payable-on-death beneficiary (K.S.A. 58-4904(e))

Applies to tax years beginning after December 31, 2021; unchanged for 2026.

## Notes
- Only amounts that produced a Kansas subtraction in some year need to come back
- A qualified withdrawal for an eligible first-time home purchase is not added back
- This is a Kansas-only account; there is no federal counterpart, so federal forms do not report it

## Required Information
- Total contributions and earnings subtracted in prior years
- Amount, date and use of each 2026 withdrawal
- Date the account was opened and funds deposited
- Beneficiary designation

## Questions
- Do you have a Kansas first-time home buyer savings account?
- Did you withdraw money in 2026, and what was it used for?
- Did you buy a qualifying first home with it?
- Is a payable-on-death beneficiary named on the account?

## Common Errors
- Not adding back a withdrawal used for something other than an eligible home purchase
- Adding back amounts that were never subtracted for Kansas
- Overlooking the beneficiary-designation rule

## Prompt
- I took money out of my Kansas first-time home buyer account but did not buy a house.
- I closed my first-time home buyer savings account this year.
