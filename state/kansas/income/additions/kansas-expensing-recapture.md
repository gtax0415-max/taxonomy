# Kansas Expensing Recapture
## Description
Addition that takes back part of a Kansas expensing deduction claimed in an earlier year when the asset is sold, disposed of, or moved out of Kansas before its federal recovery period ends. The Kansas expensing deduction (K.S.A. 79-32,143a) is available again to all income tax filers for tax years after 2020.

## Amount
Recapture = Kansas expensing previously allowed × (years remaining in the recovery period ÷ total recovery period)
- Years remaining = recovery period − (2026 − year placed in service), counting the year of disposition
- If the recovery period has already ended, there is NO recapture
- Net the 2026 recapture against any new 2026 expensing:
  - Recapture larger than new expensing → addition on Schedule S, line A3
  - New expensing larger → subtraction on the Kansas expensing deduction line instead
- Partners and shareholders multiply by their ownership percentage

## Example
Asset with a 5-year recovery period placed in service in 2024; Kansas expensing claimed then was $1,160. It is sold in 2026. Years remaining = 5 − (2026 − 2024) = 3. Recapture = $1,160 × 3/5 = $696 added back for 2026.

## What qualified for the original deduction
- MACRS (IRC §168) tangible property with a recovery period of 25 years or less, placed in service in Kansas
- Off-the-shelf computer software (IRC §197(e)(3))
- NOT residential rental or nonresidential real property

## Notes
- The original deduction was based on depreciable basis minus federal bonus depreciation and §179, times a statutory factor; recapture uses that previously allowed amount
- An asset used for the expensing deduction cannot also support certain Kansas credits (e.g., R&D, HPIP)
- No 2026 change to the statute was found; OBBBA's restored 100% federal bonus depreciation (property acquired after January 19, 2025) shrinks the net basis for NEW Kansas expensing but does not change recapture of earlier deductions

## Required Information
- Year placed in service, recovery period and method for each expensed asset
- Kansas expensing claimed for that asset in the earlier year
- Date the asset was sold, disposed of, or moved out of Kansas
- Ownership percentage for pass-through assets

## Questions
- Did you claim a Kansas expensing deduction in an earlier year?
- Did you sell, dispose of, or move any of those assets out of Kansas in 2026?
- Has the asset's recovery period already ended?
- Do you receive expensing information from a partnership or S corporation?

## Common Errors
- Forgetting recapture when an expensed asset is sold
- Recapturing after the recovery period has ended
- Reporting the gross recapture without netting new 2026 expensing
- Ignoring the ownership percentage for pass-through property

## Prompt
- I sold equipment I expensed on my Kansas return two years ago.
- I moved my business truck out of Kansas this year.
