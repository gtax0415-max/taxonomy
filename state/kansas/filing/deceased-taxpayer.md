# Deceased Taxpayer or Spouse
## Description
A final Kansas return must be filed for a taxpayer who died during 2026. Mark the box on page 1 if the taxpayer, or the spouse on a joint return, died during the tax year, and include the decedent's SSN in the heading.

## Rules
- A surviving spouse who files a joint FEDERAL return must also file a joint Kansas return
- Signature: the executor or administrator signs; on a joint return filed by the surviving spouse, write "Deceased" and the date of death on the decedent's signature line
- Refund documentation:
  - Surviving spouse, refund of $100 or less: enclose ONE of federal Form 1310, death certificate, obituary, funeral home notice, letters testamentary, or the Kansas decedent refund claim
  - Surviving spouse, refund over $100, or ANY refund claimed by someone other than the surviving spouse: enclose proof of death AND the Kansas decedent refund claim (Form RF-9)

## Notes
- Income, deductions and exemptions follow the federal final return; a full-year exemption is allowed for the decedent
- The surviving spouse may keep joint status for 2026 if not remarried before year-end
- Later years: a qualifying surviving spouse files as head of household for Kansas (see qualifying-surviving-spouse.md)

## Required Information
- Date of death
- Name and role of the person signing
- Proof of death and appointment documents
- Whether a refund is expected and its amount

## Questions
- Did you or your spouse die during 2026?
- Who is filing: the surviving spouse or a personal representative?
- Is a refund expected, and is it more than $100?

## Common Errors
- Missing the deceased box or the decedent's SSN
- Sending a refund claim over $100 without the decedent refund claim form
- A surviving spouse filing separately in Kansas after filing jointly federally

## Prompt
- My husband died this year. How do I file our Kansas return?
- I am the executor for my mother's final tax return.
