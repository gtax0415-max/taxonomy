# Married Filing Separate
## Description
Allowed in Kansas only if the spouses filed separately for federal purposes. Each spouse files their own K-40 reporting only their own income and deductions.

## 2026 amounts
- Exemption: $9,160 each
- Standard deduction: $4,120 each (65-or-older or blind boxes: $4,820 / $5,520 / $6,220 / $6,920)
- Brackets: 5.20% up to $23,000, 5.58% above

## Rules
- If one spouse itemizes on Kansas Schedule A, the other MUST also itemize
- The status cannot be changed from joint to separate after the return's due date
- Spouses who filed jointly federally must file jointly in Kansas

## Notes
- The federal SALT cap for separate filers ($20,200 in 2026) does not apply to Kansas property taxes
- Kansas credits figured as a percentage of a federal credit (EITC, child and dependent care) follow whatever the separate federal return allowed

## Required Information
- Spouse's name, SSN and deduction method
- Each spouse's own income and withholding

## Questions
- Did you file separately on your federal return?
- Is your spouse itemizing on their Kansas return?
- Do you need to keep your income separate for a specific reason?

## Common Errors
- One spouse taking the standard deduction while the other itemizes
- Filing separately in Kansas after filing jointly federally
- Splitting joint items incorrectly

## Prompt
- We file separately. Can I take the Kansas standard deduction if my husband itemizes?
- What is the Kansas standard deduction for married filing separately?
