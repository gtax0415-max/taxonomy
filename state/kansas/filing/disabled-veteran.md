# Disabled Veteran Exemption
## Description
An additional $2,320 Kansas exemption for each honorably discharged veteran certified by the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs to receive disability compensation at the 100% rate. The amount rose from $2,250 to $2,320 for tax years 2025 and later (2025 HB 2231; KDOR Notice 25-07); unchanged for 2026.

## Eligibility
- Honorably discharged from active service in any branch of the U.S. armed forces
- Certified by the VA as receiving disability compensation at the 100% rate
- The disability is PERMANENT and was sustained through military action or accident, or resulted from disease contracted in active service

## Amount
- $2,320 per qualifying veteran: count the filer and, on a joint return, the spouse

## Notes
- VA disability compensation itself is not taxable federally and is not in Kansas AGI; this exemption is a separate benefit
- A combined 100% rating paid at the 100% rate qualifies; a rating below 100% paid at the 100% rate through individual unemployability should be confirmed against the VA letter's wording

## Required Information
- VA letter with the rating, payment rate and permanence
- Discharge character

## Questions
- Are you or your spouse a veteran receiving VA disability pay at the 100% rate?
- Is the disability rated permanent?
- Was the discharge honorable?

## Common Errors
- Using the old $2,250 amount
- Claiming with a rating under 100% or a non-permanent rating
- Not counting a qualifying spouse on a joint return

## Prompt
- I'm a 100% disabled veteran. Is there a Kansas exemption?
- My husband is rated 100% permanent and total by the VA.
