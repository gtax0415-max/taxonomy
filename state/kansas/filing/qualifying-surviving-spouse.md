# Qualifying Surviving Spouse
## Description
Kansas has no qualifying surviving spouse status. A filer who is a qualifying surviving spouse on the 2026 federal return marks HEAD OF HOUSEHOLD on the K-40.

## Who qualifies federally for 2026
- Spouse died in 2024 or 2025 and the filer did not remarry before the end of 2026
- A dependent child or stepchild lived in the home all year
- The filer paid more than half the cost of keeping up the home
- In the year of death (2026), the surviving spouse files JOINTLY instead

## Kansas result
- Filing status: head of household
- Exemption: $9,160 plus the additional head-of-household $2,320 (confirm: the statute ties that extra exemption to filing "as head of household" federally under 26 U.S.C. §2(b), and a qualifying surviving spouse files under §2(a); the 2026 KDOR instructions should settle whether the $2,320 applies)
- Standard deduction: $6,180
- Bracket: 5.20% up to $23,000, 5.58% above, the same as single. The federal joint-rate benefit does NOT carry to Kansas

## Required Information
- Date of spouse's death
- Qualifying child details
- 2026 federal filing status

## Questions
- Did your spouse die in 2024 or 2025?
- Have you remarried?
- Does a dependent child live with you?

## Common Errors
- Using married filing joint brackets or deductions in Kansas
- Using the status for a death in 2026 (file jointly) or more than two years later
- Assuming the head-of-household extra exemption without confirming

## Prompt
- My wife died last year and I have kids at home. What is my Kansas filing status?
- I file as qualifying surviving spouse federally.
