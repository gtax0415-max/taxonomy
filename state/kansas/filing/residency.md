# Resident
## Description
A Kansas resident for income tax is anyone who LIVES in Kansas, wherever they work. Someone away for a time who intends to return is still a resident. Full-year residents mark Resident and are taxed on all income.

## Filing requirement for a 2026 full-year resident
File if required to file a federal return, OR if Kansas AGI exceeds the Kansas standard deduction plus exemption allowance. Gross income thresholds (unchanged for 2026; no 2026 amounts are indexed):
- Single: $12,765 (65+ or blind $13,615; both $14,465)
- Married filing joint: $26,560 (one box $27,260; two $27,960; three $28,660; four $29,360)
- Head of household: $17,660 (one box $18,510; two $19,360)
- Married filing separate: $13,280 (one box $13,980; two $14,680)

A return must be filed to get a refund of Kansas tax withheld, whatever the income.

## Special cases
- MILITARY: service pay is taxable only by the state of legal residence. A Kansas home of record who has not established residency elsewhere remains a Kansas resident
- Spouse residency split on a joint federal return: file jointly in Kansas as nonresidents (see nonresident-part-year.md)
- Native American income from sources on the filer's own reservation, while living there, is exempt

## Required Information
- Where the filer lived for all of 2026
- Intent to return, if temporarily away
- Military home of record, if applicable

## Questions
- Did you live in Kansas all of 2026?
- If you were away, do you intend to return to Kansas?
- Are you in the military, and what is your home of record?

## Common Errors
- Treating a Kansas resident working in Missouri as a nonresident
- A Kansas-domiciled service member stationed elsewhere not filing
- Not filing to recover Kansas withholding below the filing threshold

## Prompt
- I live in Kansas but work in Kansas City, Missouri.
- I'm in the military stationed outside Kansas. Do I file in Kansas?
