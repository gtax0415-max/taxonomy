# Filing Status
## Description
Kansas filing status must be the SAME as the federal filing status, with one exception: a federal qualifying surviving spouse files as head of household in Kansas. Status drives the exemption allowance, the standard deduction and the tax brackets.

## 2026 amounts by status
- Single: exemption $9,160; standard deduction $3,605; 5.20% bracket up to $23,000
- Married filing joint: exemption $18,320; standard deduction $8,240; 5.20% bracket up to $46,000
- Married filing separate: exemption $9,160; standard deduction $4,120; 5.20% bracket up to $23,000
- Head of household: exemption $9,160 + $2,320; standard deduction $6,180; 5.20% bracket up to $23,000

Income above the bracket amount is taxed at 5.58%. No 2026 rate reduction: the revenue trigger was not met (KDOR Notice 25-06). None of these amounts is indexed.

## Rules
- Joint federal return → joint Kansas return, even if one spouse is a nonresident (both then file as nonresidents and allocate with Schedule S Part B)
- Separate federal returns → separate Kansas returns
- Federal qualifying surviving spouse → head of household in Kansas

## Required Information
- 2026 federal filing status
- Residency of each spouse

## Questions
- What filing status did you use on your 2026 federal return?
- Did you file as qualifying surviving spouse federally?
- Was one spouse a Kansas resident and the other not?

## Common Errors
- Choosing a different status from the federal return
- Marking qualifying surviving spouse (there is no such box on the K-40)
- Filing separately in Kansas after filing jointly federally

## Prompt
- What filing status do I use for Kansas?
- My spouse lives in another state but we file jointly.
