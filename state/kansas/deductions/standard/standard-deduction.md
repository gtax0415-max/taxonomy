# Kansas Standard Deduction
## Description
Kansas has its own standard deduction, far smaller than the federal one. It is NOT indexed for inflation, so the 2026 amounts are the same as 2024 and 2025. A bill to raise it for 2026 (HB 2629) died in committee.

## Amounts for 2026
- Single: $3,605
- Married filing joint: $8,240
- Head of household: $6,180
- Married filing separate: $4,120
- Add the 65-or-older/blind amounts if they apply (see age-and-blindness.md)

Compare: the 2026 federal standard deduction is $16,100 single / $32,200 joint. Kansas does not use it.

## Choosing standard vs itemized
- Use the larger of the Kansas standard deduction or Kansas itemized deductions (Kansas Schedule A)
- The Kansas choice is independent of the federal choice: a filer who took the federal standard deduction may itemize for Kansas, and vice versa
- Married filing separately: if one spouse itemizes, the other must also itemize

## Related 2026 amounts (line 5, not this line)
- Exemption allowance: $9,160 single/HOH/MFS; $18,320 married filing joint
- Each dependent: $2,320; head of household gets one additional $2,320

## Notes
- Filing status must match the federal return (qualifying surviving spouse files as head of household for Kansas)
- No 2026 rate cut: the revenue trigger in 2025 SB 269 was not met (KDOR Notice 25-06), so rates stay 5.20% and 5.58%

## Required Information
- Federal filing status
- Ages and blindness of filer and spouse
- Total of possible Kansas itemized deductions (to compare)

## Questions
- What is your filing status?
- Were you or your spouse 65 or older, or blind, at the end of 2026?
- Do your property taxes, mortgage interest, medical expenses and charity total more than your Kansas standard deduction?

## Common Errors
- Entering the federal standard deduction on the Kansas return
- Assuming the Kansas amount rose with inflation for 2026
- Assuming Kansas must follow the federal itemize/standard choice
- Spouses filing separately using different methods

## Prompt
- What is the Kansas standard deduction for 2026?
- I took the standard deduction federally. Can I itemize in Kansas?
