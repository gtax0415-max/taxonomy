# Standard Deduction for People 65 or Older and/or Blind
## Description
Additional Kansas standard deduction for each filer or spouse who is 65 or older, and for each who is blind. Set by K.S.A. 79-32,119(b) and unchanged since 1998; not indexed for inflation.

## Amounts for 2026
Additional amount per box checked:
- Single or head of household: $850
- Married (joint or separate): $700

Total line 4 amounts by boxes checked (base + additional):
- Single: 1 box $4,455 | 2 boxes $5,305
- Head of household: 1 box $7,030 | 2 boxes $7,880
- Married filing joint: 1 box $8,940 | 2 boxes $9,640 | 3 boxes $10,340 | 4 boxes $11,040
- Married filing separate: 1 box $4,820 | 2 boxes $5,520 | 3 boxes $6,220 | 4 boxes $6,920

## Eligibility for 2026
- AGE: born before January 2, 1962 (a person born January 1, 1962 is treated as 65 on December 31, 2026)
- BLIND: totally blind, or vision no better than 20/200 in the better eye with correction, or field of vision 20 degrees or less, as of December 31, 2026 (federal definition)
- Married filing separate: a spouse's boxes count only if that spouse had no income and could not be claimed as a dependent, following the federal rule

## Notes
- Kansas copies the federal boxes but uses its own, much smaller dollar amounts. The 2026 federal additional amounts ($1,650 married; $2,050 single or head of household) and the new federal $6,000 senior deduction (OBBBA, 2025–2028) do NOT carry to Kansas
- Only applies when taking the Kansas standard deduction; it is lost if the filer itemizes on Kansas Schedule A

## Required Information
- Date of birth of filer and spouse
- Blindness certification, if applicable
- Filing status

## Questions
- Were you or your spouse born before January 2, 1962?
- Are you or your spouse legally blind?
- Will you take the Kansas standard deduction or itemize?

## Common Errors
- Using the 2025 cutoff (born before January 2, 1961) on a 2026 return
- Using the federal additional amounts instead of $850/$700
- Expecting the federal $6,000 senior deduction to reduce Kansas income
- Claiming the additional amount while itemizing

## Prompt
- I turned 65 this year. Does Kansas give me a bigger standard deduction?
- My spouse is blind. What is our Kansas standard deduction?
