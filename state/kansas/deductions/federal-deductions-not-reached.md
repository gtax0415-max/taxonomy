# Federal Deductions Kansas Does Not Reach
## Description
Kansas starts from federal AGI, so every federal deduction taken AFTER AGI is lost for Kansas unless Kansas has its own version. Clients often expect these to lower Kansas tax; they do not.

## Federal 2026 deductions with NO Kansas effect
- Federal standard deduction ($16,100 single / $32,200 joint / $24,150 head of household) — Kansas uses its own ($3,605 / $8,240 / $6,180)
- Federal itemized deductions as such — Kansas uses its own Schedule A (property tax, mortgage interest, medical, charity only)
- Qualified business income (QBI) deduction, §199A — 20%, made permanent by OBBBA
- OBBBA Schedule 1-A deductions (2025–2028):
  - Qualified tips, up to $25,000
  - Qualified overtime premium, up to $12,500 ($25,000 joint)
  - Car loan interest on a new U.S.-assembled vehicle, up to $10,000
  - Senior deduction, $6,000 per person 65 or older
- Non-itemizer charitable deduction (new in 2026), $1,000 / $2,000 joint

## Partial Kansas counterparts
- Seniors: Kansas adds $850 or $700 to its standard deduction for age 65 or blindness, and exempts all Social Security and public pensions
- Charity: deductible only if the filer itemizes on Kansas Schedule A

## Notes
- Above-the-line federal deductions (IRA, HSA, student loan interest, self-employed health insurance, half of SE tax, educator expenses) ARE already in federal AGI, so they do carry into Kansas
- No 2026 Kansas law or KDOR notice adopts the tips, overtime, car-loan or senior deductions; confirm with the 2026 instructions when posted

## Required Information
- Which below-AGI federal deductions the client claimed

## Questions
- Did you claim the federal tips, overtime, car loan interest or senior deduction?
- Did you claim the QBI deduction?

## Common Errors
- Subtracting the federal tips or overtime deduction on Schedule S
- Using the federal standard deduction on the K-40
- Expecting the QBI deduction to lower Kansas tax

## Prompt
- Does Kansas give the no-tax-on-tips deduction?
- Will my overtime deduction lower my Kansas tax?
