# Medical and Dental Expenses
## Description
Kansas itemized deduction for unreimbursed medical and dental expenses allowed under IRC §213, reduced by 7.5% of FEDERAL adjusted gross income.

## Amount
1. Total unreimbursed medical and dental expenses (if itemized federally, the federal Schedule A, line 1 amount)
2. Federal AGI (2026 Form 1040, line 11a)
3. Floor = 7.5% of line 2
4. Deduction = line 1 − line 3 (not less than zero)

The 7.5% floor is permanent federally and unchanged for 2026.

## Qualified expenses
- Doctors, dentists, nurses, hospitals, prescription drugs and insulin
- Medical and dental insurance premiums paid by the filer (including Medicare Part B/D and qualified long-term care premiums within federal age limits)
- Transportation and lodging primarily for medical care (2026 IRS medical mileage rate applies)
- Hearing aids, dentures, eyeglasses and contact lenses

## What does NOT qualify
- Expenses reimbursed by insurance or paid from an HSA/FSA
- Premiums paid by an employer or paid pre-tax
- Cosmetic procedures and general health items

## Notes
- The floor uses FEDERAL AGI, not Kansas AGI
- Self-employed health insurance already deducted in federal AGI cannot be counted again
- 2026 SB 368 created a Kansas subtraction for health care sharing ministry expenses (KDOR Notice 26-07), but it starts with tax year 2027. For 2026, health care sharing payments are not insurance premiums and are not deductible medical expenses

## Required Information
- Total medical expenses paid in 2026 and reimbursements
- Premiums paid personally
- Federal AGI

## Questions
- What medical and dental costs did you pay out of pocket in 2026?
- Were any reimbursed or paid from an HSA or FSA?
- Did you pay your own health insurance premiums after tax?
- Did you pay into a health care sharing ministry? (Not deductible for 2026)

## Common Errors
- Using Kansas AGI for the 7.5% floor
- Including HSA/FSA-paid or reimbursed amounts
- Including employer-paid or pre-tax premiums
- Deducting health care sharing ministry payments as insurance premiums

## Prompt
- I had large medical bills in 2026.
- Can I deduct my health insurance premiums in Kansas?
