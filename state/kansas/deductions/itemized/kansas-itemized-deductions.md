# Kansas Itemized Deductions
## Description
Kansas lets any filer itemize on Kansas Schedule A, whether or not they itemized federally, and use the larger of itemized or the Kansas standard deduction. Kansas allows only FOUR categories, each defined by reference to the Internal Revenue Code.

## What Kansas allows
- Medical and dental expenses over 7.5% of FEDERAL AGI (IRC §213) → see medical-expenses.md
- Real estate and personal property taxes, with NO federal SALT cap (IRC §164) → see taxes-paid.md
- Qualified residence (mortgage) interest and points (IRC §163(h)) → see mortgage-interest.md
- Charitable contributions (IRC §170) → see charitable-gifts.md

## What Kansas does NOT allow
- State and local income taxes or general sales taxes
- Casualty and theft losses, gambling losses, and other federal "other itemized deductions"
- Investment interest expense

## Comparison point for 2026
Kansas standard deduction: $3,605 single / $8,240 joint / $6,180 head of household / $4,120 separate. Because these are low, many filers who take the federal standard deduction ($16,100 / $32,200) still benefit from itemizing for Kansas — property tax plus mortgage interest alone often exceeds them.

## Notes
- Married filing separately: both spouses must use the same method
- The federal $40,400 SALT cap for 2026 does not apply in Kansas for the taxes Kansas allows
- The 2026 Kansas Schedule A was not yet posted as of October 2026; line numbers follow the 2025 form
- New federal 2026 itemized rules (0.5%-of-AGI charitable floor; 2/37 limit for the 37% bracket) are not addressed on the 2025 Kansas form. Whether Kansas applies them must be confirmed against the 2026 instructions

## Required Information
- Totals for each of the four categories
- Federal AGI (for the medical floor)
- Filing status and spouse's method if filing separately

## Questions
- Do you own a home and pay property tax or mortgage interest?
- Did you have large medical bills?
- Did you give to charity?
- If married filing separately, is your spouse itemizing?

## Common Errors
- Assuming you cannot itemize in Kansas because you did not itemize federally
- Including state income tax or sales tax
- Applying the federal SALT cap to Kansas property taxes
- Using Kansas AGI instead of federal AGI for the medical floor

## Prompt
- Should I itemize on my Kansas return?
- I took the federal standard deduction but have a mortgage. What about Kansas?
