# Home Mortgage Interest and Points
## Description
Kansas itemized deduction for qualified residence interest under IRC §163(h): interest on loans secured by a main or second home, including home equity loans and refinances. Kansas follows the federal limits.

## Amount
- Line 8a: interest and points reported on Form 1098
- Line 8b: interest paid to someone who did not issue a Form 1098 (e.g., seller financing)
- Line 8c: points not reported on Form 1098
- Line 9: total

## Federal limits Kansas follows (2026)
- Acquisition debt limit: $750,000 ($375,000 married filing separately) for loans after December 15, 2017; $1 million for older loans. OBBBA made the $750,000 limit permanent
- Home equity interest counts only if the loan was used to buy, build or substantially improve the home that secures it
- Points to buy a main home are generally deductible in the year paid; refinance points are spread over the loan term

## 2026 change to watch
OBBBA treats qualified MORTGAGE INSURANCE PREMIUMS as deductible interest again starting in 2026. The 2025 Kansas Schedule A left line 8d "reserved." Whether Kansas allows PMI on its 2026 Schedule A must be confirmed when the 2026 form is posted.

## What does NOT qualify
- Personal interest (car loans, credit cards). The new federal car-loan interest deduction (2025–2028) is a federal below-the-line deduction and does NOT carry to Kansas
- Interest on rental or business property (deducted on those schedules)
- Home equity interest used for non-home purposes

## Required Information
- All Forms 1098
- Loan origination dates and balances
- Use of any home equity proceeds
- Points paid at purchase or refinance

## Questions
- Do you have a mortgage or home equity loan on your main or second home?
- When was each loan taken out, and what is the balance?
- Did you use home equity money for anything other than the home?
- Did you pay points or mortgage insurance in 2026?

## Common Errors
- Deducting interest above the $750,000 debt limit
- Including home equity interest used for other purposes
- Deducting all refinance points in one year
- Claiming the federal car-loan interest deduction on Kansas Schedule A

## Prompt
- I have a mortgage on my home in Kansas.
- Can I deduct my PMI on my 2026 Kansas return?
