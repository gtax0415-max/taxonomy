# Kansas Tax on Lump Sum Distributions
## Description
A RESIDENT who paid federal tax on a qualifying lump-sum distribution under IRC §402(e) using Form 4972 (10-year averaging or 20% capital gain treatment) pays Kansas tax equal to 13% of that federal tax. Nonresidents leave this line blank.

## Amount
- Kansas tax = 13% × federal tax on the lump sum (Form 4972)
- KPERS lump sum taxed federally: prorate the federal tax first. Multiply it by the Kansas-taxable share of the distribution (accumulated interest plus contributions made since July 1, 1984 that were not already added back on Kansas returns) ÷ total distribution
- Unchanged for 2026

## Who it applies to
- Form 4972 is available only to participants born before January 2, 1936 (or their beneficiaries), so this line is very rare by 2026
- Ordinary rollovers and regular 1099-R distributions are taxed through federal AGI, not here

## Required Information
- Federal Form 4972 tax
- For KPERS: breakdown of contributions and interest

## Questions
- Did you use federal Form 4972 for a lump-sum distribution in 2026?
- Was the distribution from KPERS?
- Were you a Kansas resident when you received it?

## Common Errors
- Claiming it as a nonresident
- Not prorating a KPERS lump sum
- Using 13% of the whole distribution instead of 13% of the federal tax

## Prompt
- I used 10-year averaging on a lump-sum pension distribution.
- My father, born in 1934, took a lump sum from his retirement plan.
