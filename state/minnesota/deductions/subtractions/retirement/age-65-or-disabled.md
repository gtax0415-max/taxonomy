# Age 65 or Older / Disabled Subtraction (Schedule M1R)
## Description
A modest subtraction for low-income seniors (born before January 2, 1962) and for people with permanent and total disability who received federally taxable disability income. Amounts are not indexed.
## 2026 figures (2026 Schedule M1R final draft)
| Filing situation | AGI must be under | Nontaxable SS + RRB under | Base amount (line 1) | Income offset (line 10) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single, HOH, QSS — 65+ or disabled | $33,700 | $9,600 | $9,600 | $14,500 |
| MFJ — both 65+ or disabled | $42,000 | $12,000 | $12,000 | $18,000 |
| MFJ — one 65+ or disabled | $38,500 | $12,000 | $12,000 | $14,500 |
| MFS — lived apart all year | $21,000 | $6,000 | $6,000 | $9,000 |
## Computation
1. Base amount (if under 65 and disabled, limited to taxable disability income)
2. Minus nontaxable Social Security, nontaxable RRB benefits, and certain pension/disability amounts (federal Schedule R line 13b)
3. Minus 50% of AGI (adjusted: plus Form 4972 lump sums, minus taxable RRB) above the line 10 amount
4. Result, if positive, is the subtraction
## Eligibility notes
- Disabled filers must have received federally taxable disability income in 2026
- A physician's statement (federal Schedule R instructions) or VA certification is kept on file; Schedule R need not be filed
- MFS only if the spouses lived apart all of 2026
## Questions
- Were you born before January 2, 1962, or permanently disabled with taxable disability income?
- How much nontaxable Social Security did you receive?
## Common Errors
- Using the 2025 birth-date cutoff (January 2, 1961)
- Forgetting to reduce by nontaxable Social Security
