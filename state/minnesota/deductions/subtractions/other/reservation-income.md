# Reservation Income for American Indians
## Description
An enrolled member of an American Indian tribe who LIVES and EARNS income on the reservation of the tribe of enrollment subtracts that reservation-source income, to the extent federally taxable.
## Examples of reservation-source income
Wages for work on the reservation; pensions from reservation work; military pensions based on Minnesota-exempt military pay; interest from an on-reservation bank; per-capita casino distributions; Social Security from reservation employment; rents, royalties, and gains from on-reservation property; business, partnership, S corporation, and farm income from on-reservation activity; reservation-based unemployment; gambling winnings on the reservation.
## Minnesota Chippewa Tribe exception
Members of Bois Forte, Fond du Lac, Grand Portage, White Earth, Mille Lacs, and Leech Lake who live and earn on ANOTHER of those six reservations still qualify. This does not extend to Red Lake.
## Other rules
- Do not subtract income already removed elsewhere on Schedule M1M or M1MB
- Related federally deducted expenses are added back (Schedule M1M, Line 3)
- A Child and Dependent Care Credit must be apportioned by income taxable to Minnesota
- Report a physical address, not a PO box
## Note
The 2026 instructions refer to "line 19a/19b" for the tribe and address; the 2026 form uses Lines 21a and 21b.
## Prompt
- I am a Leech Lake member living and working on the reservation.
