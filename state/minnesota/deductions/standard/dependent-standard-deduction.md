# Dependent Standard Deduction
## Who uses it
Anyone who CAN be claimed as a dependent on another return (or whose spouse can, on a joint return), whether or not they are actually claimed.
## 2026 figures (TY2026 inflation-adjusted amounts)
MINIMUM: $1,300
EARNED-INCOME AMOUNT: earned income + $350
CEILING: the basic standard deduction, $15,300
AGE 65 / BLIND ADD-ON: $2,000 per box ($1,600 if married or QSS)
## Computation
1. Greater of $1,300 or (earned income + $350)
2. $15,300 + add-ons for age and blindness
3. Standard deduction = lesser of Step 1 and Step 2
(Earned income above $950 makes the earned-income amount exceed the $1,300 minimum.)
## Filing requirement link
A dependent must file a Minnesota return if gross income (earned plus unearned) exceeds this standard deduction.
## Other effects of being a dependent
- No dependent exemption on Form M1, Line 5
- Not eligible for the Renter's Credit, Working Family Credit, Child Tax Credit, or 529 credit
## Required Information
- Earned income (W-2 Box 1, net self-employment, taxable scholarships)
## Questions
- Can a parent or someone else claim you as a dependent?
- How much did you earn from work in 2026?
## Common Errors
- Using the 2025 minimum ($1,250)
- Giving a dependent the full $15,300
