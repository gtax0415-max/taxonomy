# Standard Deduction Limitation (High Income)
## Description
Minnesota reduces the standard deduction at high incomes, using the same thresholds as the itemized deduction limitation. There is no federal equivalent.
## 2026 figures (TY2026 inflation-adjusted amounts)
| | All filers except MFS | Married filing separately |
|---|---|---|
| First threshold | $244,400 | $122,200 |
| Second threshold | $337,800 | $168,900 |
| Maximum reduction | 80% of the standard deduction | 80% |
| 80% applies outright when AGI exceeds | $1,107,750 | $1,107,750 |
## Worksheet A (AGI over the first threshold, up to $1,107,750)
1. AGI (Form M1, Line 1)
2. Lesser of (AGI − $244,400) or $93,400 [MFS: AGI − $122,200, max $46,700]
3. Step 2 × 3%
4. AGI − $337,800 if positive [MFS: − $168,900], else 0
5. Step 4 × 10%
6. Step 3 + Step 5
7. Standard deduction from the table (or dependent standard deduction)
8. Step 7 × 80%
9. Lesser of Step 6 and Step 8
10. Allowed standard deduction = Step 7 − Step 9
## Worksheet B (AGI over $1,107,750)
Allowed standard deduction = 20% of the table amount.
## 2025 — for comparison
Thresholds $238,950 / $330,300 / $1,083,150.
## Note
The $93,400 and $46,700 caps are the gaps between the two thresholds ($337,800 − $244,400 and $168,900 − $122,200), derived from the 2026 indexed thresholds; they appear on the 2026 Schedule M1SA worksheet for the itemized version.
