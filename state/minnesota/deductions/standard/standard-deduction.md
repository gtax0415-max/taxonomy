# Minnesota Standard Deduction
## 2026 — MN Department of Revenue published figures
(TY2026 inflation-adjusted amounts; 2026 Schedule M1SA standard deduction table)
| Filing status | 0 boxes | 1 box | 2 boxes | 3 boxes | 4 boxes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single | $15,300 | $17,300 | $19,300 | — | — |
| Married filing jointly | $30,600 | $32,200 | $33,800 | $35,400 * | $37,000 |
| Qualifying surviving spouse | $30,600 | $32,200 | $33,800 | — | — |
| Married filing separately | $15,300 | $16,900 | $18,500 | $20,100 | $21,700 |
| Head of household | $23,000 | $25,000 | $27,000 | — | — |
* The 2026 final-draft Schedule M1SA prints $34,400 here; $35,400 follows the $1,600-per-box rule. Confirm on the final form.
ADDITIONAL AMOUNT PER BOX: $2,000 (single, HOH) / $1,600 (married, QSS)
## 2025 — for comparison
$14,950 single and MFS / $29,900 MFJ and QSS / $22,500 HOH.
## Boxes
- AGE 65 OR OLDER: born before January 2, 1962
- BLIND: totally blind on December 31, 2026, or vision no better than 20/200 in the better eye with correction, or field of vision 20 degrees or less (keep the eye doctor's statement)
- MFS: check boxes only for yourself, unless the spouse had no gross income and cannot be claimed as a dependent
## Not allowed
- Married filing separately when the spouse itemizes on the Minnesota return
- Nonresident aliens (unless a treaty allows it)
- Short-period returns due to a change in annual accounting period
## Compared with federal 2026
The federal standard deduction is higher; Minnesota's is set separately and is also reduced at high income (standard-deduction-limitation.md).
## Questions
- Were you or your spouse born before January 2, 1962, or blind?
- Can anyone claim you as a dependent?
- Is your AGI over $244,400 ($122,200 MFS)?
## Common Errors
- Using the federal standard deduction amount
- Using the age-65 date for 2025 (January 2, 1961) instead of January 2, 1962
- Taking the standard deduction while married filing separately when the spouse itemizes

- What is my Minnesota standard deduction?
