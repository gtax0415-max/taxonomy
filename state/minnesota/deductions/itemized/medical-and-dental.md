# Medical and Dental Expenses (Schedule M1SA, Lines 2–4)
## 2026 rule
DEDUCTION: unreimbursed medical and dental expenses above 10% of AGI (federal floor is 7.5%)
## 2026 figures (2026 Schedule M1SA instructions)
LONG-TERM CARE PREMIUM LIMITS by age at the end of 2026: 40 or under $500 / 41–50 $930 / 51–60 $1,860 / 61–70 $4,960 / 71 or older $6,200
MEDICAL MILEAGE: 20.5 cents per mile (or actual gas and oil), plus parking and tolls
DEPENDENT GROSS INCOME TEST for "could have claimed" persons: $5,300
## Deductible (examples)
Health and dental insurance premiums paid with after-tax dollars (reduced by any self-employed health insurance deduction), Medicare Part B and D premiums, qualified long-term care premiums within the age limits, prescriptions and insulin, doctors and therapists, hospital care, nursing help, addiction treatment, smoking cessation, weight-loss programs for a diagnosed disease, eyeglasses, hearing aids, laser eye surgery, medical travel and lodging, breast pumps and lactation supplies.
## Not deductible
Premiums paid pre-tax (cafeteria plan), diet food, cosmetic surgery (with exceptions), life or income-protection insurance, Medicare tax, nursing for a healthy baby, cannabis and other federally illegal drugs, unapproved imported drugs, over-the-counter medicines except insulin, rest travel, funeral costs.
## Whose expenses
Taxpayer, spouse, dependents, a child of divorced parents not claimed, and anyone who could have been a dependent except for gross income ($5,300 or more), filing jointly, or the taxpayer being someone else's dependent.
## Interaction with credits
Long-term care premiums that produce a full deduction here cannot also produce the Long-Term Care Insurance Credit (minnesota/credits/health-insurance/long-term-care-insurance-credit.md).
## Questions

- Were your premiums paid through payroll pre-tax?
## Common Errors
- Using the federal 7.5% floor
- Including pre-tax premiums
- Not reducing for reimbursements received in 2026 for 2026 expenses
