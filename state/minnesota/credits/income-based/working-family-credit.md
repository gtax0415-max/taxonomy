# Working Family Credit (WFC)
## Description
Fully refundable Minnesota credit for working people with low to moderate income, plus a fixed add-on for "qualifying older children" ages 18–23. It is Minnesota's counterpart to the federal Earned Income Credit and is claimed on the SAME schedule as the Minnesota Child Tax Credit.
## How it works
1. Base credit = 4% of the first $9,690 of earned income (max $388)
2. Add a flat amount for qualifying older children (does NOT require earned income)
3. Add any Child Tax Credit (family-dependent/child-tax-credit.md)
4. The COMBINED total is phased out by 12% of the GREATER of AGI or earned income above the threshold (9% if the household has qualifying older children but no child under 18)

## 2025 — for comparison
Base credit 4% of first $9,480 (max $379). Phase-out threshold $37,910 MFJ / $31,950 others.
## How filing status changes the numbers
- The maximum credit is the same for every filing status. Only the phase-out threshold moves
- Married filing jointly gets a higher threshold ($38,770 vs $32,680), a flat difference of $6,090 — NOT double
- MARRIED FILING SEPARATELY is barred, except a taxpayer with a qualifying child who lived with them more than half the year AND who either did not share a principal abode with the spouse for the last six months of 2026 or is legally separated under Minnesota law
## Key differences from the federal EIC
- ITIN ALLOWED: the taxpayer, spouse, and children may use an ITIN instead of an SSN
- YOUNGER CHILDLESS WORKERS: without a qualifying child, the age window is 19–64 (federal EIC: 25–64)
- QUALIFYING OLDER CHILD add-on has no federal equivalent and is available even with zero earned income
- Phase-out uses ONE combined pot (WFC + Child Tax Credit), unlike the federal system where EIC and CTC phase out independently
## Key eligibility rules
- Full-year or part-year Minnesota resident. FULL-YEAR NONRESIDENTS are NOT eligible. Part-year residents prorate by the Schedule M1NR percentage
- Not a dependent or qualifying child of another person
- Investment income at or under $12,200 (if over, use step 2 of the federal Form 1040 line 27 instructions to test eligibility)
- Without qualifying children: main home in the U.S. more than half the year
- NOT eligible if under a federal 2-year or 10-year EIC ban
- Military members stationed outside the U.S. on extended active duty (over 90 days) are treated as Minnesota residents for that period; nontaxable combat pay may be included in earned income
## Earned income
Generally Form 1040 line 1z plus net self-employment earnings (via federal EIC Worksheet B), plus Medicaid waiver payments excluded from income, plus nontaxable combat pay if elected. Excludes taxable scholarships not on a W-2, inmate wages, and pensions from nonqualified deferred compensation or non-governmental 457 plans.
## The hidden marginal rate
In the phase-out range the combined credit falls 12 cents per extra dollar of income. Stacked on the 5.35% Minnesota bottom bracket and any federal EIC phase-out (16%–21%), a Minnesota worker in both phase-out ranges can lose well over a third of each additional dollar.
## Required Information
- Earned income (W-2 Box 1, Schedule SE net earnings, nontaxable combat pay election)
- Federal AGI and investment income
- Qualifying older children (from Schedule M1DQC): names, ages, student or disability status
- Residency status and Schedule M1NR percentage if part-year
## Questions
- What was your earned income and AGI for 2026?
- Do you have children ages 18–23 who are students or disabled (qualifying older children)?
- Are you between 19 and 64 if you have no children?
- Is your investment income under $12,200?
- Were you a Minnesota resident all year?
- Are you under an IRS ban from claiming the federal EIC?
## Common Errors
- Treating the WFC and Child Tax Credit as separate phase-outs (they share one)
- Phasing out on AGI only, when the rule uses the GREATER of AGI or earned income
- Denying the credit to a 19–24-year-old childless worker because they fail the federal EIC age test
- Denying the credit to a family that uses ITINs
- Claiming it as a full-year nonresident
