# Pass-Through Entity (PTE) Tax Credit
## Description
Refundable credit passed to partners, S corporation shareholders, and beneficiaries when the entity elected to pay Minnesota income tax at the entity level. The entity-level election is a workaround for the federal SALT deduction cap, and this credit prevents the owner from being taxed again on the same income.
## 2026 status
- EXTENDED by the 2026 Tax Bill through tax year 2027. The Minnesota PTE tax expires for taxable years beginning after December 31, 2027
- Revenue may DENY the credit if the entity has not actually paid the tax (effective May 28, 2026)
- 2026 first-quarter estimated PTE payments are timely if paid by the second-quarter due date
- The credit for PTE taxes paid to another state is extended to taxable years beginning before January 1, 2028
## How it works
- Enter each entity's Minnesota Tax ID and credit amount on Schedule M1REF Line 10 (attach a statement if more than three)
- Include the KS / KPI / KF schedules with the return; without them the refund is delayed or denied
- On the income side, the owner's share of PTE tax deducted by the entity is ADDED BACK to Minnesota income (Schedule M1M) and the Adjusted Gross Income Worksheet for PTE Deductions is used for AGI-based limits
## Required Information
- Schedules KS, KPI, or KF from each electing entity
- Entity Minnesota Tax ID numbers
## Questions
- Does any partnership, S corporation, or trust you own an interest in pay Minnesota PTE tax?
- Do you have the Minnesota K-schedule showing your credit?
## Common Errors
- Claiming the credit without the matching addition for the PTE tax deducted at the entity level
- Omitting the K-schedule or Tax ID
- Claiming a credit when the entity has not paid
## Prompt
- My S corporation elected the Minnesota PTE tax.
- My K-1 shows Minnesota pass-through entity tax paid.
