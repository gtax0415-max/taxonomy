# Minnesota Individual Income Tax Credits (Tax Year 2026)
## Description
Minnesota credits are claimed on Form M1 through two summary schedules. Every credit lands on one of them, so routing is the first question for any Minnesota credit.
- NONREFUNDABLE credits → Schedule M1C, line 19 → Form M1, Line 16. They can reduce Minnesota tax to zero but no further
- REFUNDABLE credits → Schedule M1REF, line 14 → Form M1, Line 22. They are treated as payments and can produce a refund with no tax owed
## How Minnesota credits relate to the federal return
- Minnesota starts from federal AGI (Form M1, Line 1 = Form 1040, Line 11), so most credit phase-outs use federal AGI
- The 2026 Tax Bill (Minnesota Laws 2026, Chapter 128, signed May 27, 2026) moved Minnesota's Internal Revenue Code conformity date to MAY 1, 2026, adopting most of H.R. 1 (OBBBA). Schedule M1NC, Federal Adjustments, is discontinued for 2026
- Minnesota DECOUPLED from the federal Child and Dependent Care Credit and did NOT adopt OBBBA's expansion of it. The federal and Minnesota versions now differ for 2026
- Several Minnesota credits borrow federal definitions: the Child and Working Family Credits use federal Earned Income Credit eligibility rules; the K-12 Education Credit uses the EIC qualifying-child definition; the Dependent Care Credit uses the federal "qualifying person" and "qualified expense" definitions

## Other lines that interact with credits
- Form M1, Line 14b — REPAYMENT of excess advance Child Tax Credit payments (from Schedule M1CWFC, line 27). This is an additional TAX, the Minnesota parallel to excess APTC repayment on the federal side
- Form M1, Line 14a — recapture of the Education Savings Account credit (Schedule M1529) when 529 money is used for nonqualified expenses
## Filing requirement note
A Minnesota resident may file solely to claim refundable credits even when income is below the filing threshold. Anyone who received 2026 advance Child Tax Credit payments MUST file a 2026 return.
## Questions
- Are you a full-year resident, part-year resident, or nonresident? (Most refundable credits are unavailable to full-year nonresidents; part-year residents prorate)
- What is your filing status? (Married filing separately is barred or restricted for several credits)
- Do you have children, and how old are they at the end of 2026?
- Did you rent your home in Minnesota during 2026?
## Prompt
- What Minnesota tax credits can I claim?
- Is this Minnesota credit refundable?
- I live in Minnesota and have kids. What credits am I missing?
