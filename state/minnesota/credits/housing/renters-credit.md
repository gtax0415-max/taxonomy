# Renter's Credit (Schedule M1RENT)
## Description
Refundable credit for renters, based on household income and the rent paid for a primary residence in Minnesota. It replaced the renter's property tax refund (Form M1PR) beginning with tax year 2024. No federal counterpart.
## How it works
1. Household income = AGI (Form M1 line 1) − $5,300 if you or your spouse were 65+ (born before January 2, 1962) or disabled − dependent subtraction − SEIU stipend subtraction. Part-year residents count only income received while a Minnesota resident
2. "Rent constituting property taxes" = 17% of total rent on line 3 of all CRPs
3. Look up the credit in the Renter's Credit Table (Form M1 instructions) using household income and that 17% figure. When the 17% figure is $2,500 or more, use the Renter's Worksheet
4. If Medical Assistance paid part of the rent (CRP Line A), prorate

Household income limit under $77,570; maximum credit $2,720.
## Key eligibility rules
- Full-year or part-year Minnesota resident. Full-year nonresidents are NOT eligible
- Rented and lived in a Minnesota unit where property taxes, or payments in lieu of property taxes, were payable in 2026. A property-tax-EXEMPT building does not qualify
- Must NOT be a dependent and must not be claimable as one
- Need a 2026 CRP from the landlord (landlords must issue CRPs through e-Services); include copies with the return
- Not eligible if all rent was paid by Medical Assistance, SSI, Minnesota Supplemental Aid, or Housing Support
- Claimed on the income tax return — a renter with no filing requirement must still file Form M1
## Filing situations
- MFJ living apart all year: pick ONE spouse's rent and income
- MFS living together all year: only one spouse claims, using combined rent and combined AGI
- Roommates: each gets a separate CRP with an equal share regardless of who actually paid
- Rented in 2026 and owned and lived in a home on January 2, 2027: Renter's Credit for the rental period AND Form M1PR as a homeowner
- Overlapping leases: use the overlapping-rental worksheet; only the primary residence counts
## Rent greater than AGI
If total CRP rent exceeds AGI, list the nontaxable income used to pay rent (nontaxable Social Security, government assistance, scholarships). Without it the refund is delayed.
## Required Information
- All 2026 CRPs (Line 3 rent, Line A Medical Assistance)
- AGI; income received while a nonresident (part-year)
- Age 65+ or disability status
- Number of dependents
- Nontaxable income if rent exceeds AGI
## Questions
- Did you rent and live in Minnesota in 2026, and do you have your CRP?
- What was your household income?
- Are you or your spouse 65 or older, or disabled?
- Could anyone claim you as a dependent?
- Did you live in a nursing home, adult foster care, or assisted living?
## Common Errors
- Filing Form M1PR instead of Schedule M1RENT (the renter's refund moved to Form M1 in 2024)
- Using rent paid instead of 17% of rent in the table
- Using AGI instead of household income (forgetting the dependent and 65+/disabled subtractions)
- Claiming it as a dependent (common for students living with roommates and supported by parents)
- Claiming for a property-tax-exempt building
- Omitting the nontaxable-income explanation when rent exceeds AGI
