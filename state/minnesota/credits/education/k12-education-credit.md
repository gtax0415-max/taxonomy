# K-12 Education Credit (Schedule M1ED)
## Description
Refundable credit for education-related expenses paid for a qualifying child in kindergarten through grade 12. No federal counterpart.
## How it works
Credit = 75% of qualifying expenses, capped at $1,500 per qualifying child, reduced above an income threshold.
## 2026 — MN Department of Revenue published figures
(TY2026 inflation-adjusted amounts; 2026 Schedule M1ED and M1REF final drafts)
CREDIT RATE: 75% of qualifying expenses
MAXIMUM CREDIT: $1,500 per qualifying child
PHASE-OUT THRESHOLD: $77,550 AGI
PHASE-OUT RATE: 25% of AGI over threshold with one child; 50% with two or more
COMPUTER HARDWARE AND EDUCATIONAL SOFTWARE: no more than $200 counted
| Qualifying children in K-12 | AGI must be less than |
|---|---|
| 1 or 2 | $83,550 |
| 3 | $86,550 |
| 4 | $89,550 |
| 5 | $92,550 |
| 6 or more | $95,550 plus $3,000 for each additional child |
## 2025 — for comparison
AGI limit $81,820 for 1 or 2 children.
## Qualifying child
Same as the federal EIC qualifying child: your child, descendant, sibling, niece, or nephew in grades K-12, who lived with you more than half the year, not claimed by anyone else. The 2026 Schedule M1ED instructions state the child must have an SSN, although the schedule no longer has a field for it.
## Qualifying expenses
- Fees for academic classes or individual instruction outside the regular school day, taught by a QUALIFIED INSTRUCTOR (tutoring, music lessons, academic summer camps, after-school enrichment, driver's ed instructor fees if offered by the school)
- Educational materials used during the school day (textbooks, paper, pencils, notebooks)
- Purchase or rental of musical instruments used during the regular school day
- Fees paid to OTHERS for transportation to and from school (school in MN, IA, ND, SD, or WI)
- Home computer hardware and educational software (up to $200)
QUALIFIED INSTRUCTOR: not the child's sibling, parent, or grandparent, AND a Minnesota-licensed teacher (or directly supervised by one), a teacher at an accredited private school, a holder of a bachelor's degree, or a member of the Minnesota Music Teachers Association.
NOT QUALIFYING (credit or subtraction): driving your own child, overnight class trips, religious materials, sports camps or lessons, books for tutoring or enrichment, preschool or post-high-school classes, school lunches, uniforms, monthly internet fees, noneducational software.
SUBTRACTION ONLY (not credit): private school tuition and tuition for college courses used for high school graduation.
## Credit vs. subtraction
The K-12 Education Subtraction on Schedule M1M covers the same expenses plus private school tuition. Limits (confirmed on the 2026 Schedule M1M instructions) are $1,625 per child in grades K-6 and $2,500 per child in grades 7-12, with no income limit; the school must be in Minnesota, Iowa, North Dakota, South Dakota, or Wisconsin. See minnesota/deductions/subtractions/education/k12-education-subtraction.md. An expense used for the credit cannot also be used for the subtraction.
## Key eligibility rules
- AGI under the limit for the number of K-12 children
- MARRIED FILING SEPARATELY cannot claim the credit (subtraction still available)
- Part-year residents and nonresidents prorate by the Schedule M1NR percentage
## Required Information
- Each child's name, grade, date of birth, and type of school (public, private, home)
- Expenses by category and by child
- Name of the qualified instructor and organization for any instruction fees
- AGI
## Questions
- Do you have children in grades K-12 who lived with you more than half the year?
- What did you spend on school supplies, tutoring, music lessons or instruments, academic camps, or transportation?
- Did you pay private school tuition (subtraction, not credit)?
- What was your AGI?
## Common Errors
- Including private school tuition in the credit
- Claiming lessons from a parent or sibling, or from someone who is not a qualified instructor
- Counting more than $200 of computer costs
- Claiming sports camps or internet fees
- Using the same expense for both the credit and the subtraction
- Claiming the credit while married filing separately
