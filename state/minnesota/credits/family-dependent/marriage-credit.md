# Marriage Credit (Schedule M1MA)
## Description
Nonrefundable credit that offsets the "marriage penalty" two-earner couples face under Minnesota's joint brackets compared with two single filers. No federal counterpart. Claimed on about 427,000 returns in TY2021 (Minnesota Tax Expenditure Review Commission).
## 2026 — MN Department of Revenue published figures
(2026 Schedule M1MA final draft)
MAXIMUM CREDIT: $1,894
MINIMUM JOINT TAXABLE INCOME (Form M1, Line 9): $49,000
MINIMUM INCOME OF THE LESSER-EARNING SPOUSE: $31,000
TABLE / COMPUTATION BREAKPOINT: lesser-earning spouse's income of $125,000
## 2025 — for comparison
Table breakpoint was $123,000.
## How it works
1. Each spouse's qualifying income = wages + net self-employment income + taxable pensions + taxable Social Security
2. Take the LESSER of the two (line 6). Under $31,000 → no credit
3. If Minnesota taxable income is under $49,000 → no credit
4. Under $125,000 → look up the table. $125,000 or more → compute single-rate tax on each spouse's share, compare with the joint tax, cap at $1,894
## What counts as income for this credit
- INCLUDES: wages, self-employment earnings, taxable pensions and annuities, taxable IRA distributions, taxable Social Security
- EXCLUDES: military wages subtracted on Schedule M1M, Railroad Retirement Board benefits, excluded Medicaid waiver payments, 401(k) and other elective deferrals, unemployment, interest and dividends, alimony and child support, nontaxable combat pay, military quarters and subsistence allowances
## Key eligibility rules
- Filing status MARRIED FILING JOINTLY
- Both spouses have taxable earned income, taxable pension, or taxable Social Security
- Nonrefundable — useless beyond Minnesota tax liability
## Required Information
- Each spouse's wages, self-employment income, taxable pensions, and taxable Social Security (split using each spouse's SSA-1099)
- Minnesota taxable income (Form M1, Line 9)
- Schedule M1NR percentage if part-year
## Questions
- Are you married filing jointly and do both of you have earnings, pensions, or Social Security?
- What did the lower-earning spouse earn in 2026? (At least $31,000 needed)
- Is your Minnesota taxable income at least $49,000?
## Common Errors
- Including 401(k) deferrals or unemployment in a spouse's income
- Not splitting taxable Social Security between spouses
- Using the Part 2 computation when the lesser income is under $125,000 (the table is required there)
- Expecting a refund from it (it is nonrefundable)
## Prompt
- Does Minnesota have a marriage penalty credit?
- We both work. Is there a Minnesota credit for married couples?
