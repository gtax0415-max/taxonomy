# Credit for Qualifying Older Children
## Description
Flat, refundable add-on for dependents too old for the Minnesota Child Tax Credit. Legally it is part of the Working Family Credit and is computed on Schedule M1CWFC, but Revenue lists it as its own credit. It does NOT require earned income.
## Who is a qualifying older child
Per the 2026 Schedule M1DQC steps (near-final draft, 9/16/2026), a child who meets the relationship, residency (more than half the year), and ID tests and is NOT under 18 at the end of 2026, and who is ANY of these:
- Permanently and totally disabled at any time in 2026 (any age)
- Age 18 at the end of 2026 (under 19)
- Age 19 through 23 at the end of 2026, younger than you (or your spouse if MFJ), and a full-time student for some part of 5 calendar months
Children under 18 get the $1,800 Child Tax Credit instead. The child needs an SSN, ITIN, or ATIN issued or applied for by the return due date.
A child claimed only because the custodial parent released the exemption on federal Form 8332 (Schedule M1DQC row 7) CANNOT be a qualifying older child.
Schedule M1DQC rows: row 9 full-time student, row 10 disabled, row 12 qualifying older child.
## 2026 — MN Department of Revenue published figures
(TY2026 inflation-adjusted amounts; 2026 Schedule M1CWFC final draft)
| Qualifying older children | 2026 add-on |
|---|---|
| One | $1,020 |
| Two | $2,330 |
| Three or more | $2,770 |
PHASE-OUT: combined with the Working Family Credit and Child Tax Credit, beginning at $32,680 ($38,770 MFJ)
PHASE-OUT RATE: 9% of the greater of AGI or earned income over the threshold if the household has ONLY qualifying older children (no child under 18); 12% if it also has a child under 18
Number of qualifying older children is reported on Schedule M1REF, Line 2b.
## Key eligibility rules
Same as the Working Family Credit: full- or part-year resident (nonresidents excluded), not a dependent, investment income at or under $12,200, no federal EIC ban, MFS barred except the separated-spouse exception.
## Required Information
- Each older child's name, date of birth, SSN or ITIN, student or disability status, months lived with you
## Questions
- Do you have an 18-year-old, a full-time student age 19–23, or a disabled adult child living with you?
## Common Errors
- Leaving out 18-year-olds (too old for the Child Tax Credit, but eligible here)
- Using the 12% phase-out rate when the household has only older children (9%)
- Assuming earned income is needed
## Prompt
- My son is 20 and in college. Is there a Minnesota credit for him?
- Is there a Minnesota credit for an 18-year-old?
