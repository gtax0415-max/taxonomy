# Advance Payments of the Child Tax Credit
## Description
Minnesota lets families receive part of NEXT year's Child Tax Credit during the year instead of waiting to file. There is no current federal equivalent (the federal advance CTC existed only in 2021). For tax year 2026 this topic has TWO separate jobs on the same schedule:
1. RECONCILE advances of the 2026 credit (elected on the 2025 return and paid during 2026)
2. ELECT advances of the 2027 credit (paid during 2027)
The underlying credit is in family-dependent/child-tax-credit.md.
## 2026 — MN Department of Revenue figures
(2026 Schedule M1CWFC final draft, 10/1/2026)
ADVANCE AMOUNT FOR 2027: 50% of the 2026 child tax credit after phase-out (Schedule M1CWFC line 28), multiplied by the share of qualifying children who were UNDER 17 at the end of 2026 (line 29)
NUMBER OF PAYMENTS: 3 equal installments (line 31 = line 30 ÷ 3)
TIMING: payments begin after July 1, 2027
2027 MINIMUM CREDIT BASE: 50% of the 2026 child tax credit, reduced if the family has fewer qualifying children in 2027
CREDIT PER CHILD USED IN THE COMPUTATION: $1,800 (2026)
## Who can elect 2027 advances
ALL of these must be true:
- Eligible for a 2026 Minnesota Child Tax Credit
- At least one qualifying child UNDER 17 at the end of 2026
- Minnesota resident on December 31, 2026
- ORIGINAL return filed on or before April 15, 2027 (cannot elect on an amended return)
## Reconciling 2026 advances
- Anyone who received 2026 advances MUST file a 2026 Minnesota return, even with no other filing requirement
- Line A = total 2026 advances from the Summary Letter; Line B = 2025 Minimum Credit Base (2025 M1CWFC line 28); Line C = 2025 number of qualifying children (2025 M1CWFC line 7)
- The MINIMUM CREDIT BASE protects families whose income rose: the 2026 credit is treated as at least the 2025 base, scaled down by (2026 children ÷ 2025 children) when the number of children fell
- Excess advances are REPAID on Form M1, Line 14b, which increases tax or reduces the refund
## Filing-status changes between 2025 and 2026
| 2025 | 2026 | Line A (advances) | Line B / C |
|---|---|---|---|
| MFJ | Single, MFS, or HOH | 50% of the joint advances | Each spouse uses the joint 2025 amount / number |
| HOH | MFJ | Combine both spouses' advances | Combine both 2025 returns |
| MFJ | Qualifying surviving spouse | All advances | Joint 2025 amount / number |
Joint filers are each treated as receiving half of the advances; after divorce each former spouse reports 50%. If a joint filer dies before filing, all advances are attributed to the surviving spouse.
## Common repayment triggers
- Fewer qualifying children in the year of reconciliation
- No longer eligible for the Minimum Credit Base
- Moving from full-year resident to part-year or nonresident
- Advances exceeding the year's credit
## Benefits and other programs
SNAP benefits or eligibility can be affected by advance payments. Taking the credit at filing time does not affect SNAP. (Minnesota Department of Children, Youth, and Families: 1-800-657-3698.)
## Required Information
- 2026 Summary Letter amounts
- 2025 Schedule M1CWFC lines 7 and 28
- Number of qualifying children under 17 at the end of 2026
- Bank routing and account numbers (optional; paper checks otherwise)
- Phone number for optional text-message updates
## Questions
- Did you receive Minnesota advance child tax credit payments during 2026?
- Did your filing status or number of children change from 2025?
- Do you want part of your 2027 Minnesota child credit paid in advance?
- Will you file your original return by April 15?
- Do you receive SNAP?
## Common Errors
- Not filing a 2026 return after receiving advances
- Entering the full joint advance amount after a divorce instead of 50%
- Trying to elect advances on an amended return or a late original return
- Including 17-year-olds in the advance computation
- Forgetting the Line 14b repayment when advances exceed the credit
