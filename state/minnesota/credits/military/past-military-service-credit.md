# Credit for Past Military Service
## Description
Nonrefundable credit for lower-income veterans who have separated from service. It is the alternative to the military pension subtraction; a taxpayer cannot have both.
## 2026 figures
(2026 Schedule M1C final draft)
MAXIMUM CREDIT: $750 per qualifying veteran ($1,500 on a joint return if both spouses qualify)
PHASE-OUT: reduced by 10% of AGI over $30,000 → zero at $37,500
## Key eligibility rules
- The veteran (or spouse on a joint return) has separated from service
- AGI under $37,500
- Did NOT claim the military pension or retirement pay subtraction on Schedule M1M
- At least ONE of:
  - Honorably discharged and receives a military pension or other retirement pay for the service
  - Served at least 20 years
  - Has a VA-rated service-connected disability of 100% total and permanent
- Part-year residents and nonresidents prorate by the Schedule M1NR percentage
## Credit or subtraction
For most retirees with a military pension, the pension SUBTRACTION is worth more. The credit fits low-AGI veterans whose small or zero tax makes a subtraction worth little, and those with a 100% disability rating but no pension.
## Required Information
- Proof of honorable discharge, 20 years of service, or 100% disability rating
- AGI
- Whether the military pension subtraction was claimed
## Questions
- Have you separated from the military, and do you get a pension, have 20+ years, or have a 100% VA disability rating?
- Is your AGI under $37,500?
- Did you claim the military pension subtraction?
## Common Errors
- Claiming both the credit and the military pension subtraction
- Forgetting to double the credit when both spouses qualify
## Prompt
- I am a retired veteran with low income. Is there a Minnesota credit?
