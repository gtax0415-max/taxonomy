# Military Personnel (Residency, Spouses, Extensions)
## Residency
- A Minnesota resident who enlists stays a Minnesota resident until establishing domicile elsewhere
- Do not complete Schedule M1NR unless you or your spouse is a part-year resident or nonresident
- Filing test for part-year residents and nonresidents: leave out active-duty pay for service OUTSIDE Minnesota (while a resident) and active-duty pay for service IN Minnesota (while a nonresident)
## Military spouses
- RESIDENT spouse of a member stationed outside Minnesota: income earned in another state is still assignable to Minnesota
- NONRESIDENT spouse: personal service income from work in Minnesota may be exempt if the member is in Minnesota under military orders, the member is domiciled elsewhere, and the spouse is in Minnesota solely to be with the member (Income Tax Fact Sheet 5)
## Subtractions and credits (2026)
- Active-duty pay: Schedule M1M Line 22 (residents) or Line 24 (nonresidents) — deductions/subtractions/military/active-duty-military-pay.md
- National Guard and Reserve pay: Line 23
- Military pension: Line 27 (bars the Credit for Past Military Service)
- Addition for federally excluded Sinai and other hazardous-duty pay: Line 8
- Credit for Past Military Service (Schedule M1C Line 4); combat zone credit $120 per month (Form M99)
- Civilian employees of the military do not get the military pay subtraction
- Nonmilitary income taxed by another state while a Minnesota resident: credit for tax paid to another state
## Extensions
- In a combat zone or contingency operation: file and pay up to 180 days after leaving the zone or after continuous hospitalization for injuries; attach a statement
- Stationed outside the U.S. but not in a combat zone: until October 15 to file; payment still due April 15
