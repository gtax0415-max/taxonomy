# Residency, Part-Year Residents, and Nonresidents (Schedule M1NR)
## Residency tests
- DOMICILE: Minnesota was your permanent home
- 183-DAY RULE (statutory resident): in Minnesota 183 days or more AND you or your spouse owned, rented, or occupied an abode (house, townhouse, condo, apartment, mobile home, or cabin with cooking and bathing facilities usable year-round). Treated as a resident for the period the abode was maintained
- Military: Minnesotans who enlist remain residents until they establish domicile elsewhere (filing/military-personnel.md)
- Abroad: Minnesota domiciliaries who claim the federal foreign earned income exclusion and do not homestead in Minnesota are treated as nonresidents for that period (income/types-of-income/federal-foreign-income-exclusion.md)
## Who uses Schedule M1NR
Full-year nonresidents and part-year residents (you or your spouse) who must file because Minnesota gross income is $15,300 or more, or who file to get a refund or claim refundable credits. Enter dates of residency and the other state at the top.
## How it works (2026 near-final draft, 8/4/2026)
- Column A: federal amounts. Column B: Minnesota portion — all income while a Minnesota resident plus Minnesota-source income while a nonresident
- Line 15: total income (A and B); additions and subtractions are allocated the same way (for example, line 9 out-of-state bond interest, line 10 bonus depreciation addition, line 24 bonus depreciation subtraction, line 25 U.S. bond interest and nonresident military pay received while a nonresident, line 26 prior-year partnership or S corporation sale)
- Line 30: the Minnesota PERCENTAGE (decimal) used to prorate many credits (Marriage Credit, Child and Working Family Credits, K-12, 529, Past Military Service, and others)
- Line 32: tax = tax on ALL income as a full-year resident × Minnesota percentage → Form M1, Line 13
- Lines 28 and 29 → Form M1, Lines 13a and 13b (enter negative 13b as negative)
- Minnesota gross income below $15,300: complete all lines anyway when claiming refundable credits (each credit schedule has a worksheet)
## Minnesota-source income for nonresidents (assigned to Minnesota)
| Income | Assigned to Minnesota? |
|---|---|
| Wages, salaries, tips, bonuses, severance, stock options | Yes, for work performed in Minnesota (except reciprocity wages) |
| Interest, dividends | No |
| State refunds, alimony, unemployment | No |
| Qualified pensions, IRAs, annuities, deferred compensation | No |
| Nonqualified plans | Yes, if earned in Minnesota |
| Gains on tangible property (not used in a business) | Yes, for property in Minnesota |
| Gain on a partnership interest | Yes, to the extent of the partnership's Minnesota property and sales |
| Sole proprietorship, partnership, S corporation, farm income | Yes, to the extent conducted in Minnesota |
| Rent and royalty income | Yes, for property located or used in Minnesota |
| Installment sales | Gain yes if the asset was in Minnesota and the gain is assigned to Minnesota; interest no |
| Gambling winnings | Yes, from Minnesota casinos (including tribal), lottery tickets bought in Minnesota, lawful gambling and pari-mutuel wagering in Minnesota |
## Married couples
Spouses who file jointly federally file jointly in Minnesota even if only one has Minnesota income; Schedule M1NR shows each spouse's residency.
## Michigan and North Dakota residents
Reciprocity wages are excluded from column B. If only reciprocity wages, use the Schedule M1M Line 20 subtraction instead of M1NR (deductions/subtractions/other/reciprocity-income.md).
## Credits for part-year residents
- Child and Working Family Credits, Renter's Credit, Child and Dependent Care Credit: part-year residents qualify on a prorated basis; full-year nonresidents do not (except as each credit file states)
- Credit for tax paid to another state: available for income earned while a Minnesota resident (Schedule M1CR)
## Questions
- When did you move into or out of Minnesota? Where was your permanent home?
- Did you spend 183+ days in Minnesota and keep a place to live there?
- What income did you earn in Minnesota while a nonresident?
## Common Errors
- Computing tax only on Minnesota income instead of tax on all income × percentage
- Assigning interest, dividends, or IRA distributions received as a nonresident to Minnesota
- Ignoring the 183-day rule for a seasonal home
