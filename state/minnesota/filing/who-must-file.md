# Who Must File a Minnesota Income Tax Return (2026)
## Full-year residents
A full-year resident who is not a dependent and is required to file a federal return must file Form M1. The 2026 gross income thresholds (derived from the 2026 standard deduction):
| Filing status | Age situation | File if income is at least |
|---|---|---|
| Single | Born on or after January 2, 1962 | $15,300 |
| Single | Born before January 2, 1962 | $17,300 |
| Married filing jointly | Both born on or after January 2, 1962 | $30,600 |
| Married filing jointly | One born before January 2, 1962 | $32,200 |
| Married filing jointly | Both born before January 2, 1962 | $33,800 |
| Head of household | Born on or after January 2, 1962 | $23,000 |
| Head of household | Born before January 2, 1962 | $25,000 |
| Married filing separately | Any age | $5 |
| Qualifying surviving spouse | Born on or after January 2, 1962 | $30,600 |
| Qualifying surviving spouse | Born before January 2, 1962 | $32,200 |
## Must file regardless of income
- Received ADVANCE PAYMENTS of the 2026 Minnesota Child Tax Credit (elected on the 2025 return)
- Electing advance payments of the 2027 Child Tax Credit (must file an original return by April 15)
## Should file to get money back
- Minnesota tax was withheld (W-2, 1099, W-2G, KPI/KS/KF)
- Estimated payments were made
- To claim refundable credits: Child and Working Family, Child and Dependent Care, K-12 Education, Renter's Credit, Parents of Stillborn Children, and others
## Residency
A Minnesota RESIDENT is someone whose permanent home (domicile) was Minnesota, OR who was in Minnesota 183 days or more AND kept an abode there (house, apartment, condo, mobile home, or cabin with cooking and bathing facilities usable year-round). A 183-day statutory resident is a resident for the period the abode was maintained.
## Part-year residents and nonresidents
File Form M1 with Schedule M1NR if Minnesota gross income is $15,300 OR MORE. Minnesota gross income = all income while a resident + Minnesota-source income while a nonresident:
- Wages for work in Minnesota
- Gross rents and royalties from Minnesota property
- Gains on Minnesota land or tangible property
- Gross gambling winnings in Minnesota
- Gains on a partnership interest (to the Minnesota share), goodwill or non-compete income tied to a Minnesota business, accelerated installment gains
- Gross income from a business conducted in Minnesota
Married couples who file jointly federally file jointly in Minnesota even if only one spouse has Minnesota income.
## Dependents
File if gross income (earned + unearned) exceeds the dependent standard deduction: the greater of $1,300 or earned income + $350, up to $15,300 plus age/blind amounts (minnesota/deductions/standard/dependent-standard-deduction.md).
## Reciprocity (Michigan and North Dakota residents)
Wages covered by reciprocity are not Minnesota income. File only to recover Minnesota withholding (minnesota/deductions/subtractions/other/reciprocity-income.md), or with Schedule M1NR if other Minnesota-source income is $15,300 or more.
## Aliens
- Resident aliens (federal definition): same rules as citizens
- Nonresident aliens who are full-year Minnesota residents under the 183-day rule and must file federally must file Form M1
- Nonresident aliens who are part-year residents or nonresidents under the 183-day rule must file with Schedule M1NR if Minnesota-source gross income is at least $5
## Deceased taxpayers
A return is required for a decedent who met the filing requirement or received advance Child Tax Credit payments. Write "DECD" and the date of death after the name; a personal representative attaches the court appointment; with no spouse or representative, use Form M23 to claim the refund.
## Questions
- Where was your permanent home in 2026? How many days were you in Minnesota?
- What was your total income, and how much came from Minnesota sources?
- Did you receive Minnesota advance child tax credit payments?
- Can someone claim you as a dependent?
## Common Errors
- Using the 2025 thresholds ($14,950 and the January 2, 1961 birth date)
- Not filing after receiving advance Child Tax Credit payments
- A renter skipping the return and missing the Renter's Credit
- Ignoring the 183-day rule for a seasonal Minnesota home
