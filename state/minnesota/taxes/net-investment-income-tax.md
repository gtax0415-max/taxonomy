# Minnesota Net Investment Income Tax (Schedule NIIT)
## Description
Since tax year 2024, Minnesota imposes an additional 1% tax on net investment income above $1,000,000 for individuals, estates, and trusts. It is separate from, and in addition to, the federal 3.8% NIIT.
## 2026 figures (2026 Schedule NIIT draft)
RATE: 1%
THRESHOLD: $1,000,000 of Minnesota net investment income — the SAME for every filing status (not indexed, not doubled for joint filers)
CORPORATIONS: not subject
## Minnesota net investment income
Starts from federal net investment income under IRC section 1411 (Form 8960 line 8: interest, dividends, capital gains, rents, royalties, nonqualified annuities, passive business income), with Minnesota modifications:
- EXCLUDE net gains from sales of Class 2a agricultural property in Minnesota
- EXCLUDE interest and fund dividends from U.S. obligations that federal law bars states from taxing
- Allocate to Minnesota for nonresidents and part-year residents
## Key rules
- No credit for taxes paid to another state against Minnesota NIIT
- Cannot be paid through composite income tax or PTE tax. Partners and shareholders who satisfy their Minnesota filing through composite or PTE tax must still file Form M1 with Schedule NIIT, reporting only the NIIT, and check the NIIT box at the bottom of Form M1
- Subject to estimated tax rules — adjust withholding or estimated payments
## Legislative note
A 2026 bill (H.F. 4123) proposed adding active trade or business income to the base. The 2026 Schedule NIIT still starts from Form 8960 line 8, so the proposal is not reflected for 2026.
## Required Information
- Federal Form 8960; Class 2a farmland sale details; U.S. obligation interest; Schedule M1NR if not a full-year resident
## Questions
- Is your net investment income over $1 million?
- Did you sell Minnesota farmland classified 2a?
## Common Errors
- Doubling the $1 million threshold for married couples
- Claiming the other-state credit against it
- Assuming a PTE or composite election covers it
## Prompt
- I sold stock for a $3 million gain. Is there extra Minnesota tax?
