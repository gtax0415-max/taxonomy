# Dividends from Mutual Funds (Exempt-Interest Dividends)
## Description
Exempt-interest dividends from a municipal bond mutual fund are federally tax-free. Minnesota taxes the part that comes from non-Minnesota bonds, under a 95% rule.
## 2026 rule — the 95% test (per fund)
- If 95% OR MORE of the fund's exempt-interest dividends came from Minnesota bonds → add back only the NON-Minnesota portion
- If LESS than 95% came from Minnesota bonds → add back ALL of the fund's exempt-interest dividends, including the Minnesota portion
This is a cliff: a national muni fund with, say, 8% Minnesota bonds is 100% taxable to Minnesota.
## Related
- Dividends from funds holding U.S. government obligations are SUBTRACTED (minnesota/deductions/subtractions/other/net-interest-from-us-bonds.md)
## Required Information
- Box 12 of each Form 1099-DIV
- Each fund's percentage of income from Minnesota obligations (fund tax letter)
## Questions
- Do you own municipal bond mutual funds? Are any Minnesota-specific funds?
## Common Errors
- Pro-rating a national fund by its Minnesota percentage (only allowed at 95%+)
- Missing the add-back because the dividend is "tax-exempt" on the 1099
## Prompt
- I have a tax-exempt municipal bond fund. What does Minnesota tax?
