# Lump-Sum Distributions
## Description
Taxpayers born before 1936 can use federal Form 4972 to tax a qualified lump-sum retirement distribution outside regular income (10-year averaging and/or the 20% capital gain election). Because that income is not in federal AGI, Minnesota picks it up in two places.
## How it works
- CAPITAL GAIN portion (Form 4972, line 6) → ADDITION on Schedule M1M, Line 4
- ORDINARY portion taxed by Form 4972 → separate Minnesota tax on Schedule M1LS, reported on Form M1, Line 14a (check box c)
- Enclose Form 4972
## Interactions
- Schedule M1R (age 65/disabled subtraction): add Form 4972 line 10 and the line 6 capital gain to AGI when testing income
- Only Minnesota residents at the time of any part of the distribution file Schedule M1LS
## Required Information
- Form 4972 and Form 1099-R
## Questions
- Did you use federal Form 4972 for a lump-sum distribution?
## Common Errors
- Forgetting the Line 4 capital gain addition
- Omitting Schedule M1LS
## Prompt
- I used Form 4972 for my pension lump sum.
