# 529 Distributions Used for K-12 or Postsecondary Credentialing Expenses
## Description
Federally, 529 money can pay K-12 tuition and (after July 4, 2025, under OBBBA) postsecondary credentialing expenses tax-free. Minnesota does NOT treat those as qualified expenses, so it adds back the earnings.
## 2026 rule
Add the LESSER of:
1. Total earnings on Form 1099-Q for the year, or
2. 2026 distributions used for K-12 tuition and other K-12 expenses PLUS distributions used for qualified postsecondary credentialing expenses
## What counts as nonqualified for Minnesota
- K-12: tuition, course materials, books, instructional materials, tutoring, standardized and AP test fees, college admission exams
- Credentialing: postsecondary credential programs, registered apprenticeships, occupational or professional licenses, recognized credentials under the Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act
## 2026 change
The 2026 Tax Bill added postsecondary credentialing expenses to this addition, effective for payments received starting July 5, 2025.
## Separate recapture
If Minnesota 529 credits or subtractions were claimed in prior years, distributions not used for Minnesota-qualified expenses can also trigger RECAPTURE TAX on Schedule M1529 (Form M1, Line 14a).
## Required Information
- Form 1099-Q; amounts spent on K-12 and credentialing
## Questions
- Did you use 529 money for private school tuition or a certificate or license program?
## Common Errors
- Adding back the full distribution instead of the lesser of earnings or K-12/credentialing amounts
- Missing the separate Schedule M1529 recapture
## Prompt
- I used my 529 for private school tuition. Does Minnesota tax it?
