# Federal Foreign Income Exclusion — Minnesota Treatment
## Description
Minnesotans who live and work abroad and qualify for the federal foreign earned income exclusion can be treated as NONRESIDENTS of Minnesota for that period.
## Minnesota rule
A Minnesota domiciliary is treated as a nonresident for the period abroad if BOTH:
- They qualify for and claim the federal foreign earned income exclusion (Form 2555: bona fide residence or physical presence test)
- They do NOT homestead property in Minnesota
During that period they generally pay no Minnesota tax on foreign earned income and some other income.
## Filing effect
- Use Minnesota's nonresident income rules to decide what is Minnesota-source (minnesota/filing/who-must-file.md)
- File only if Minnesota gross income meets the filing requirement ($15,300 for 2026 nonresidents and part-year residents)
- Years of leaving and returning: file as a PART-YEAR resident, taxing all income while a Minnesota resident
## Interactions
- Excluded income is not in federal AGI, so it never starts in Minnesota income anyway; the nonresident treatment matters most for income NOT covered by the exclusion (for example, investment income received while abroad)
- Homesteading Minnesota property while abroad defeats nonresident treatment
- Claiming Form 2555 bars the federal Earned Income Credit; Minnesota's Working Family Credit uses federal EIC eligibility rules
## Questions
- Did you live abroad in 2026 and claim the foreign earned income exclusion?
- Did you keep a homesteaded property in Minnesota?
- When did you leave or return?
## Common Errors
- Keeping a Minnesota homestead while claiming nonresident treatment
- Filing as a full-year resident in the year of departure or return
