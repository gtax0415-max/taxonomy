# Underpayment of Estimated Tax Penalty (Schedule M15)
## 2026 figures (2026 Schedule M15 near-final draft, 8/3/2026)
THRESHOLD: no penalty unless 2026 tax (Form M1 Line 17) minus withholding and refundable credits (Lines 20 + 22) is $500 or more
REQUIRED ANNUAL PAYMENT: lesser of 90% of 2026 tax (66.7% farmers and commercial fishermen) or 100% of 2025 tax (110% if 2025 federal AGI was over $150,000)
PENALTY RATE USED ON THE 2026 SCHEDULE: 7% per year on each underpaid installment
INSTALLMENTS: 25% each, due April 15, June 15, September 15, 2026 and January 15, 2027
ANNUALIZED METHOD PERCENTAGES: 22.5% / 45% / 67.5% / 90% for periods ending March 31, May 31, August 31, December 31, 2026
## No penalty if
- No Minnesota tax liability on Line 17 of the 2025 Form M1, a full-year Minnesota resident in 2025, and the 2025 return covered 12 months
- The IRS waived the federal penalty (newly retired or disabled, casualty, disaster, other unusual circumstances) — attach the federal request
- Farmers and commercial fishermen who filed and paid in full by March 1, 2027, or paid two-thirds by January 15, 2027
## Methods
- OPTIONAL SHORT METHOD: only if no estimated payments (withholding only) or four equal on-time payments. Penalty = 2% of the total underpayment, reduced for amounts paid before April 15, 2027 (days × 0.000192)
- REGULAR METHOD: column by column; withholding and refundable credits are spread evenly over the four periods unless shown otherwise; a refund applied from the prior year counts toward the first period; overpayments first cure earlier underpayments
- Filing and paying in full by January 31, 2027 lets the tax paid count as paid on January 15, 2027
- ANNUALIZED INCOME METHOD: for uneven income; uses federal Form 2210 Schedule AI with Minnesota deductions, additions, subtractions, and Minnesota AMT (exemptions $97,470 / $48,740 / $73,100, rate 6.75%). If used for one installment, use it for all
## Nonresidents and part-year residents
Compute the required annual payment using adjusted gross income assignable to Minnesota.
## Questions
- Did you owe $500 or more after withholding and refundable credits?
- Did you make estimated payments, and on what dates?
- Was your 2025 federal AGI over $150,000?
## Common Errors
- Ignoring refundable credits, which Minnesota counts as payments
- Using 100% instead of 110% of prior-year tax when 2025 AGI exceeded $150,000
- Using the short method after paying an installment early (it overstates the penalty)
