# Gain on the Sale of a Principal Residence
## Description
Gain on selling a main home is exempt up to the limit, matching the federal exclusion. Only gain above the limit is reported.

## Notes
- Up to $250,000 per taxpayer; $500,000 for a married couple filing on the same return (Status 2 or 4)
- The home must have been owned and used as the principal residence for at least two of the five years ending on the sale date
- Gain above the limit is reported through AR1000D (and gets the 50% long-term exclusion if held over a year)
- No change for 2026

## Required Information
- Sale price, selling costs, basis, and improvements
- Ownership and use dates
- Any depreciation taken for business or rental use

## Questions
- Did you sell your home in 2026?
- Did you own and live in it for at least two of the last five years?
- Was any part used for business or rental?

## Common Errors
- Reporting excluded gain
- Claiming the exclusion without meeting the two-year tests
- Forgetting depreciation recapture from business or rental use

## Prompt
- I sold my house this year at a profit.
- We sold our home of 10 years for a $300,000 gain.
