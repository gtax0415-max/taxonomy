# Traditional IRA Distributions Before 59 1/2 and Taxable Annuities
## Description
Taxable distributions that do NOT qualify for the $6,000 retirement exemption: early traditional IRA withdrawals and taxable lump sums and annuities not from an employer plan. Arkansas also charges 10% of the federal early-distribution additional tax.

## Notes
- Enter taxable early traditional IRA distributions and other taxable annuities; attach 1099-Rs and federal Form 5329
- IRA withdrawals before 59 1/2 for medical expenses, higher education, or a first-time home do NOT qualify for the $6,000 exemption
- IRA distributions on death or disability DO qualify (line 18 instead)
- Line 32: 10% of the federal Form 5329 Part I additional tax
- Rollovers are not taxable but must be documented
- 529-to-Roth rollovers are exempt (Act 802 of 2025)
- Roth conversions: the taxable amount from federal Form 8606 (pro-rata rule) goes here
- No line 32 tax when a federal exception applies (Form 1099-R code 2, or an exception claimed on Form 5329)
- No change for 2026

## Required Information
- Each 1099-R and its distribution code
- Age at distribution
- Form 5329

## Questions
- Did you take money from a traditional IRA before age 59 1/2?
- Was it because of death or disability?
- Did you roll it over within 60 days?

## Common Errors
- Applying the $6,000 exemption to an early IRA withdrawal
- Forgetting the line 32 additional tax
- Putting death or disability distributions here instead of line 18
- Reporting a documented rollover as taxable

## Prompt
- I took $10,000 from my IRA at age 45.
- I inherited an IRA and took a distribution.
