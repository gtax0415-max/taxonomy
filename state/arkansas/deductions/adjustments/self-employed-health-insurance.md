# Self-employed Health Insurance (Worksheet)
## Description
Deduction for health insurance premiums paid by a self-employed person (sole proprietor, partner, or more-than-2% S corporation shareholder) for themselves, a spouse, and dependents. Figured on the Arkansas worksheet and limited to the business's net profit.

## Amounts
- Deduction limited to net profit from the business under which the plan is established (less any self-employed retirement contributions for that business)
- Qualified long-term care premiums are included only up to the 2026 age-based limits:
  - Age 40 or younger: $500
  - 41–50: $930
  - 51–60: $1,860
  - 61–70: $4,960
  - 71 or older: $6,200
- Medicare premiums (Parts A, B, D) can qualify

## Eligibility
- Net profit from self-employment in 2026
- Not eligible to participate in an employer-subsidized health plan (own or spouse's) for the months claimed

## Notes
- Premiums paid with a premium tax credit reduce the deductible amount; the federal Form 8962/7206 interaction should be resolved first
- Premiums deducted here cannot also be itemized as medical expenses on Form AR3

## Required Information
- Premiums paid by month and by person covered
- Net self-employment profit from the business sponsoring the plan
- Months eligible for an employer-subsidized plan
- Ages for any long-term care premiums

## Questions
- Were you self-employed with a net profit in 2026?
- Did you pay your own health, dental, or long-term care premiums?
- Were you or your spouse eligible for a subsidized employer plan in any month?
- Did you receive a premium tax credit?

## Common Errors
- Deducting more than net business profit
- Claiming months when an employer-subsidized plan was available
- Including long-term care premiums above the age limits
- Also itemizing the same premiums as medical expenses

## Prompt
- I'm self-employed and pay my own health insurance.
- I'm a partner in an LLC and pay for my family's coverage.
