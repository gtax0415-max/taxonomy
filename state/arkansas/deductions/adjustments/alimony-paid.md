# Alimony and Separate Maintenance Paid
## Description
Deduction for alimony or separate maintenance paid under a court order. Arkansas lists it as its own adjustment and its instructions contain no cutoff for divorces after 2018, so it can apply even where the federal deduction is gone.

## Amount
- No dollar cap: total court-ordered payments made in 2026
- The recipient's NAME and SSN must be entered on AR1000ADJ line 12

## Notes
- FEDERAL DIFFERENCE: for divorce or separation instruments executed after December 31, 2018, alimony is not deductible federally (TCJA). Arkansas adopts IRC sections individually and its instructions only require a court order, so the deduction remains on the Arkansas return. Confirm this when the 2026 AR1000F instructions are released
- Child support and property settlements are NOT alimony
- The recipient reports the payments as income on the Arkansas return

## Required Information
- Court order or decree, and its date
- Payments made in 2026
- Recipient's name and SSN
- Any part of the payments designated as child support

## Questions
- Do you pay alimony or separate maintenance under a court order?
- How much did you pay in 2026?
- What is the recipient's name and Social Security number?
- Is any part of the payment child support?

## Common Errors
- Skipping the Arkansas deduction because the federal return doesn't allow it
- Including child support or a property settlement
- Leaving out the recipient's SSN
- Deducting voluntary payments not required by a court order

## Prompt
- I pay my ex-spouse alimony every month.
- My divorce was in 2020. Can I still deduct alimony in Arkansas?
