# Contributions to an Intergenerational Trust
## Description
Arkansas-only deduction for contributions to a long-term intergenerational trust, a trust set up for a minor to fund that person's retirement. There is no federal counterpart. Earnings grow tax-deferred, and distributions are taxable to the beneficiary.

## Amounts
- Maximum deduction: $4,000 per year
- 2026: unchanged; no Arkansas act amended this deduction

## Eligibility
- Trust established for an individual UNDER AGE 18
- Trustee must be an Arkansas resident
- No trust funds may be distributed to the beneficiary until the beneficiary reaches AGE 55
- The deduction is taken by the contributor

## Notes
- All distributions (principal and earnings) are taxable to the beneficiary when received
- A trust that allows earlier distributions, or has a non-resident trustee, does not qualify
- Not the same as a 529 plan (line 2) or an ABLE account (line 18)

## Required Information
- Trust agreement and its terms
- Beneficiary's date of birth and age when the trust was established
- Trustee's name and Arkansas residence
- Contributions made in 2026

## Questions
- Did you contribute to a trust for a child's or grandchild's retirement?
- Was the beneficiary under 18 when the trust was created?
- Is the trustee an Arkansas resident?
- Does the trust bar distributions until age 55?

## Common Errors
- Deducting more than $4,000
- Claiming contributions to an ordinary trust, custodial (UTMA) account, or 529 plan here
- Trustee living outside Arkansas
- Trust terms allowing distributions before age 55

## Prompt
- I set up a retirement trust for my grandchild.
- Can I deduct money I put in a trust for my kids in Arkansas?
