# Military Reserve Expenses
## Description
Deduction for unreimbursed travel expenses of a member of a reserve component (Army, Navy, Marine Corps, Air Force, Coast Guard Reserve, Army or Air National Guard, Public Health Service reserve) who travels more than 100 miles from home for reserve duty. Follows the federal rule figured on Form 2106.

## Amounts
- Travel more than 100 miles from home and overnight
- Lodging and meals limited to the federal per diem rate for the location
- Car expenses at the 2026 business standard mileage rate of 72.5 cents per mile, plus parking and tolls
- No overall dollar cap

## Notes
- Different from Arkansas's military income exemption (Act 508 of 2025 exempts military compensation and retirement benefits). That exemption goes on its own line, not here
- Reimbursed expenses are not deductible
- Travel of 100 miles or less is not deductible

## Required Information
- Dates and locations of reserve duty
- Miles from home to the duty location
- Unreimbursed lodging, meals, and transportation
- Federal Form 2106

## Questions
- Are you a member of the Reserves or National Guard?
- Did you travel more than 100 miles from home for drills or training in 2026?
- Were any of your costs reimbursed?

## Common Errors
- Claiming trips of 100 miles or less
- Exceeding the federal per diem rate
- Including reimbursed costs
- Putting military pay exemptions on this line

## Prompt
- I'm in the National Guard and drive 150 miles to drill each month.
- Can I deduct travel for Army Reserve training in Arkansas?
