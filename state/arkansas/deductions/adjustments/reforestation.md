# Reforestation Deduction
## Description
Deduction for the cost of forestation or reforestation of timber property held for the production of timber, following the federal IRC Section 194 rules.

## Amounts
- Expense up to $10,000 of reforestation costs per qualified timber property per year ($5,000 if married filing separately)
- Costs above $10,000 are amortized over 84 months (half-year convention)
- No overall annual cap across properties

## Qualified expenses
- Site preparation
- Seeds or seedlings
- Labor and tool costs, including depreciation of equipment used in planting

## Notes
- Do not deduct here any reforestation cost already deducted on Schedule C or Schedule F
- Costs reimbursed under a government cost-share program and excluded from income are not deductible
- The property must be at least one acre and held for timber production

## Required Information
- Each qualified timber property and its acreage
- Reforestation costs by property and year
- Prior-year amortization schedule
- Cost-share payments received

## Questions
- Did you plant or replant trees on land held for timber in 2026?
- How much did you spend per property?
- Are you amortizing reforestation costs from earlier years?
- Did you receive cost-share payments?

## Common Errors
- Deducting the same costs on Schedule C/F and here
- Expensing more than $10,000 per property
- Forgetting ongoing amortization from earlier years
- Including the cost of the land

## Prompt
- I replanted pine on my timberland after harvesting.
- I spent $14,000 reforesting 40 acres.
