# Payments to an HSA
## Description
Deduction for Health Savings Account contributions made directly by the taxpayer (not through payroll). Arkansas follows the federal deduction figured on Form 8889, and the federal form must be attached.

## Amounts
- Contribution limit for 2026: $4,400 self-only, $8,750 family (2025: $4,300 / $8,550) (Rev. Proc. 2025-19)
- Catch-up at age 55 or older: $1,000 (not indexed)
- HDHP for 2026: minimum deductible $1,700 self-only / $3,400 family; out-of-pocket maximum $8,500 / $17,000
- Limits are reduced by any Archer MSA contributions and prorated for months not covered (unless the last-month rule applies)

## Notes
- Payroll (W-2 code W) contributions are already excluded from wages and are NOT deducted again
- The 2026 federal OBBBA changes (bronze and catastrophic marketplace plans treated as HDHPs, direct primary care arrangements) apply on Form 8889. Arkansas adopts IRC sections individually, so confirm DFA follows them when 2026 instructions are released
- Coverage by Medicare or a non-HDHP plan ends eligibility

## Required Information
- Personal (non-payroll) HSA contributions for 2026
- Employer/payroll contributions (W-2 code W)
- Coverage type and months covered by the HDHP
- Age at year-end
- Federal Form 8889

## Questions
- Did you contribute to an HSA outside of payroll in 2026?
- Was your HDHP self-only or family coverage, and for how many months?
- Were you 55 or older by December 31, 2026?
- Did you enroll in Medicare during the year?

## Common Errors
- Deducting W-2 code W amounts that were already excluded
- Using 2025 limits ($4,300 / $8,550)
- Not attaching federal Form 8889
- Exceeding the limit after a mid-year change in coverage
- Putting Archer MSA contributions on this line

## Prompt
- I contributed to my HSA directly from my bank account.
- My employer funds my HSA through payroll. Do I deduct that in Arkansas?
