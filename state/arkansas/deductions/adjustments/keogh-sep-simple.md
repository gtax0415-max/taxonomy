# KEOGH, Self-employed SEP and SIMPLE Plans
## Description
Deduction for contributions a self-employed person makes to their own Keogh (HR-10), SEP, SIMPLE, or solo 401(k) plan. The allowed amount depends on the plan type and follows the federal limits.

## Amounts
- Overall defined contribution limit (Keogh, SEP, solo 401(k)): $72,000 (2025: $70,000)
- SEP / profit-sharing: employer contribution up to 25% of compensation (about 20% of net self-employment earnings for the owner)
- Solo 401(k) elective deferral: $24,500 (2025: $23,500); catch-up at 50+ $8,000; ages 60–63 $11,250
- SIMPLE: $17,000 (2025: $16,500); catch-up at 50+ $4,000; ages 60–63 $5,250
- Compensation considered: capped at $360,000

## Notes
- For the owner, "compensation" is net self-employment earnings after the deduction for half of self-employment tax and after this contribution
- Contributions made for employees are a business expense on Schedule C/F, not this line
- Contributions to an employer's 401(k) through W-2 wages are already excluded and do not belong here

## Required Information
- Plan type and contributions for 2026 (including those made by the due date)
- Net self-employment earnings
- Age at year-end
- Federal Schedule SE

## Questions
- Are you self-employed with your own retirement plan?
- Is it a SEP, SIMPLE, solo 401(k), or other Keogh plan?
- How much did you contribute for yourself for 2026?
- Were you 50 or older (or 60–63) at year-end?

## Common Errors
- Using 25% of net profit instead of the reduced owner rate (about 20%)
- Entering contributions made for employees
- Using 2025 limits
- Including W-2 401(k) deferrals

## Prompt
- I'm self-employed and contributed to a SEP-IRA.
- I have a solo 401(k) for my consulting business.
