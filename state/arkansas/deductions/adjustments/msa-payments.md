# Payments to an MSA
## Description
Deduction for taxpayer contributions to an existing Archer Medical Savings Account. New Archer MSAs cannot be opened after 2007, but contributions to existing accounts continue. The taxpayer must be covered ONLY by a qualifying high deductible health plan (HDHP).

## Amounts
HDHP requirements for 2026 (Archer MSA, not HSA):
- Self-only: annual deductible $2,900 to $4,400; out-of-pocket maximum $5,850
- Family: annual deductible $5,850 to $8,750; out-of-pocket maximum $10,700

Contribution limit (monthly, prorated by months covered):
- Self-only: 1/12 of 65% of the plan's annual deductible
- Family: 1/12 of 75% of the plan's annual deductible
- Also limited to compensation from the employer providing the HDHP (or net self-employment income)

## Notes
- Contributions made by the employer (W-2 code R) are already excluded and are NOT deducted again
- If both the taxpayer and the employer contribute in the same year, the taxpayer's contribution is not deductible
- Archer MSA contributions reduce the maximum allowed to an HSA
- Archer MSAs are now rare; check that the account really is an Archer MSA and not an HSA (AR1000ADJ line 5)

## Required Information
- Taxpayer contributions for 2026 (Form 5498-SA)
- Employer contributions (W-2 code R)
- HDHP deductible, out-of-pocket maximum, coverage type and months covered
- Federal Form 8853

## Questions
- Do you have an Archer MSA (opened before 2008)?
- Did you or your employer contribute to it in 2026?
- Was your health plan self-only or family, and what was the deductible?
- How many months were you covered?

## Common Errors
- Entering HSA contributions here instead of on line 5
- Deducting employer contributions already excluded from wages
- Using the HSA HDHP limits instead of the Archer MSA limits
- Not prorating for partial-year coverage

## Prompt
- I still have an Archer MSA and contributed to it.
- My employer put money in my medical savings account.
