# Student Loan Interest (Worksheet)
## Description
Deduction for interest paid on a qualified student loan, figured on the Arkansas worksheet. The structure mirrors the federal deduction, but the phase-out is tested against Arkansas modified AGI.

## Amounts
- Maximum deduction: $2,500 of interest paid (not indexed)
- Phase-out for 2026 (federal figures, Rev. Proc. 2025-32):
  - Single, head of household, surviving spouse (Status 1, 3, 6): $85,000–$100,000 (unchanged from 2025)
  - Married filing jointly or separately on the same return (Status 2, 4): $175,000–$205,000 (2025: $170,000–$200,000)
- Arkansas's 2025 worksheet used $85,000/$170,000 starting points, matching the 2025 federal figures. Confirm the 2026 Arkansas worksheet when DFA releases it

## Eligibility
- Interest was paid during 2026 on a qualified student loan
- Filing status is NOT married filing separately on different returns (Status 5)
- Not claimed as a dependent on another return
- Status 4 filers: the income test uses the COMBINED income of both spouses

## Required Information
- Interest paid (Form 1098-E)
- Arkansas modified AGI
- Filing status and dependency status

## Questions
- Did you pay interest on a student loan in 2026?
- Is your filing status married filing separately on different returns?
- Can someone else claim you as a dependent?
- Is your Arkansas income near the phase-out range?

## Common Errors
- Entering more than $2,500
- Claiming it under Status 5
- Status 4 filers testing each spouse's income separately instead of combined
- Using the 2025 joint phase-out ($170,000) for 2026
- Deducting principal or interest on a non-qualified (e.g., personal) loan

## Prompt
- I paid $1,800 in student loan interest this year.
- We file married separately on the same Arkansas return. Can we deduct student loan interest?
