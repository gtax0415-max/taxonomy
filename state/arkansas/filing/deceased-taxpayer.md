# Deceased Taxpayer or Spouse
## Description
A final return for a taxpayer who died during 2026 is filed as if they had lived the whole year, with the deceased box checked beside their name.

## Notes
- Check the deceased box next to the primary's or spouse's name
- File as if the person lived the entire year; the surviving spouse may still file jointly or Status 4 for 2026
- The full personal tax credit is allowed
- 65-OR-OVER credit: the box counts only if the person actually reached 65 before death. The 2026 test is age 65 on January 1, 2027 for a living taxpayer
- Refund checks are issued to "Estate of [name]"; the bank may require a death certificate, will, or power of attorney to cash it
- A dependent who died during the year still counts for the full dependent credit
- After 2026, a surviving spouse with a dependent child may qualify for Status 6 in 2027 and 2028

## Required Information
- Date of death
- Date of birth (for the age test)
- Who is filing (surviving spouse or personal representative)

## Questions
- Who died, and on what date?
- Was the person 65 or older at death?
- Is a surviving spouse filing, or an executor?

## Common Errors
- Checking the 65-or-over box for someone who died before turning 65
- Prorating the personal credit for the part of the year the person lived
- Not checking the deceased box, delaying the refund

## Prompt
- My father passed away this year. How do I file his Arkansas return?
- My spouse died in 2026. Can we still file jointly?
