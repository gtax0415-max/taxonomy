# Standard Deduction (Form 140, Line 43, Box 43S)
## Description
Take the larger of the standard deduction or itemized deductions; check box 43S for the standard deduction. Arizona's standard deduction is a base amount adjusted for inflation in the same manner as the federal basic standard deduction (A.R.S. 43-1041(H)).
## Amounts
| Filing status | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Single | $15,750 | $16,100 |
| Married filing joint | $31,500 | $32,200 |
| Married filing separate | $15,750 | $16,100 |
| Head of household | $23,625 | $24,150 |

2026: $16,100 / $24,150 / $32,200. A.R.S. 43-1041 adjusts Arizona's standard deduction for inflation in the same manner as the federal basic standard deduction, so the 2026 amounts are expected to equal the federal $16,100 / $24,150 / $32,200; ADOR publishes the official figures with the 2026 form. The statutory base is $15,750 single and married filing separate, $23,625 head of household and $31,500 married filing joint.
## Rules
- Married filing separate: one spouse may not claim the standard deduction while the other itemizes; if this happens the standard deduction is disallowed regardless of which return was filed first. If one spouse claims 100% of the allowable itemized deductions, the other enters 0 on line 43.
- Surviving spouse: a spouse who died during the year is treated as married at the close of the year; enter the joint amount if a joint return would have been filed, or the separate amount if separate returns would have been filed.
- Standard deduction filers may add the charitable increase on line 44 and the long-term care premium subtraction (page 6, M).
- There is no additional standard deduction for age or blindness; Arizona uses the line 38 and 39 exemptions.
## Example
Head of household with line 42 of $45,000 and no itemized deductions: line 43 = $24,150, box 43S checked.
## Notes
- For 2026 the charitable increase on line 44 is 100% of contributions, capped at $1,000 (single, separate) and $2,000 (joint)
- For 2026 itemized state and local taxes are capped at $10,000 (Laws 2026, Chapter 140), which can make the standard deduction the better choice
- Form 140 line and box numbers follow the 2025 Form 140; ADOR had not released the 2026 Form 140 when this was revised, so confirm them against it
## Required Information
- Filing status
- Spouse's deduction method if married filing separate
- Itemized deduction totals, to compare
## Questions
- What is your filing status? If married filing separate, does your spouse itemize?
- Are your itemized deductions (after Arizona adjustments) more than the standard deduction?
## Common Errors
- One spouse taking the standard deduction while the other itemizes
- Adding a federal age 65 or blind amount to the Arizona standard deduction
## Prompt
- What is the Arizona standard deduction for 2026?
