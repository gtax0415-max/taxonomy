# Credit for Contributions to Qualifying Charitable Organizations (Form 321; Form 301, Lines 6 and 39)
## Description
A nonrefundable credit for cash contributions to certified qualifying charitable organizations (QCOs). Only individuals may claim it; partnerships and S corporations cannot pass it through. Unused credit carries forward five years.
## Maximum credit
| Filing status | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Single, head of household | $495 | $506 |
| Married filing joint | $987 | $1,009 |
| Married filing separate | one-half of the joint credit, up to $495 | one-half of the joint credit, up to $506 |
## What is a QCO
A charity exempt under IRC 501(c)(3), or a designated community action agency receiving Community Services Block Grant money (42 U.S.C. 9901), that spends at least 50% of its budget on services to Arizona residents who receive TANF, are low-income, or have a chronic illness or physical disability (a primary diagnosis of a severe physical condition that may require ongoing medical or surgical intervention). Services meet immediate basic needs and are provided and used in Arizona: cash assistance, medical care, behavioral health services, child care, food, clothing, shelter, job training and placement, workforce readiness and development, and similar assistance. The charity must be certified by ADOR; use the list for the year of the gift (the 2027 list for January 1 to April 15, 2027 gifts claimed on the 2026 return). Gifts through an Umbrella Charitable Organization qualify if 100% goes to a certified QCO (receipt must certify this).

## Rules
- Married filing separate: include all cash contributions made by you and your spouse.
- Do not include 2026 contributions already claimed on the 2025 return (those made January 1 to April 15, 2026).
- More than ten recurring donations to one QCO may be summarized with one date (for example 12/31/26).
- A contribution made January 1 to April 15, 2027 may be claimed on the 2026 or 2027 return; if claimed on 2026, adjust the 2027 Schedule A.
- Carryover is only the unused part of the allowed credit, not gifts above the cap. Track it on the Form 301 Credit Carryover Worksheet (credit available, credit used, carryover to 2027).
- A prior Form 321 carryover from foster care gifts is still claimed on Form 321.
## Example
Single, gave $600 to a certified QCO in 2026, 2026 tax $250: credit $506; uses $250 against tax and carries $256 forward; the $94 above the cap is never a credit.
## Notes
- For 2026, Laws 2026, Chapter 140 changes the charitable standard deduction increase to 100% of qualifying contributions, capped at $1,000 single or married filing separate and $2,000 married filing joint; a gift used for this credit still cannot be counted there
- Gifts made January 1 to April 15, 2027 can go on the 2026 return at the 2026 cap, or on the 2027 return at the higher 2027 cap ($513 single, married filing separate or head of household; $1,022 married filing joint)
- Form 301 line numbers follow the 2025 Form 301; ADOR had not released the 2026 Form 301 when this was revised, so confirm line numbers against it
## Required Information
- Receipts from certified QCOs or UCO funds showing the 5-digit code, date and amount
- Gifts made January 1 to April 15, 2027 that you want on the 2026 return
- Filing status (and both spouses' gifts if married filing separate)
- Prior Form 321 carryovers from 2021 to 2025
## Questions
- Which certified QCOs did you give to, when, and how much? Do you have the 5-digit codes?
- Did you give between January 1 and April 15, 2027 and want it on the 2026 return?
- Did you itemize or use the charitable standard deduction increase?
## Common Errors
- Including foster care charity gifts (Form 352)
- Using the 2025 cap
- Deducting the same gift on Schedule A
