# Credit for Solar Energy Devices (Form 310; Form 301, Lines 3 and 36)
## Description
An Arizona resident who is not a dependent of another taxpayer may claim a credit for installing a solar energy device in his or her Arizona residence: 25% of the cost including installation, up to $1,000, with a cumulative $1,000 limit per residence (statutory amounts under A.R.S. 43-1083, not indexed for inflation, so unchanged for 2026). Unused credit carries forward five years. A credit of up to $1,000 may also be claimed for a second personal home, but a taxpayer claiming for both homes is limited to $1,000 in the taxable year; track each home on its own Form 310. Nonresidents filing Form 140NR cannot use it.
## Qualifying devices (residential)
Solar domestic water heating; solar pool and spa heating; photovoltaic systems (including for an RV used as a residence); solar photovoltaic phones and lighting; passive solar building systems (Trombe walls, thermal mass); solar daylighting systems (not conventional skylights); wind generators; wind-powered pumps. Only complete systems qualify, not individual components.
## Not qualifying
Conventional plumbing, controls, heating and cooling systems, windows and window treatments, skylights, appliances and fans not made for photovoltaic use, paint and roof coatings, insulation and radiant barriers, weather stripping, misting systems, vegetation, solar cars, and a builder-installed solar water heater plumbing stub out. Leased devices and power purchase agreements do not qualify (ITR 13-4, Pub. 543).

## Depreciation
No depreciation deduction is allowed for a device on which the credit is claimed; depreciation included in federal AGI must be added to Arizona gross income.
## Example
A homeowner buys and installs a $14,000 photovoltaic system in 2026, having claimed $300 for a solar water heater at the same home in 2018: line 3 $3,500, line 4 $1,000, line 5 $300, line 7 $1,000, line 8 $700, line 9 $700.
## Notes
- STILL AVAILABLE FOR 2026. HB 4152 would have repealed this credit from January 1, 2026, but it was vetoed; the enacted omnibus (HB 4168, Laws 2026, Chapter 140) did not repeal it
- Form 301 line numbers follow the 2025 Form 301; ADOR had not released the 2026 Form 301 when this was revised, so confirm line numbers against it
## Required Information
- Invoice for the device including installation, and the seller's certificate
- Address of the residence (and of any second home)
- Prior Form 310 credits at the same residence (1995 through 2025) and carryovers from 2021 to 2025
- Whether any depreciation of the device was deducted federally
## Questions
- Did you buy (not lease) a solar device for your Arizona home in 2026? What did it cost installed?
- Have you claimed this credit before at the same address?
## Common Errors
- Claiming for a leased system or PPA
- Exceeding $1,000 per residence over all years
- Treating the credit as repealed for 2026 (the repeal bill was vetoed)
