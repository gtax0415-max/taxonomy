# Filing Status (Form 140, Boxes 4 to 7)
## Description
Check one box, 4 through 7. The Arizona filing status may differ from the federal one. A person who is married for federal purposes is married for Arizona purposes and must file married filing joint or married filing separate. A single person files single or, if qualified, head of household.
## Statuses
- Box 4, Married filing joint: married as of December 31, 2026, whether or not living together. Allowed even if the spouses filed separate federal returns, and if the spouse died during 2026 and the survivor did not remarry in 2026. Not allowed on Form 140 if the spouse is a nonresident alien, a resident of another state, or a part-year resident.
- Box 5, Head of household: allowed only if you qualify to file as head of household on the federal return, or you qualify as a qualifying widow or widower (qualifying surviving spouse) on the federal return. Enter the name of the qualifying child or dependent on the line provided.
- Box 6, Married filing separate: married as of December 31, 2026 and filing separately, even if a joint federal return was filed. Enter the spouse's name and SSN on the second line 1.
- Box 7, Single: single on December 31, 2026: never married; legally separated under a decree of divorce or of separate maintenance; or widowed before January 1, 2026, not remarried in 2026, and not qualifying as a qualifying widow or widower with dependent children on the federal return. For the year of a divorce see ITR 14-2 and Pub. 200.
## Federal qualifying surviving spouse
Arizona has no qualifying surviving spouse status. A taxpayer who qualifies federally as a qualifying surviving spouse (qualifying widow or widower) files as head of household (box 5), with the head of household standard deduction.
## Community property and married filing separate
Arizona is a community property state. A separate return must reflect one-half of the community income from all sources plus all of that spouse's separate income.
- Joint federal return, separate Arizona returns: adjust income so each return reflects the correct share, using page 5, item A (addition) or page 6, item A (subtraction). Keep a schedule of the adjustment (Pub. 200).
- Separate federal returns: each federal AGI should already reflect the correct income, so no adjustment is needed.
- Deductions and credits are accounted for on the same basis as community income. Both spouses must itemize or both must take the standard deduction; one may not take the standard deduction while the other itemizes. If one spouse claims 100% of the allowable itemized deductions, the other enters 0 on line 43.
- A dependent supported from community income may be claimed by either spouse, but not both (ITR 93-18, ITR 93-19). In some cases community income may be treated as separate income (ITR 93-22).
- Joint Arizona estimated payments allocated to separate returns: ITR 02-3.
- Credits with caps are generally limited to one-half of the joint amount for each spouse (Forms 310, 321, 322, 323, 340, 348, 352).
## Items that depend on filing status
| Item | Single | Head of household | Married filing joint | Married filing separate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Standard deduction (line 43) | $16,100 | $24,150 | $32,200 | $16,100 |
| Charitable increase cap (line 44) | $1,000 | not named in the statute | $2,000 | $1,000 |
| Dependent Tax Credit phase-out start (federal AGI) | $200,000 | $200,000 | $400,000 | $200,000 |
| Increased Excise Tax Credit income limit | $12,500 | $25,000 | $25,000 | $12,500 |

Standard deduction: $16,100 / $24,150 / $32,200 (federal 2026 amounts). A.R.S. 43-1041 adjusts Arizona's standard deduction for inflation in the same manner as the federal basic standard deduction, so the 2026 amounts are expected to equal the federal $16,100 / $24,150 / $32,200; ADOR publishes the official figures with the 2026 form. The Dependent Tax Credit phase-out and the excise credit income limits are statutory and unchanged for 2026. The 2026 charitable increase replaces the 2025 percentage method (A.R.S. 43-1041); the statute names no separate head of household cap.
## Example
Spouses each earn $50,000 of wages, all community income, file a joint federal return and separate Arizona returns. Each spouse's federal AGI share as filed starts at that spouse's own $50,000 wages; since one-half of the community income is also $50,000 each, no page 5 or page 6, item A adjustment is needed. If one spouse earned $80,000 and the other $20,000, each must report $50,000: the higher earner subtracts $30,000 on page 6, A and the other adds $30,000 on page 5, A.
## Notes
- Form 140 line, box and page item references follow the 2025 Form 140; ADOR had not released the 2026 Form 140 when this was revised, so confirm them against it
## Required Information
- Federal filing status (Form 1040)
- Marital status on December 31, 2026
- Spouse's name, SSN and residency
- Records of community and separate income, deductions and payments
## Questions
- What filing status did you use on your 2026 federal return?
- Were you married on December 31, 2026? Did your spouse die in 2026 or 2025?
- If filing separately, which income, deductions and payments were community and which were separate?
- Is your spouse a full-year Arizona resident?
## Common Errors
- Filing single when married for federal purposes
- Choosing a qualifying surviving spouse status that Arizona does not have instead of head of household
- One spouse itemizing while the other takes the standard deduction on separate returns
- Reporting only one's own wages on a separate return instead of one-half of community income
## Prompt
- We filed jointly federally; can we file separately in Arizona?
- I am a qualifying surviving spouse federally. What status do I use in Arizona?
