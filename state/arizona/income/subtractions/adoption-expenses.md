# Adoption Expenses (Page 6, D)
## Description
Subtract the lesser of qualified adoption expenses or the cap, only in the year the final adoption order is granted. Expenses incurred in prior years may be included.

| Filing status | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Single, head of household | $3,000 | $5,000 |
| Married filing joint | $3,000 | $10,000 |
| Married filing separate (combined) | $3,000 | $10,000 |

The 2026 increase comes from HB 2155 (2025), effective for taxable years beginning January 1, 2026 (A.R.S. 43-1022).
## Qualified adoption expenses
- Unreimbursed medical and hospital costs
- Adoption counseling
- Legal and agency fees
- Other nonrecurring costs of adoption
## Married filing separate
Either spouse may take the entire subtraction or they may divide it, but the combined total cannot exceed $10,000.
## Example
A married couple filing jointly pays adoption costs of $18,000 in 2025 and $9,000 in 2026; the final order is granted in October 2026. Total $27,000, capped at $10,000: page 6, D = $10,000 on the 2026 return. A single parent in the same situation subtracts $5,000.
## Notes
- Expenses reimbursed by an employer or agency do not qualify
- Form 140 line and page 6 item letters follow the 2025 Form 140; ADOR had not released the 2026 Form 140 when this was revised, so confirm them against it
## Required Information
- The final adoption order (date granted)
- Receipts for medical, hospital, counseling, legal, agency and other nonrecurring costs, including prior years
- Reimbursements received
- Filing status
## Questions
- When was the final adoption order granted?
- What adoption expenses did you pay in all years, and were any reimbursed?
## Common Errors
- Subtracting expenses in a year before the final order
- Exceeding $10,000 on combined separate returns
- Using the old $3,000 cap for 2026, or treating the cap as $40,000
