# Contributions to 529 College Savings Plans and 529A ABLE Accounts (Form 140, Lines 34a to 34c)
## Description
Subtract amounts contributed directly during the year on behalf of a designated beneficiary, to the extent not deducted in federal AGI:
- Box 34a: 529 college savings plans, up to $2,000 per beneficiary ($4,000 for a married couple filing jointly)
- Box 34b: 529A Achieving a Better Life Experience (ABLE) accounts, up to $2,000 per beneficiary ($4,000 joint)
- Line 34c: box 34a + box 34b

The $2,000 / $4,000 limits are statutory, not indexed for inflation, so unchanged for 2026. Contributing more than the limit does not increase the subtraction. Married filing separate: either spouse may take the subtraction, or they may divide it, with a combined total of not more than $4,000 per beneficiary.
## What qualifies (529)
A 2026 contribution to a plan that existed before 2026, to a plan established in another state, or to any 529 plan for a child, grandchild, niece, nephew or anyone else. A rollover from one plan to another does not qualify.
## Example
A married couple filing jointly contributes $5,000 to a 529 for each of two children. The subtraction is limited to $4,000 per beneficiary: box 34a = $8,000, line 34c = $8,000.
## Notes
- A later nonqualified withdrawal is added back on page 5, I
- Form 140 line and box numbers follow the 2025 Form 140; ADOR had not released the 2026 Form 140 when this was revised, so confirm them against it
## Required Information
- Contribution statements for each 529 and ABLE beneficiary for 2026
- Records showing which amounts were rollovers
- Filing status
## Questions
- How much did you contribute in 2026 to each 529 and ABLE beneficiary?
- Were any amounts rollovers?
## Common Errors
- Subtracting the full contribution above the per-beneficiary limit
- Subtracting a rollover
- Married filing separate spouses together taking more than $4,000 per beneficiary
