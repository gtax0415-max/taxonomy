# Exchange of One Kind of Legal Tender for Another (Page 6, R and Page 5, O)
## Description
Arizona excludes net capital gain, and disallows net capital loss, from exchanging one kind of legal tender for another. "Legal tender" is a medium of exchange, including specie, authorized by the U.S. Constitution or Congress for the payment of debts, public charges, taxes and dues; "specie" means coins having precious metal content. Net all gains and losses from all such exchanges, including amounts on Form 165, 120S and 141AZ Schedule K-1.
- Net gain included in Arizona gross income → page 6, R (subtraction).
- Net loss included in Arizona gross income → page 5, O (addition).
## Interaction with the 25% long-term gain subtraction
Long-term gain from post-2011 assets subtracted on page 6, R cannot also be counted on line 23. The net long-term capital gain worksheet adds back the page 5, O loss (line 2) and removes the page 6, R gain (line 4).
## Example
A collector sells gold coins (specie) for a $5,000 long-term gain and silver coins for a $1,000 loss, both acquired after 2011. Net gain $4,000 → page 6, R = $4,000, and $4,000 is entered on line 4 of the net long-term capital gain worksheet.
## Notes
- Form 140 line, box and page item references follow the 2025 Form 140; ADOR had not released the 2026 Form 140 when this was revised, so confirm them against it
## Required Information
- Records of each exchange of legal tender, with dates and amounts
- Any Forms 165, 120S or 141AZ Schedule K-1 reporting such gains or losses
## Questions
- Did you sell or exchange gold or silver coins or other legal tender in 2026?
- Did any K-1 report such gains or losses?
## Common Errors
- Subtracting the gain on page 6, R and again counting it on line 23
- Netting the legal tender results with other capital gains
