# Residency Status
## Description
Residency decides whether all income is taxed (full-year resident) or only income earned while a resident or from Colorado sources (part-year resident or nonresident).

## Notes
- Part-year and nonresident filers apportion tax and also apportion the child tax credit, EITC and child care credit
- There is no TABOR refund for 2026, so the full-year residency test for it does not matter this year
- Example: a couple lived in Denver all of 2026 → Full-Year. If one spouse moved in on July 1, 2026 → Part-Year or Nonresident with the apportionment schedule

## Required Information
- Dates of Colorado residence for each spouse
- Colorado-source income

## Questions
- Did you live in Colorado all of 2026?
- If not, when did you move in or out?
- Will you be outside the United States on April 15, 2027?

## Common Errors
- Marking Full-Year when one spouse moved in during the year
- Leaving out the apportionment schedule for a part-year return

## Prompt
- I moved to Colorado mid-year; which box do I mark?
