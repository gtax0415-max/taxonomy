# Amended Returns
## Description
A Colorado return must be amended when a federal item used to compute Colorado tax changes, whether from an IRS audit or the taxpayer's own amended federal return. Taxpayers may also amend to correct their own errors.

## Notes
- DEADLINE AFTER A FEDERAL CHANGE: 180 days after the final determination date (C.R.S. 39-22-601.5, HB23-1277, for determinations on or after January 1, 2024; the older 30-day rule no longer applies)
- Final determination date: for an IRS audit, when no federal adjustments remain open; for the taxpayer's own amended federal return, the date it was filed
- Refund claims: generally 4 years from the original due date (or 3 years from the last payment, if later)
- DOR can assess generally within 4 years after the original return was filed; after a federal change, at least 1 year after the report is filed, and up to 6 years if it is not reported
- Example: IRS increases 2026 federal taxable income by $3,000 in a determination final on June 1, 2028 → amend by November 28, 2028 and pay 4.4% of the change ($132) plus interest

## Required Information
- Federal change and its final date
- Corrected amounts
- All schedules

## Questions
- Did your 2026 federal return change after you filed?
- When did the IRS change become final, or when did you file the federal amendment?

## Common Errors
- Not amending Colorado after a federal change
- Using the old 30-day deadline
- Sending only the changed schedule

## Prompt
- The IRS changed my return; do I need to amend in Colorado?
