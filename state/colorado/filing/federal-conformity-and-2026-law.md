# Federal Conformity and 2026 Colorado Law Changes
## Description
Colorado starts from federal taxable income, so federal law changes reach Colorado unless Colorado adds back or subtracts. This file summarizes what is different for 2026.

## 2026 summary
RATE AND TABOR
- Tax rate 4.4%; FY 2025-26 revenue was $175.9 million below the TABOR limit, so no temporary rate cut and no TABOR sales tax refund on 2026 returns

ADDITIONS
- Standard or itemized deduction addback for AGI over $300,000: caps cut to $1,000 / $2,000 (Proposition MM)
- Federal qualified overtime deduction added back (HB25-1296)
- QBI addback made permanent (HB25B-1001)
- Business meals addback continues (HB23-1008, through 2030)
- No addback for federal tips, car loan interest or senior deductions

SUBTRACTIONS
- New: Segal AmeriCorps Education Award (HB24-1240) and first responder death benefit (SB25-310)
- HB21-1002 carryforward: $150,000 annual cap ends
- Last year: employer home savings contribution subtraction

CREDITS
- Colorado EITC 25% (was 50%); Family Affordability Tax Credit $0
- Child care credit 70% of the full federal credit (was 50%); low-income child care credit ended
- Business personal property and early childhood educator credits ended
- Innovative vehicle credit $750 base; e-bike $250
- Heat pump contractor credit $1,000 / $2,000 / $250, halved for existing buildings ($500 / $1,000 / $0)
- Enterprise zone $2 million per-taxpayer cap

## Notes
- The 2026 Colorado forms are not published; Colorado line numbers follow the 2025 forms
- 2026 Form 1040 changes that affect Colorado references: QBI deduction moved to line 13b and Schedule 1-A deductions to line 13a; new line 12f for the non-itemizer charitable deduction
- Interest on late payments: 2026 8% / 11%; 2027 7% / 10% (discounted / regular)
- Example: hourly worker deducts $8,000 of overtime federally; federal taxable income $40,000 → Colorado taxable income $48,000 → tax about $2,110 at 4.4%

## Required Information
- Federal return with all schedules

## Questions
- Did you claim any new federal deductions for 2026 (tips, overtime, seniors, car loan interest)?
- Was your federal AGI over $300,000?

## Common Errors
- Using the 2025 $12,000 / $16,000 deduction caps
- Entering a TABOR amount for 2026
- Not adding back federal overtime for 2026
- Using the 2025 EITC (50%) or child care (50%) rates

## Prompt
- What changed on the Colorado return for 2026?
