# Charitable Contribution Subtraction for Non-Itemizers
## Description
Taxpayers who take the federal standard deduction can subtract charitable gifts that would have been deductible if they had itemized, above a $500 floor (C.R.S. 39-22-104(4)(m)).

## Amount
- Contributions that would have been deductible on Schedule A, minus $500
- No carryforward

## 2026 open issue — new federal deduction for non-itemizers
- Starting 2026, federal law (OBBBA, IRC 170(p)) gives non-itemizers a charitable deduction of up to $1,000 single / $2,000 joint, claimed on new Form 1040 line 12f (below AGI). It already lowers federal taxable income, which is Colorado's starting point
- No Colorado bill or DOR guidance yet says whether the Colorado subtraction must be reduced for that federal deduction. When a similar federal deduction existed in 2020 – 2021, DOR did not bar the Colorado subtraction or require a reduction
- Not confirmed until the 2026 Colorado instructions are published

## Notes
- Example: non-itemizer gives $2,300 in cash to qualified charities → subtract $1,800 (before any 2026 adjustment for the federal deduction)

## Required Information
- Total contributions and receipts
- Whether the filer itemized
- Any federal non-itemizer charitable deduction taken

## Questions
- Did you itemize on your 2026 federal return?
- If not, how much did you give to qualified charities?
- Did you take the new federal charitable deduction for non-itemizers?

## Common Errors
- Claiming while itemizing
- Forgetting the $500 reduction
