# Colorado Marijuana and Natural Medicine Business Deduction
## Description
Federal law (IRC 280E) bars deductions for businesses trafficking in Schedule I or II controlled substances. Colorado lets its licensed marijuana and natural medicine businesses subtract the expenses that would have been deductible without 280E.

## Amount
- Profit on the actual federal schedules minus profit on pro forma schedules prepared as if 280E did not apply

## 2026 change — medical marijuana rescheduling
- A Justice Department order published April 28, 2026 moved state-licensed MEDICAL marijuana to Schedule III. Treasury has said guidance should apply this to the whole tax year that includes April 28, 2026, but the IRS notice had not been issued when this was written
- For medical-only businesses, 280E may disallow little or nothing for 2026, so this subtraction shrinks or disappears
- Adult-use marijuana remains Schedule I (no final DEA rule), so 280E still applies to adult-use businesses and this subtraction still matters
- DOR has not issued 2026 guidance

## Notes
- Attach the actual and pro forma schedules and the license number
- Example: adult-use dispensary, actual Schedule C profit $210,000; pro forma profit with rent and wages allowed $95,000 → subtract $115,000

## Required Information
- Actual federal schedules
- Disallowed expenses
- License number and type (medical, adult-use, natural medicine)

## Questions
- Do you own a Colorado-licensed marijuana or natural medicine business?
- Is it medical, adult-use, or both?
- What expenses did 280E disallow on your 2026 federal return?

## Common Errors
- Omitting the pro forma schedules or license number
- Subtracting expenses for a medical business that were already deducted federally after rescheduling
