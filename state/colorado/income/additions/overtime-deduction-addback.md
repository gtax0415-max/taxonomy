# Qualified Overtime Deduction Addback
## Description
Starting in 2026, Colorado adds back the federal deduction for qualified overtime pay, so overtime stays taxable in Colorado (HB25-1296). The 2025 return needs no addback.

## Amount
- The full overtime deduction claimed on federal Schedule 1-A

## Notes
- Attempts to repeal or limit it failed (HB25B-1020 in 2025; SB26-056 in 2026)
- A lawsuit challenging HB25-1296 was filed in 2025; no ruling found
- The 2026 form is not published. Reports say it will have a new line for the overtime addback (possibly line 8); until then, use Other Additions
- Example: hourly worker deducts $6,400 of qualified overtime on Schedule 1-A; federal taxable income $38,000 → line 1 $38,000; addition $6,400; line 10 $44,400

## Required Information
- Federal overtime deduction

## Questions
- Did you deduct qualified overtime on your 2026 federal return? How much?

## Common Errors
- Leaving the overtime deduction out of Colorado income for 2026
- Also adding back the tips deduction

## Prompt
- Is overtime taxed in Colorado in 2026?
