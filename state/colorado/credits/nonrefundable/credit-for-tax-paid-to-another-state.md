# Credit for Tax Paid to Another State
## Description
Nonrefundable credit that keeps Colorado residents from paying full tax twice on income also taxed by another state. The credit is the smaller of the other state's tax or the Colorado tax on that income.

## Amount
- Colorado tax (Form DR 0104 lines 13 + 14) × (modified Colorado AGI from the other state ÷ total modified Colorado AGI), percentage to four decimals
- Credit = the smaller of that amount or the tax actually owed to the other state
- No carryforward

## Eligibility
- Colorado resident (full-year, or part-year for the resident period)
- Income also taxed by another state for 2026

## Notes
- A partner or shareholder who did not file in the other state may use the entity's statement of their share of that state's tax
- How elective pass-through entity taxes paid to other states are treated is not separately addressed by DOR
- Example: Colorado tax $4,400; Kansas-source income $20,000 of $100,000 → 20.0000% → $880; Kansas tax $950 → credit $880

## Required Information
- Other state's return as filed
- Income sourced to that state
- K-1 or entity statements for pass-through tax

## Questions
- Did you pay income tax to another state on income also taxed by Colorado?
- Were you a Colorado resident all year?
- Did a partnership or S corporation pay tax to another state for you?

## Common Errors
- Claiming the other state's full tax instead of the smaller amount
- Not attaching the other state's return
- Claiming as a nonresident

## Prompt
- Can I get credit in Colorado for tax paid to another state?
