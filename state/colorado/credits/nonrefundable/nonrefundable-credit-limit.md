# Nonrefundable Credits: Available, Used and the Tax Limit
## Description
Colorado nonrefundable credits can only reduce tax to zero. Each credit in DR 0104CR Part III shows the amount available (this year's credit plus carryforward) and the amount used. The total used, plus the credit for tax paid to another state, goes to Form DR 0104 line 17.

## Amount
- Column A: credit available (2026 credit + carryforward)
- Column B: credit used this year
- Line 64: totals of columns A and B for lines 35 – 63
- Line 65: column B total + line 34 (tax paid to another state) → Form DR 0104, line 17
- Form DR 0104 lines 17 – 20 together cannot exceed line 16 (tax subtotal, lines 13 – 15)

## Notes
- Round to the nearest dollar
- When credits exceed the tax, use first the credits that cannot be carried forward
- Example: tax on line 16 $900. Long-term care credit $300 (no carryforward) and wildfire mitigation credit $1,000 (no carryforward for 2026). Using both up to $900 means $400 of credit is lost
- Credits from a partnership or S corporation come from the Colorado K-1, with the entity name, account number and ownership percentage where required

## Required Information
- Each credit's available amount and carryforward
- Tax on Form DR 0104, line 16

## Questions
- Which Colorado nonrefundable credits do you have this year or carried forward?
- Does any credit exceed your tax?

## Common Errors
- Using more than line 16 across lines 17 to 20
- Including certificate numbers in the totals
- Carrying forward a credit that has no carryforward

- How do Colorado nonrefundable credits work?
