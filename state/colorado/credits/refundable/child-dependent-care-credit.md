# Child and Dependent Care Expenses Credit
## Description
Refundable Colorado credit equal to a percentage of the federal child and dependent care credit, for residents with federal AGI of $60,000 or less. HB24-1134 raised the rate for 2026 and lets filers with no federal tax use the full federal credit they were eligible for. It also absorbed the separate low-income credit, which ended after 2025.

## Amount
- 2026: 70% of the federal credit (Form 2441), figured WITHOUT the federal tax-liability limit (IRC section 26)
- 2025 and earlier: 50% of the federal credit actually allowed
- AGI limit: $60,000 (not indexed for 2026; indexed starting 2027)
- Because it is a share of the federal credit, the larger 2026 federal credit under OBBBA flows through automatically

## Eligibility
- Paid for care of a qualifying person so you (and your spouse) could work or look for work
- Federal AGI $60,000 or less
- Full-year or part-year Colorado resident
- Married filing separately: generally not eligible; DOR follows the federal rule (married couples must file jointly unless they lived apart and meet the federal exception)

## Notes
- Colorado uses the federal credit BEFORE the federal tax-liability limit, not the credit actually allowed
- Example: joint filers, AGI $54,000, $6,000 of day care for one child. Federal credit before the tax limit $1,200 even though federal tax is $0 → Colorado credit $1,200 × 70% = $840

## Required Information
- Federal Form 2441
- Provider names, addresses, ID numbers and amounts paid
- Qualifying persons' names, birth years and SSNs or ITINs
- Earned income of each spouse
- Residency status

## Questions
- Did you pay for child or dependent care so you could work in 2026?
- What is your federal AGI and filing status?
- Who were the providers, and do you have their ID numbers?
- Was your federal credit reduced because you had little or no federal tax?

## Common Errors
- Using the 2025 50% rate for 2026
- Using the federal credit after the tax-liability limit
- Claiming with AGI over $60,000
- Omitting provider ID numbers

## Prompt
- Does Colorado have a child care credit?
- I paid for day care but owed no federal tax.
