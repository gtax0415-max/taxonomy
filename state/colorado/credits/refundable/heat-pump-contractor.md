# Heat Pump Credit for Registered Contractors
## Description
Refundable credit for contractors registered with the Colorado Energy Office that install qualifying heat pump technology in Colorado and pass part of the credit to the customer as a discount on the invoice (HB23-1272, C.R.S. 39-22-554). Tax years 2024 – 2032.

## Amounts
Statutory credit per installation for 2026 – 2028:
- Air-source heat pump or variable refrigerant flow system: $1,000 (was $1,500 for 2024–2025)
- Ground-source, water-source or combined-source system: $2,000 (was $3,000)
- Heat pump water heater: $250 (was $500)
- The contractor must give the customer at least one-third of the credit as a discount; minimum discounts per CEO for 2026: $333 / $667 / $83

2026 REDUCTION FOR EXISTING BUILDINGS — confirmed:
- C.R.S. 39-22-554(3)(f) cuts the credit 50% for installations in EXISTING buildings when the June forecast projects revenue growth under 4%, and a reduced credit of $250 or less becomes $0
- The June 2025 forecast triggered the cut for 2026 (CPR, Colorado Sun; the final HB26-1289 fiscal note says the credits are halved for 2026). HB26-1289 removes the trigger only for later years
- 2026 amounts for EXISTING buildings: air-source $500; ground-source $1,000; heat pump water heater $0 (halved to $125, under the $250 floor). New construction keeps $1,000 / $2,000 / $250
- The Colorado Energy Office web page still shows only the unreduced amounts; the statute and fiscal note control

## Eligibility
- Registered with CEO
- Discount shown as a separate line on the customer invoice
- Equipment installed in Colorado during 2026

## Notes
- DR 1322 is completed but not attached to the return
- Example: six air-source installs in new construction × $1,000 = $6,000

## Required Information
- CEO registration
- Invoice for each installation, with equipment type and building type
- Colorado K-1 for any pass-through share

## Questions
- Are you a contractor registered with the Colorado Energy Office?
- How many of each heat pump type did you install in 2026?
- Were they in existing buildings or new construction?

## Common Errors
- A homeowner claiming the contractor credit
- Using the 2024–2025 amounts for 2026
- Ignoring the 2026 existing-building reduction

## Prompt
- Can homeowners claim the Colorado heat pump credit?
- I'm an HVAC contractor who installs heat pumps in Colorado.
