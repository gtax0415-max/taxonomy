# Employee Ownership Tax Credits
## Description
Two OEDIT-certified credits that encourage employee ownership: one for the costs of converting a business to (or expanding) employee ownership, and one for costs of sustaining a young employee-owned business.

## Amounts
ESTABLISH OR EXPAND (HB21-1311, as amended by HB25-1021):
- 75% of qualifying conversion costs for 2026, up from 50% for 2025
- Caps: ESOP $150,000; worker cooperative $40,000; other employee ownership forms and expansions $25,000
- Statewide cap of $3 million a year; available through tax year 2031

STRENGTHEN AND THRIVE (HB24-1157):
- 50% of qualifying costs (accounting, legal, advisory) up to $50,000 a year
- For businesses employee-owned for 7 years or less
- Statewide cap of $1.5 million a year; available tax years 2025 – 2033

## Eligibility
- Colorado business with an OEDIT credit certificate for the year
- Credit equals the amount on the certificate

## Notes
- OEDIT's website may still show the old 50% conversion rate; the 75% rate applies to 2026 conversions under HB25-1021
- Example: worker cooperative conversion costs $50,000 → 75% = $37,500 (under the $40,000 cap)

## Required Information
- OEDIT certificate
- Type of conversion and qualifying cost invoices
- Colorado K-1 for any pass-through share

## Questions
- Did your business convert to or expand employee ownership in 2026?
- Is your business employee-owned, and for how many years?
- Did OEDIT issue a tax credit certificate?

## Common Errors
- Using the 50% conversion rate for 2026
- Claiming more than the certificate amount
- Claiming without the certificate

## Prompt
- Is there a Colorado credit for employee ownership?
- We converted our company to an ESOP this year.
