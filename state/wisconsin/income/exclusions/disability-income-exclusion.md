# Disability Income Exclusion (Schedule 2440W)
## Description
Wisconsin keeps the old federal sick-pay exclusion for people under 65 who retired on permanent and total disability: up to $100 per week, $5,200 per year.
## Tests (all required)
- Disability income from the employer's accident/health or pension plan in place of wages, not otherwise exempt from Wisconsin tax
- Under 65 at the end of 2026 — someone born on or before January 1, 1962 is treated as 65 by December 31, 2026 (January 1 birthdays count as a year older) and does not qualify
- Retired on disability and permanently and totally disabled at retirement (or on January 1, 1976/1977 if retired before 1977)
- Had not reached the employer's mandatory retirement age on January 1, 2026
- Did not elect before 1984 to treat the income as a pension
- If married at year-end, file JOINTLY (not available MFS)
- Wisconsin resident when the income was received
## Permanent and total disability
Cannot engage in substantial gainful activity (more than make-work duties, paid at least minimum wage) because of a condition that has lasted or is expected to last 12+ months or result in death. Physician's statement required once (check line 9 in later years); VA Form 21-0172 or the federal Schedule R statement may substitute; each spouse needs one.
## Computation
| Line | Step |
|---|---|
| 1 | Disability pay in FAGI, less amounts otherwise exempt |
| 2a-2d | $100 × weeks with payments of $100+, plus actual amounts in lower weeks, plus prorated short weeks |
| 4 | Smaller of line 1 or line 2 total |
| 5 | Reduce dollar for dollar by FAGI (Form 1 line 3) over $15,000 |
| 6 | Exclusion (full-year) |
| 7-8 | Part-year: weekly average × weeks as a Wisconsin resident |
Exclusion is fully phased out at FAGI of $20,200 (one person eligible) or $25,400 (both spouses).
## 2026 parameters
$100/week, $5,200 maximum, $15,000 phase-out start — statutory, no indexing.
## Interactions
Reduces qualified earned income for the married couple credit.
## Prompt
- I retired on disability at 58.
