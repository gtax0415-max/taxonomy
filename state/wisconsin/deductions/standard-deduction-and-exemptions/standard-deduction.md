# Wisconsin Standard Deduction (Sliding Scale)
## Description
Wisconsin's standard deduction is a fixed amount at low income that PHASES DOWN to zero as Wisconsin income rises. It is unrelated to the federal standard deduction and is used by everyone (there is no Wisconsin itemizing).
## 2026 schedules (OFFICIAL)
| Filing status | Full deduction if Wisconsin income is not over | Phase-down | Zero at Wisconsin income over |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single | $20,119 → $13,960 | $13,960 less 12% of income over $20,120 | $136,453 |
| Head of household | $20,119 → $18,030 | $18,030 less 22.515% of income over $20,120 (to $58,827); then $13,960 less 12% of income over $20,120 | $136,453 |
| Married filing jointly | $29,039 → $25,840 | $25,840 less 19.778% of income over $29,040 | $159,690 |
| Married filing separately | $13,779 → $12,280 | $12,280 less 19.778% of income over $13,780 | $75,869 |
For comparison, 2025 maximums were $13,560 single, $17,520 HOH, $25,110 MFJ, $11,930 MFS.
## Special rules
- DEPENDENTS (anyone claimable on another's return): smaller of the table amount or earned income + $450, but not less than $1,350 (2025 worksheet figures; confirm for 2026)
- Short-period returns and Form 4563 filers: no standard deduction
- Form 1NPR: compute using FEDERAL AGI instead of Wisconsin income, then prorate by Wisconsin income ÷ federal AGI
- Estates and trusts: none
## Notes
- The phase-down acts as a hidden marginal rate: e.g., a single filer in the phase-down range loses 12 cents of deduction per extra dollar, adding about 0.5 to 0.6 percentage points to the marginal rate (12% × the 4.4% or 5.3% bracket rate)
- Federal itemized deductions above the Wisconsin standard deduction may yield the itemized deduction credit (Form 1, line 13)
## Questions
- What is your filing status and Wisconsin income (Form 1, line 7)?
- Can anyone claim you as a dependent?
## Common Errors
- Using federal AGI instead of Wisconsin income (full-year residents)
- Using the federal standard deduction amount
