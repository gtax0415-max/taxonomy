# Wisconsin Retirement Income Subtractions
## Description
Wisconsin taxes most pensions and IRA distributions, but subtracts several specific types, and since 2025 offers a broad subtraction for taxpayers 67 and older. Social Security is entirely exempt (see ../../income/exclusions/federal-income-exempt-in-wisconsin.md). Order matters: lines 12-15 come first; line 16 applies only to income not already subtracted; line 17 to income not subtracted on 12-16.
## Line by line
| SB line [M] | Subtraction | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 12 [46] | Military and uniformed services retirement | U.S. military retirement (DFAS, incl. Survivor Benefit Plan, RSFPP) and Coast Guard, NOAA corps, Public Health Service corps — fully exempt |
| 13 [47] | Certain local and state retirement | Milwaukee City Employees, Milwaukee Police, Milwaukee Fire Fighters, Milwaukee Public School Teachers, Milwaukee County Employees, Milwaukee Sheriff, Wisconsin State Teachers systems — only if retired before 1964, a member on December 31, 1963 (account established before 1964), or beneficiary of such a person; prorate by exempt service years |
| 14 [48] | Federal retirement | U.S. Civil Service Retirement System under the same pre-1964 rules (Thrift Savings Plan never qualifies) |
| 15 [49] | Railroad retirement, railroad unemployment, sickness benefits | Fully exempt |
| 16 [50] | RETIREMENT INCOME SUBTRACTION (credits restricted) | See below |
| 17 [51] | $5,000 retirement income subtraction (income restricted) | See below |
## Retirement income subtraction — age 67 (SB 16)
| Item | 2026 |
|---|---|
| Age | 67 or older on December 31, 2026 (born January 1, 1960 or earlier — January 1 birthdays count as a year older) |
| Maximum | $24,000; $48,000 on a joint return if BOTH spouses are 67+ (regardless of which spouse received the income) |
| Qualified income | Distributions from qualified retirement plans and IRAs included in Wisconsin income and not subtracted on lines 12-15 |
| Residency | Full-year or part-year residents (part-year prorated by Wisconsin income ÷ FAGI); NONRESIDENTS ineligible |
| Indexing | None — statutory amounts (2025 Wis. Act 15) |
| Clarified by 2025 Wis. Act 174 (April 2026) | Age 67 before the close of the taxable year; the no-credits rule (including carryovers) applies to part-year residents too; part-year subtraction capped at $24,000 × Wisconsin income ÷ FAGI |
CREDITS RESTRICTED: claiming it bars EVERY Wisconsin credit for the year — Schedule CR credits (and their carryforwards) plus all credits on Form 1 lines 13-20 and 30-35 (Form 1NPR lines 40-50 and 60-65), e.g., itemized deduction, school property tax, married couple, child and dependent care, other-state tax, EIC, farmland preservation, homestead, veterans property tax credits. Compute both ways and choose the better result.
## $5,000 retirement income subtraction — age 65 (SB 17)
- Taxpayer (or each spouse on a joint return) 65+ on December 31, 2026
- Federal AGI under $15,000 ($30,000 joint; for MFS the combined AGI of both spouses must be under $30,000)
- Up to $5,000 per qualifying person of taxable retirement income not already subtracted on lines 12-16
- Statutory thresholds; no indexing
## Interactions
- Retirement payments that are exempt here are not subject to the 33% Wisconsin penalty on early distributions (Form 1, line 25)
- Homestead and farmland preservation household income still include nontaxable pensions
## Questions
- What types of retirement income do you receive, and from which systems?
- Were you (and your spouse) 67 by December 31, 2026? 65?
- What credits would you give up by taking the age-67 subtraction?
## Common Errors
- Taking the age-67 subtraction when homestead, EIC, or large credit carryforwards are worth more
- Subtracting TSP or post-1963 system benefits
- Applying the $48,000 joint maximum when only one spouse is 67
