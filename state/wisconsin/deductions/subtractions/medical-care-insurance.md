# Medical Care Insurance Subtraction
## Description
Subtract after-tax premiums for medical care insurance covering you, your spouse, and dependents — surgical, medical, hospital, major medical, dental, vision — including Medicare premiums deducted from Social Security (Parts B and D) and Marketplace premiums.
## How it works
- Premiums paid − federal net premium tax credit (Schedule 3, line 9) + excess advance PTC repaid (Schedule 2, line 1a) = eligible amount; Marketplace premiums are counted AFTER advance PTC
- Self-employed: also remove premiums deducted federally (self-employed health insurance deduction, Schedule 1 line 17) and premiums for an employee-spouse deducted on Schedule C/F
- Limited to the ratio of Wisconsin income to federal AGI (1NPR) and to Wisconsin income
- Amounts subtracted here are removed from medical expenses for the itemized deduction credit
## NOT eligible
Pre-tax/cafeteria-plan (sec. 125) premiums; W-2 Box 12 code DD employer coverage (unless the premium is in Box 1 wages); premiums paid with untaxed HSA distributions; long-term care (separate subtraction), life, disability-income, hospital indemnity, auto medical coverage; premiums paid by tax-free retirement-plan distributions.
## 2026 notes
- The enhanced federal premium tax credit ended after 2025, so more 2026 Marketplace premium is paid out-of-pocket and excess advance credit must be repaid in full — both raise this subtraction (see federal/credits/health-insurance/premium-tax-credit.md)
- A 2026 bill (2025 Senate Bill 1091) proposed a state premium credit replacing the lost enhancement; it was introduced in March 2026 and is not law as of this writing — confirm before relying on it
## Questions
- Who paid the premiums, and were they pre-tax?
- Did you buy Marketplace coverage and get advance PTC?
- Are Medicare premiums withheld from your Social Security?
## Common Errors
- Subtracting pre-tax payroll premiums
- Double-counting the self-employed health insurance deduction
- Forgetting Medicare Part B/D withholding
