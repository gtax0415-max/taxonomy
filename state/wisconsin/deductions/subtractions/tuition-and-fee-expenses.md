# Tuition and Fee Expenses Subtraction
## Description
Subtract tuition and mandatory student fees paid during the year for yourself, your spouse (joint return), and dependents at qualifying post-secondary schools, up to a per-student maximum, phased out by federal AGI.
## Parameters
| Item | 2025 (published) | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum per student | $7,649 | Indexed — not yet published; DOR history: 2021 $6,973, 2022 $6,976, 2023 $6,974, 2024 $7,333, 2025 $7,649 |
| Single / HOH phase-out (FAGI) | $69,480 – $83,370 | Indexed — not yet published |
| MFJ phase-out | $111,160 – $138,960 | Indexed — not yet published |
| MFS phase-out | $55,580 – $69,480 | Indexed — not yet published |
Use the 2026 Schedule SB instructions when released.
## Qualifying schools
- Universities, colleges, technical colleges in Wisconsin; Wisconsin post-secondary schools approved by the Educational Approval Program
- Minnesota public institutions under the Minnesota-Wisconsin reciprocity agreement
- Classes outside Wisconsin if tuition is paid to a Wisconsin university/college/technical college; online/correspondence courses taken in Wisconsin from a qualifying school
- Any course type qualifies (e.g., recreational courses at a technical college); required books paid to the school count as fees
## NOT eligible
Pre-K through 12; room, board, athletic and other separate charges; amounts paid with tax-free scholarships, Pell grants, or employer reimbursement; amounts withdrawn from Edvest/Tomorrow's Scholar if anyone previously subtracted the contributions; amounts used for the AmeriCorps subtraction.
## Notes
Part-year residents and nonresidents qualify, prorated by Wisconsin income ÷ federal AGI.
## Questions
- What tuition and mandatory fees did you pay in 2026, for whom, and to which school?
- Was any of it paid with scholarships, 529 funds, or employer money?
## Common Errors
- Using 2025 limits on the 2026 return
- Subtracting tuition paid from a 529 already subtracted
## Prompt
- I paid my daughter's UW tuition. Is there a Wisconsin deduction?
