# Itemized Deduction Credit (Form 1, Schedule 1)
## Description
Wisconsin has no itemized deductions. Instead, if certain federal itemized deductions exceed the Wisconsin standard deduction, 5% of the excess is a nonrefundable credit. 598,478 claims used $335.9 million for tax year 2024 (Wisconsin Tax Bulletin 234).
## How it works
| Schedule 1 line | Item |
|---|---|
| 1 | Medical and dental expenses (federal Schedule A), less the portion attributable to medical care and long-term care insurance subtracted on Schedule SB lines 6-7 (worksheet in Form 1 instructions) |
| 2 | Interest paid — EXCLUDING interest on a second home outside Wisconsin, a residence that is a boat, debt to buy or hold U.S. government securities, and S corporation interest taken as a subtraction |
| 3 | Gifts to charity — excluding S corporation contributions taken as a subtraction (SB line 42) |
| 4 | Casualty losses |
| 5 | Total |
| 6 | Wisconsin standard deduction (Form 1, line 8) |
| 7-9 | (Line 5 − line 6) × 5% |
Taxes (state income, property) and other Schedule A items are NOT used. Amounts allocated by an S corporation or partnership that elected entity-level tax cannot be used.
## 2026 — Wisconsin's IRC differs from federal
Use Schedule A as computed under Wisconsin's IRC (as of December 31, 2022) and Wisconsin FAGI (Schedule I Part II):
- Medical: 7.5%-of-AGI floor applied to Wisconsin FAGI
- Interest: home mortgage interest under sec. 163(h)(3) EXCLUDING subparagraph (F) — i.e., the TCJA $750,000 acquisition-debt limit and home-equity interest suspension in (F) do not apply for Wisconsin in 2026; mortgage insurance premiums are NOT deductible
- Charity: cash gifts limited to 50% of contribution base (not the federal 60%); Wisconsin did NOT adopt the new 0.5%-of-AGI floor
- Itemized deduction limitation (sec. 68): Wisconsin applies the pre-TCJA "Pease" reduction (lesser of 80% of deductions or 3% of income above the threshold) for 2026, not the federal 35% benefit cap — review the 2026 Schedule I instructions if FAGI under Wisconsin's IRC exceeds $200,000
- Gambling losses (not a Schedule 1 line) are not subject to the federal 90% limit for Wisconsin
(Wisconsin Tax Bulletin 234.)
## Rate and parameters
5% (statutory); the comparison standard deduction is the 2026 sliding-scale amount, which is zero at Wisconsin income above $136,453 single / $159,690 joint, so higher-income filers compare against $0.
## Questions
- Do your medical, mortgage interest, charity, and casualty items exceed your Wisconsin standard deduction?
- Did you subtract health insurance premiums on Schedule SB?
## Common Errors
- Including state taxes or property taxes
- Including medical premiums already subtracted
- Using federal (OBBBA-era) amounts instead of Wisconsin-IRC amounts

- I don't itemize federally. Can I still get Wisconsin's itemized deduction credit?
