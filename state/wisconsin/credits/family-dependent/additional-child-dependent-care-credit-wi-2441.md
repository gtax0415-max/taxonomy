# Additional Child and Dependent Care Credit (Schedule WI-2441)
## Description
Wisconsin's own version of the federal child and dependent care credit, in place since tax year 2024. It mirrors federal Form 2441 except for a much higher expense limit and its handling of dependent care benefits. Nonrefundable; no carryforward.
## How it works
1. Line 3: qualified expenses incurred AND paid in the year (include amounts paid with tax-free benefits; they come out on Line 4)
2. Line 4: dependent care benefits excluded from wages or deducted on Schedule C/E/F
3. Line 6: smaller of (expenses − benefits) or the cap
4. Lines 7-9: limited to the lower-earning spouse's earned income
5. Lines 10-12: × percentage from the federal-AGI table
6. Line 13: + credit for prior-year expenses paid this year (Worksheet A)
## Parameters (2025 schedule; carried to 2026 pending the 2026 form)
| Item | Wisconsin | Federal (for comparison) |
|---|---|---|
| Expense cap, one qualifying person | $10,000 | $3,000 |
| Expense cap, two or more | $20,000 | $6,000 |
| Percentage table (by federal AGI, Form 1040 Line 11) | 35% at $15,000 or less, falling 1 point per $2,000, to 20% over $43,000 | changed for 2026 by federal law |
| Maximum credit at 20% | $2,000 (one) / $4,000 (two or more) | |
| Maximum credit at 35% | $3,500 / $7,000 | |
## 2026 changes and conformity
- Wisconsin follows the IRC as of December 31, 2022 for 2026, so the 2025 federal law changes to the dependent care credit percentages do NOT carry over; expect the Wisconsin table to remain 35%-20% (confirm on the 2026 schedule)
- DEPENDENT CARE BENEFITS: federal exclusion rises to $7,500 ($3,750 MFS) for 2026, but Wisconsin allows only $5,000 ($2,500 MFS); excess is Wisconsin-taxable wages (Wisconsin Tax Bulletin 234). Confirm on the 2026 instructions how Line 4 treats benefits above $5,000
- Line 2 timing: expenses incurred in 2026 but paid in 2027, or prepaid for 2027 care, belong on the 2027 schedule; 2025 expenses paid in 2026 go through Worksheet A
## Eligibility
- FULL-YEAR Wisconsin resident (on a joint Form 1NPR, one spouse must be a full-year resident)
- Filing status single, head of household, or married filing jointly; married filing separately only if the federal living-apart requirements are met (check Line A — usually better to file head of household, married for Wisconsin)
- Care so the taxpayer (and spouse) could work or look for work; earned income required (student/disabled spouse deemed $250/$500 per month — Line B)
- Qualifying person: dependent child under 13, or a spouse/dependent physically or mentally unable to self-care who lived with the taxpayer more than half the year
- Provider may not be the spouse, the child's parent, a dependent, or the taxpayer's child under 19
- Only one taxpayer may treat a person as a qualifying person: most nights, then highest federal AGI
## Notes
- Must list two qualifying persons to use the $20,000 cap even if expenses were for only one
- An expense used as a medical expense for the Wisconsin itemized deduction credit cannot also be used here
- Provider codes (Line 1d): A unrelated provider, B employer-provided, C relative or close acquaintance, D household employee, E other
- Retirement income subtraction bar — CONFIRMED: a taxpayer who claims the age-67 retirement income subtraction may not claim any credit on Schedule CR or on Form 1NPR lines 40-50 and 60-65 (2025 Schedule M instructions, line 50) — the Form 1 equivalents are lines 13-20 and 30-35, which include this credit (Form 1, line 14)
## Required Information
- Provider name, address, SSN/EIN (Form W-10) and amounts paid
- Qualifying persons' names, SSNs, codes, and expenses
- W-2 Box 10 and federal Form 2441 Part III; earned income of both spouses; federal AGI
## Questions
- How much did you pay for child or dependent care in 2026, for how many qualifying persons?
- Did you receive dependent care FSA or employer benefits?
- Were you a full-year Wisconsin resident?
- Did you pay any 2025 care costs in 2026?
## Common Errors
- Using the federal $3,000/$6,000 caps
- Listing only one child when two qualify
- Excluding more than the Wisconsin benefit limit
- Paying a relative under 19 or a dependent

- I pay $15,000 a year for daycare for two kids in Wisconsin.
