# Schedule I — Federal-to-Wisconsin Conformity Adjustments (credit impacts)
## Description
Not a credit, but it changes the amount of credits. Wisconsin computes income under its own fixed definition of the Internal Revenue Code. For 2026 that is the IRC as amended to December 31, 2022, with listed exceptions (Wisconsin Tax Bulletin 234). Where federal and Wisconsin law differ, Schedule I converts federal AGI, itemized deductions, and federal credits to Wisconsin amounts.
## Why it matters for credits
- PART III — WISCONSIN EARNED INCOME CREDIT: the Wisconsin EIC is a percentage of the federal EIC. If Part I changes FAGI or earned income, the federal EIC must be RECOMPUTED under Wisconsin's IRC; the Col. II amount (not the federal amount) goes to Form 1, Line 30. Lines i) and ii) report whether each version is based on earned income or FAGI (informational only)
- PART II — ITEMIZED DEDUCTION CREDIT: AGI-dependent deductions (medical, charity) are recomputed using Wisconsin FAGI; Col. II feeds the itemized deduction credit on Schedule 1
- Other federal-credit-based Wisconsin credits use Part III Line 1b
## Recurring differences (examples)
- Federal bonus depreciation and Section 179 differences (Lines 1b/2b), creating ongoing annual adjustments until assets are fully depreciated
- Federal capital and ordinary gain/loss recomputed with Wisconsin basis (Lines 1c-1f, 2c-2f)
- Discharge of indebtedness on a principal residence (Line 1a)
- Student loan forgiveness: the federal exclusion for most discharges under IRC 108(f)(5) ended after 2025; Wisconsin's death/disability exclusion applied only to 2018-2025 discharges, so 2026 death/disability discharges excluded federally are TAXABLE for Wisconsin unless another sec. 108 exception applies (Line 1g). PSLF-type forgiveness under 108(f)(1) remains excluded for both
- HSA adjustment (Line 2a)
## 2026 status (Wisconsin Tax Bulletin 234, July 2026)
For 2026 Wisconsin still follows the IRC as amended to December 31, 2022 (static conformity). Most 2025 federal (P.L. 119-21) changes do NOT apply, and TCJA provisions that were suspended through 2025 are RESTORED for Wisconsin in 2026. Differences that affect credits:
- Dependent care benefits: Wisconsin exclusion stays $5,000 ($2,500 MFS) vs. federal $7,500 — affects Schedule WI-2441
- Student loans forgiven for death/disability in 2026: not automatically excluded for Wisconsin — raises Wisconsin FAGI and can reduce the Wisconsin EIC
- Itemized deduction limitation (sec. 68): Wisconsin applies the pre-TCJA "Pease" reduction (lesser of 80% of deductions or 3% of income over the threshold) — affects the itemized deduction credit when FAGI under Wisconsin's IRC exceeds $200,000
- Mortgage interest under pre-TCJA 163(h)(3) without (F), so no mortgage insurance premiums; charitable gifts limited to 50% of contribution base with no 0.5% floor; gambling losses not subject to the 90% limit — all feed Part II and the itemized deduction credit
- Research and experimental expenses: pre-TCJA section 174 for domestic and foreign — affects Schedule R
- Trump accounts not recognized; moving expense exclusion/deduction available as before TCJA; employer student loan payments taxable
- Depreciation: IRC as of January 1, 2014 with listed exceptions — no bonus depreciation; current-law sections 179-179E followed
The 2026 Schedule I instructions were not yet released at the time of the bulletin.
## Federal 2025-law deductions with NO Wisconsin effect
The federal deductions for qualified tips, qualified overtime, the additional senior deduction, and car loan interest are taken after AGI (they do not change federal AGI), so they never enter the Wisconsin computation and need no Schedule I entry. A Wisconsin subtraction for tips and overtime was proposed in the May 2026 special session but was NOT enacted (the Senate did not pass it).
## Notes
- A different federal election may be used for Wisconsin (e.g., Section 179 amount) via a pro forma federal return or Schedule I; the accounting method must match federal
- Affected federal forms must be recomputed and marked "Revised for Wisconsin"
- Pass-through owners take Wisconsin differences from Schedules 2K-1, 3K-1, 5K-1

- Did you have student loans forgiven?
- Are you claiming the Wisconsin earned income credit or the itemized deduction credit?

- Forgetting continuing depreciation adjustments from prior years
