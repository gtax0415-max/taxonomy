# Homestead Credit (Schedule H / H-EZ)
## Description
Refundable credit that returns part of property taxes — or the property-tax share of rent — to low-income Wisconsin homeowners and renters. 71,256 claimants received $34.4 million for tax year 2024, down from about 80,000 and $38 million for 2023 (Wisconsin Tax Bulletins 234 and 230) — the frozen income limit keeps shrinking the eligible pool.
## How it works
1. Household income (Lines 7-12c): Wisconsin income + nontaxable income + add-backs − $500 per qualifying dependent
2. Property taxes or rent (Lines 13-15): homeowners use net property taxes; renters use 20% of rent if heat included, 25% if heat NOT included
3. Line 16: smaller of Line 15 or $1,460
4. Line 17: Table A amount from household income (the household's "own share" of property tax)
5. Line 18: Line 16 − Line 17
6. Line 19: Table B credit (80% of Line 18) — maximum $1,168
## Parameters
| Item | 2025 (published, Schedule H) | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Household income must be LESS than | $24,680 | $24,680 expected (statutory; no change enacted) |
| Property tax / rent cap (Line 16) | $1,460 | $1,460 expected |
| Maximum credit (Table B at Line 18 ≥ $1,460) | $1,168 | $1,168 expected |
| Table A: household income producing $0 on Line 17 | under $8,060 | $8,060 expected |
| Dependent deduction | $500 each | $500 expected |
| Rent conversion | 20% heat included / 25% heat not included | Expected unchanged |
| Filing deadline | April 15, 2030 | April 15, 2031; cannot file before January 1, 2027 |
These amounts are set in sec. 71.54 and have been flat since tax year 2023. Several 2025-26 bills would have raised them (e.g., Assembly Bill 461 / Senate Bill 60, raising the income limit to $35,000), but none appears among the enacted laws in Wisconsin Tax Bulletins 233-234 and the Legislature adjourned its floor period in February 2026, so 2026 is expected to use the same amounts. Confirm on the 2026 Schedule H.
## Eligibility — Step 1 (all must be YES)
- Claimant (or spouse) is 62 or older on December 31, 2026, OR disabled, OR has EARNED INCOME
- Claimant is 18 or older on December 31, 2026
- Full-year Wisconsin legal resident (January 1 - December 31)
- Household income below the limit
- Owned or rented the Wisconsin homestead lived in during 2026
## Eligibility — Step 2 (any YES disqualifies)
- Claimed as a dependent on someone else's federal return (does not apply if 62+)
- Living in a nursing home AND receiving Title XIX medical assistance
- Received Wisconsin Works (W2) payments of any amount, or county relief of $400+, in EVERY month of the year
- Lived all year in property-tax-exempt housing (a sec. 66.1201 housing authority paying in lieu of taxes is not "exempt")
- Claimed farmland preservation credit, or veterans and surviving spouses property tax credit, for the same year
- Another household member filed Schedule H
- Filing on behalf of a deceased person
- Claimed the RETIREMENT INCOME SUBTRACTION (2025: Schedule SB Line 16 or Schedule M Line 50)
## Key definitions
- EARNED INCOME: wages, salaries, tips, other employee pay, and net self-employment earnings; NOT unemployment. Wages and SE losses net together — if the total is zero or less, a filer under 62 and not disabled fails the test
- DISABLED: unable to engage in substantial gainful employment due to a medically determinable impairment expected to last 12+ months; attach a physician's statement, VA 100% disability statement, or SSA document
- HOMESTEAD: the home occupied plus up to 1 acre (up to 120 acres if part of a farm); excludes business/rental portions (Schedule 2)
- HOUSEHOLD: claimant and spouse living together; separated spouses each form their own household
- QUALIFYING DEPENDENT: claimable on your federal return AND lived in your homestead more than 6 months (birth, death, adoption, and new stepchild exceptions)
## Household income — commonly missed items
Nontaxable Social Security, SSI, SSD, CTS (including Medicare premiums withheld), railroad retirement, nontaxable pensions and IRA distributions, 401(k)/457 deferrals and IRA/SEP/SIMPLE deductions, tax-exempt and U.S. bond interest, scholarships and military pay, child support, W2/county relief/kinship care, workers' compensation and sick pay, excluded home-sale gain, NOL and capital loss carryforwards, depreciation and Section 179 (including standard-mileage depreciation), and DISQUALIFIED LOSSES (Schedule 4 — business, capital, rental, pass-through, and farm losses are added back unless primary income is farming with gross receipts under $250,000).
## Special schedules
- Schedule 1: home on more than 1 acre, not a farm — prorate taxes to 1 acre plus buildings
- Schedule 2: home partly used for business/rental — personal-use share only
- Schedule 3: months with W2 payments or county relief of $400+ — taxes/rent reduced to the months WITHOUT such payments
- Schedule 4: disqualified losses
- Schedule GL: if you SOLD your principal residence in 2026, compute the gain (selling price − selling expenses − adjusted basis) and include gain excluded federally in household income on Schedule H, line 11c; a loss is not deductible; not used for involuntary conversions; Schedule H-EZ cannot be used in the year of sale
- Shared Living Expenses Schedule (Rent Certificate page 2): unequal cost sharing with roommates
## Schedule H-EZ allowed only if
Income only from wages taxable to Wisconsin, interest, dividends, unemployment, pensions, Social Security/SSI, railroad retirement, support, public assistance, and gambling (not on Schedule C); no repayment of prior nontaxable income; not married or divorced during the year; spouse a full-year resident; no separate homes; home used only personally, on 1 acre or less, and not sold. H-EZ is filed only with Form 1, never with Form 1NPR.
## Rent Certificate rules (see also the Rent Certificate form)
- Landlord completes and HAND-signs; the renter never signs; no alterations
- Excludes government-paid rent, security deposits, late fees, prior-year rent
- If the landlord will not sign, attach canceled checks or money orders; unverified rent is disallowed
## Notes
- WisTax (DOR's free online system) can file most claims, except: more than four rent certificates, divorced during the year, nursing home residents, Schedules 2-4, or certain income types
- E-filed claims must send attachments within 48 hours using Form W-RA if not attached electronically
- Penalties: reckless claims can bar ALL refundable credits for 2 years with a 25% penalty; fraudulent claims bar them for 10 years with a 100% penalty
- Homeowners 65+ may also use WHEDA's Property Tax Deferral Loan Program
## Required Information
- Rent certificate(s) or 2026 property tax bill
- All household income for both spouses, taxable and nontaxable
- Disability verification if under 62 and claiming disability
- Dependents' months in the home
- Months of W2 or county relief payments
## Questions
- Were you 18+ and a full-year Wisconsin resident?
- Are you 62+, disabled, or did you have positive earned income?
- Do you rent (was heat included?) or own?
- What was total household income, including Social Security and other nontaxable income?
- Did you claim the retirement income subtraction, farmland preservation, or veterans property tax credit?
- Did you receive W2 payments or county relief in any month?
## Common Errors
- Omitting nontaxable income (Social Security, pension, deferrals)
- Using the wrong rent percentage for heat
- Claiming with zero or negative earned income while under 62 and not disabled
- Not adding back disqualified losses or depreciation
- Claiming alongside the retirement income subtraction or farmland preservation credit
## Prompt
- I rent and my landlord gave me a rent certificate.
- I'm on Social Security and rent an apartment in Wisconsin.
- Can I get money back for my property taxes on a low income?
