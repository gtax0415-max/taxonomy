# Credit for Net Income Tax Paid to Another State (Schedule OS)
## Description
Nonrefundable credit for Wisconsin residents (full- or part-year), estates, and trusts that paid net income tax to another state or D.C. on income Wisconsin also taxes. The second-largest Wisconsin individual credit by dollars.
## How it works
- Part I: other-state-source income and adjustments, one column per state (not federal totals)
- Part II: individual tax paid — income taxed by both states, total income taxed by the other state, and the other state's NET tax after all nonrefundable AND refundable credits (not withholding or estimates)
- Part III: pass-through entity tax paid to other states on the owner's share
- Part IV: combines Parts II and III against Wisconsin income (Form 1, Line 7); credit cannot exceed Wisconsin net tax on the dual-taxed income
## Key limitations
- The Wisconsin-tax limitation does NOT apply to income taxed by Minnesota, Iowa, Illinois, or Michigan
- Only state income taxes — not local/city taxes, Puerto Rico or territories, sales, property, or severance taxes
- M&A credit and this credit cannot both be based on the same qualified production activities income; one must be recomputed
- No credit for tax paid by a partnership/S corp that ELECTED ENTITY-LEVEL TAX IN WISCONSIN (its income is excluded from the owner's Wisconsin income)
- CAPITAL GAINS: Wisconsin taxes only 70% of long-term gain (40% for farm assets), so the dual-taxed amount is usually smaller than the other state's
- Part-year residents: only income received while a Wisconsin resident
- Nonresidents: generally ineligible, except owners of fiscal-year pass-throughs for a period of Wisconsin residence
## Reciprocity — do NOT use Schedule OS
Wages earned as an employee in Illinois, Indiana, Kentucky, or Michigan: file that state's return for a refund of withholding. Non-wage income (business, rental, unemployment, real estate sales) taxed by those states can use Schedule OS.
## Entity-level tax in the other state
- If the entity paid other-state entity-level tax, did NOT elect Wisconsin entity-level tax, and the owner got a full REFUNDABLE credit in the other state: report as individual tax (Lines 1-24, 30-41), skip Lines 25-29, and do not reduce net tax by that refundable credit
- If the owner got a NONREFUNDABLE credit in the other state: report via Part III
## Servicemembers and spouses
Under 50 U.S.C. 4001, a servicemember or spouse may elect residence; without electing Wisconsin, generally no credit.
## 2026 notes
No Wisconsin statutory change identified. Retirement income subtraction bar — CONFIRMED: a taxpayer who claims the age-67 retirement income subtraction may not claim any credit on Schedule CR or on Form 1NPR lines 40-50 and 60-65 (2025 Schedule M instructions, line 50) — the Form 1 equivalents are lines 13-20 and 30-35, which include this credit (Form 1, line 20).
## Required Information
- Both pages of Schedule OS; copy of the other state's return; other-state W-2s/withholding statements
- K-1s and entity statements for pass-through tax
## Questions
- Which state did you pay tax to, and on what income?
- Were you a Wisconsin resident when you earned it?
- Is the income wages from Illinois, Indiana, Kentucky, or Michigan?
- Did a pass-through entity pay tax for you, and did it elect entity-level tax in Wisconsin?
## Common Errors
- Using withholding instead of net tax
- Claiming for reciprocity-state wages
- Using 100% of a long-term gain as dual-taxed income
- Omitting the state code on Form 1, Line 20
- Claiming for an entity that elected Wisconsin entity-level tax
## Prompt
- I live in Wisconsin and work in Minnesota.
- I sold a rental property in Illinois.
