# Wisconsin Credits — Index (Tax Year 2026)
## Description
Top-level map of the Wisconsin credits in this taxonomy, organized the same way as federal/credits/. Each folder has a category file plus one file per credit.
## Form basis — read first
For 2026 Wisconsin still conforms to the IRC as amended to December 31, 2022 (Wisconsin Tax Bulletin 234, July 2026): most 2025 federal law changes do not apply, and TCJA provisions suspended through 2025 are restored for Wisconsin.
Every Wisconsin schedule in the source set is the 2025 revision (Wisconsin DOR does not release 2026 schedules until late in 2026). The rules below are carried forward to 2026 because they are statutory and no 2026 change was found, and the year-dependent dates (lookback years, carryforward window, filing deadlines) have been rolled forward one year. LINE NUMBERS are 2025 numbering and must be confirmed against the 2026 Form 1, Form 1NPR, and Schedule CR when released.

## Wisconsin concepts that have no federal equivalent
- CREDIT IS INCOME. Most Wisconsin business credits (EC, BD, EIT, CM, farmland preservation) must be reported as income on the Wisconsin return for the year computed — even if the credit is carried forward rather than used. EXCEPTION: manufacturing and agriculture credits are income on the return for the year AFTER they are computed (the 2025 M&A credit is income on the 2026 return)
- BUSINESS INCOME LIMITATION. Individual M&A credits can only offset Wisconsin tax on the income of the business that generated them
- TRANSFERABLE CREDITS. The historic rehabilitation supplement (and certain angel, seed, economic development, and new film credits) can be sold; DOR must certify each transfer, and the seller and buyer recognize capital gain
- THE RETIREMENT INCOME SUBTRACTION BAR. Beginning 2025 (2025 Wis. Act 15), a taxpayer 67+ who claims the retirement income subtraction (up to $24,000; $48,000 joint if both spouses are 67+) may NOT claim any credit on Schedule CR or on Form 1 lines 13-20 and 30-35 (Form 1NPR lines 40-50 and 60-65) — i.e., every Wisconsin credit, including EIC, homestead, farmland preservation, school property tax, child care, and other-state credits — and may not use carryforwards that year. Continues for 2026. See ../deductions/retirement/retirement-income-subtractions.md
- OPTIONAL USE OF NONREFUNDABLE CREDITS. Because the statutes say credits "may" be claimed, the taxpayer chooses how much of each nonrefundable credit to use in a year (except the manufacturer's sales tax credit). See credit-administration/schedule-cf-carryforward.md
- WEDC CERTIFICATION. Most business credits require certification from the Wisconsin Economic Development Corporation, and the certification must be attached
- PASS-THROUGH K-1s. Partnerships, LLCs, and tax-option (S) corporations generally cannot claim business credits; they pass through on Schedules 3K-1 / 5K-1, and fiduciaries allocate on Schedule 2K-1

## 2026 law changes reflected (Wisconsin Tax Bulletins 232-234)
- New credits: stillborn child, employer-provided child care, rail infrastructure (2026); film credits (Act 15) clarified by Act 174
- Research credit carryforward extended to 50 years (Act 220); low-income housing bond-financing requirement removed (Act 236); historic supplement: $50,000 substantial-rehabilitation test and 15-year parcel cap (Act 238); enterprise zone expanded to one aviation biofuel business (Act 164); business development credit covers third-party workforce housing/child care contributions (WTB 232)
- Obsolete credits repealed by Act 118, with repeal delayed until remaining carryforwards expire
## Not covered (not individual credits)
- Community development entity investment credit (insurance companies only; Act 191)
- Long-term care insurance assessment credit (insurers; taxable years beginning after December 31, 2026 — not 2026; Act 227)
## Not yet in this taxonomy
All individual Form 1 credits are now built. Remaining items are corporation-only (community development finance credit carryforward, Schedule CR Part II) and fiduciary-specific variations.
