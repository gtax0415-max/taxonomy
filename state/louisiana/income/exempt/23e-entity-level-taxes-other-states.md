# Entity-Level Taxes Paid to Other States (Code 23E)
## Description
When a partnership or S corporation pays another state's entity-level income tax (a "PTET"), a Louisiana owner may deduct their share instead of claiming a credit.

## Rules
- Deduct the owner's share of entity-level tax paid to another state on or after January 1, 2018
- The other state's tax must be based solely on net income (no capital component)
- Limited to the extent the related income is included in Louisiana income
- This deduction is INSTEAD OF the credit for taxes paid to other states for the same tax; never both
- Per LDR's instructions, allowed only if the entity did not make the Louisiana entity-level election

Example: a partner's $1,500 share of another state's PTET → $1,500 deduction.

## Notes
- Authority: R.S. 47:33(A)(7). Unchanged for 2026
- At Louisiana's 3% rate a $1,500 deduction saves about $45; the credit (see credit-for-taxes-paid-other-states) may be worth more, so compare

## Required Information
- Owner's share of tax and income
- Whether the credit is being claimed instead

## Questions
- Did a business you own pay income tax to another state at the entity level?
- Are you claiming a credit for that tax instead?

## Common Errors
- Taking both the deduction and the credit
- Deducting a tax with a capital component

## Prompt
- My partnership paid another state's PTET. What do I do on my Louisiana return?
