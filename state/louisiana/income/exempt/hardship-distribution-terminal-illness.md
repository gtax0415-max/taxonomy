# Hardship Distributions After a Terminal Illness Diagnosis (New for 2026)
## Description
Louisiana residents diagnosed with a terminal illness may deduct hardship distributions taken from their retirement accounts.

## Rules
- Deduct amounts received as hardship distributions from elective-deferral retirement plans (such as 401(k) or 403(b)) for an immediate and heavy financial need, as determined under the plan
- No dollar limit
- Taxpayer must be diagnosed with a terminal illness by a licensed physician; on a joint return, the spouse's diagnosis also qualifies
- "Terminal illness" uses the definition in Louisiana's Right to Try Act (R.S. 40:1169.3), which may call for a second physician's evaluation; confirm what LDR will require
- State retirement plans are not elective-deferral plans and are not covered

Example: a filer with a terminal diagnosis takes a $15,000 401(k) hardship withdrawal → $15,000 deduction.

## Notes
- Authority: R.S. 47:297.26, enacted by Act 251 of the 2025 Regular Session, for tax periods beginning on or after January 1, 2026
- No Schedule E code or LDR guidance yet; use the code on the 2026 form

## Required Information
- Distribution amount and plan
- Physician's diagnosis and date

## Questions
- Did you or your spouse take a hardship withdrawal after a terminal diagnosis?
- Was it from a 401(k), 403(b), or similar plan?

## Common Errors
- Deducting an ordinary early withdrawal or IRA distribution that isn't a plan hardship distribution
- Deducting distributions from a state retirement system
