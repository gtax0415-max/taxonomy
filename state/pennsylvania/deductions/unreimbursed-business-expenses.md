# Unreimbursed Employee Business Expenses (PA Schedule UE)
## Description
Pennsylvania's ONE surviving employee expense deduction, and the ONLY exception to the rule that federal below-the-line deductions do not reach a PA return. It does NOT go on line 10.
## It reduces a CLASS, not the total
THIS IS THE DISTINCTION THAT MATTERS:
```
Line 1a   Gross compensation
Line 1b   LESS allowable employee business expenses, PA Schedule UE
Line 1c   NET COMPENSATION
...
Line 9    Total PA taxable income
Line 10   Other deductions, Schedule O
```
Schedule UE reduces the COMPENSATION CLASS at line 1b, BEFORE the classes are totalled. Schedule O reduces the TOTAL at line 10, after.
Putting a UE expense on line 10 is wrong. So is putting an HSA contribution on line 1b.
## The consequence of the class system
Because UE expenses reduce the compensation class specifically, THEY CANNOT CREATE OR INCREASE A LOSS THAT SHELTERS OTHER INCOME. PA line 9 adds only POSITIVE amounts from each class. Expenses exceeding compensation do not flow anywhere else — they simply stop at zero for that class.
A taxpayer with $10,000 of compensation and $14,000 of genuine unreimbursed expenses gets a $10,000 reduction, not a $4,000 loss against interest or business income.
## PA kept what the TCJA eliminated
| | Federal after 2017 | Pennsylvania |
|---|---|---|
| Unreimbursed employee expenses | ELIMINATED | ALLOWED on Schedule UE |
The federal 2% miscellaneous deduction is gone. Pennsylvania's survives. A union member, tradesperson, or outside salesperson with real out-of-pocket costs has a PA deduction and no federal one.
NOTE THE CONTRAST WITH NEW YORK, which also kept these expenses but as an ITEMIZED deduction on Form IT-196 subject to a 2% floor. Pennsylvania has no itemized deductions and no floor — the expense reduces compensation directly.
## The general test
The expense must be ORDINARY, NECESSARY, REASONABLE, ACTUALLY PAID, DIRECTLY RELATED to the job, and UNREIMBURSED. An expense the employer would have reimbursed on request is not deductible, whether or not the employee asked.
Detailed category rules, the allowable and disallowed lists, and the treatment of vehicle, travel, home office, education, and union expenses are in the PA SCHEDULE UE INSTRUCTIONS, which run to several pages and change more often than the line 10 deductions. Read them for any substantial claim.
## Who can use it
EMPLOYEES only, against COMPENSATION. A self-employed person deducts business expenses on PA Schedule C against the business class instead — the same expense, a different schedule and a different class.
## 2026
No change announced. The DFO-02 What's New for 2025 notes that SCHEDULE UE HAS BEEN REDESIGNED for clarity and electronic compatibility; check the current schedule rather than a prior year's layout.
## Required Information
- Each expense by category, with receipts and logs
- The employer's reimbursement policy
- Gross compensation the expenses are claimed against, by employer
- For vehicle expenses, a mileage log distinguishing business from commuting
## Questions
- Were the expenses required by your job and not reimbursed?
- Would your employer have reimbursed them had you asked?
- Are you an employee, or self-employed and therefore on Schedule C?
- Do the expenses exceed your compensation, in which case the excess is lost?
## Common Errors
- Claiming UE expenses on line 10 instead of line 1b
- Expecting excess expenses to shelter interest, business, or other class income
- Claiming expenses the employer would have reimbursed
- Including commuting mileage
- A self-employed person using Schedule UE instead of Schedule C
- Using a prior-year Schedule UE after the redesign
## Prompt
- I bought my own tools for work in Pennsylvania.
- Can I deduct union dues on my PA return?
- My employer does not reimburse my mileage.
