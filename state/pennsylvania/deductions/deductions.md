# Pennsylvania Deductions
## Description
Pennsylvania allows FIVE DEDUCTIONS and nothing else. There is NO standard deduction, NO itemized deductions, and NO personal exemption. The flat 3.07% tax applies to income with almost nothing subtracted from it.
The five are enumerated in statute, claimed on PA SCHEDULE O, and entered on PA-40 LINE 10 with a one-letter code.
## Folders and files

## Where deductions sit on the PA-40
```
Line 1a   Gross compensation
Line 1b   LESS unreimbursed employee business expenses, PA Schedule UE
Line 1c   Net compensation
Lines 2-8 The other seven income classes
Line 9    TOTAL PA TAXABLE INCOME — positive amounts only
Line 10   OTHER DEDUCTIONS — Schedule O, codes M H S T A C
Line 11   ADJUSTED PA TAXABLE INCOME
Line 12   PA TAX LIABILITY, line 11 x 3.07%
```
TWO DIFFERENT MECHANISMS. Schedule UE reduces one CLASS of income before the classes are totalled. Schedule O reduces the TOTAL after. Putting a UE expense on line 10, or a Schedule O deduction against compensation, produces the wrong answer in both directions.
## The five deductions and their codes
| Code | Deduction | 2025 and 2026 limit |
|---|---|---|
| M | MEDICAL SAVINGS ACCOUNT contributions | The federal limit |
| H | HEALTH SAVINGS ACCOUNT contributions | The federal limit |
| S | STUDENT LOAN INTEREST | $2,500 |
| T | TUITION ACCOUNT PROGRAM, IRC 529 | $19,000 per beneficiary, per taxpayer |
| A | PENNSYLVANIA ABLE, IRC 529A | $19,000, the annual federal gift tax exclusion |
| C | COMBINED, two or more of the above | — |
SIX CODES for FIVE deductions, because C covers a combination.
## THE CAP THAT OVERRIDES EVERYTHING
LINE 10 CANNOT EXCEED LINE 9. Deductions cannot reduce taxable income below zero, cannot create a loss, and cannot carry forward. An unused deduction is simply lost.
ON A JOINT RETURN there is a second, tighter test: the amount on line 10 also CANNOT EXCEED the Total PA Taxable Income that WOULD HAVE BEEN CALCULATED for that taxpayer or spouse HAD THEY FILED SEPARATELY. So a spouse with no income cannot absorb the other spouse's deduction. You must compute a hypothetical separate figure to test it.
## The class system constrains everything upstream
Line 9 adds ONLY THE POSITIVE amounts from lines 1c through 8. A LOSS IN ONE CLASS CANNOT REDUCE INCOME IN ANOTHER CLASS. A taxpayer with $50,000 of compensation and a $30,000 business loss has $50,000 of PA taxable income, not $20,000.
This is the most consequential structural difference between the PA return and the federal one, and it sits above the deduction lines rather than in them.
## 2026 parameters
| Item | 2025 | 2026 | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| PA tax rate | 3.07% | 3.07% | Flat, not indexed |
| Student loan interest | $2,500 | $2,500 | Statutory |
| IRC 529 per beneficiary, per taxpayer | $19,000 | $19,000 | Tracks the annual federal gift tax exclusion |
| IRC 529A ABLE | $19,000 | $19,000 | Annual federal gift tax exclusion |
| MSA and HSA | Federal limits | Federal limits | PA follows federal |
THE 2026 GIFT TAX EXCLUSION IS UNCHANGED AT $19,000 under Rev. Proc. 2025-32, so the 529 and ABLE caps do not move. A third-party calculator site publishes $17,000 for 2026; that is the 2023 figure and is WRONG.

## Questions
- Did you contribute to an HSA, MSA, PA 529, or PA ABLE account?
- Did you pay student loan interest?
- Do you have unreimbursed employee expenses, which go to Schedule UE, not Schedule O?
- Is your PA taxable income large enough to absorb the deduction?
- If married filing jointly, can each spouse's own income support their own deduction?
