# Deductions Pennsylvania Does NOT Allow
## Description
The general rule: DEDUCTIONS AND EXEMPTIONS USED TO CALCULATE FEDERAL ADJUSTED GROSS INCOME OR FEDERAL TAXABLE INCOME ARE NOT PERMITTED for Pennsylvania, with the narrow exceptions in other-deductions/ and unreimbursed-business-expenses.md.
This file exists because a preparer arriving from a federal return will look for all of the below, and the answer is the same each time.
## Above-the-line federal adjustments — NOT allowed
| Federal | Pennsylvania |
|---|---|
| IRA deduction | NO PROVISION |
| Moving expenses | NO PROVISION |
| One-half of self-employment tax | NO PROVISION |
| Self-employed health insurance | NO PROVISION |
| Self-employed SEP, SIMPLE, and qualified plans | NO PROVISION |
| Penalty on early withdrawal of savings | NO PROVISION |
| Alimony paid | NO PROVISION |
ONE EXCEPTION HAS CHANGED. STUDENT LOAN INTEREST used to appear on this list. It is now DEDUCTIBLE up to $2,500 on Schedule O with code S. The Department's 2018 Deductions and Credits guide still shows "No provision" and is out of date.
## Below-the-line federal deductions — NOT allowed
| Federal | Pennsylvania |
|---|---|
| Standard deduction | NO PROVISION |
| Personal exemption | NO PROVISION |
| Medical and dental expenses | NO PROVISION |
| Taxes paid | NO PROVISION |
| Interest, including mortgage interest | NO PROVISION |
| Gifts to charity | NO PROVISION |
| Casualty and theft losses | NO PROVISION |
| Miscellaneous deductions | NO PROVISION |
| Qualified business income, IRC 199A | NO PROVISION |
ONE PARTIAL EXCEPTION: JOB EXPENSES. Federal unreimbursed employee expenses were eliminated by the TCJA. Pennsylvania still allows them, but on PA SCHEDULE UE against the compensation class, not as a general deduction. See unreimbursed-business-expenses.md.
## Two cautions the PA-40 instructions give explicitly
DO NOT REPORT MEDICAL INSURANCE PREMIUMS OR ITEMIZED MEDICAL EXPENSES from federal Schedule A on line 10. Line 10 is for the five statutory deductions only. A medical expense that is not an MSA or HSA contribution has no place on a PA return at all.
DO NOT CLAIM A DEDUCTION FOR A ROLLOVER between two IRC 529 plans, or between two IRC 529A plans, or for CHANGING BENEFICIARIES within either. Moving money that is already in the system is not a new contribution.
## What this means in practice
A Pennsylvania return for a homeowner with a large mortgage, high property taxes, substantial charitable giving, and significant medical expenses looks EXACTLY THE SAME as one for a renter with none of those things, at the same income. None of it is deductible.
The relief that would otherwise come through deductions comes instead through TAX FORGIVENESS on Schedule SP, which is income-tested rather than expense-tested. See state/pennsylvania/credits/income-based/tax-forgiveness.md.
## Questions
- Are you looking for a PA equivalent of a federal deduction?
- Was the expense an HSA, MSA, 529, ABLE contribution, or student loan interest? Those five are allowed
- Was it an unreimbursed EMPLOYEE expense? That goes to Schedule UE
- Would Tax Forgiveness help instead, since it tests income rather than expenses?
## Common Errors
- Carrying a federal itemized deduction onto the PA return
- Reporting medical insurance premiums on line 10
- Claiming a deduction for a 529 or ABLE rollover or beneficiary change
- Assuming the QBI deduction reduces PA income
- Assuming student loan interest is still disallowed, per the outdated 2018 guide
## Prompt
- Can I deduct my mortgage interest in Pennsylvania?
- Does PA allow itemized deductions?
- I have big medical bills, do they help my PA return?
