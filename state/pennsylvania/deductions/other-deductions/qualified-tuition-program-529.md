# IRC Section 529 Qualified Tuition Program Contributions
## Description
CODE T. Pennsylvania's most valuable deduction by dollar amount, and the only one with a per-beneficiary structure that multiplies across children.
## Amount: $19,000 PER BENEFICIARY, PER TAXPAYER
For 2025 and 2026 the limit is $19,000 PER BENEFICIARY, PER TAXPAYER.
A TAXPAYER AND SPOUSE MAY EACH DEDUCT $19,000 PER BENEFICIARY. So a married couple with three children can deduct up to:
```
$19,000 x 2 taxpayers x 3 beneficiaries = $114,000
```
This is the largest deduction available anywhere on a Pennsylvania return, and it dwarfs everything else in this folder.
## The limit tracks the federal gift tax exclusion
The cap equals the ANNUAL FEDERAL GIFT TAX EXCLUSION under IRC 2503(b) per beneficiary per taxpayer.
| Year | Annual gift tax exclusion | PA 529 cap |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 | $19,000 | $19,000 |
| 2026 | $19,000 | $19,000 |
THE 2026 FIGURE IS UNCHANGED. Rev. Proc. 2025-32 holds the exclusion at $19,000 for 2026. IRC 2503(b)(2) indexes a $10,000 base and rounds DOWN to the next whole $1,000, so the published figure sits flat for a year or two then jumps. 2025 was a jump year; 2026 is flat.
A third-party calculator site publishes $17,000 per beneficiary for 2026; THAT IS THE 2023 FIGURE AND IS WRONG.
## Any state's 529 plan qualifies
Pennsylvania's deduction is not limited to the PA 529 Guaranteed Savings Plan or Investment Plan. Contributions to ANY IRC Section 529 Qualified Tuition Program qualify.
There is no federal deduction for 529 contributions at all, so this is a PA-only benefit with no federal counterpart to compare against.
## NO deduction for rollovers or beneficiary changes
The instructions state it twice:
- NO DEDUCTION for a ROLLOVER from one IRC 529 plan into another
- NO DEDUCTION for CHANGING BENEFICIARIES within an IRC 529 account
Only NEW MONEY entering the system qualifies. Moving existing money does not, however it is characterised by the plan.
## The cap that can waste it
LINE 10 CANNOT EXCEED LINE 9, and on a joint return it cannot exceed what each spouse's own income would have supported had they filed separately.
A COUPLE WHERE ONE SPOUSE EARNS EVERYTHING CANNOT STACK BOTH $19,000 ALLOWANCES AGAINST THE EARNING SPOUSE'S INCOME. The non-earning spouse's allowance is real but has no income to absorb it, and it does not carry forward. Contributions should be made by the spouse with income, from that spouse's own funds, if the deduction is the objective.
## Interaction with the refund donation lines
A taxpayer may also route part of a PA REFUND into a PA 529 account using DONATION CODE H on PA-40 lines 32 through 36, which requires PA Schedule P. Whether a refund routed that way supports a Schedule O deduction, and in which year, is a question for the Schedule O and Schedule P instructions. Do not assume it does. See state/pennsylvania/donations/refund-donation-lines.md.
## 2026
$19,000 per beneficiary, per taxpayer. Unchanged.
## Required Information
- Contributions made during the calendar year, by beneficiary and by contributing spouse
- Each beneficiary's name and account number
- Confirmation that amounts are new contributions, not rollovers or beneficiary changes
- Each spouse's own PA taxable income, for the joint-return cap
## Questions
- How much did you contribute, to which beneficiaries, and which spouse contributed?
- Were any amounts rollovers from another 529 or a beneficiary change?
- Is the plan a PA 529 or another state's? Either qualifies
- On a joint return, does each contributing spouse have enough own income to absorb their share?
## Common Errors
- Using $17,000 or another stale gift-tax figure for 2026
- Treating the cap as per taxpayer rather than PER BENEFICIARY PER TAXPAYER, and understating a multi-child deduction
- Claiming a deduction for a rollover or beneficiary change
- Stacking both spouses' allowances against one spouse's income on a joint return
- Assuming only the PA 529 plan qualifies
