# Medical Savings Account and Health Savings Account Contributions
## Description
CODE M for a Medical Savings Account, CODE H for a Health Savings Account. Two of the five Pennsylvania deductions, and the only two that simply mirror federal treatment.
## Pennsylvania follows the federal rules exactly
- IF YOU CAN TAKE THE DEDUCTION FEDERALLY, you may claim it for PA
- IF YOU CANNOT TAKE IT FEDERALLY, YOU MAY NOT CLAIM IT FOR PA
- THE AMOUNT CANNOT EXCEED THE FEDERAL LIMITS
There is no separate PA computation, no separate PA limit, and no PA-only eligibility. Federal eligibility is the whole test.
## The attachments are mandatory
You must complete PA SCHEDULE O and include BOTH:
- A copy of FEDERAL FORM 1040 SCHEDULE 1
- PAGES 1 AND 2 OF FEDERAL FORM 1040
This is a heavier documentation requirement than the other three deductions carry, because the PA amount is derived entirely from the federal return.
## What is NOT deductible here
THE INSTRUCTIONS CARRY AN EXPLICIT CAUTION: do NOT report MEDICAL INSURANCE PREMIUMS or ITEMIZED MEDICAL EXPENSES from federal Schedule A on line 10.
This line is for CONTRIBUTIONS TO AN ACCOUNT, not for medical spending. A taxpayer with $20,000 of unreimbursed medical bills and no HSA has nothing to claim.
Pennsylvania allows no medical expense deduction of any kind. See not-allowed.md.
## Archer MSAs are largely historical
Archer Medical Savings Accounts have been closed to new participants for many years, so CODE M is rare. Most taxpayers with a code here use H. For Archer MSA background see Personal Income Tax Bulletin 2006-05.
## The distribution side
PA follows federal treatment on contributions. The taxability of DISTRIBUTIONS is a separate question governed by PA's income class rules, not by this deduction. A qualified distribution used for medical expenses is generally not taxable; a nonqualified distribution may be. Do not assume symmetry with the federal answer without checking the class treatment.
## 2026
No change. PA continues to follow the federal limits, so the 2026 HSA contribution limits flow through automatically with no change in PA law.
## Required Information
- Federal Schedule 1 as filed, and pages 1 and 2 of federal Form 1040
- The federal deduction amount actually claimed
- Confirmation the contribution was eligible federally
## Questions
- Did you claim an HSA or Archer MSA deduction on your federal return?
- Do you have federal Schedule 1 and pages 1 and 2 of Form 1040 to attach?
- Are you trying to deduct medical PREMIUMS or EXPENSES, which do not qualify?
## Common Errors
- Claiming a PA deduction where no federal deduction was allowed
- Exceeding the federal limit
- Omitting the federal Schedule 1 or the Form 1040 pages
- Reporting medical insurance premiums or Schedule A medical expenses on line 10
- Using code M for an HSA; H is the HSA code
## Prompt
- I contributed to my HSA, can I deduct it in PA?
- Does Pennsylvania allow medical deductions?
