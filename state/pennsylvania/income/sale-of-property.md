# Net Gain or Loss from the Sale, Exchange, or Disposition of Property (PA-40 Line 5)
## Description
Gains and losses from ANY KIND of intangible property and any real or tangible property, plus your share from a partnership or PA S corporation. One of only THREE CLASSES THAT CAN SHOW A LOSS.
## FOUR FEDERAL CONCEPTS THAT DO NOT EXIST IN PA
PA LAW DOES NOT:
1. DISTINGUISH LONG-TERM FROM SHORT-TERM gains or losses
2. HAVE PROVISIONS RELATING TO CASUALTY LOSSES
3. HAVE PROVISIONS RELATING TO SALES TO RELATED PARTIES
4. ALLOW THE INSTALLMENT SALE METHOD for the sale of INTANGIBLE property such as STOCK OR GOODWILL
The fourth is the one that causes trouble in practice. A business sale structured as an installment sale of goodwill is reported IN FULL for PA in the year of sale, while the federal return spreads it. The PA tax arrives years before the cash does.
## What IS allowed
LIKE-KIND EXCHANGES: the deferral of tax due on gains from like-kind exchanges of property IS ALLOWED. The DFO-02 What's New notes SCHEDULE D WAS UPDATED FOR 2025 to accommodate like-kind exchanges and to add a DIGITAL ASSET QUESTION.
IRC SECTION 1033 involuntary conversions: gains not required to be reported federally are NOT REQUIRED TO BE REPORTED FOR PA on transactions occurring AFTER SEPTEMBER 11, 2016.
IRC SECTION 1035 insurance contract exchanges: do NOT report gain or loss on tax-exempt exchanges of:
- A life insurance contract for another life insurance, endowment, or annuity contract
- An annuity contract for another annuity contract
- An endowment contract for an annuity contract
- One endowment contract for another where payments begin on or before the original contract's dates
IF THE EXCHANGE TRANSFERS PROPERTY TO A NON-U.S. CITIZEN, THE GAIN OR LOSS IS NOT TAX-EXEMPT. Cash or other boot has its own treatment.
## What goes on Schedule D
Land and buildings; properties held for investment INCLUDING RENTAL PROPERTIES; stocks and bonds; and other property dispositions.
Also routed here:
- An EXCESS FORFEITED INTEREST PENALTY over the related interest income, from line 2
- Payments for giving up ALL RIGHTS to mineral interests, patents, or copyrights, from line 6
- Distributions in excess of basis from a partnership or PA S corporation
- Income from the sale or exchange of a CHARITABLE GIFT ANNUITY
## Offsetting
You MAY offset YOUR OWN net gains against YOUR OWN losses in determining this line. If you realize an OVERALL NET LOSS, FILL IN THE OVAL next to line 5.
SPOUSES MAY NOT OFFSET EACH OTHER. The Department's worked example: Mary's $6,000 net gain and Ben's $6,000 net loss produce a REPORTED GAIN OF $6,000, not zero. See class-system-and-losses.md.
NO CARRYFORWARD. An unused capital loss is lost permanently — there is no PA equivalent of the federal $3,000 deduction or the indefinite carryover.
## Capital gain distributions are NOT here
Box 2a mutual fund capital gain distributions are DIVIDEND income on line 3, not line 5, and therefore CANNOT be offset by losses reported here. See dividends.md.
## Sales tax and basis
If you ELECTED TO CURRENTLY EXPENSE sales tax on acquired property for PA purposes, THE PA BASIS DIFFERS FROM THE FEDERAL BASIS and depreciation must be adjusted accordingly. YOU CANNOT EXPENSE SALES TAX AND STILL DEPRECIATE IT. If you do not expense it, add it to basis and depreciate the aggregate.
## Required Information
- Proceeds and basis for each disposition
- PA basis where it differs from federal, including expensed sales tax
- Whether property is intangible, where the installment method is barred
- Like-kind exchange documentation
- Which spouse owned each item
## Questions
- Are you selling intangible property on an installment basis? PA taxes it all now
- Do you have losses you expect to carry forward? PA has none
- Does your PA basis differ from federal because of expensed sales tax?
- Are you married with gains in one spouse and losses in the other?
## Common Errors
- Using the installment method for stock or goodwill
- Expecting a capital loss carryforward or a $3,000 ordinary offset
- Netting a spouse's loss against the other spouse's gain
- Reporting Box 2a capital gain distributions here
- Claiming a casualty loss, for which PA has no provision
- Using federal basis where sales tax was currently expensed for PA
