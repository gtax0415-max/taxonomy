# Retirement Income
## Description
Pennsylvania taxes almost no retirement income, which is why a PA retiree's return is often nearly blank. The rule is not an exemption but a TIMING DIFFERENCE: PA taxes the CONTRIBUTION and exempts the DISTRIBUTION; the federal system does the reverse.
## What is NOT taxable
PENNSYLVANIA DOES NOT IMPOSE INCOME TAX ON PAYMENTS COMMONLY RECOGNIZED AS RETIREMENT BENEFITS DISTRIBUTED FROM ELIGIBLE EMPLOYER-SPONSORED RETIREMENT PLANS.
Eligible employer-sponsored plans CAN, BUT DO NOT NECESSARILY, include:
- Employer-sponsored deferred compensation plans
- Pension or profit sharing plans
- 401(k) plans
- Thrift plans and thrift savings plans
- Employee welfare plans
Eligible NON-employer-sponsored plans CAN, BUT DO NOT NECESSARILY, include IRAs and Roth IRAs.
"CAN, BUT DO NOT NECESSARILY" IS THE OPERATIVE PHRASE. Eligibility is plan-specific. ASK THE EMPLOYER OR PLAN ADMINISTRATOR whether the plan is eligible for PA purposes. Do not assume from the plan type.
SOCIAL SECURITY is not taxed by Pennsylvania at all.
## The four exceptions that ARE taxable
All amounts from an eligible plan are taxable IN THE YEAR RECEIVED, EXCEPT:
1. Payments received AFTER YOU QUALIFY FOR RETIREMENT AND RETIRE
2. Payments ROLLED OVER into another deferred payment program or retirement IRA, but ONLY where the transferred amounts are not federally taxable
3. Distributions from an employer-sponsored deferred compensation plan representing YOUR PREVIOUS CONTRIBUTIONS
4. Payments to the ESTATE OR DESIGNATED BENEFICIARY upon death
Anything outside those four is TAXABLE COMPENSATION on line 1a.
## THE TWO-PART TEST
A distribution is exempt only if you have BOTH QUALIFIED FOR RETIREMENT UNDER THE PLAN AND ACTUALLY RETIRED.
Reaching 59½ is NOT the test. Someone who is 62, still working, and draws from a 401(k) has NOT retired, and the distribution is TAXABLE COMPENSATION.
Conversely someone who retires at 55 under a plan permitting it has met both parts, and the distribution is exempt.
PA IMPOSES NO ADDITIONAL 10% PENALTY on an early distribution. It simply taxes the amount as compensation.
## Contributions are taxed going in
Contributions to an employer-sponsored plan ARE PA-TAXABLE COMPENSATION even where not federally taxable and even where excluded from W-2 state wages. See compensation.md.
Because the contribution was already taxed, a distribution of YOUR OWN PREVIOUS CONTRIBUTIONS is not taxed again.
## Roth conversions
No PA tax is due on the difference between the amount distributed from a traditional IRA and your previous contributions IF:
1. You rolled the ENTIRE withdrawal DIRECTLY, trustee to trustee, from the traditional IRA to the Roth IRA; or
2. You withdrew and within 60 DAYS invested THE ENTIRE 100 PERCENT of the amount received into a Roth IRA
CAUTION THAT COSTS MONEY: IF FEDERAL TAX WAS WITHHELD FROM A ROLLOVER DISTRIBUTION, THE AMOUNT WITHHELD MUST ALSO BE REIMBURSED INTO THE NEW IRA for the rollover to be nontaxable for PA. A taxpayer who lets the withholding stay behind has failed the 100 percent test and creates PA taxable income.
For detail see Tax Bulletin 2008-01.
## Annuities are INTEREST, not compensation
An annuity NOT part of an employer-sponsored retirement program produces PA-taxable income when payments begin, and it is reported as INTEREST INCOME on PA Schedule A line 11, not as compensation. The same applies to life insurance and endowment contract amounts reportable federally. See interest.md.
## Early retirement incentives
Payments received AS AN INDUCEMENT TO RETIRE EARLY ARE TAXABLE.
## Documentation
PAPER FILERS MUST SUBMIT COPIES OF ALL FORMS 1099-R FOR ALL TYPES OF DISTRIBUTIONS, REGARDLESS OF WHETHER THE DISTRIBUTION IS TAXABLE OR NONTAXABLE for PA purposes. The Department needs them to verify that a nontaxable amount is genuinely nontaxable.
E-FILERS submit 1099-Rs only where PA tax was withheld.
## Tax Forgiveness does NOT count retirement income
Retirement benefits from PA-eligible plans received after qualifying and retiring are EXCLUDED from Schedule SP eligibility income, as are Social Security, Railroad Retirement, military pensions, and Civil Service annuities. A PA retiree can therefore have substantial cash income and qualify for full Tax Forgiveness. See credits/income-based/tax-forgiveness.md.
## Required Information
- Every Form 1099-R received
- Plan eligibility confirmation from the employer or administrator
- The date you qualified for retirement under the plan and the date you retired
- For a rollover, proof the full amount including any federal withholding reached the new account
## Questions
- Had you qualified for retirement under the plan AND actually retired when the distribution was made?
- Is the plan an ELIGIBLE PA plan? Ask the administrator
- Was any federal tax withheld from a rollover, and did you replace it?
- Is the payment an annuity outside an employer plan, which is interest income?
## Common Errors
- Assuming age 59½ makes a distribution nontaxable; retirement is the test
- Drawing from a 401(k) while still working and treating it as exempt
- Assuming a plan is eligible from its type rather than confirming
- Failing to replace federal withholding in a rollover, breaking the 100 percent test
- Reporting an annuity as compensation instead of interest
- E-filing habits carried to a paper return, where ALL 1099-Rs must be attached
## Prompt
- Does Pennsylvania tax my pension?
- I am 62 and still working but took money from my 401(k).
- I converted my IRA to a Roth.
