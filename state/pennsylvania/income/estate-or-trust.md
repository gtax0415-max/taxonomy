# Estate or Trust Income (PA-40 Line 7)
## Description
As a beneficiary you report the TOTAL PA-TAXABLE INCOME THAT THE ESTATE OR TRUST MUST DISTRIBUTE, PAY, OR CREDIT TO YOU, on PA SCHEDULE J.
## YOU CANNOT REPORT A LOSS
YOU CANNOT REPORT A LOSS AS A BENEFICIARY FOR PA PURPOSES. This class cannot be negative under any circumstances.
## If you received a federal Schedule K-1 instead
YOU SHOULD RECEIVE A PA SCHEDULE RK-1 AND/OR NRK-1 from the estate or trust. If you received a FEDERAL Schedule K-1 instead:
REPORT ONLY THE TOTAL INCOME, NOT TAKING INTO ACCOUNT ANY LOSSES, shown on the federal K-1.
ADD the total of income reported as INTEREST, DIVIDENDS, LONG-TERM AND SHORT-TERM GAINS, ANNUITIES, ROYALTIES, TRADE OR BUSINESS INCOME, RENTAL INCOME, and similar items.
IF YOU INCLUDED ANY OF THESE AMOUNTS ON A PA SCHEDULE OTHER THAN SCHEDULE J, ADJUST THAT SCHEDULE ACCORDINGLY — otherwise the same income is counted twice.
INCLUDE A PHOTOCOPY OF THE FEDERAL SCHEDULE K-1 with your return for EACH NONRESIDENT estate or trust, and for EACH estate or trust from which you FAIL TO RECEIVE a PA Schedule RK-1 or NRK-1.
## The gift, bequest, and inheritance exclusion
CASH AND PROPERTY YOU ACQUIRE FROM AN ESTATE OR TRUST BY GIFT, BEQUEST, DEVISE, OR INHERITANCE IS NOT TAXABLE.
YOU MUST REPORT ANY PA-TAXABLE INCOME THAT YOU SUBSEQUENTLY EARN OR RECEIVE FROM THAT CASH OR PROPERTY. The corpus is free; what it later earns is not.
## Revocable trusts
The SETTLOR who establishes a revocable trust, including a revocable grantor trust, DOES NOT FILE A PA-41 if under the governing instrument they retain the authority to completely revoke the trust without the declaration of new uses, and the related conditions. The income is reported by the settlor directly.
## PA does NOT follow the federal grantor trust rules
Federally, grantor trust income belongs to the settlor. FOR PA PURPOSES IT IS TAXABLE TO THE TRUST.
This mismatch has a hard consequence for the resident credit: where a PA resident trust receives income sourced to a state that follows the federal base, PA taxes the TRUST and the other state taxes the SETTLOR. Because they are DIFFERENT TAXPAYERS for PA purposes, NEITHER may claim a resident credit. The double tax is real and unrelieved. See credits/other-jurisdiction/resident-credit.md.
The DFO-02 What's New for 2025 notes THE RULES REGARDING GRANTOR TRUSTS HAVE CHANGED. Check the current PA-41 guidance before relying on prior-year treatment.
## A resident credit cannot be passed to you
An estate or trust MAY EARN a resident credit for tax paid to another state, but IT MAY NOT PASS THAT CREDIT THROUGH to a PA resident beneficiary, EVEN WHERE THE INCOME IS DISTRIBUTED. Estates and trusts are NOT pass-through entities for PA personal income tax purposes.
Likewise, RESTRICTED CREDITS awarded to or passed to an estate or trust MAY NOT be passed to its beneficiaries.
## Required Information
- PA Schedule RK-1 or NRK-1 from each estate or trust
- Federal Schedule K-1 photocopies where a PA schedule was not received
- Identification of any amounts already reported on another PA schedule
- The governing instrument, where revocability matters
## Questions
- Did you receive a PA Schedule RK-1 or NRK-1, or only a federal K-1?
- Have you reported any of the same income on another PA schedule?
- Is the trust revocable, which changes who files?
- Did the trust pay tax to another state? The credit stops there
## Common Errors
- Reporting a loss as a beneficiary
- Netting losses against income on a federal K-1 instead of taking total income only
- Double-counting income already reported on another PA schedule
- Omitting the federal K-1 photocopy where required
- A beneficiary claiming a resident credit the trust earned
- Applying federal grantor trust treatment to a PA return

- I am a beneficiary of my mother's trust.
