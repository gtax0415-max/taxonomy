# The Class System and Loss Rules
## Description
The rules that make a Pennsylvania return behave unlike a federal return. Read this before any individual class file.
## RULE 1: Line 9 adds only POSITIVE amounts
ADD ONLY THE POSITIVE INCOME AMOUNTS FROM LINES 1c THROUGH 8. DO NOT ADD, SUBTRACT, OR TAKE LOSSES INTO CONSIDERATION.
A loss in one class contributes ZERO to line 9. It does not reduce the total.
## RULE 2: No cross-class offset
YOU CANNOT USE A LOSS REPORTED IN ONE INCOME CLASS TO REDUCE INCOME IN ANY OTHER CLASS.
| | Federal | Pennsylvania |
|---|---|---|
| Business loss against wages | Allowed | NOT ALLOWED |
| Capital loss against ordinary income | Up to $3,000 | NOT ALLOWED |
| Rental loss against other income | Subject to passive limits | NOT ALLOWED |
A taxpayer with $50,000 of compensation and a $30,000 Schedule C loss reports $50,000 on line 9.
## RULE 3: Losses only on lines 4, 5, and 6
LOSSES MAY ONLY BE REPORTED ON LINES 4, 5, AND 6 — business, sale of property, and rents/royalties. Fill in the OVAL next to the line when reporting a loss.
The other five classes CANNOT show a loss:
- COMPENSATION cannot go below zero, even with large Schedule UE expenses
- INTEREST and DIVIDENDS cannot be negative
- ESTATE OR TRUST INCOME: you CANNOT report a loss as a beneficiary for PA purposes
- GAMBLING: losses are deductible only up to winnings, never below zero
## RULE 4: Within a class, you may offset your own income against your own losses
Inside lines 4, 5, and 6 you MAY offset your OWN income against your OWN losses from other activities in the SAME class. A profitable flower shop and a losing consultancy net against each other on line 4.
## RULE 5: No carryforward, no carryback
YOU CANNOT CARRY FORWARD OR CARRY BACK GAINS OR LOSSES TO OTHER TAX YEARS. A loss unused in the year it arises is gone permanently.
There is no PA net operating loss for individuals, no capital loss carryover, and no passive activity carryforward. PA requires PASSIVE RENTAL AND ROYALTY LOSSES TO BE REPORTED IN FULL in the year incurred rather than carried over.
## RULE 6: SPOUSES ARE SEPARATE TAXPAYERS
This is the rule that surprises people most, and it applies WHETHER FILING JOINTLY OR SEPARATELY.
SPOUSES MAY NOT USE EACH OTHER'S EXPENSES TO REDUCE INCOME OR OFFSET EACH OTHER'S INCOME AND LOSSES.
PA LAW PROVIDES NO ADVANTAGE TO FILING JOINTLY. A joint return is FOR CONVENIENCE ONLY.
The three-step rule:
1. IF EACH SPOUSE HAS A NET PROFIT, gain, or income — ADD them and report the total
2. IF EACH HAS A NET LOSS — add the losses, report the total, and fill the oval
3. IF ONE HAS INCOME AND THE OTHER A LOSS — REPORT ONLY THE INCOME. DO NOT TAKE THE SPOUSE'S LOSS INTO ACCOUNT
### The Department's worked examples
LINE 4. Mary has a flower shop with a $5,000 profit. Ben has a PA S corporation RK-1 with $35,000 of business income. They JOINTLY own a retail store with an $8,000 loss.
Each takes HALF the joint loss. Mary: $5,000 less $4,000 = $1,000. Ben: $35,000 less $4,000 = $31,000. THEY REPORT $32,000.
LINE 5. The retail store sells at a $6,000 loss, split equally. Mary sold her own stock for a $9,000 gain; Ben sold his own stock for a $3,000 loss.
Mary: $9,000 less her $3,000 half = $6,000 GAIN. Ben: $3,000 loss plus his $3,000 half = $6,000 LOSS.
BEN'S LOSS CANNOT REDUCE MARY'S GAIN. THEY REPORT A $6,000 GAIN.
LINE 6. A jointly owned rental loses $2,000, split equally. Mary's rental partnership share is a $1,000 loss. Ben's PA S corporation rental share is a $4,000 loss.
Mary's total loss $2,000; Ben's total loss $5,000. Both are losses, so they add: $7,000 loss reported with the oval filled.
### What this means in practice
A couple where one spouse runs a profitable business and the other a losing one gets NO NETTING. The profitable spouse pays 3.07% on the full profit while the other spouse's loss evaporates.
JOINTLY OWNED activity is SPLIT BETWEEN THE SPOUSES first, then each spouse's own total is tested.
## RULE 7: Ownership must be tracked
Because spouses are separate taxpayers, every item of income, gain, loss, and expense must be attributable to ONE SPOUSE or SPLIT between them. Records that do not identify the owner cannot be reported correctly on a joint return.
This also drives Schedule G-L, where SEPARATE SCHEDULES ARE REQUIRED for taxpayer and spouse, and Schedule OC, where married taxpayers generally MUST FILE SEPARATELY.
## The contrast with Tax Forgiveness
Line 9 excludes losses and excludes most nontaxable income. ELIGIBILITY INCOME on Schedule SP is far broader and includes items PA never taxes. The two measures are not interchangeable, and a taxpayer with a low line 9 may still have high eligibility income. See credits/income-based/tax-forgiveness.md.
## Required Information
- Each item of income and loss, by class AND by owning spouse
- For jointly owned activity, the split
- Confirmation that no loss has been used against another class
## Questions
- Which classes have losses, and are they on lines 4, 5, or 6?
- Are you married, and does each spouse have their own income and losses?
- Is any activity jointly owned and therefore split?
- Are you expecting a carryforward from a prior year? PA has none
## Common Errors
- Netting a business loss against compensation
- Deducting a capital loss against ordinary income
- Carrying forward a prior-year loss
- Offsetting one spouse's loss against the other spouse's income on a joint return
- Reporting a loss on a line other than 4, 5, or 6
- Reporting a loss as an estate or trust beneficiary
- Assuming a joint return produces a tax advantage
