# Interest Income (PA-40 Line 2)
## Description
A class that CANNOT SHOW A LOSS and that absorbs several items the federal system classifies elsewhere — most notably ANNUITIES.
## Annuities are interest, not retirement income
If you invested in an ANNUITY, INCLUDING A RETIREMENT ANNUITY THAT IS NOT PART OF AN EMPLOYER-SPONSORED PROGRAM, you may have PA-taxable income when payments begin. IF THE AMOUNT IS REPORTABLE FEDERALLY, IT IS REPORTED AS INTEREST INCOME FOR PA on PA SCHEDULE A LINE 11.
The same applies to LIFE INSURANCE AND ENDOWMENT CONTRACT amounts reportable federally.
This catches people who expect an annuity to follow the retirement rules. A commercial annuity bought with personal funds is NOT an eligible employer-sponsored plan, so the retirement exemption does not apply and the income lands here instead.
## Charitable gift annuities
A gift annuity to a charitable organization from which you receive periodic payments produces PA-taxable income, reported as interest on PA SCHEDULE A LINE 12, using the amounts reported or included in federal gross income — BOTH ORDINARY INCOME AND CAPITAL GAINS INCOME — regardless of when reporting began.
The PA BASIS in a charitable gift annuity DIFFERS FROM THE FEDERAL BASIS for annuities whose payments began before January 1, 2005. A later sale or exchange is reported on PA Schedule D.
## Forfeited interest penalty
A penalty for premature redemption or withdrawal of a time savings account or CD may be offset ONLY AGAINST THE INTEREST INCOME RECEIVED IN THE SAME YEAR FROM THAT SAME ACCOUNT OR CERTIFICATE.
YOU CANNOT OFFSET IT AGAINST OTHER INTEREST INCOME.
If the penalty EXCEEDS the related interest, THE EXCESS MAY BE REPORTED AS A LOSS ON PA SCHEDULE D — moving it to the line 5 class, which can take a loss, because line 2 cannot.
This is a rare and useful cross-class routing, and it is specifically authorised.
## Tax-exempt interest
PA and federal exemptions are NOT the same. The issuer determines the answer. Nontaxable interest must still be identified because of the Tax Forgiveness rule below.
## Nontaxable interest still counts for Tax Forgiveness
YOU MUST INCLUDE NONTAXABLE INTEREST AND DIVIDENDS AS ELIGIBILITY INCOME ON PA SCHEDULE SP.
So exempt interest is excluded from line 2 and INCLUDED in the Schedule SP test. A taxpayer with a large municipal bond portfolio may have little PA taxable income and too much eligibility income to qualify for forgiveness.
## No loss possible
Line 2 cannot be negative. See class-system-and-losses.md.
## Schedule A reconciles to the federal figure
PA Schedule A STARTS FROM THE INTEREST REPORTED ON YOUR FEDERAL RETURN and adjusts. PA EXEMPTS direct obligations of Pennsylvania and of the U.S. GOVERNMENT, and ADDS BACK federally tax-exempt interest from other issuers. It also pulls in 529 non-educational distributions and HSA/MSA distributions as interest. See federal-to-pa-adjustments.md for the full line map.
## Required Information
- Form 1099-INT and 1099-OID from each payer
- Annuity, life insurance, and endowment statements
- The issuer of any tax-exempt interest
- Early withdrawal penalty amounts and the related account
## Questions
- Do you receive annuity payments outside an employer plan?
- Did you pay an early withdrawal penalty on a CD?
- Do you have tax-exempt interest, which still counts for Tax Forgiveness?
- Do you have a charitable gift annuity?
## Common Errors
- Treating a commercial annuity as exempt retirement income
- Offsetting a forfeited interest penalty against unrelated interest
- Omitting nontaxable interest from Schedule SP eligibility income
- Reporting an annuity on line 1a as compensation
## Prompt
- I get payments from an annuity I bought myself.
- I cashed a CD early and paid a penalty.
- Is my municipal bond interest taxed in PA?
