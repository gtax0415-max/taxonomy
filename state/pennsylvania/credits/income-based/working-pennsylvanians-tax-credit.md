# Working Pennsylvanians Tax Credit (WPTC)
## Description
Pennsylvania's FIRST earned income credit, created in the 2025-26 budget signed by Governor Shapiro on NOVEMBER 12, 2025. EFFECTIVE TAX YEAR 2025, first claimed during the 2026 filing season. Continued by the 2026-27 budget signed July 12, 2026.
## It IS refundable
Confirmed. The credit is REFUNDABLE — a taxpayer receives it even with no Pennsylvania income tax liability. That matters enormously here, because PA's flat 3.07% rate means a low-income worker's entire PA liability is often smaller than the credit itself.
Refundability also means the Schedule OC ordering cap does NOT strangle it. Had it been treated as a restricted credit, Tax Forgiveness would have consumed the liability first and left nothing for exactly the population the credit targets.
## Amount
10% OF YOUR FEDERAL EARNED INCOME CREDIT.
| Tax year | Maximum | Derivation |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 | $805 | 10% of the $8,046 federal maximum |
| 2026 | VERIFY — roughly $823 on the published federal figures | 10% of the $8,231 federal maximum under Rev. Proc. 2025-32 |
THE $805 FIGURE IS THE TAX YEAR 2025 NUMBER. Because the credit is a straight 10% of the federal EITC and the federal maximum is indexed annually, the PA maximum should move with it. The Department has not published a separate 2026 ceiling; confirm against the 2026 PA-40 instructions rather than carrying $805 forward.
## The 10% rate in context
The federal EITC is the entire input, and PA adds 10% on top of it. A filer at the federal maximum of $8,046 for 2025 receives about $805 from Pennsylvania.
Unlike the federal credit, which phases in and out on its own schedule, the PA credit simply tracks whatever the federal computation produced.

## Eligibility
- EARNED INCOME IN PENNSYLVANIA — wages, salary, or self-employment income
- QUALIFY FOR THE FEDERAL EITC
- File BOTH the federal Form 1040 AND the PA-40
Anyone qualifying federally AUTOMATICALLY qualifies for the state credit. There is no separate PA income test, no separate investment income limit, and no separate residency test beyond PA earned income.
Federal eligibility is therefore the whole question, and every federal rule flows through: the investment income cliff, the valid-SSN requirement, the bar on married filing separately, and the disallowance periods after an improper claim.
## Attach the federal Form 1040
The Department requires A COPY OF THE FILED FEDERAL FORM 1040 with the PA-40. Without it the credit cannot be verified.
## The Department computes it automatically for joint e-filers
For taxpayers who file their federal and state returns ONLINE AT THE SAME TIME, the Department automatically calculates the WPTC. It also mails explanatory letters to recipients. A separate ONLINE CALCULATOR is available at pa.gov/wptc to check eligibility and estimate the amount.
## First-year results
$216.7 million delivered to more than 876,000 Pennsylvanians in the first filing season, against a $193 million projection for 940,000. The Department expected roughly 64,000 more claimants as late returns processed.
## Notes
- The federal EITC is the input, so the federal return must be complete first
- A taxpayer barred from the federal EITC after a reckless or fraudulent claim loses the PA credit with it
- The credit varies by filing status, income, and number of dependents, following the federal structure exactly
## Required Information
- The federal EITC amount actually allowed, from Form 1040 line 27
- A copy of the filed federal Form 1040
- Federal Schedule EIC, if children are claimed
- Earned income sourced to Pennsylvania

## Common Errors
- Assuming PA has no earned income credit, which was true before the 2025-26 budget
- BELIEVING THE CREDIT STARTS WITH TAX YEAR 2026. It is effective TAX YEAR 2025
- Treating it as nonrefundable and skipping it for a client with no PA liability
- Omitting the federal Form 1040 copy
- Carrying the $805 maximum into 2026 without confirming the indexed figure
- Using 10% of a federal credit that was reduced or disallowed on audit
- Applying a percentage other than 10%
