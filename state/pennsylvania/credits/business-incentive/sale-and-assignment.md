# Sale and Assignment of Restricted Credits
## Description
Pennsylvania permits certain restricted credits to be SOLD OR ASSIGNED to unrelated taxpayers. There is an active broker market. The tax consequences are unusual and catch both sides — BOTH the seller AND the purchaser report a gain.
## Which credits are saleable
- Research and Development
- Film Production
- Neighborhood Assistance Program
- Resource Enhancement and Protection (REAP)
- Keystone Innovation Zone
- Keystone Special Development Zone
- Historic Preservation Incentive
The portion exceeding the awardee's own liability may be carried forward as the enabling legislation allows, UNLESS the awardee elects to sell, assign, or pass it through to owners.
## The seller reports a gain, with ZERO basis
The sale is taxable as a GAIN ON THE SALE OF INTANGIBLE PROPERTY, reported on PA-40 Schedule D (or PA-41 Schedule D, or PA-20S/PA-65 Schedule D).
THE COST BASIS IS USUALLY ZERO. The credit was awarded on income or expenses already reflected in a current or prior return, with no adjustment required to obtain it. So essentially the entire sale price is gain.
- ACQUISITION DATE: the date the credit was AWARDED
- DATE SOLD: the date the sale was consummated
- SALES PRICE: gross proceeds LESS any broker or agent commissions
## The purchaser ALSO reports a gain
This is the part people miss. A purchaser who buys a credit for LESS THAN ITS FULL VALUE must report a gain, because they acquire something worth more than they paid.
- BASIS: the FULL purchase price, including commissions the purchaser paid
- DATE ACQUIRED: the purchase date
- DATE SOLD: the TAX YEAR END DATE for the year the credit is applied, usually December 31
- SALES PRICE: the full value of the credit ACTUALLY ALLOWED on the return
Buying an $100,000 credit for $92,000 produces roughly $8,000 of PA taxable gain in the year it is used.
## Why the mechanics handle unusable credits automatically
By recording only the credit AMOUNT ALLOWED as the sales price, and the full purchase price as basis, a purchased credit that cannot be fully used because of the liability cap requires NO pro-ration of cost and NO separate loss reporting. The unused portion simply produces less gain.
## The one-year holding rule
An awardee must generally WAIT ONE YEAR from the date the credit is granted before selling or assigning it. Any return filed before that year ends must claim as much of the credit as the taxpayer can use. This is stated for REAP and applies to the sale regime generally — verify per credit.
## Documentation
A taxpayer who sells a credit, or offsets liability with a purchased or assigned credit, does NOT need to send sale documentation with the PA return. The Department reserves the right to request it.
Purchased credits must carry credit description code PA in the description box on Schedule OC.
## Joint returns are barred
Joint returns CANNOT be filed when restricted credits are claimed. Separate PA-40s are required for taxpayer and spouse.
## Entity filing requirement before sale
Entities no longer filing the RCT-101 Corporate Tax Report, following the Capital Stock and Foreign Franchise Tax phase-out, MUST FILE the PA-20S/PA-65 Information Return BEFORE selling a restricted credit.
## Required Information
- Award date and certified amount
- Sale or purchase date and gross proceeds or price
- Broker commissions paid, by whom
- The credit amount actually allowed on the return
- Confirmation the one-year holding period was met
## Questions
- Are you the seller or the purchaser?
- When was the credit originally awarded?
- What did you pay, or receive, and what commissions were involved?
- How much of a purchased credit can you actually use this year?
- Has the entity filed its PA-20S/PA-65 before selling?
## Common Errors
- A purchaser failing to report a gain on the discount
- A seller using a basis other than zero
- Selling within one year of the award
- Filing jointly while claiming a purchased credit
- Omitting the PA description code on Schedule OC
- Pro-rating the cost of an unusable purchased credit instead of letting the mechanics handle it
- Selling before filing the required PA-20S/PA-65
