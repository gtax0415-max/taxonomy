# Other Jurisdiction Credits
## Description
Relief from double taxation when a Pennsylvania resident's income is taxed by another state. PA's version is narrow in three specific ways, each of which surprises people.
## Credits in this folder
- Resident Credit for Tax Paid to Another State (resident-credit.md) — PA Schedule G-L → PA-40, Line 22
## Three limits worth knowing
| Situation | Pennsylvania |
|---|---|
| FOREIGN COUNTRIES | NO CREDIT since January 1, 2014 |
| POLITICAL SUBDIVISIONS of other states | NO CREDIT, ever |
| RECIPROCAL STATES | NO CREDIT on compensation — those states do not tax it |
PA denies a credit for LOCAL taxes of other states entirely. A PA resident working in a city that imposes its own income tax gets nothing from PA for it.
CONTRAST WITH THE FEDERAL POSITION: the federal foreign tax credit relieves tax paid to another COUNTRY. PA relieves tax paid to another STATE and, since 2014, NOT to a foreign country at all. The two credits cover opposite territory and neither substitutes for the other.
## The reciprocal agreements
Pennsylvania has compensation reciprocity with SIX STATES:
INDIANA · MARYLAND · NEW JERSEY · OHIO · VIRGINIA · WEST VIRGINIA
No resident credit is granted for tax paid to these states on COMPENSATION, because they do not tax a PA resident's compensation in the first place. The right answer is not a credit — it is to stop the withholding at the source by filing the other state's nonresident exemption certificate.
Reciprocity covers COMPENSATION ONLY. Business income, rental income, and gains from those same states are NOT covered and DO generate a resident credit.

## Questions
- Which states taxed your income?
- Was it compensation, or business and rental income?
- Is the other state one of the six reciprocal states?
- Did you pay tax to a city or county of another state?
