# Pennsylvania Credits
## Description
Pennsylvania's credit system looks nothing like the federal one, because the underlying tax is different. PA imposes a FLAT 3.07% tax on eight separate classes of income, with NO standard deduction, NO personal exemption, and NO itemized deductions. There is very little tax to credit against, so PA has historically offered very few personal credits.
That changed recently. Three of the five personal credits in this folder did not exist when the Department's 2018 Deductions and Credits guide was written.
## Folders
- family-dependent/ — Child and Dependent Care Enhancement Credit, Schedule DC
- income-based/ — Tax Forgiveness (Schedule SP) and the new Working Pennsylvanians Tax Credit
- other-jurisdiction/ — Resident credit for tax paid to another state, Schedule G-L
- civic-volunteer/ — Organ and Bone Marrow Donor credit
- business-incentive/ — the restricted tax credits on Schedule OC
## Where credits sit on the PA-40
```
Line 12   PA tax liability (taxable income x 3.07%)
Line 13   Total PA tax withheld
Line 21   TAX FORGIVENESS CREDIT, from Schedule SP
Line 22   RESIDENT CREDIT, from Schedule G-L (PA-41 Line 15)
Line 23   TOTAL OTHER CREDITS, from Schedule OC line 11 and Schedule DC (PA-41 Line 16)
Line 24   TOTAL PAYMENTS AND CREDITS
Line 29   Overpayment
Line 30   Refund
Line 31   Credit to next year's estimated account
Lines 32-36  REFUND DONATION LINES — codes A through J. NOT CREDITS. See state/pennsylvania/donations/
```
## The ordering rule
PA prescribes a strict order, and it matters because most PA credits are nonrefundable:
1. RESIDENT CREDIT first
2. TAX FORGIVENESS second
3. RESTRICTED CREDITS (Schedule OC) last
Schedule SP makes the first step explicit: line 12 is the PA liability, line 13 subtracts the resident credit, and the forgiveness percentage applies to the NET figure on line 14. Schedule OC credits are then capped by what survives. The Department's example: a $4,000 liability reduced by a $3,000 resident credit leaves only $1,000 of room for Schedule OC credits.

## Lines 32 through 36 are NOT credits and are NOT in this domain
The PA-40 carries five REFUND DONATION LINES with organization CODES A THROUGH J. They GIVE MONEY AWAY and produce no Pennsylvania tax benefit, so they live in a separate domain at STATE/PENNSYLVANIA/DONATIONS/, not here.
## Property Tax/Rent Rebate is NOT on the PA-40
Pennsylvania's Property Tax/Rent Rebate Program is a real and substantial benefit for older and disabled Pennsylvanians, but it is a SEPARATE PROGRAM claimed on FORM PA-1000, not a credit on the PA-40. It is administered as a rebate check: real money, wrong form.
MAXIMUM STANDARD REBATE $1,000, following the first expansion since 2006. AN APPLICATION IS REQUIRED EVERY YEAR. The deadline for rebates on property taxes or rent paid in 2025 was EXTENDED TO DECEMBER 31, 2026. Rebates are issued starting July 1 by law. See pa.gov/ptrr.
## 2026 TAX PARAMETERS
Confirmed figures for tax year 2026 and the 2026-27 budget signed JULY 12, 2026, a $50.85 billion plan that CONTINUES ALL SEVEN of the Governor's tax cuts.
| Item | 2025 | 2026 | Status |
|---|---|---|---|

| Working Pennsylvanians Tax Credit | $805 max, 10% of federal EITC | Continues; VERIFY the indexed maximum | Funded at over $200 million for a second year |
| Child and Dependent Care Enhancement | 100% of federal, $600 to $2,100 | 100% of federal | $444.5 million over four years |

## Where the 2026 figures are genuinely uncertain
THREE ITEMS TO VERIFY rather than carry forward:
1. THE WPTC MAXIMUM. $805 is 10% of the 2025 federal EITC ceiling of $8,046. The federal ceiling is indexed and rises to $8,231 for 2026, which implies roughly $823. The Department has published no separate 2026 figure
2. THE CHILD CARE CREDIT CEILING. PA pays 100% OF THE FEDERAL CREDIT, and the federal credit's top rate rises from 35% to 50% beginning 2026 under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. If PA's 100% match is applied to the new federal amounts, THE PA MAXIMUM RISES AUTOMATICALLY WITH NO CHANGE IN PA LAW. Confirm against the 2026 Schedule DC before quoting $2,100
3. TAX FORGIVENESS THRESHOLDS. Statutory and long unchanged. A third-party calculator site asserts expanded 2026 thresholds; that is UNCONFIRMED by the Department
## Recent history
- WORKING PENNSYLVANIANS TAX CREDIT created in the 2025-26 budget signed November 12, 2025, EFFECTIVE TAX YEAR 2025. First filing season delivered $216.7 million to more than 876,000 Pennsylvanians
- CHILD AND DEPENDENT CARE ENHANCEMENT CREDIT enacted 2022 at 30% of the federal credit, RAISED TO 100% for tax years after December 31, 2022, lifting the maximum from $630 to $2,100
- EMPLOYER CHILD CARE CONTRIBUTION TAX CREDIT created in 2024, up to 30% of eligible employer contributions to employee child care costs. It appears on Schedule OC for 2025 as ECTC
- STUDENT LOAN INTEREST became deductible on Schedule O, up to $2,500 per filer. A deduction, not a credit, and it reverses the 2018 guide's "no provision"
- SCHEDULE OC significantly redesigned for tax year 2025 (rev. EX MOD 08-25), with TWO NEW CREDITS: Employer Childcare (ECTC) and 529 / ABLE Employer Matching Contribution (SAAB). Its total now goes to PA-41 LINE 16, not line 14 as the 2018 guide states
- SCHEDULE SP for 2025 carries revision EX MOD 04-25. Its eligibility income tables are statutory and unchanged
- SCHEDULE G-L is still the 03-24 revision. No 2026 revision has issued
## Local taxes are outside this folder
PA has local Earned Income Tax collected by municipalities and school districts, and the Philadelphia Wage Tax. NEITHER APPEARS ON THE PA-40. Philadelphia residents file with the City, not the Commonwealth, and no resident credit is available for tax paid to a political subdivision of ANOTHER state. See other-jurisdiction/.
