# Filing Status (S, J, M, F, or D)
## Description
The PA-40 offers FIVE OVALS in the Filing Status block, of which three are marital statuses and two describe a circumstance.
| Oval | Meaning |
|---|---|
| S | SINGLE |
| J | MARRIED, FILING JOINTLY |
| M | MARRIED, FILING SEPARATELY |
| F | FINAL RETURN, with a reason indicated |
| D | DECEASED |
## PA has no head of household and no qualifying surviving spouse
| Federal status | PA oval |
|---|---|
| Single | S |
| Married filing jointly | J |
| Married filing separately | M |
| HEAD OF HOUSEHOLD | S — no PA equivalent exists |
| QUALIFYING SURVIVING SPOUSE | S — no PA equivalent exists |
A federal HOH or QSS filer marks SINGLE for Pennsylvania. PA loses nothing by this, because it has no rate schedule, no standard deduction, and no exemptions for a filing status to adjust.
THE ONE PLACE IT MATTERS is Tax Forgiveness, where Schedule SP has its OWN status block at line 19a with its own definitions. A federal HOH filer is UNMARRIED for Schedule SP and uses Eligibility Income Table 1.
## Filing jointly is for convenience only
PA LAW PROVIDES NO ADVANTAGE TO FILING A JOINT RETURN. Spouses:
- May NOT offset each other's income and losses
- May NOT use each other's expenses
- Are computed SEPARATELY in every class
- Must complete SEPARATE Schedules A, B, D, and G-L where any adjustment applies
A joint return is a single piece of paper carrying two separate computations. See income/class-system-and-losses.md.
## When separate returns are REQUIRED
- MOST SCHEDULE OC RESTRICTED CREDITS require separate returns, except EITC and OSTC
- WHERE ONE SPOUSE IS A DEPENDENT on another person's federal return, otherwise-qualifying married taxpayers must file separately for Tax Forgiveness
THE CONFLICT TO CHECK: the Child and Dependent Care Enhancement Credit requires a JOINT return under IRC 21(e)(2), while restricted credits require SEPARATE returns. A married couple cannot claim both in the same year.
## Married filing separately still uses JOINT eligibility income
A married taxpayer CANNOT claim Tax Forgiveness independently of a spouse. Both must use JOINT eligibility income and Table 2 EVEN WHEN FILING SEPARATELY, and each must certify the figures match. THE SPOUSE'S SSN MUST APPEAR EITHER WAY.
## The federal contrast
Federal filing status drives the standard deduction, the rate brackets, and dozens of phase-outs. PA HAS NONE OF THOSE, so the PA oval carries far less weight — it mainly controls whether one return or two is filed and which Schedule SP table applies.
## Required Information
- Marital status on December 31
- Whether any credit being claimed forces joint or separate filing
- Both SSNs, including on a separate return
- The date of death, where the D oval is used
## Questions
- What was your marital status on December 31?
- Are you a federal head of household or qualifying surviving spouse, which PA does not recognise?
- Are you claiming a restricted credit, which generally requires separate returns?
- Are you claiming the child care credit, which requires a joint return?
## Common Errors
- Looking for a head of household oval on the PA-40
- Assuming a joint return produces a PA tax advantage
- Filing jointly while claiming a Schedule OC restricted credit
- Claiming both a restricted credit and the child care credit as a married couple
- Omitting the spouse's SSN on a married-filing-separately return
## Prompt
- I file as head of household federally, what do I use for PA?
- Should we file jointly in Pennsylvania?
- My spouse and I file separately.
