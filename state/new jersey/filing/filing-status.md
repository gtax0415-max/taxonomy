# Filing Status (Lines 1–5)
## Description
New Jersey generally follows the FEDERAL filing status. Fill in only one oval.
## Statuses
1. SINGLE — not married or in a civil union on December 31, 2026, and not head of household or qualifying widow(er)
2. MARRIED/CU COUPLE, FILING JOINT RETURN
3. MARRIED/CU PARTNER, FILING SEPARATE RETURN — enter the spouse's/partner's SSN
4. HEAD OF HOUSEHOLD — meets the federal head of household requirements
5. QUALIFYING WIDOW(ER)/SURVIVING CU PARTNER — meets the federal qualifying surviving spouse requirements and the spouse died in 2024 or 2025; fill in the oval for the year of death
## Why filing status matters in New Jersey
- Tax rate schedule: Table A (single, MFS) vs Table B (joint, HOH, qualifying widow(er))
- Filing threshold: $10,000 vs $20,000
- Retirement exclusion maximums: $100,000 / $75,000 / $50,000
- Married filing separately is barred from the NJ Child Tax Credit and restricted for the NJ EITC and Child and Dependent Care Credit
## Civil unions
Partners in a NJ civil union file using the married statuses under NJ law; their NJ status may not match their federal status. For credits based on federal amounts (EITC, Child and Dependent Care Credit), they compute a mock joint federal amount and fill in the civil union ovals.
## Questions
- What filing status did you use on your federal return?
- Are you in a New Jersey civil union?
- Did your spouse die in 2024 or 2025, and do you have a dependent child?
- Was your spouse a nonresident of New Jersey all year?
## Common Errors
- Using a different status from the federal return without a civil union or nonresident-spouse reason
- Selecting qualifying widow(er) when the spouse died in 2023 or 2026
- Omitting the spouse's SSN when filing separately
## Prompt
- My wife and I file jointly federally. Can we file separately in New Jersey?
- My husband lives in Pennsylvania and I live in New Jersey.
