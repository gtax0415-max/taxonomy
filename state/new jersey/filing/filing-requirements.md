# Filing Requirements and Residency
## Who must file (2026 — thresholds unchanged)
A resident must file if NJ gross income from everywhere for the entire year is MORE than:
| Filing status | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Single; married/CU partner filing separately | $10,000 |
| Married/CU couple filing jointly; head of household; qualifying widow(er)/surviving CU partner | $20,000 |
Also file if NJ tax was withheld, estimated payments were made, or the taxpayer qualifies for a refundable credit and is owed a refund. Exempt income (Social Security, unemployment, etc.) is ignored for this test.
## Residency
- DOMICILE is the permanent home the taxpayer intends to return to; a person has only one. It does not change until the taxpayer moves with intent to abandon NJ domicile
- A home maintained only temporarily for a particular purpose (e.g., a temporary job assignment) is not a permanent home; a vacation home is not a permanent home
- A non-domiciliary who keeps a permanent home in NJ and spends more than 183 days in NJ is a resident
- NJ residents living or working abroad have the same filing requirements as residents at home
- See GIT-6, Part-Year Residents and Nonresidents
## Part-year residents
- Enter the From/To dates of NJ residency (MM/DD/26)
- Report income only for the resident period; prorate exemptions, deductions, credits, and retirement exclusions by months of residence (15+ days = one month)
- There is no single part-year return — file NJ-1040 for the resident period and NJ-1040NR for NJ-source income as a nonresident
- A part-year resident whose full-year income is at or below the threshold and who files for a refund must enclose the federal return (or a statement that none was filed)
## Header items
- SOCIAL SECURITY NUMBERS for taxpayer and spouse/CU partner (joint)
- COUNTY/MUNICIPALITY CODE — four-digit code of the CURRENT residence from the table in the instructions
- Name, address; ovals for foreign address and change of address
- FEDERAL EXTENSION oval — fill in if a federal extension was filed
- FISCAL YEAR FILERS — month the fiscal year ends; the return is due the 15th day of the fourth month after year end
## Extension of time to file
- Six-month extension if at least 80% of the tax liability (Line 45) is paid by April 15, 2027, AND either a federal extension is filed (enclose a copy if filed on paper, fill in the oval) or Form NJ-630 is filed by April 15, 2027
- No extension of time to PAY; penalties and interest run from April 15, 2027 on unpaid tax
- If the 80% test or the extended deadline is missed, the extension is denied and penalties run from the original due date
- Military personnel in combat zones have separate extension rules
## Digital assets question (required)
- Answer YES if the taxpayer (or spouse) RECEIVED a digital asset as a reward, award, or payment for property or services, or SOLD, EXCHANGED, or otherwise DISPOSED of a digital asset or a financial interest in one during 2026
- Digital assets include cryptocurrencies, stablecoins, and NFTs — anything recorded on a cryptographically secured distributed ledger
- Report the income in its category: capital asset dispositions on Schedule NJ-DOP (Line 19); compensation on Line 15; business inventory on NJ-BUS-1; anything else on Line 26
## Questions
- Where was your permanent home during 2026, and did you move?
- What is your NJ gross income for the whole year?
- Did you have NJ tax withheld or qualify for the NJ EITC or Child Tax Credit?
- Did you receive, sell, or exchange any cryptocurrency or other digital asset?
- Did you file a federal extension?
## Common Errors
- Using federal AGI instead of NJ gross income for the filing threshold
- Part-year residents reporting full-year income on the NJ-1040
- Leaving the digital asset question blank
- Treating an extension as extra time to pay
- Low-income filers not filing and forfeiting refundable credits
