# Medical Expenses (Worksheet F)
## Description
Unreimbursed medical expenses above 2% OF NJ GROSS INCOME, plus Archer MSA contributions and the self-employed health insurance deduction. The 2% floor is far lower than the federal 7.5% floor, so many taxpayers who cannot deduct medical costs federally can in New Jersey — and no itemizing is required.
## Worksheet F
1. Total unreimbursed medical expenses
2. Line 29 (NJ gross income) × 2%
3. Line 1 minus Line 2 (not below zero)
4. Qualified Archer MSA contributions (federal Form 8853)
5. Self-employed health insurance deduction
6. Total (Lines 3 + 4 + 5) → Line 31
## Qualifying expenses (generally the same as federal)
Physician, dental, and other medical fees; prescription eyeglasses and contacts; hospital and nursing care; medicines and drugs; prosthetics; physician-directed X-rays and diagnostics; transportation essential to medical care; health insurance premiums, including Medicare Part B.
## Notes
- Covers the taxpayer, spouse/domestic partner, and dependents claimed
- CHILD UNDER 27: federal rules allow health insurance for any child under 27; NJ allows it ONLY if the child is the taxpayer's dependent (TAM 2011-14)
- Self-employed health insurance is deducted HERE, not on Schedule NJ-BUS-1
- Part-year residents: only expenses incurred and paid while a resident
## Required Information
- Receipts and insurance reimbursement records
- Premium totals (including Medicare Part B)
- Form 8853; self-employed premium records
- NJ gross income (Line 29)
## Questions
- What did you pay out of pocket for medical, dental, vision, and prescriptions?
- Did you pay health insurance premiums yourself, including Medicare?
- Are you self-employed and paying your own health insurance?
- Do you cover an adult child who is not your dependent?
## Common Errors
- Applying the federal 7.5% floor instead of 2%
- Using federal AGI instead of NJ gross income for the floor
- Including premiums for a non-dependent adult child
- Including expenses reimbursed by insurance or paid pre-tax
## Prompt
- I had $8,000 in medical bills. Can I deduct them in New Jersey?
- I pay for my own health insurance as a freelancer.
