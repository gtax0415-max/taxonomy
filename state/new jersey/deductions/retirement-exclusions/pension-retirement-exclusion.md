# Pension/Retirement Exclusion
## Description
Exclusion of taxable pension, annuity, and IRA income (Line 20a) for taxpayers age 62+ or disabled, with total income of $150,000 or less.
## Maximum exclusion by filing status and Line 27 income (2026 — unchanged)
| Filing status | Line 27 $0–$100,000 | $100,001–$125,000 | $125,001–$150,000 | Over $150,000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Married/CU couple filing jointly | $100,000 | 50% of Line 20a | 25% of Line 20a | None |
| Single, head of household, qualifying widow(er) | $75,000 | 37.5% of Line 20a | 18.75% of Line 20a | None |
| Married/CU partner filing separately | $50,000 | 25% of Line 20a | 12.5% of Line 20a | None |
Line 28a = lesser of Line 20a (Line A) or the chart amount (Line B).
## Eligibility
- Taxpayer (and/or spouse if joint) age 62 or older, OR blind/disabled under Social Security guidelines, on December 31, 2026
- Line 27 total income $150,000 or less
- JOINT FILERS: if only one spouse qualifies, only THAT spouse's pension income can be excluded
## Notes
- The test is TOTAL income (Line 27), before the exclusion
- Part-year residents: FULL-YEAR income must be $150,000 or less; if full-year income is $100,000 or less, prorate the maximum by months of residence; if over $100,000, do not prorate
- See GIT-1 & 2, Retirement Income
## Required Information
- Line 20a by spouse
- Ages and disability status
- Line 27 total income
## Questions
- Which spouse receives the pension, and is that spouse 62+ or disabled?
- Is your Line 27 income close to $100,000, $125,000, or $150,000?
## Common Errors
- Excluding the pension of a spouse under 62 who is not disabled
- Testing NJ gross income (Line 29) instead of total income (Line 27)
- Missing the cliff — a Roth conversion or large capital gain pushing Line 27 over $150,000 eliminates the exclusion
## Prompt
- I'm 64 and get a $60,000 pension. My wife is 58.
- How much retirement income can I exclude in New Jersey?
