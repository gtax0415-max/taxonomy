# Other Retirement Income Exclusion (and Special Exclusion)
## Description
Lets a taxpayer age 62+ apply the UNUSED portion of the pension exclusion maximum to OTHER income (interest, dividends, capital gains, etc.), provided earned income is very small. Also covers the Special Exclusion for people who will never receive Social Security.
## Unclaimed exclusion (Worksheet D)
1. Line 27 must be $150,000 or less
2. Maximum exclusion from the chart (same tiers as Line 28a, but the percentages apply to LINE 27, not Line 20a): $100,000 / $75,000 / $50,000 up to $100,000 of income; 50% / 37.5% / 25% of Line 27 from $100,001–$125,000; 25% / 18.75% / 12.5% of Line 27 from $125,001–$150,000
3. Subtract Line 28a; if nothing is left, stop
4. EARNED-INCOME TEST: wages (Line 15) + business profits (Line 18) + partnership income (Line 21) + S corporation income (Line 22) must NOT exceed $3,000. If over $3,000, no unclaimed exclusion
5. The remaining maximum goes on Line 28b
## Special Exclusion
If the taxpayer (and spouse, if joint) will NEVER be eligible for Social Security or Railroad Retirement because the employer did not participate: $6,000 (joint, head of household, qualifying widow(er)) or $3,000 (single, married filing separately). Not available to anyone who will ever be eligible. See GIT-1 & 2.
## Eligibility
- Age 62 or older on December 31, 2026 (disability alone does NOT qualify for this line)
- Joint filers: if only one spouse is 62+, only that spouse's income can be excluded
## Notes
- The $3,000 earned-income test is a CLIFF: $3,001 of wages eliminates the entire unclaimed exclusion
- Part-year residents use Worksheet E: full-year income tests; prorate the maximum by months only if full-year income is $100,000 or less
## Required Information
- Lines 15, 18, 21, 22, 27, 28a
- Ages of both spouses
- Social Security / Railroad Retirement eligibility status
## Questions
- Are you 62 or older?
- Did you have more than $3,000 in wages or self-employment income?
- Will you ever be eligible for Social Security or Railroad Retirement?
## Common Errors
- Claiming it with more than $3,000 of earned income
- Applying the tier percentages to Line 20a instead of Line 27
- Claiming it based on disability alone
- Claiming the Special Exclusion when eligible for Social Security in the future
## Prompt
- I'm 70, retired, and live on interest and dividends. Can I exclude them?
- I work part-time in retirement. Does that affect my exclusion?
