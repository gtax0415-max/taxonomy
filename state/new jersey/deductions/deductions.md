# New Jersey Exclusions, Exemptions, and Deductions
## Description
Top-level index of everything that reduces New Jersey income. New Jersey has NO standard deduction and NO federal-style itemized deductions. Instead it has three stages:
1. RETIREMENT EXCLUSIONS (Lines 28a–28c) reduce total income (Line 27) to NJ GROSS INCOME (Line 29)
2. EXEMPTIONS AND DEDUCTIONS (Lines 30–37c) reduce NJ gross income to TAXABLE INCOME (Line 39)
3. The PROPERTY TAX DEDUCTION (Line 41) reduces taxable income to NJ TAXABLE INCOME (Line 42), which is the base for tax and for the income tests of the Child Tax Credit and Child and Dependent Care Credit

## Deductions that do NOT exist in New Jersey
Unlike federal law there is no deduction for: charitable contributions (except qualified land conservation), mortgage interest, state and local taxes (other than the property tax deduction), unreimbursed employee expenses, moving expenses, student loan interest (other than NJCLASS), traditional IRA contributions, HSA contributions, educator expenses, or the 2025–2028 federal tips, overtime, and senior deductions.

## What changed for 2026
- ALTERNATIVE BUSINESS CALCULATION ADJUSTMENT (Line 35): still 50% of the business increment for gross income of $500,000 or less; cut to 25% for gross income over $500,000 up to $1 million; ELIMINATED over $1 million (FY2027 budget, A5323/S4537, effective for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2026)
- Exemption and deduction dollar amounts are NOT indexed and are unchanged: $1,000 personal, $6,000 veteran, $1,500 dependent, $15,000 property tax deduction, 2% medical floor, $10,000 / $2,500 / $10,000 education deductions, $10,000 organ donation
- Age cut-offs roll forward one year: senior exemption for those born in 1961 or earlier; college-student exemption for dependents born in 2005 or later
## Part-year residents
Exemptions (Line 30) and most deductions are prorated by months of NJ residence (15+ days = one month). Several income tests use FULL-YEAR income (retirement exclusions, college affordability deductions).
