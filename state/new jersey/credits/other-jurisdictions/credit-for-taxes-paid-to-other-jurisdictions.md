# Credit for Income Taxes Paid to Other Jurisdictions (Schedule NJ-COJ)
## Description
Nonrefundable credit for income or wage taxes paid to another state, a political subdivision of a state (such as a city), or D.C., on income that New Jersey ALSO taxed in the same year.
## How it works
- Line 1: income taxed by the other jurisdiction (and also by NJ)
- Line 2: NJ gross income (Form NJ-1040, Line 29)
- Line 3: Line 1 ÷ Line 2 = maximum allowable credit percentage, carried to SEVEN decimal places
- Lines 4–7 recompute NJ tax (column A with the property tax deduction, column B without)
- Line 8: NJ tax × the Line 3 percentage
- Box 9a: tax paid to the other jurisdiction on the Line 1 income — the final LIABILITY from that jurisdiction's return, or the amount withheld only if that jurisdiction does not require a return
- Line 9: the credit is the SMALLER of Line 8 or Box 9a
- It is NOT dollar-for-dollar: if the other jurisdiction's rate is higher than New Jersey's, the excess is lost
- Capped at the tax on Line 43; NONREFUNDABLE
## Jurisdictions that do NOT qualify
- U.S. Government
- Puerto Rico
- Any foreign country or territory
## Pennsylvania reciprocity
Under the NJ-PA Reciprocal Personal Income Tax Agreement, Pennsylvania does not tax a NJ resident's WAGES (salaries, tips, commissions, bonuses). No credit is available for PA tax wrongly withheld on wages — get a refund from Pennsylvania instead. Other PA-source income (self-employment, gains on property) is NOT covered and can qualify. The agreement does NOT cover the Philadelphia wage tax or any other PA municipal tax, which can qualify.
## Special computations
- NEW YORK: Line 1 generally uses the New York State amount actually taxed on Form IT-203, with adjustments where needed. Lump-sum distributions taxed by New York need a separate schedule
- PHILADELPHIA: Line 1 is the lesser of (A) Philadelphia wage tax paid ÷ the Philadelphia wage tax rate, or (B) Philadelphia wages included on Line 15. Subtract any Philadelphia wage tax refund received
- PHILADELPHIA BUSINESS: only the income-based portion of the Business Income and Receipts Tax counts; the gross-receipts portion does not
- S CORPORATIONS: no credit for another jurisdiction's tax on S corporation income allocated to New Jersey, for taxes imposed on or paid on behalf of another person, or for taxes on distributions
- PART-YEAR RESIDENTS: include only other-jurisdiction income earned while a NJ resident, taxed by both

## Notes
- Line 2 uses NJ GROSS income (Line 29), not taxable income
- Enter the jurisdiction code in the boxes at Line 44 and enclose every Schedule NJ-COJ
- See NJ publications GIT-3W (wage income) and GIT-3B (business/nonwage income)
## Required Information
- Other jurisdiction's return and the tax actually paid (not merely withheld)
- Income taxed by each jurisdiction
- NJ gross income (Line 29)
- Philadelphia wage tax paid, rate, and any refund
- Property tax deduction/credit eligibility (for Worksheet I)
## Questions
- Which states or cities taxed your income, and how much tax did you actually pay after filing their returns?
- Is any of it Pennsylvania wage income (covered by reciprocity)?
- Did you pay Philadelphia wage tax or New York City tax?
- Are you eligible for the property tax deduction or credit?
- Were you a New Jersey resident for all of the year?
## Common Errors
- Using tax WITHHELD instead of the final tax liability shown on the other state's return (withholding is used only when that jurisdiction requires no return)
- Claiming a credit for foreign or federal taxes
- Claiming a credit for PA tax on wages instead of seeking a PA refund
- Using NJ taxable income instead of NJ gross income on Line 2
- Combining several jurisdictions on one schedule
- Using Worksheet H instead of Worksheet I for the property tax benefit
- Rounding the Line 3 percentage to fewer than seven decimals
## Prompt
- I live in New Jersey and work in Manhattan.
- I work in Philadelphia and pay the city wage tax.
- I paid tax to both New Jersey and another state on the same income.
