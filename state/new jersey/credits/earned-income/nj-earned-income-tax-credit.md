# New Jersey Earned Income Tax Credit (NJEITC)
## Description
Refundable credit equal to 40% OF THE FEDERAL EARNED INCOME CREDIT. It reduces NJ tax and any excess is refunded. New Jersey also extends the credit to childless workers who fail ONLY the federal age test.
## How it works
- Standard case: NJEITC = 40% × federal EIC actually allowed
- Age-expansion case: a worker with NO qualifying child who is at least 18 (no upper age limit) and meets every other federal EIC rule receives a fixed amount equal to 40% of the federal maximum childless EIC. For 2025 this was $260 (40% × $649). For 2026 the federal maximum is $664, so the amount should be about $266 — CONFIRM the exact figure in the 2026 NJ-1040 instructions
## 2026 federal parameters that drive the NJ credit
| Qualifying children | 2026 federal maximum EIC | NJEITC at 40% |
|---|---|---|
| None | $664 | $265.60 |
| One | $4,427 | $1,770.80 |
| Two | $7,316 | $2,926.40 |
| Three or more | $8,231 | $3,292.40 |
- 2026 federal investment income limit: $12,200. Exceeding it eliminates BOTH the federal credit and the NJ credit
## Eligibility
- Allowed a federal EIC, OR qualifies under the NJ age expansion described above
- Age-expansion claimants cannot be listed as a dependent on another return
- MARRIED FILING SEPARATELY: eligible only with a qualifying child who lived with the taxpayer more than half of 2026 AND the spouses lived apart for the last six months of 2026. The same rule applies to civil union partners not filing jointly
## Notes
- NJ follows the federal computation, so every federal EIC rule (earned income, AGI phase-out, investment income, SSN requirements, qualifying-child tests) carries over
- Part-year residents: prorate the federal EIC (or the fixed age-expansion amount) by months of NJ residence
- Filing a return is required to get the credit even when income is below the NJ filing threshold
- The Division may request documentation of eligibility after filing
## Required Information
- Federal EIC amount and Schedule EIC
- Number of qualifying children
- Taxpayer and spouse ages, and dependency status
- Filing status and, for separate filers, living arrangements during the year
- Months of NJ residence if part-year
## Questions
- Did you claim, and were you allowed, the federal Earned Income Credit?
- Did the IRS calculate your EIC for you?
- If you have no qualifying child, were you denied the federal credit only because you are under 25 or over 64?
- Are you married filing separately, and if so, did a qualifying child live with you and did you live apart from your spouse for the last six months of the year?
## Common Errors
- Entering 40% of the federal credit BEFORE federal disallowance instead of the amount actually allowed
- Missing the NJ age expansion for childless workers age 18–24 or 65 and older
- Using the 2025 fixed amount ($260) for 2026
- Claiming while married filing separately without meeting both conditions
- Not prorating for part-year residence
- Not filing because income is under the filing threshold
