# New Jersey Child Tax Credit
## Description
Refundable credit for each dependent child age 5 or younger, for resident taxpayers with New Jersey taxable income of $80,000 or less. Separate from, and in addition to, the federal Child Tax Credit and the NJ Child and Dependent Care Credit.
## Big change for 2026: amounts up 25%
S-4531 (P.L.2026, c.26), enacted with the FY2027 budget, raises every tier by 25% for tax years 2026, 2027, and 2028. Amounts revert to the prior table in 2029. The $80,000 ceiling and the age limit did NOT change.
| NJ taxable income (Line 42) | 2026 credit per child | 2025 credit per child |
|---|---|---|
| $30,000 or less | $1,250 | $1,000 |
| Over $30,000 – not over $40,000 | $1,000 | $800 |
| Over $40,000 – not over $50,000 | $750 | $600 |
| Over $50,000 – not over $60,000 | $500 | $400 |
| Over $60,000 – not over $80,000 | $250 | $200 |
| Over $80,000 | Not eligible | Not eligible |
## How it works
- Look up NJ taxable income from Line 42 in the table, then MULTIPLY the per-child amount by the number of qualifying children
- The tiers are a STEP function, not a phase-out: one dollar over a tier boundary drops the credit to the next tier for EVERY child
- Refundable — excess over tax is paid as a refund
## Eligibility
- New Jersey resident for all or part of 2026
- Valid Social Security number or ITIN
- Allowed to claim the child as a dependent for FEDERAL income tax purposes
- Child is age 5 or younger on December 31, 2026 (born in 2021 or later)
- NJ taxable income (Line 42) of $80,000 or less
- NOT married filing separately. Must file as single, married/CU couple filing jointly, head of household, or qualifying widow(er)/surviving CU partner
## Notes
- The income test uses NJ TAXABLE income (Line 42), not federal AGI and not NJ gross income. Pension exclusions, exemptions, and the property tax deduction all lower Line 42 and can move a family into a higher tier
- Because the property tax deduction reduces Line 42, choosing the deduction over the $50 property tax credit can increase this credit. Evaluate both together
- Part-year residents: prorate by months of NJ residence (15+ days = one month)
- Low-income families who are not required to file can still claim the credit through NJ SimpleFile
- No supporting documents are enclosed with the return, but the Division may request them after filing
## Required Information
- Each child's name, SSN/ITIN, and birth year (Form NJ-1040, Line 14)
- NJ taxable income from Line 42
- Filing status
- Months of NJ residence if part-year
## Questions
- How many children will be age 5 or younger on December 31, 2026?
- Can you claim each child as a dependent on your federal return?
- What is your NJ taxable income (Line 42)?
- Are you married filing separately?
## Common Errors
- Using the 2025 table ($1,000 top tier) instead of the 2026 table ($1,250 top tier)
- Using federal AGI or NJ gross income instead of NJ taxable income for the tier test
- Counting children age 6 or older — the NJ age limit is much lower than the federal age limit (under 17)
- Claiming the credit while married filing separately
- Forgetting to enter the number of qualifying children in the box at Line 65
- Low-income non-filers missing the credit entirely because they assume no return is needed
## Prompt
- I have a 3-year-old and live in New Jersey.
- Is there a New Jersey child tax credit?
- My income is too low to file. Can I still get the NJ child credit?
