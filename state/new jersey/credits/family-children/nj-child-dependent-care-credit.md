# Child and Dependent Care Credit (New Jersey)
## Description
Refundable New Jersey credit equal to a PERCENTAGE OF THE FEDERAL child and dependent care credit, for residents with NJ taxable income of $150,000 or less. If there is no federal credit, there is no New Jersey credit.
## How it works
Worksheet J multiplies the federal credit by a percentage based on NJ taxable income (Line 42):
| NJ taxable income (Line 42) | Percentage of federal credit |
|---|---|
| $30,000 or less | 50% |
| Over $30,000 – not over $60,000 | 40% |
| Over $60,000 – not over $90,000 | 30% |
| Over $90,000 – not over $120,000 | 20% |
| Over $120,000 – not over $150,000 | 10% |
| Over $150,000 | Not eligible |
- Like the Child Tax Credit, this is a STEP table — crossing a boundary cuts the percentage on the ENTIRE federal credit
- Refundable — excess over tax is refunded
## Big change for 2026: the federal credit is bigger
New Jersey did not amend this credit for 2026, but the FEDERAL credit it is built on was enhanced by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act starting in 2026. The federal credit percentage now starts at up to 50% for the lowest incomes (up from 35%) and phases down more gradually, while the federal expense limits stay at $3,000 for one qualifying person and $6,000 for two or more. A larger federal credit flows straight through to a larger NJ credit. CONFIRM the final federal percentages against the 2026 Form 2441.
The federal exclusion for employer dependent care benefits (W-2 Box 10) also rises to $7,500 for 2026. Benefits excluded from income REDUCE the expenses eligible for the federal credit, and therefore reduce the NJ credit.
## Eligibility
- New Jersey resident
- Allowed a federal credit for child and dependent care expenses
- NJ taxable income (Line 42) of $150,000 or less
- Married filing separately only if the taxpayer meets one of the federal exceptions that allow the federal credit on a separate return
## Notes
- The income test uses NJ TAXABLE income (Line 42), not federal AGI
- Civil union couples who cannot file a joint federal return must compute a mock joint federal Form 2441 to get the base amount
- Part-year residents: use FULL-YEAR taxable income to test eligibility, then prorate the credit by months of NJ residence
- Enclose a copy of federal Form 2441 with the NJ return
## Required Information
- Federal Form 2441 and the federal credit amount
- NJ taxable income from Line 42
- Dependent care benefits from W-2 Box 10
- Filing status; civil union status
- Months of NJ residence if part-year
## Questions
- Did you claim the federal Child and Dependent Care Credit?
- What is your NJ taxable income (Line 42)?
- Did your employer provide dependent care benefits (W-2 Box 10)?
- Are you a civil union couple filing jointly in New Jersey?
## Common Errors
- Assuming the NJ credit is unchanged for 2026 because New Jersey law did not change — the federal base did
- Applying the NJ percentage to care EXPENSES instead of to the federal CREDIT
- Using federal AGI rather than NJ taxable income for the percentage
- Forgetting to reduce expenses by excluded employer dependent care benefits
- Confusing this credit with the NJ Child Tax Credit (both can be claimed)
- Not enclosing federal Form 2441
## Prompt
- I pay for daycare so I can work. Is there a New Jersey credit?
- I got the federal dependent care credit. Do I get one from New Jersey too?
