# Property Tax Credit
## Description
Refundable credit of $50 ($25 for married filing separately when spouses share a main home) for New Jersey homeowners and tenants whose main home was subject to property taxes. It is the ALTERNATIVE to the Property Tax Deduction — taxpayers get whichever is worth more.
## How it works
- Enter property taxes paid (homeowners) or 18% of rent paid (tenants) on Line 40a
- Worksheet H compares the tax saved by the deduction against the $50 credit
- If the credit wins, enter it on Line 56; if the deduction wins, enter it on Line 41 and leave Line 56 blank
- For most taxpayers with meaningful property taxes the deduction wins; the credit typically wins at low income or low tax
## Eligibility
- Domiciled in New Jersey and maintained a main home there as a homeowner or tenant during the year
- The main home was subject to property taxes, paid directly or through rent
- Tenants in multi-unit buildings must have had access to kitchen and bath facilities
- Owners of a unit in a multi-unit property: no more than four units, and no more than one commercial unit
- NJ gross income (Line 29) above the filing threshold — $20,000 (joint, head of household, qualifying widow(er)) or $10,000 (single, married filing separately)
- EXCEPTION: taxpayers (or a spouse) age 65+ or blind/disabled at year end with income at or below the threshold still qualify for the credit
## Not eligible
- Vacation homes, second homes, or property the taxpayer does not live in permanently
- Disabled veterans with a 100% property tax exemption on the main home (a tenant renting part of that property may still qualify)
- Homeowners who made P.I.L.O.T. (payments-in-lieu-of-tax) payments
- Tenants in tax-exempt property — for example, subsidized housing, campus housing, nonprofit-owned property, or a residence on which P.I.L.O.T. payments were made
## Special situations
- Shared ownership with someone other than a spouse: count only the taxpayer's ownership share of taxes
- Owner-occupied multi-unit: count only the share of taxes for the unit occupied
- Mobile home in a park: treated as a TENANT — use 18% of site fees
- Condos, co-ops, continuing care communities paying a share of taxes, life tenants, and 99-year leaseholders are treated as HOMEOWNERS
- Married filing separately with a shared main home: half the taxes on Line 40a, and $25 credit
- Part-year residents: prorate the $50 by months of occupancy of the qualified NJ home
## Form NJ-1040-HW
Seniors 65+ and blind/disabled taxpayers who are NOT required to file (NJ gross income ≤ $20,000, or ≤ $10,000 if single or married filing separately) claim the credit on Form NJ-1040-HW. Proof of age or disability is required the first time. Do NOT file both NJ-1040 and NJ-1040-HW.
## Required Information
- Property taxes due and paid on the main home, or rent / site fees paid
- Homeowner, tenant, or both (Line 40b)
- Ownership share and number of units for shared or multi-unit property
- Months of occupancy
- Age and disability status
## Questions
- Did you own or rent your main home in New Jersey in 2026?
- How much property tax or rent did you pay?
- Did you share the home or its ownership with anyone other than your spouse?
- Are you 65 or older, blind, or disabled?
- Are you also claiming a credit for taxes paid to another state?
## Common Errors
- Claiming BOTH the deduction and the credit
- Entering 100% of rent instead of 18%
- Claiming for a vacation or second home
- Claiming as a tenant in campus, subsidized, or other tax-exempt housing
- Using Worksheet H when Schedule NJ-COJ is involved (Worksheet I is required)
- Filing both NJ-1040 and NJ-1040-HW
- Confusing this credit with ANCHOR, Senior Freeze, or Stay NJ benefits, which are separate applications
## Prompt
- I rent in New Jersey. Can I claim something for rent?
- Should I take the property tax deduction or the credit?
- I'm 70, my only income is Social Security, and I own my home.
