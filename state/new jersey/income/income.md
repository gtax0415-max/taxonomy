# New Jersey Income (Form NJ-1040, Lines 15–27)
## Description
Top-level index of the income a New Jersey resident reports on Form NJ-1040. New Jersey does NOT start from federal adjusted gross income. It builds "gross income" from its own list of separate CATEGORIES, each computed under New Jersey rules, and taxes residents on income from everywhere (worldwide).
## The category rules — the single most important concept
- Each line (15, 16a, 17, 18, 19, 20a, 21, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26) is a separate category
- Gains and losses can be NETTED only WITHIN the same category (e.g., gambling losses against gambling winnings)
- A net loss in one category CANNOT offset income in another (e.g., a rental loss cannot reduce wages)
- A category with a net loss is left BLANK — never zero, never negative
- No carryback or carryforward of category losses on the NJ-1040. The only relief is the Alternative Business Calculation Adjustment (Line 35) — see new-jersey/deductions/business/alternative-business-calculation-adjustment.md
- No distinction between active and passive losses
- Line 27 total income = Lines 15 + 16a + 17 through 20a + 21 through 26 (Lines 16b and 20b are informational and are NOT added)

## What changed for 2026
- QUALIFIED SMALL BUSINESS STOCK (QSBS) GAIN EXCLUDED. For dispositions on or after January 1, 2026, gain excluded federally under IRC §1202 is also excluded from NJ gross income (A4455/S4503, enacted June 30, 2025). Affects Line 19
- ALTERNATIVE BUSINESS CALCULATION ADJUSTMENT CUT FOR HIGH INCOMES (FY2027 budget, A5323/S4537). Affects how business-category losses are used — see the deductions folder
- FEDERAL OBBBA DEDUCTIONS DO NOT APPLY. The Division of Taxation confirmed that the federal deductions for tips, overtime, and seniors do not affect the NJ-1040. Tips and overtime remain fully taxable wages in New Jersey
- DEPRECIATION DECOUPLING WIDENS IN EFFECT. Federal 100% bonus depreciation and the much larger federal §179 limit do not carry over; NJ still caps §179 at $25,000 and disallows bonus depreciation above 30% (Worksheet GIT-DEP)
## Digital assets
Page 1 of the NJ-1040 asks whether the taxpayer received, sold, exchanged, or otherwise disposed of a digital asset. Digital asset income is reportable in the category it falls into (wages paid in digital assets on Line 15; disposition of a capital asset on Schedule NJ-DOP; etc.).
## Part-year residents
Report only income received while a New Jersey resident. Partnership and S corporation income are prorated by the days in the entity's fiscal year that the taxpayer was a resident.
