# Exempt (Nontaxable) Income — New Jersey
## Description
Income that New Jersey does not tax. Many of these items differ from federal treatment, in both directions.
## Exempt in New Jersey
- Social Security benefits (all of them, regardless of income) and Railroad Retirement Tier 1 and Tier 2
- U.S. military pensions and survivor benefit payments
- Life insurance proceeds paid on death; employee death benefits
- Permanent and total disability benefits, including VA benefits
- Temporary disability benefits from the State of New Jersey or paid as third-party sick pay
- FAMILY LEAVE INSURANCE (FLI) benefits
- UNEMPLOYMENT compensation received from the state (but NOT supplemental unemployment benefits)
- Workers' Compensation; election worker compensation
- Gifts and inheritances
- Qualifying scholarships and fellowship grants
- New Jersey Lottery prizes of $10,000 or less (also multistate games NJ participates in, per prize)
- Interest and gains from obligations of New Jersey and its political subdivisions, and from direct federal obligations (U.S. Savings Bonds, Treasury bills, notes, bonds)
- Mutual fund distributions attributable to federal-obligation interest; certain distributions from NJ qualified investment funds
- Earnings on QUALIFIED distributions from 529 tuition programs, NJBEST, and 529A ABLE accounts
- Employer and employee 401(k) contributions up to the federal limit
- Certain cafeteria plan and commuter transportation benefits (NOT salary-reduction arrangements — see TB-39 and TB-24(R))
- Archer MSA contributions and distributions excluded federally
- Homeless assistance payments; welfare; child support; charitable assistance
- New Jersey property tax relief benefits (ANCHOR, Senior Freeze, Stay NJ)
- Income tax refunds from any jurisdiction (but NOT interest on refunds)
- NJ Earned Income Tax Credit payments
- Cancellation of debt
- Forgiven PPP loans; Economic Impact Payments
- Holocaust reparations; wrongful-imprisonment damages; IRC §139 disaster relief; September 11th Victim Compensation Fund payments; excluded Difficulty of Care payments
## Federal vs New Jersey differences worth flagging
- TAXABLE in NJ but NOT federally: interest on other states' municipal bonds; foreign earned income excluded federally under Form 2555; employee contributions to 403(b), 457, SEP, federal TSP, and other non-401(k) plans
- EXEMPT in NJ but taxable federally: Social Security, unemployment, U.S. Treasury interest, military pensions
- For 2026: federal deductions for tips, overtime, and seniors (OBBBA) have NO NJ counterpart
## Questions
- Did you receive Social Security, unemployment, disability, or family leave benefits?
- Did you receive a military pension?
- Did you win a New Jersey lottery prize, and was each prize $10,000 or less?
- Do you hold U.S. Treasury or New Jersey municipal bonds?
## Common Errors
- Reporting Social Security or unemployment on the NJ-1040
- Treating other states' municipal bond interest as exempt (it is taxable in NJ)
- Treating 403(b) or 457 contributions as excluded (only 401(k) contributions are)
- Counting exempt income when testing the filing threshold
## Prompt
- Is Social Security taxed in New Jersey?
- Is unemployment taxable in New Jersey?
- Are my Treasury bond interest payments taxed by New Jersey?
